# The April Collision
*Synthesis | 14min | 2026-02-12*

---

On February 8th, 2026, Prime Minister Rie Takaichi won a landslide victory in Japan's general election. The Liberal Democratic Party secured **316 seats** — the highest result since 1955. Combined with coalition partners, they hold a **352-seat supermajority** in the lower house.

That result changed everything.

Because it set up a collision that's now **eight weeks away**.

Let me walk you through what just happened, why April matters, and what it means for Japan's fiscal doom loop — and for the research we're tracking.

---

## The Supermajority

Here's what 316 LDP seats means in practical terms.

In Japan's political system, the **lower house** (the House of Representatives) can override the **upper house** (the House of Councillors) with a two-thirds supermajority.

Takaichi's coalition holds **352 seats out of 465**. That's **75.7%** — well above the two-thirds threshold.

She doesn't need to negotiate. She doesn't need to compromise. She can pass legislation, override the upper house, and reshape fiscal policy **unilaterally**.

This is the strongest single-party mandate Japan has seen in **71 years**.

And she's using it to pursue what she calls "Sanaeconomics" — a fiscal expansion program that includes:
- A ¥122 trillion budget (up from ¥115 trillion)
- A two-year suspension of the 10% food tax
- Expanded subsidies for utilities, healthcare, and housing
- Accelerated defense spending
- Infrastructure investment across rural and urban areas

All of this requires **more borrowing**. More Japanese Government Bond (JGB) issuance. Into a market where the structural buyer — life insurance companies — has already started retreating.

But that's not the collision I'm talking about.

---

## Real Wages at Zero

Here's the data point that matters most: **real wage growth in Japan is 0.0%**.

Not negative. Not positive. **Exactly zero.**

Real wages are nominal wages adjusted for inflation. If your paycheck goes up 2% but prices go up 2%, your real wage growth is zero. You're not getting ahead.

And that number — 0.0% — is a **trap** for the Bank of Japan.

Let me explain why.

The Bank of Japan spent the last two years trying to **normalize monetary policy**. For decades, Japan had deflation. Prices were falling. The BOJ held interest rates at **-0.1%** (yes, negative) and did unlimited quantitative easing to try to create inflation.

Mission accomplished. Inflation came back. Core CPI is running at 2.4% — above the BOJ's 2% target.

So now, BOJ Governor Kazuo Ueda wants to **raise interest rates** to something closer to normal. He's hinted at a **terminal rate** of 1.0% or higher — still low by global standards, but a massive shift for Japan.

The problem? **Consumers can't afford it.**

If real wages are 0.0%, and the BOJ raises rates, that increases debt servicing costs for households, corporations, and the government — but **wages aren't keeping up**. You're squeezing consumption.

Consumption is **55% of Japan's GDP**. If it contracts, the economy tips into recession.

So Ueda is trapped. He **wants** to normalize rates. But he **can't** unless real wages turn positive.

And that brings us to **Shunto**.

---

## Shunto: The Wage Negotiation

Every spring, Japan goes through a ritual called **Shunto** — the "spring wage offensive."

It's a synchronized negotiation between labor unions and major corporations. The outcomes set the tone for wage growth across the entire economy.

**Shunto 2025** delivered roughly **5.1% nominal wage increases** — the highest in 33 years. But inflation was running at 2.5-3.0%, so real wage growth was only around 2%.

**Shunto 2026** is happening in **late March**.

Here's what's at stake:

If Shunto delivers **nominal wage growth of 4.5% or higher**, and inflation cools to 2.0-2.5%, then real wages turn **positive** (around 2.0-2.5%).

That gives Ueda the political and economic cover to **hike rates** at the BOJ's April meeting.

But if Shunto disappoints — if wage growth comes in at **3.0% or lower** — and inflation stays at 2.5%, then real wages stay **flat or negative**.

In that scenario, Ueda **cannot hike** without crushing consumption. He has to pause.

---

## Takaichi's Ceiling

Now here's where it gets messy.

Prime Minister Takaichi has drawn a **0.75% ceiling** on BOJ interest rates.

Her economic advisor, Sanae Takaichi (no relation), has stated explicitly: the BOJ should not raise rates above **0.75%** in the near term. Doing so, she argues, would choke off the fiscal stimulus that Japan needs to escape stagnation.

Currently, the BOJ's policy rate is **0.50%**. Ueda has signaled he wants to move toward **0.75-1.0%** this year.

So the ceiling is **one or two rate hikes away**.

What happens if Ueda tries to breach it?

Takaichi has tools:
- **Finance Minister Katayama** can attend BOJ meetings and voice objections (not binding, but public pressure)
- **Diet questioning** — Takaichi can summon Ueda to testify before parliament
- **BOJ Law revision** — The LDP supermajority could theoretically **change the Bank of Japan Act** to give the government more direct control over monetary policy

That last option is the nuclear one. It's never been seriously pursued in modern Japan. But with a 352-seat supermajority, it's no longer unthinkable.

The question is: does Ueda fold, or does he push?

---

## The April BOJ Meeting

The Bank of Japan meets on **April 24-25, 2026**.

By that point:
- **Shunto results** will be public (late March)
- **March inflation data** will be released (early April)
- **Real wage trajectory** will be clear

If **Shunto delivers strong wage growth** (4.5%+) and **inflation cools** (2.0-2.5%), Ueda has cover to hike to 0.75% or signal that 1.0% is coming.

But if **Shunto disappoints** (3.0% or lower) and **inflation stays elevated** (2.5%+), Ueda faces a choice:

**Option A:** Hike anyway, argue that inflation control is the priority
- Risk: Crushes consumption, triggers recession, Takaichi goes nuclear

**Option B:** Pause, defer to real wage weakness
- Risk: Inflation expectations de-anchor, yen weakens further, imported inflation accelerates

This is the **collision**.

## The Fiscal-Monetary Doom Loop

Let me step back and explain why this matters beyond Japan.

Japan is running a **fiscal doom loop**:

**Step 1:** The government runs deficits (¥122 trillion budget vs. ~¥100T revenue)

**Step 2:** To fund the deficit, the government issues JGBs (borrows)

**Step 3:** Life insurers — the traditional buyers — are selling, not buying, because yields are too low relative to their liabilities

**Step 4:** To make JGBs attractive, yields have to rise (prices fall)

**Step 5:** Rising yields increase the government's debt servicing costs

**Step 6:** Higher debt servicing costs require more borrowing

**Step 7:** More borrowing pushes yields higher → repeat

The only way to **break the loop** is:
- **Stop the fiscal expansion** (Takaichi won't)
- **Suppress yields** (Ueda can't without restarting QE)
- **Let the yen collapse** (the escape valve)

For the last six months, the yen has been the escape valve. USD/JPY hit **159** in early February. That's a 35-year low for the yen.

A weak yen makes exports more competitive and inflates away debt in real terms. But it also **imports inflation** — energy, food, and raw materials cost more. And that's what's keeping inflation elevated at 2.5%.

So the yen weakens → inflation stays high → Ueda wants to hike → Takaichi says no → fiscal expansion continues → JGB yields rise → yen weakens further.

**This is circularity.** And April is when we find out whether it accelerates.

---

## D2: Monetary Dominance

In our scenario framework, we track five possible paths for Japan:

- **D1 (Fiscal Dominance):** Takaichi wins. BOJ capitulates. Yields suppressed through YCC or massive QE. Yen collapses.
- **D2 (Monetary Dominance):** Ueda wins. BOJ normalizes rates despite Takaichi. JGB market breaks. Debt spiral.
- **D3 (Orderly Normalization):** Compromise. Gradual fiscal consolidation + gradual rate hikes. Yen stabilizes. (Baseline, now unlikely.)
- **D4 (External Shock):** Geopolitical or market crisis forces both sides to capitulate. Temporary ceasefire. Problem deferred.
- **D5 (Structural Reform):** Japan implements productivity gains, wage growth, sustainable fiscal path. (Low probability.)

Before the election, **D3** (orderly normalization) was at 60% probability. After the landslide, it's down to **10-15%**.

**D1** (fiscal dominance) surged from 10% to **30-35%**. The supermajority gives Takaichi the power to override or reshape the BOJ.

But here's the twist: **D2** (monetary dominance) also increased — from 5% to **10-15%**.

Why? Because Ueda might **push back**.

If Shunto delivers strong wage growth and Ueda hikes to 0.75% over Takaichi's objections, that's a **declaration of independence**. The BOJ asserting that price stability is non-negotiable, even if it conflicts with fiscal policy.

That would be historic. And it would set up a showdown between the most powerful Prime Minister in 71 years and a central bank governor trying to restore credibility.

April is when we find out which path we're on.

## The Transmission to U.S. Markets

You might be thinking: this is a Japan problem. Why does it matter for the research we're tracking?

Two reasons.

**First: Japanese life insurers are selling U.S. Treasuries.**

We covered this in "The Stealth Exit." Japanese life insurers hold roughly **$1.5 trillion** in foreign bonds, mostly U.S. Treasuries. They've been selling at a rate of **$10-15 billion per month** since mid-2025.

Why? Because 30-year JGB yields are now **3.57%** — high enough to be attractive again. If you're a Japanese insurer, you can buy domestic bonds, avoid currency risk, and get yields that meet your liability targets.

So the fiscal-monetary collision in Japan **directly impacts U.S. Treasury demand**.

If Ueda hikes and JGB yields rise further (say, 30Y to 4.0%), Japanese insurers accelerate their Treasury selling. That adds to the **$300 billion demand hole** we're already tracking from China, Japan, and Saudi Arabia.

**Second: The yen is a funding currency.**

At 159 yen to the dollar, the yen is **extremely weak**. That makes it attractive for carry trades — borrow in yen at 0.50%, invest in U.S. assets yielding 4-5%, pocket the spread.

But if Ueda hikes aggressively and the BOJ signals it's serious about normalization, the yen **strengthens**. Suddenly, those carry trades unwind. Investors sell U.S. assets to pay back yen loans before the exchange rate moves against them.

In August 2024, a similar dynamic — when the BOJ surprised markets with a 15-basis-point hike — triggered a **6% SPX drop in three days**.

So April's BOJ decision has **direct transmission** to U.S. equity and Treasury markets.

## The Counter-Argument

Let me steelman the other side.

**Argument 1:** "Takaichi won't actually override the BOJ. It's too risky politically."

Response: Maybe. But she has a **71-year high mandate** and explicit economic advisors arguing for a 0.75% ceiling. The power is there. The question is whether she uses it.

**Argument 2:** "Ueda will compromise. Central bankers don't pick fights with governments."

Response: Historically true. But Ueda was appointed explicitly to **normalize policy** after decades of unconventional easing. If he folds now, the BOJ loses all credibility. That's a high cost.

**Argument 3:** "Shunto will deliver strong wage growth, so this is a non-issue."

Response: Possible. If Shunto delivers 4.5-5.0% and inflation cools, the collision might not happen. But corporate profit margins are under pressure. Wage growth above 4% is hard to sustain without inflation staying elevated. We'll see.

## The Bottom Line

Prime Minister Takaichi won a 71-year high mandate on February 8th. She's pursuing fiscal expansion — more borrowing, more subsidies, more spending.

BOJ Governor Ueda wants to normalize interest rates. But real wages are 0.0%, which traps him. He can't hike without crushing consumption.

The **Shunto wage negotiations** in late March will determine whether Ueda has cover to hike. The **April 24-25 BOJ meeting** is when the decision gets made.

If Ueda hikes to 0.75% over Takaichi's objections, we have a **collision** — the most powerful Prime Minister in 71 years versus a central bank trying to restore independence.

If Ueda folds, we're on the path to **fiscal dominance** — yields suppressed, yen collapse accelerates, imported inflation becomes structural.

Either way, the outcome matters for U.S. markets. Japanese life insurers are already selling $10-15 billion per month of U.S. Treasuries. If JGB yields rise further, that selling accelerates.

And if the BOJ surprises markets with aggressive normalization, carry trades unwind, and we see transmission into U.S. equities and funding markets — just like we did in August 2024.

**Eight weeks.** That's how long until the collision.
