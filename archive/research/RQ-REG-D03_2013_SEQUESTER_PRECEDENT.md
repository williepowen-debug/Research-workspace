## Executive Summary

**Core Finding:** The 2013 sequestration is a **poor historical precedent** for estimating DOGE impact on DC-area banks. The two events are fundamentally different in nature, scale, and permanence.

| Dimension | 2013 Sequester | 2025-26 DOGE |
|-----------|----------------|--------------|
| **Nature** | Furloughs (unpaid leave) | Permanent RIFs (separations) |
| **Federal Jobs Lost (DC Metro)** | ~7,600 over 12 months | ~22,100 in 5 months |
| **National Layoffs** | 1 (DOJ) | ~300,000 |
| **Private Sector Response** | Absorbed losses | Spillover layoffs in contractors |
| **Recovery Expectation** | Jobs return after budget deal | Jobs eliminated permanently |
| **Bank Credit Impact** | Minimal — EGBN posted RECORD earnings | TBD — leading indicators negative |

**Adjustment Factor:** If 2013 furloughs caused X credit impact, permanent 2026 separations should cause **3-5X multiplier** based on permanence, scale, and contractor spillover.

## 2013 Sequestration: What Actually Happened

### The Mechanics
- **Budget Control Act of 2011** triggered automatic spending cuts March 1, 2013
- $85B in budget authority cuts, but only **$42B in actual cash outlays** in FY2013
- Cuts phased over time — not all spending would have occurred in FY2013 anyway
- Furloughs = temporary unpaid leave, NOT job loss
- **GAO finding:** Justice Department laid off **ONE person** as a result

### DC Metro Economic Impact
| Metric | 2012-2013 Change | Source |
|--------|------------------|--------|
| Federal Jobs Lost | -7,600 | JBREC 2013 |
| Private Sector Jobs Gained | +46,700 | JBREC 2013 |
| Net Job Change | **+50,000** | JBREC 2013 |
| Unemployment Spread (DC vs National) | Narrowed from 3.5pp to 2.0pp | JBREC 2013 |

**Key Insight:** Private sector growth **absorbed and exceeded** federal sector losses in 2013.

### DC Bank Performance in 2013
- **Eagle Bancorp (EGBN):** Announced RECORD quarterly earnings Q4 2013
  - Net income up 33% YoY
  - Loan growth 18%
  - No credit deterioration from sequester

**Why Banks Thrived During Sequester:**
1. Furloughs were temporary — federal employees expected backpay/return
2. Private sector absorbed displaced workers
3. No contractor spillover — contracts continued
4. Housing market recovering from 2008-2012 trough
5. Federal share of economy remained high (37%)

## 2025-26 DOGE: Fundamentally Different

### Current Data (Brookings DMV Monitor, Richmond Fed)

| Metric | 2025 (Jan-June) | vs 2013 |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| Federal Jobs Lost (DMV) | -22,100 (5 months) | **2.9x** annualized rate |
| DC UCFE Claims vs Prior Year | **+543%** | Unprecedented |
| Virginia UCFE Claims vs Prior Year | +215% | |
| Maryland UCFE Claims vs Prior Year | +111% | |
| Continued Claims | Elevated for months | 2013 resolved in weeks |
| Private Sector Response | Contractor layoffs | 2013: absorption |
| Home Listings | +64% YoY (double national) | Distress signal |
| Regional Unemployment | +0.6pp faster than national | |

### Critical Differences

**1. Permanence**
- 2013: Furloughs = temporary. Workers returned. Backpay issued.
- 2026: RIFs = permanent separations. No jobs to return to.

**2. Scale**
- 2013: ~800K furloughed nationally, ~7,600 net job loss in DC over 12 months
- 2026: ~300K terminated nationally, ~22,100 in DC in 5 months

**3. Contractor Spillover**
- 2013: Contracts continued. Contractors absorbed.
- 2026: $59B in contracts terminated, $44B in grants cut
  - Leidos, ABT Global, ACDI/VOCA laying off
  - Professional/Technical Services sector: highest UI claims

**4. Private Sector Capacity**
- 2013: Private sector added 46,700 jobs to absorb 7,600 federal losses
- 2026: Private sector job growth "plateaued" (Brookings)
  - Jobs added are in construction, hospitality, healthcare — skill mismatch
  - 81.9% of WARN notices in DC/Baltimore metros

**5. Market Context**
- 2013: Post-2008 recovery, housing market bottom, rates declining
- 2026: Post-rate-hike environment, CRE under stress, regional bank fragility

## Adjusted Forecast: DOGE Impact on DC Banks

### Why 2013 Is Not a Guide

The 2013 sequestration **did not stress DC-area bank credit** because:
1. Jobs were temporarily furloughed, not eliminated
2. Private sector absorbed displacement
3. No contractor spillover
4. Rapid resolution through budget deals

**None of these conditions hold in 2026.**

### Estimated Multiplier

| Factor | 2013 Baseline | 2026 Adjustment | Multiplier |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| Permanence | Temporary | Permanent | 2-3x |
| Scale | -7,600 jobs/year | -53,000 jobs/year | 3x |
| Contractor Spillover | None | Significant | 1.5x |
| Private Absorption | Strong | Weak | 1.5x |
| **Combined** | | | **3-5x** |

If 2013 furloughs had caused measurable credit deterioration (they didn't), 2026 should cause 3-5x that impact. Since 2013 caused ~0% impact, we need alternative models.

### Alternative Precedents to Research

1. **BRAC Closures (1990s-2000s)** — permanent military base closures
2. **2008-2009 Financial Crisis** — DC fared better, but some stress
3. **Coal Country Bank Failures (2010s)** — industry-specific regional stress
