When you pre-register a read of what an institution will *say* — a central-bank statement, an OPEC communiqué, an earnings call, a regulator's release — it is natural to write tells as absences: *"no mention of participation, the hiring rate, or long-term unemployment."* **An absence-tell is only evidence if the instrument had both room and habit to speak on the topic.** In a deliberately spare source it fires for free, and it fires identically whether the issuer disagrees with you or simply says little about anything.

**LABOR, 2026-07-31 (grading a frozen FOMC labor-language card registered 7/24).** Branch (a) at ~70% rested substantially on absence-tells. They all fired — and told me almost nothing. The statement body was **~140 words**; the Chair had explicitly *"pulled back from forward guidance"* and described being *"somewhat spare on our words"* as a deliberate choice. A Fed that fully agreed with my read would have produced the same silence. Meanwhile the presser had **zero** occurrences of wages, hiring, participation, claims, payrolls, quits, openings, layoffs, benchmarks, or long-term unemployment across 41,950 characters — impressive-looking as a table, nearly worthless as discrimination, because the base rate of the instrument mentioning *any* of them was already near zero.

**What actually carried information, and it was not the counting:**
- **Presence-tells** — the two things it *did* say (*"labor markets, solid, steady"*; the reaction function *"where the labor markets are more or less at equilibrium"*) pinned the position precisely.
- **A refusal under direct expert questioning** — a WSJ reporter offered the labor-transmission channel by name (*"if that's not the channel you're relying on, what is?"*) and the Chair declined it. **A refusal to take an offered frame is strong evidence; silence in a document that says little is weak evidence.** One good question beats any number of absence counts.

**Second defect in the same card, same root:** the baseline was the June **minutes**, and a tell was written against minutes language (*"payroll gains had strengthened this year"*) then graded against a **statement** — where that phrase had never appeared. Different instrument, different authorship, different cadence. The tell had to be scored **ungradable** and deferred to the minutes. **One baseline per instrument; if a card grades two instruments, write two baselines.**

**Third, and the one with the real cost: a correct branch call can carry a wrong implication.** The card resolved to its 70% branch — and that branch's *stated consequence* ("the Fed is reading these gauges as strength, so benign prints become hawkish fuel") was **refuted by the very same text**. The institution was not reading the gauges hawkishly; it had set the whole topic aside as a satisfied constraint. Scoring the branch as a hit while a seven-day-old standing thesis built on its implication went unexamined would have been the expensive outcome. **Grade the branch and the implication as two separate line items** — the branch is where calibration lives, the implication is where the thesis damage lives ([[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]]).

**How to apply.** Before registering a language read: (1) **base-rate the instrument** — pull the last few of *this document type* from *this issuer* and count words spent on your topic; if it is habitually near zero, absence-tells are not tests ([[finding_base_rate_the_instrument_before_its_event_table]]). (2) **Rank tells at registration**: direct-question responses > presence/phrasing tells > absence-tells; where an absence-tell is used, condition it (*"no mention of X **in a document that discusses the topic for ≥N words**"*). (3) **One baseline per instrument**, diffed like-for-like. (4) **Write the branch's implication as a separately gradable claim**, so a right-bucket/wrong-consequence result cannot pass as a clean hit. (5) An unchanged sentence across two releases **is** a finding — pre-commit to that reading so it is not mistaken for no-data ([[finding_silent_blank_evades_review]]).

Applies to anyone grading official language: FOMC/BOJ/ECB/PBOC statements and minutes, OPEC communiqués, regulator releases, earnings-call scripts.
