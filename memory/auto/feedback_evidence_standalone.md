When presenting new research or data (e.g., microstructure analysis, dark pool data), always evaluate what the evidence says ON ITS OWN before layering in thesis context. Will values seeing how evidence stands independently — it helps him understand whether we're finding genuine signal or just confirming what we already believe.

**Why:** Will asked whether REGINALD's WAL earnings view was based on just the current session's microstructure data or the full WAL research folder. The answer was "just the session data" — and the view changed materially after reading the WAL subfolder. Will appreciated the transparency and said this approach "helps me understand how the evidence we are zeroed in on stands on its own."

**How to apply:** When summarizing findings from any research task: (1) State what this data says independently. (2) Note what it DOESN'T tell us. (3) Then layer in what it means combined with other evidence. Never skip step 1 by jumping straight to thesis-confirming conclusions.
