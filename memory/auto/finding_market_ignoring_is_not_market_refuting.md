**The tape not caring is not the tape disagreeing.** When a position or thesis is going the wrong way, the honest question is *which claim just took damage* — and there are two, with different evidence standards:

- **MECHANISM claim** — "X is deteriorating, and here is the primary-source series showing it." Refuted only by **contrary measurement of the same series**.
- **TIMING claim** — "and the market will reprice it by date D." Refuted by **price**.

Price is dispositive for the second and **carries no information about the first**. Collapsing them is the fastest way to abandon a working instrument, or to keep a broken one because the price happened to cooperate.

**Why (OTTO, 2026-08-14).** OTTO held a primary-source read on Carvana's loan-sale economics: **Other (finance) GPU/unit $2,869 → $2,807 → $2,666** across three quarters, decline *accelerating*, Adj EBITDA margin 12.4% → 10.4% on a **record** quarter — plus an independent 10-D panel showing the same shelf's collateral worse than deep-subprime comparables, seasoning-controlled. Two weeks later CVNA traded **$75.92, +14.5% ABOVE the pre-print close**, having fully round-tripped the guide-down and gone on to near its pre-short-report level. The market had priced a 52%-revenue-growth quarter with a compressing finance line as **good**.

**Nothing in that tape touched the series.** No filing revised the GPU numbers; no 10-D contradicted the panel. The mechanism claim was **ignored**, not falsified. What the price *did* refute was any near-term timing claim — and OTTO had deliberately never made one, so the correct entry was: *"the instrument is intact; the timing leg keeps disconfirming; conviction unchanged; expected payoff further out."* The tempting entry — quietly downgrading the finance-GPU work because it "didn't play out" — would have destroyed a measurement to explain a price.

**The symmetric trap is worse, and it is the one that costs money.** The same conflation run the other way lets a *rising* position confirm a thesis it never tested. If price is allowed to be evidence about mechanism, it will be evidence in whichever direction it happens to move, which means it is not evidence at all.

**How to apply:**
- When a price moves against you, **write the two claims out separately before writing the conclusion**, and state which one the move actually addresses. If you find you never made a timing claim, say so explicitly — "this does not refute anything I claimed" is a legitimate and important finding, not a dodge.
- **A thesis with no timing claim cannot be refuted by price. That is a property of the thesis, not a defence of it** — and it is exactly why such theses need a *separate* forward test with a real resolve date, or they become unfalsifiable. Pair this with [[finding_prereg_verdict_boundary_must_be_a_number]].
- **Never retire an instrument on price.** Retire it on a failed positive control, a contrary measurement, or a demonstrated design defect — see [[finding_claim_outlives_its_discredited_instrument]] for the mirror case (instrument fails ≠ claim false).
- Record the adverse tape **in the state file, at the same prominence as supporting evidence** ([[finding_documented_divergence_as_discipline]]). OTTO's own prior line — "the guide-down was absorbed in three sessions" — was *understated to the point of being wrong in spirit* eleven days later, and a stale price in a dashboard is how a timing failure hides.

**Distinct from** [[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]] (a prediction resolves on the letter you wrote, not the thesis behind it) — that one is about *grading*; this one is about *what price is admissible evidence for* in the first place.
