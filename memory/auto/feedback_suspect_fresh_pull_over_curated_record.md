When a number you just pulled disagrees with one already in your curated records, the instinct is to trust the fresh pull and "correct" the stored one. **That instinct is backwards.** "Just fetched" and "reliable" are independent axes — reliability comes from source TIER and prior verification, not from recency. A curated record earned its place (it was sourced + cross-checked when logged); a fresh aggregator hit has zero provenance. So **suspect the fresh pull first**, and treat a fresh-vs-stored conflict as a signal to verify BOTH against a primary — never as license to overwrite the record.

**Why:** Sat 6/20, with `cpi_japan.py` down (manual CPI pull required), a boot web-search surfaced **stockpil.com** (a low-tier content-farm aggregator) reporting Japan April core CPI 2.2% / May 2.1%. SAM carried the wrong 2.1% into the boot report AND flagged its own CORRECT record (April core 1.4 / core-core 1.9) as the suspect "discrepancy." Had it "corrected" the record, prediction **SAM-27** (April core <2.0%) flips from a confirmed TRUE to a FALSE — corrupting the calibration scoreboard off one bad aggregator pull. Cross-check vs CNBC / Japan Times / Trading Economics resolved it: SAM's records matched the wire EXACTLY (April 1.4 / 1.9; May headline 1.5 / core 1.4 / core-core 1.8); stockpil was simply wrong. **Re-confirmed 6/21 — and the trap reproduced live:** a broad unrestricted search re-surfaced stockpil first and parroted its 2.1% again, while a wire-restricted search + the official Stats-Bureau series (via Trading Economics) returned the right figures. Will's "cross-check, not footnote" insistence forced both resolutions.

**How to apply:** On any manual data pull, **first-touch a wire/primary-tier source** (Reuters/CNBC/Bloomberg/Japan Times for prints; the issuing agency — BOJ/MOF/Stats Bureau — for the official series), NOT a content-farm aggregator that ranks first in a naive search. Think in source tiers: **primary/wire > your own verified record > aggregator.** When a fresh number fights a stored one, get ≥2 independent wire confirmations and resolve the conflict AT THE SOURCE before editing any doc or score — be most suspicious of the input with the least provenance, which is usually the thing you just grabbed. Beware partial-correctness (stockpil got the May headline 1.5% right while blowing the core 2.1% vs actual 1.4%) — it makes a bad source look credible. In a WebSearch, restricting `allowed_domains` to wire outlets is a cheap way to first-touch the right tier.
