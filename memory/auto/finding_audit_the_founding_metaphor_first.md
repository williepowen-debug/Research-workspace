When a thesis is built on a **historical analogy** ("China will bankrupt the US the way the US bankrupted the USSR") or any load-bearing base-rate premise, **run a cheap audit of that premise FIRST**, before the expensive empirical legs. Sequence the whole slate **cheapest-kill-first**: order questions by which one, if it comes back "wrong," reshapes or kills the most downstream work — and do the cheap ones of those first (pre-mortem ordering).

**Why:** 2026-07-24, Will's "China bankrupts the US via AI spending (reverse-Cold-War)" thesis. The P0 analog audit found the founding metaphor is **largely a myth** — serious post-archival scholarship (Podvig, Kotkin, Miller, Gaidar, Zubok) rejects/heavily-qualifies "Reagan/SDI spent the USSR into bankruptcy"; the USSR chose *cheap asymmetric* countermeasures it never deployed, its military burden was chronic/flat (rose via a shrinking denominator, not a Reagan surge), and collapse was overdetermined by internal stagnation + an oil/hard-currency shock. One cheap fan-out (2M tokens) reframed the entire research program *before* any expensive empirical legs — away from "can China force the US to overspend" toward "does either power carry the USSR's *actual* kill-mechanism." P1 then confirmed the transmission also fails on the modern side (private AI capex reaches the federal balance sheet via ~$0 committed dollars).

**How to apply:** for any analogy- or base-rate-driven ask, make prompt #0 an audit of the premise itself ("did X actually cause Y, or is that popular oversimplification?" / "what's the real base rate?"). Tag popular/triumphalist narratives vs scholarly consensus; the strongest-defensible version of the claim is usually far weaker than the popular one. Then re-sort the remaining slate against the corrected premise. A second reusable trap surfaced same-day: **announced ≠ committed dollars** (Stargate "$500B" → $0 federal + largely un-raised; DeepSeek "$5.6M" = notional marginal cost) — always separate headline announcements from actual appropriated/obligated figures.
