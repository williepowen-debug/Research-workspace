When a signal stack combines a **base-rate / population layer** (mechanism-agnostic — "is the signature present, what does history say it pays") with **mechanism-discriminator layers** (each tuned to recognize one specific success/failure mechanism), the two layer types fail differently and must not be weighted as co-equal confirmers. The base-rate layer is robust to mechanism-surprise because it never asks *why*. A discriminator is only as good as its enumerated mechanism set: when the live driver is a mechanism it doesn't know, it does **not** abstain — it emits its default reading (e.g. "inactive / absorbed") as a **confident wrong veto** on what may be a base-rate winner.

**Why:** VIOLET's 4-layer divergence stack got its first clean live test on the 6/5/26 NFP shock (VIX +40%). L1 (population/base-rate, mechanism-agnostic) paid forward exactly — +40% at td-4, inside its 65% peak>50%@fwd60 base rate. All three mechanism-discriminators failed because the actual driver (consensus-miss labor shock + AI/factor concentration unwind) was in none of their vocabularies: L2 "absorbed-trap" assumed a consensus-aligned catalyst (NFP was a 2.15× miss); L3 "Volmageddon-shape matrix" hit a quadrant it had never observed (N=1); L4 "short-vol crowding" (COT) was *factually correct that crowding was absent* and that correctness was useless because the move came from where L4 never looks. Clinching evidence: the **same** L2–L4 filters were RIGHT on the prior Episode-17 fire (correctly vetoed a losing trade — the mechanism there genuinely was absorbed-trap) and WRONG on 6/5 (would have vetoed a +40% winner). Same reading, opposite correct outcomes, because the discriminating mechanism differed and a discriminator sees only one. This is a structural property of the stack, not a calibration error inside any layer.

**How to apply:** (1) Anchor sizing on the mechanism-agnostic / base-rate layer — it is the only one validated under a *novel* mechanism. (2) Give every discriminator layer an explicit "**mechanism-not-recognized → abstain / return null**" output, so when the live driver isn't its mechanism it *defers* to the base rate instead of overriding it. A null defers; a false "inactive" vetoes — and a confident wrong veto on a base-rate winner is worse than having no filter at all. (3) Treat triangulation across discriminators skeptically when they share an assumed mechanism: three views of the same assumption *feel* like confirmation but aren't. Generalizes to any agent stacking a base-rate signal with mechanism-specific filters — CARL labor/inflation regime filters, BROCK credit-stress discriminators, HENRY gamma-regime gates.
