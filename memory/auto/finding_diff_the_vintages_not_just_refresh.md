**For any recurring official document set — budget justification books, 10-Ks, agency outlooks, monthly statistical releases — refreshing to the latest vintage is necessary but NOT sufficient. The most decision-relevant findings are often *reversals*, and a reversal is invisible in either edition alone. It exists only in the delta.**

**Worked case (DEWEY DR-3, 2026-08-02, both [PRIMARY]):** MDA procurement justification books.
- **FY2026 book:** *"termination of SM-3 Block IB new production… After FY 2024, the Department will discontinue the SM-3 Block IB procurements in favor of SM-3 Block IIA"* — plus a line item for **"SM-3 Block IB Production Shutdown costs"** and spares bought *"in anticipation of supply chain attrition following termination of new production."*
- **FY2027 book:** FY2025 restated upward (6 → 8 CPU **+ 21 supplemental**) and **"procurement of up to seventy-eight (78) SM-3 Block IB missiles"** requested.

⇒ **A production line the Department formally decided to shut down is being run at ~78/yr under war pressure.** Neither book says this. The FY2027 book simply states a request; the FY2026 book simply states a termination. Only the pair shows the reversal — and the reversal is the finding.

**Why the standard discipline misses it:** staleness rules push you to the *newest* edition and discard the old one as superseded. But the superseded edition is the **baseline that makes the new number meaningful.** A number that is unremarkable in isolation ("78 missiles requested") becomes load-bearing against its predecessor ("this line was terminated").

**How to apply:** when a recurring official document carries a load-bearing quantity, pull the **prior edition too** and diff the same line item. Look specifically for: terminated-then-restarted programs, quantities restated upward/downward for a *past* year, line items that appear or vanish, and organizational transfers (in the same run, MDA's FY2027 book showed THAAD moved to the Army — *"No funding is requested in FY 2027 for THAAD"* — which silently relocates a whole program out of the book you were reading).
