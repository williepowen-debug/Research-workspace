**A well-written analysis keeps its constructions apart in the body and merges them in the summary — and the summary is the section written to be lifted, therefore the only section most readers will ever quote.** The result is a fidelity loss with no defective upstream: the source is correct, every reader who cites it is citing the source, and the merged form propagates anyway.

**⇒ THE LOAD-BEARING PART, and what separates this from its neighbours: nothing upstream is wrong.** `[[finding_inherited_defect_propagates_though_both_ends_act_correctly]]` needs a defective instrument; `[[finding_rederived_signal_loses_the_senders_caveats]]` needs a hop between agents. **Here there is one artifact, it is right, and both readers read it.** They read the section it advertises as the takeaway.

## The instance — n=2, two desks, same day, independently, one sentence

**2026-08-23. BOND's non-author re-derivation of MIDAS's 8/19 gold-vs-real-rates attribution** (Will-commissioned, and excellent). Its body is scrupulous:

- **§3.3** tables six rows of the **currency-stripped** construction ⇒ *"90–93% unexplained, on every window and both instruments."*
- **§3.4(c)** states both outright: *"the correct canonical figure is **87–93% (univariate)** or **90–93% (currency-stripped)**."*

**§6 — the quotable replacement sentence — merges them:** *"Real rates explain **7–15%** of the **currency-stripped** move; **87–93%** is unexplained by real rates, **on every window and both instruments**."* The **7** is the currency-stripped 7.4%; the **15** is the univariate top; the **87–93%** is univariate; the qualifier is §3.3's, about the *other* construction. **One sentence, both constructions, no label.** It is the sentence that was adopted verbatim into `HEARTBEAT §8` and therefore into the ruling.

**Desk 1 — MIDAS.** Encoded from §6 across **six surfaces**, attaching the univariate band to the currency-stripped construction. Caught it only on reopening the artifact for an unrelated reason. Its own lesson (L-26): *encode from the BODY section, not the abstract.*

**Desk 2 — PROME**, independently, hours earlier. Compared §6 against a fleet memory that cited **90–93%** *correctly labelled currency-stripped*, concluded the two contradicted, and shipped it to BOND as a 🟠 defect **in BOND's own work** — stamped *"PROME-verified"* on having confirmed that two strings differ. Its explicit reasoning: *"this is not a construction difference — if it were, the two figures would carry different qualifiers."* ⛔ **That inference used the shared qualifier as evidence AGAINST the fusion, when the shared qualifier IS the fusion's fingerprint** — it ruled out the correct explanation with the one datum pointing straight at it. PROME's own HANDOFF, daily memory and ORCH_LOG had all recorded *"currency-stripped 90-93%"* correctly hours earlier; it contradicted its own record without opening it.

## Why diligence does not catch it

1. **The summary is doing its job.** It exists to be lifted; both desks lifted it. There is no lapse to point at.
2. **Every number-keyed check passes clean.** Each figure is individually correct — and here also *Will-ruled* — so freshness, reproduction and value checks all return green. MIDAS: *"it survived because the NUMBER was right and Will-ruled."* Kin: `[[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]`.
3. **The author is the last person who can see it.** §3.4(c) and §6 are both true to whoever wrote them, because they hold the distinction in their head while writing both.
4. **Downstream it looks like a contradiction, not a merge** — which invites a reader to escalate it as someone else's defect. That is the failure mode that costs a peer their afternoon.

## How to apply

- **(a) Writing a summary of your own work:** for every place the body says *"A **or** B,"* verify the summary does not say *"A"* wearing B's qualifier. The abstract is the highest-leverage sentence you will write and gets the least review.
- **(b) Encoding from someone's artifact: cite the body section, never the abstract — and name the section you encoded from on your own surface**, so a later reader can tell which one you used.
- **(c) The self-tell, and it is the cheapest one here: if your own measured value sits OUTSIDE a band you are about to publish, that is the check firing.** MIDAS had computed the currency-stripped residual itself at **93.0–93.8%** and wrote **87–93%** beside it. A value that cannot fit inside its own band means you are labelling two constructions with one name.
- **(d) Before reporting that two artifacts contradict, find the sentence in the source that NAMES BOTH.** Do not infer from a shared qualifier that only one construction exists. Two figures differing is not a contradiction when they answer different questions — `[[finding_exact_level_authenticates_a_wrong_direction]]`, `[[finding_crosscheck_with_free_parameter_validates_nothing]]`.
- **(e) A retraction reaches the artifact that ASSERTED the claim, never the artifacts that REPEATED it.** Retracting PROME's packet left MIDAS's packet in the same inbox asking for the identical non-reconcile — sweep for surviving carriers, do not assume one banner covers the class. `[[finding_transfer_completes_only_when_the_receiver_encodes]]`.

**Both desks found it the same way, and it is the only method that worked all day: open the primary to check something you expect to simply confirm.** MIDAS: *"a confirming check is exactly the one nobody runs."*

## Facet added 2026-08-24 (VULCAN, via PROME's catch): n+1 — the merged distinction was a **WINDOW**, the carrier was a **cross-agent MESSAGE**, and it nearly produced a false defect report against the AUTHOR

**Same mechanism, three new things.** VULCAN delivered a ruling to PROME in both channels the contract requires: a memo, and a `SendMessage` summary. **The memo was correct and explicitly labelled** — *"Foundry is UP on the rolling month (TSM +1.56%)."* **The message dropped the label** into a paragraph whose every other figure was expressly *"over the same 5 sessions."* On five sessions **TSM is −4.63%: the opposite sign.** PROME nearly reported it to Will as a defect in the memo's Leg D, opened the primary instead, and found the memo right.

**① The distinction merged was a measurement WINDOW, not a construction.** The instances above merge *currency-stripped vs univariate*; this one merges *rolling-1-month vs 5-session*. **Same class, new axis** — and windows are the more dangerous axis in a market desk, because a window change can **flip the sign** while a construction change usually only moves the magnitude. Kin: `[[finding_window_start_at_an_extremum_inverts_the_move]]`, `[[finding_normalization_choice_picks_opposite_winners]]`.

**② 🔑 THE AUTHOR-SIDE TELL, and it is sharper than rule (a): a figure that omits its own window INHERITS the surrounding one, silently.** Every other number in that paragraph restated its window. The merged one was **the single figure that did not** — so it did not read as unlabelled, it read as *carrying the paragraph's label*. **An omitted qualifier is not neutral; in a qualified list it is an assertion of the neighbours' qualifier.**
- ⇒ **Sharpening of (a): don't only check that the summary doesn't say "A" wearing B's qualifier — check for the figure wearing NO qualifier inside a list where the others have one.** That is the one that got relabelled by proximity, and it is invisible to the author because the author knows which window it came from. Kin: `[[finding_scope_boundary_asserted_from_proximity]]`.

**③ SCOPE EXTENSION — a cross-agent MESSAGE is a summary, and it is the highest-travel surface there is.** Every instance above is a *document* abstract (§6, an executive summary). This one is a **`SendMessage` to a coordinator** — which is worse, because ⓐ it is what the coordinator reads *first* and often *only*, ⓑ it is what gets relayed onward to the operator, and ⓒ unlike a document it has **no body underneath it** to fall back to. The memo's correct sentence existed, but nothing in the message pointed at it.
- ⇒ **Rule (b) generalises: cite the body section in the MESSAGE too.** A summary line carrying a figure should name where in the artifact that figure is labelled, precisely so the recipient can do what PROME did.
- ⇒ And the delivery contract that says *"deliver in both channels"* is a **fidelity risk**, not just redundancy: two renderings of one finding is two chances to merge what the body separates.

**④ NEW DIRECTION — the near-miss was a FALSE DEFECT REPORT AGAINST THE AUTHOR.** Above, the merge produces a wrong claim downstream, or a false defect against a *peer*. Here the reader was about to escalate a defect **in the author's own correct work**, to the operator. **This VALIDATES rule (d) rather than extending it** — *"before reporting that two artifacts contradict, find the sentence in the source that names both"* — and it is the first recorded instance of (d) being executed and PAYING. PROME's words: *"I opened the memo instead."*
- ⚠️ **Cost asymmetry worth naming: a merged claim travelling is a slow error; a false defect report is a FAST one** — it burns the reporter's credibility, the author's session, and the operator's attention simultaneously, and it arrives wearing the reporter's diligence.

**⑤ And the honest substantive residue, recorded because the summary defect was the smaller half.** The catch exposed that the leg's **equity** half is window-dependent (TSM −4.63% / 5-sess vs +1.88% / 1-mo) while its **order-book** half (TSMC cumulative YoY **+37.0%**, SEC 6-K issuer-primary) is not. **The ruling never rested on that leg** — it rested on a pre-specified rule with two quantified legs — **but a supporting leg whose sign depends on the reader's window is a weak leg presented as a strong one.** ⇒ **When a leg fuses a fast series (equity) with a slow one (reported revenue), state each on its own clock or lead with the slow one.** The slow half is the half that is actually about the mechanism.

## ★ EXTENSION 2026-08-27 (BOND) — THE ABSTRACT IS ALSO WHERE A **CORRECTION** ARRIVES LAST. n=4 in one session, on one figure.

The memory above is about the abstract **merging** what the body separates. **The same surface has a second, independent failure mode: when the merged figure is finally corrected, the abstract is the LAST place the fix reaches — and the FIRST place a reader lands.**

**Measured, one document, one day.** A published band (`87–93%`) turned out to be a fusion of two constructions. It was corrected **four separate times**, and three of those "corrections" left a stale copy standing somewhere with higher travel than the place just fixed:

1. Fixed at the **canonical definition line** → the *suggested-publication block* still carried the fused version.
2. Fixed in the **publication block** → the definition line's **tail** still carried the superseded band **plus a vindication clause the other desk had formally refused**.
3. Fixed in the artifact → **`STATUS`** still carried the refused clause.
4. Each round was found by **someone else** (a peer re-deriving, then a coordinator reading mid-document), never by the author's own sweep.

**Why it recurs even when you know about it.** A correction is authored **at the place you were proven wrong** — the table, the definition, the cell someone cited. That is a *body* location. **The abstract is downstream of your attention precisely because it is upstream of the reader's.** And it reads as already-handled: you just fixed "the number," and the abstract contains "the number," so it feels covered.

**⇒ RULE: when correcting a figure, fix the ABSTRACT / summary / publication block FIRST, then walk outward to the body.** Inverting the natural order is the whole fix. Then grep the corrected string across every live surface before committing — **and expect the highest-travel copy to be the last one your eye finds.**

⚠️ **Two riders from the same episode:**
- **A stale tail can carry more than a stale number.** Round 2's residue included a *vindication-adjacent claim about another desk* that the other desk had explicitly declined on the record. **A superseded sentence keeps asserting things about people who have already objected.**
- **The correction that most needs this rule is the one you make while fixing this class.** Round 3 here was a derived figure repaired with *another derived figure* — authored inside the very pass that was correcting derived-figure defects. [[finding_a_correction_pass_is_unreviewed_work]] is the general form; this is its sharpest instance.
