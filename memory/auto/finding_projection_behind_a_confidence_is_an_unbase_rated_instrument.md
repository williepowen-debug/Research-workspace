**A registered threshold gets scrutiny. The projection that sets your CONFIDENCE in it usually gets none — and it is an instrument too.**

The threshold is the visible object: it has a number, a window, a resolution rule, and (if [[finding_base_rate_the_threshold_before_building_it]] was honoured) a base rate. But the *confidence* attached to it is almost always derived from a second, invisible object: a **projection** — a run-rate, a "front-loaded season" pace, a share-of-total, a "current trend continues." **That projection is a claim about the world with its own error distribution, and it is routinely asserted rather than measured.**

**The tell: a confidence that has not moved in weeks, sitting on a phrase rather than a figure.** "On pace for", "front-loaded, so full-year ≈", "annualizing to", "run-rating at."

**Incident (MARCO, 2026-08-21).** A prediction — *H-2A certifications >425K in FY2026* — had been carried at **88% for 50 days**. The threshold itself was sound. The 88% rested on: *"H-2A is front-loaded (spring planting) so full-year ≈ H1 + ~200K H2 ≈ ~455-465K."* **The ~200K H2 was never base-rated against a single prior year.**

When Q3 published: **Q3 alone came in NEGATIVE (−3.99% YoY), the first negative quarter in the series.** H2's first half delivered **95.5K**, so the assumed ~200K H2 now required a **Q4 larger than Q3 — which no year in the base rate does.**

**Base-rating it took one pass** (pulling two prior full-year files and computing the Q4 share): Q4 was **19.29%** of FY2024 and **20.34%** of FY2025 — **74,234 and 80,947**. The bar needed **75,133**, which sits *between the two historical Q4s*: the prior year's Q4 clears it, the year before's **misses by 899**. The projection went from "comfortably above, ~7-9% cushion" to "**central case, ~0.6-3.3% cushion, and a miss needs only a continuation of the trend already in evidence**." Confidence **88% → 72%**.

**What makes this worth its own rule rather than folding into threshold hygiene:** nothing was wrong with the threshold, the data, or the pull. The failure was that **a load-bearing quantitative claim was wearing a qualitative disguise** ("front-loaded season"), so it never triggered the verification reflex that a *number* would have. It also propagated: "~455-465K pace" had been shipped to consumers and was sitting in the canonical thesis doc.

## How to apply

1. **When you write a confidence, name the projection under it in figures** — not "front-loaded so it clears," but "needs X in the remaining window; prior years delivered Y and Z."
2. **Base-rate the projection against ≥2 prior periods, using the IDENTICAL aggregation**, and check the result reproduces a figure you already carry ([[finding_loadbearing_number_must_be_reproducible]]). If your reconstruction of last year doesn't match last year, stop.
3. **State the cushion as a percentage of the bar**, and state **what change would flip it** — "a miss needs only a continuation" is a very different posture from "a miss needs a reversal."
4. **A confidence that hasn't moved while its window has been consuming itself is a flag**, not evidence of stability. Age the confidence, not just the data.
