**A composite is least reliable at the amplitude that makes you want to use
it.** AEOLUS hit this three times in one session (2026-08-12, the El Niño → PJM
winter-sign unblock for WATT):

1. **The analogue set collapses exactly when the signal is strong.** "Strong El
   Niño winters" at ONI ≥ +2 gave n=2 same-amplitude analogues (2015-16,
   2023-24) — and they SPLIT 1-1 on the question asked (winter peak load).
   A composite mean over n=2 split cases is a coin flip wearing an average's
   clothes.
2. **A composite governs the MIDDLE of the distribution, not the tail you're
   pricing.** The CPC composite gives a defensible sign for winter-mean
   energy (DOWN — storm track shifts south). It gives NO sign for a 1–3 day
   peak-load tail: 2023-24 was the warmest US winter on record and PJM still
   peaked 134,777 MW through full cold-ops. Mean-suppression and
   tail-suppression are different claims; the composite only supports one.
3. **The inversion risk is highest at high amplitude** — AEOLUS's own 8/3
   snowbird packet to MARCO reasoned validly from an INVERTED composite
   premise (claimed active/cold Northern-tier; CPC says milder/less stormy).
   Strong-event narratives are memorable enough that the sign gets carried
   from memory instead of re-read at the primary.

**How to apply:** before citing any composite/analogue read at extreme
amplitude: (a) count the same-amplitude analogue set and state n next to the
claim; (b) ask whether the question is about the distribution's middle (composite
can answer) or its tail (composite cannot — use occurrence/variance framing);
(c) re-pull the primary for the SIGN, never carry it from a prior packet.
