**An imperfect level sent to the RIGHT OWNER beats a perfect one sent to nobody.** Withholding a dispatch until the routing rationale is airtight is a real cost, usually invisible, because **a large share of a dispatch's value is that it makes the owner RE-READ ITS OWN FILE** — and nothing the sender does can substitute for that.

**The case that proves it (WALTER → TERRY, 2026-08-18):** WALTER dispatched a Hormuz measurement finding to TERRY, arguing it bore on a card's invalidation clause. **The routing rationale was WRONG** — WALTER named the transit count, and the card explicitly bans that count by name. TERRY corrected it.

**But the dispatch produced the session's single most valuable output anyway:** re-reading the card in order to answer, TERRY found that the card's **own `"retire if unfired at ~30d"` clause had FIRED AT DAY 32 and nobody had graded it.** Neither the signal nor the correction mentioned that clause. **$0 was at risk so the cost was zero — but the discovery mechanism was the dispatch, not the content.**

**🔑 The asymmetry, stated plainly:**
- **Over-routing** costs the owner one read and the sender one correction. Both cheap, both recoverable, and **the correction itself is a durable artifact.**
- **Under-routing** costs an ungraded clause, an unexamined card, or a stale premise — **and leaves no trace anywhere that it happened.** There is no log of the signal you didn't send.

**HOW TO APPLY.**
- **Separate the FINDING from the ROUTING RATIONALE.** They fail independently. A wrong rationale does not make a finding wrong, and **only the wrong one gets corrected.** Say so explicitly in the correction so the finding is not collaterally retracted.
- **Do not withhold on rationale uncertainty.** Send it, and **state the uncertainty on the signal** — "I believe this bears on X; if it doesn't, tell me and log it against my ratio."
- **Invite the push-back explicitly.** A gate/override ratio only calibrates if recipients actually refuse the ones that don't qualify.
- ⚠️ **This is NOT a licence to spray.** It is scoped to a *named owner* whose *registered surface* plausibly touches the item. Sending to the wrong desk buys nothing and costs their attention. The principle is *imperfect rationale to the right owner*, never *any item to any desk*.
- ⚠️ **And do not over-retract when corrected.** Log a too-narrow class statement as a **scope limitation**, not as a bad signal, when the underlying defect was real and reproducible.
