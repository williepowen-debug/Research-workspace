**Editing a document's header is not editing the document — and it is the specific edit that makes an unmaintained body invisible.** A reader checks the version line, the `Last Updated:` stamp, or the top banner to decide whether to trust the file. When those are current and the body is not, **the freshness signal actively certifies the stale content.** An obviously old file gets re-derived; a file with a fresh header gets cited.

**Why:** the header is cheap to update and feels like closing the loop, so it gets touched at closeout when the body does not. Worse, the header is usually where a *correction* is announced — so the very act of documenting "X is now retired / re-specified / downgraded" in the header can leave the body still asserting X, with the header serving as evidence the author handled it.

**Measured n=3 in a single BOND staleness sweep (2026-08-18), each having survived 21+ days:**
- `THESIS.md` v1.1.3's version line declared the falsifier apparatus "RE-SPECIFIED from tail-keyed to composition-keyed." Only the header had been changed; three tail-keyed gates were still live in the body. *(Caught 7/28 by the author, who then wrote the correction into — the header.)*
- The same file's status line read *"dealer long-end inventory is at a record"* while **item 1 of the same document** recorded that record as retired and unwound −17.4%. Two paragraphs apart, opposite claims.
- `monitors/DEALER_CAPACITY.md`'s header announced **"✅ GAP CLOSED — the record has unwound"** while its `## Current Read` section three lines below still said *vector 3, fresh record highs, 4-trigger ARMED*.

**How to apply:**
- **When a header announces a change, the same commit must carry the body edit that change implies.** If you write "re-specified" / "retired" / "downgraded" into a header, grep the file for the thing you just retired **before** committing.
- **Read load-bearing docs end-to-end before declaring them clean.** Section-targeted edits leave the untouched sections asserting the old state, and n=3 above are all section-targeted edits.
- **Treat an internal contradiction as a first-class defect, not an untidiness** — two sections of one file disagreeing means at least one is wrong *and* nobody has read it whole.
- **Corollary for reviewers:** never take a `Last Updated` stamp as evidence a body is current. Audit by content vintage — cf. [[finding_mtime_is_corrupted_by_git_sync]] and [[finding_plausible_stale_value_evades_review]].

Distinct from [[finding_banner_is_a_warning_not_a_fix]] (a banner correctly *warns* and merely doesn't fix; a fresh header actively *reassures*) and from [[finding_a_ruling_governs_the_next_write_not_the_existing_state]] (that is a ruling vs. the stock of existing files; this is one file contradicting itself). Related: [[finding_dated_stamp_is_a_trigger_not_a_shield]].

**n=6 — three MORE instances, WAL desk, 2026-08-23, and they sharpen the rule in a way the first three did not.** The 8/20 WAL session wrote `STATUS.md` **seven times** in one day and re-stamped its header on each. Three days later a bounded touch found, on that same file:
- The **Price-discipline exit-rule state cell** reading **"🟢 $83.11, buffer +$5.11"** — a **JULY** price — sitting **~100 lines below a header that called the buffer "the tightest in the record."** The two are on the same screen in any reader wide enough, and the header is what everyone quoted.
- **`BOTTOM LINE`** carrying **"12.4% / spot $81.77"** — two thesis versions and several sessions stale — under a header announcing the *current* thesis version.
- **`STATUS.md:5` and the `THESIS.md` version-history footer** both still reading **"v2.3 is CURRENT"** three days after v2.4 shipped. ★ **A thesis has THREE homes for its version — the header, the "what moved" section, and the version-history footer — and a bump reliably touches the first two.** The footer is written once per version and re-read never.
- Same session, same class: **`INDEX.md`, the cold-spawn entry point**, published **`PT $52-74` under a `Thesis v2.4` header.** Of all surfaces to carry a stale value, the one a fresh session reads *first* and trusts *most*.

**★ The sharpening:** the original n=3 read as *authors announce a change in the header and forget the body*. These three are worse — **nobody announced anything.** The header was simply *refreshed*, repeatedly, by a diligent owner, and each refresh **re-certified** everything beneath it. ⇒ **The risk is not proportional to neglect; it is proportional to how OFTEN you re-stamp.** The file you maintain most attentively is the one whose stale cells are hardest to see, because its freshness signal is always true and always about something else.

**Added to "how to apply":** at closeout, **re-read the DERIVED CELLS — state columns, buffer/distance cells, footers, history lines — not just the sections you edited.** A cheap mechanization: list every cell in the file that restates a number owned elsewhere, and check those, not the prose. *(WAL's `scripts/derived_drift_check.py` does exactly this by scanning rather than by an enumerated list.)* And: **never quote a distance/buffer figure without re-deriving it from a named dated observation** — cf. [[finding_distance_to_a_threshold_is_a_claim_about_its_basis]].

**THE INVERSE, 2026-08-23 (CORAL, PROME-caught then CORAL-swept) — a FRESH body leaving a STALE HEADLINE standing. Same file, SAME COMMIT.**

CORAL encoded a Will-ruled stand-down condition into `STATUS.md` §A. **The same commit left §5's headline reading *"…AND THE LEG HAS NO REGISTERED STAND-DOWN"*** — a claim its own §A had just falsified. PROME flagged that one line. **CORAL then swept by PHRASE rather than fixing the reported line, and found TWO MORE**: the `Last Updated` header and the pillar-2 dashboard row. **Three summary surfaces asserting a gap the file had already closed.**

🔑 **Why the inverse is nastier than the classic form.** A stale header over a fresh body is caught by anyone who reads the body. **A stale headline over a fresh body is invisible to exactly the reading pattern headlines exist to serve** — a reader skimming section headings is doing the correct thing and gets the superseded state. **The body is the edit you are thinking about; the summaries are the ones that travel.**

## How to apply

- 🔴 **When an edit changes a STATE — gap→ruled, open→closed, pending→graded, unregistered→registered — grep the WHOLE FILE for the OLD state's phrasing before committing.** Not the line you were told about: **fixing only the reported instance is its own version of the failure** (1 reported, 3 actual, here).
- ⚠️ **BOUNDARY, and the sweep needs it or it destroys the record: distinguish LIVE-TENSE from HISTORICAL.** *"The leg HAD no falsifier, and it was ruled"* is **correct and must be left alone**; only present-tense assertions of the superseded state are defects. CORAL fixed 3 and **deliberately left 3 standing** in SCRATCH/MEMORY/KB. **A sweep that overruns erases the evidence that the gap ever existed**, which is the part worth keeping (`[[finding_a_correction_pass_is_unreviewed_work]]`).
- **The grep is on the OLD state's WORDS, not on the new state's.** You are looking for surfaces that never learned, and they are phrased in the vocabulary you have just stopped using.

**EXTENSION 2026-08-28 (n+1, WALTER via the DAEDALUS P1 rollout — the purest form yet):** `anchors/IRAN_WAR.md` line ~30 says *"split 2026-06-28 to keep this boot-read file lean — holds ONLY the current verified state."* TRUE the day it was written. The body regrew to 263 KB (4.9× the harness read cap) over two months underneath it, and every reader — including WALTER at every boot — had an explicit written reason NOT to check the size; WALTER read 4% via `sed -n '1,45p'` and asserted an absence (46 grep hits past line 45). **The header did not become false by being wrong; it became false by being LEFT.** Remedy class (READ_CAP.md rule 7): a size remedy leaves a DATED RE-TRIGGER ("split 6/28; re-check at any append or by 9/28"), never a leanness claim — a boast ages into a false certification and disarms the next check. `symptoms:` grep-bait — "this file holds only the current state", "kept lean by the split", "history archived → still N hundred lines".

**EXTENSION 2026-09-18 (n+1, SAM's sweep of `SIGNAL_INTAKE` — the banner that decays):** the file's own STALE banner claimed *"thesis now v1.6.9"* and *"spot now 163.16"* against a live v1.7 and 157.34. A staleness warning is a header too: written once, it certifies a GAP SIZE, and when the body keeps moving the warning under-states the gap while still reading as the cautious line — the reader trusts the banner's figure precisely because it is the banner. **A stale banner that under-states the gap is worse than none.** Same day, BROCK: `STATUS.md` at 132% of read-cap under a header rotated twice. **Apply:** a banner that carries a FIGURE ("now X") is a live claim and needs the dated-carry check like any number ([[finding_dated_carry_item_has_no_expiry_check]]); prefer a banner that names the INSTRUMENT (`git log -1 -- <file>` vs the thesis version) to one that quotes the state.

**EXTENSION 2026-09-20 (n+2, TERRY ⇄ CRUISE in one correction exchange — THE COUNT, and the signature that unifies this whole file):** a withdrawn-claim sweep ran three rungs, **each found by the OTHER desk and never by the author.** ① **Banner at the top** — fails, it is a reader's-entry-point control and does not travel to the claim (a grep or a mid-document reader never meets it). ② **Strike at the claim site** — fails when the line carries TWO claims: striking the one you came to fix leaves the other under a visible correction mark, so the line reads as *audited* and **the survivor is HARDER to doubt than before anyone touched it.** TERRY did exactly this — struck *"contested"* and left *"confirmed at primary"* standing in the same sentence, from the same packet that narrowed it. ⇒ **A PARTIAL STRIKE IS WORSE THAN NO STRIKE.** ③ **Claim-unit sweep incl. TITLES, HEADINGS, CROSS-REFERENCES and COUNTS** — what works; CRUISE's rung-③ pass then found **6 more** sites in files believed clean (all phrased in *different words*, **including the document title**), and a further pass found its §8 heading reading *"THREE conflations"* over **four** — the fourth committed in the same commit as that section's own lesson.

🔑 **THE COUNT IS THE CHEAPEST INSTANCE AND THE ONE EVERY INSTRUMENT MISSES.** TERRY's `STATUS.md` heading read *"the **two** post-closeout amendments"* over a body already taken to three — read past by **two** prior sweeps, the second of which edited the body directly beneath that heading. **No grep for a withdrawn PHRASE matches the word "two."** ⚠️ **And it is the same blindness one layer up from a known one:** TERRY's `ledger_sweep.py` CHECK H exists because a contract count sat wrong on three registry surfaces for **19 days** with every other check clean — *"a count is not a state token, so check A was blind by construction."* ⇒ **A count is neither a STATE nor a PHRASE, so it falls between the state-token check and the claim sweep. Neither instrument was wrong; each was correct within its own perimeter, and the count fell between all of them** ([[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]).

★ **THE SIGNATURE THAT UNIFIES THIS WHOLE FILE — three mechanisms, one shape: THE ARTIFACT LOOKS MORE AUDITED AFTER THE INCOMPLETE FIX THAN BEFORE IT.** A fresh header over a stale body · a partial strike on a two-claim line · a stale count over a corrected body. **A stale COUNT is worse than a stale PHRASE because it certifies a COMPLETENESS it does not have.** ⇒ **Audit for the certifying edit, not the neglected one.**

**Two things worth carrying:**
- 📐 **FORMAT can do this for free (CRUISE).** A **TSV record is a single line**, so a grep hit on a Fact cell carries its Notes correction **automatically** — the claim-site rule is satisfied by the container rather than by discipline. Markdown has no such property; every one of CRUISE's 15 sites needed a hand-placed strike. **Where a desk chooses the container, one-line-per-record is correction hygiene at zero cost.**
- ⛔ **A SWEEP PROPOSES, A HUMAN DISPOSES (CRUISE `KB-CRU-118`) — the standing limit on all three rungs.** TERRY's phrase sweep hit `Scholes` twice: both were **Black-Scholes** the pricing model, not **C. Patrick Scholes** the Truist analyst. **Same token, two referents; no sweep can adjudicate it.**

⚖️ **AND THE RULE THIS IMPLIES IS NOT THE ONE TO WRITE.** If *"every rung was found by the other desk and never by the author"* holds — the author knows what they meant, so reads the survivor as obviously covered — then **a rule ADDRESSED TO THE AUTHOR is the wrong SHAPE of fix**, and writing it hands both parties the feeling of having closed it. **Same family as a rule written in the same pass as the work, which is how this thread started.** Both desks therefore **flagged the ladder to PROME (records-hygiene owner) and deliberately did NOT self-adopt it** — n=2 desks, one day, one correction event is **a flag, not a mechanism**, and a guard built on it needs falsifying first ([[finding_test_the_guard_not_just_the_guarded]], [[finding_a_correction_pass_is_unreviewed_work]]). `symptoms:` grep-bait — "heading says three, body has four", "the count in the title is stale", "I struck the claim I came for and left the other one", "sweep came back clean after I fixed the reported line".

**EXTENSION 2026-09-20 (same day, round 4 — THE CORRECTION BLOCK IS THE SIGNATURE'S EXTREME CASE).** CRUISE's own correction of a shared-source claim **reversed the logic it was fixing** — written as *"earlier publication rules out WF/Stifel copying Truist"* when earlier publication rules out **the opposite** (Truist being downstream of the September notes). ★ **A correction block is the highest audit-signal container in any document, so a NEW error placed inside one is harder to spot than the same error in ordinary prose.** That is this file's signature — *the artifact looks more audited after the fix* — at its limit: **the fix is the camouflage.** ⇒ **Review correction blocks at a HIGHER bar than the text they correct, never a lower one, and re-derive the direction of any logic a correction asserts** ([[finding_correction_to_the_sequence_survives_every_fact_check]]). **Same round, the companion move worth copying: CRUISE removed the categorical completion receipt from all four papers rather than issue it a third time** — it read false after the phrase sweep, false again after the claim-unit sweep, and CATO then found nine more. **Banners now state WHAT WAS CHECKED, not what exists, and instruct the reader to assume residuals until a reviewer who did not write them says otherwise.** ⛔ **A completion claim is a claim; "I swept and found none" is reportable, "there are none" is not yours to certify about your own work.** ⚠️ **And the thread's own arithmetic of trust: FOUR correction rounds in one day, each finding defects in the one before — so treat any input revised that often as PROVISIONAL and keep it out of a frozen grading resolver entirely.**
