On a deep-research run, the **load-bearing headline figure is disproportionately likely to be a stale or dramatic VINTAGE of a periodically-revised series** — the circulating number is often the trough year, the early estimate, or last quarter's print, not the latest. Refreshing each load-bearing series to its most recent print is a **standing step**, not an occasional check.

Validated 3× in DEWEY runs (2026-06-27):
- **Ex-AI GDP** (REQ-002): the cited "+1.6% headline" was BEA's 2nd estimate; the 3rd/final revised UP to +2.1% — the whole "ex-AI contracting" framing rested on the superseded vintage.
- **Housing distress** (REQ-004): the "foreclosures surged 26%" headline was Q1-vintage; the latest May print was −5% MoM. And the "underwater rate 4-yr high" was a **metric misattribution** — the 4-yr-high was *affordability*, not the underwater rate (an 8-yr high).
- **Muni-fiscal** (REQ-003): the circulating Pew pension "$1.3T / ~66% of revenue" was FY2022 trough vintage; the latest Equable FY2025 shows funding *improving* to ~82.5% / $1.27T. The deep-research skill's adversarial pass independently **refuted** the stale figure my pull had flagged.

**Why:** periodically-revised series (BEA GDP estimates, quarterly ATTOM/ICE prints, annual Pew/Equable pension data) get cited at the moment they were most newsworthy — which is usually the most extreme/early reading. Headlines and even agent STATUS files freeze that vintage; the revision quietly reverses or softens it. A figure can be technically "true as published" yet point the wrong way once refreshed.

**How to apply:** for every load-bearing number, (1) identify the series and its release cadence, (2) pull the LATEST print + the prior one to see the revision direction, (3) check the headline isn't a *metric misattribution* (right series, wrong label). Run this as the independent primary-source pass *concurrently* with the deep-research workflow (background), not after — they converge and the skill's adversarial verify will often refute the stale figure on its own, corroborating the catch.
