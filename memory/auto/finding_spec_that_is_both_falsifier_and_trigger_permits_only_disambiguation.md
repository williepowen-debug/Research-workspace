`DELEGATION_TIER.md` test 4 fails a self-ruling when *"a falsifier becomes harder to trigger, **or** a threshold becomes easier to satisfy."* **Read as two independent clauses, that bars a difficulty change in EITHER direction for any spec that is simultaneously both** — and thesis kill-conditions usually are, because the same line that falsifies the thesis is the line that escalates the alarm.

**Measured 2026-08-21 (MIDAS, L-12/L-13).** M1 v2 kill-cond #3 is both a falsifier of the thesis and the M1→4 escalation trigger. Three genuine rigor improvements were drafted before the bar was noticed: weekly-continuity (**24× harder** — 0.79% vs 19.15% over 1,133 windows), a NO-VERDICT band around the boundary (harder), and 5-session smoothed endpoints — **which turned out to be +2.5% EASIER once measured**, not stricter as assumed. All three barred. What survived was the disambiguation, which is what the lesson had actually complained about: the sentence had two defensible readings and the grader picked one under pressure.

**Why the line is right, not bureaucratic:** re-tuning a live kill condition two weeks after it fired is tuning-to-tape **whichever direction it moves**, and an agent is the worst-placed party to judge its own direction. **The tier permits DISAMBIGUATION and forbids RETUNING** — a rule written under it cannot express a preference about the tape. That is the property that makes a self-ruling safe to bank.

**⚠️ The asymmetry to watch, because it has a gradient:** test 4 is **easier to pass by LOOSENING a pure falsifier than by tightening it** (loosening a falsifier makes it *easier to trigger*, satisfying clause 1). An agent that wants a permissive spec can therefore self-bank it while a conservative one must escalate. **Do not let that gradient pick the ruling.** Escalate the tightening; never quietly bank the loosening because it happens to clear.

**Rules:**
- **Before drafting any self-ruling, classify the target: falsifier, threshold, or both.** If "both," expect to rule only the definition and budget for an escalation packet.
- **Measure the direction of effect; do not assume it.** One of three drafted changes ran opposite to intuition, and only a base-rate computation revealed it.
- **A ruling that would re-mark an already-resolved prediction is never self-rulable** — the tier withholds authority *"to grade, resolve, or re-mark a prediction"* — so state the retroactive consequence and escalate it. Here: the escalated reading would have taken the fired-count 1/4 → 0/4.
- **Pair every ruling with its retroactive statement**, per [[finding_a_ruling_governs_the_next_write_not_the_existing_state]] — say explicitly whether existing state is re-graded, and if not, why not.
