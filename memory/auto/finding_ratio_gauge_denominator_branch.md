LABOR's Jul-2-2026 NFP grid conditioned every branch on (payrolls × U-3-up). The tape printed the un-written cell: payrolls +57K (weak) with **U-3 FALLING** to 4.2% — the labor force shrank 720K (immigration supply shock), mechanically suppressing the rate. The trigger keyed to "U-3 jump" couldn't fire on a genuinely weak print; prediction LAB-02 died on gauge choice, not mechanism.

**DEWEY C2 instance (2026-07-24), a STOCK-share variant:** the CC 90+ delinquency *balance share* rose to 13.12% (0.62pp from the 13.74% GFC peak) — but **~62% of the Q1 rise came from a shrinking denominator** (CC balances fell $25B) and aging of an existing 90+ pipeline, while the *flow* into delinquency actually softened (30+ transition ticked down, bank charge-offs fell). Two extra traps beyond the base case: (a) the denominator shrink was partly **seasonal** (Q1 paydown → reverses Q2–Q4); (b) a threshold *breach* driven this way is a **measurement/stock event, not confirmation the hypothesized mechanism fired** (the "student-loan cascade" was NOT the driver). Reading the breach as fresh flow-stress would have been the error.

**Why:** Any threshold denominated in a ratio inherits its denominator's dynamics. When the denominator becomes the fastest-moving part (participation collapse, shrinking base, index rebasing), the ratio stops measuring what the threshold assumed — in either direction.

**How to apply:** (1) Grade ratio-gauge moves JOINTLY with their denominator (U-3 with LFPR; NCO rate with loan-book size; share-of-X with X). Denominator-driven moves = NO-SIGNAL. (2) Print-reaction grids get an explicit third-axis artifact branch, written BEFORE the release. (3) Pre-registering a discriminating signature (LABOR's: U-3↓ + wages↑ + LFPR↓ = supply shock, logged Jun 16) is what makes an ambiguous print gradable same-day. (4) **Stock-share extension:** for a *stock* share near a historic threshold, decompose the move into numerator vs denominator, check the *flow* direction separately (a stock can breach on pipeline-aging while flows soften), and check denominator seasonality before reading a breach as a live mechanism — breach ≠ mechanism-confirmed. **TERRY/BRENT instance (2026-08-04) — the ENTRY-GATE variant, where the denominator bias picks the WORSE trade.** BRENT's oil deploy-gate leg (b) reads *"net debit ≤33% of spread WIDTH."* Measured across the live USO Oct-16 chain, 8 strikes × 4 widths: **bid/ask friction is ~$0.36-0.38 REGARDLESS of width.** As a share of the budget that is **7.7% for a 5-wide vs 1.8% for a 20-wide** — a **~6pp structural handicap that is a property of the denominator, not the trade.** Pass rates followed: width-5 5/8, width-20 8/8. ⚠️ **The inversion is the point: the same ratio ranks the WIDE spread better (27.0% vs 30.0%) while BRENT's own payoff table showed the NARROW one never loses by more than $30 and wins by up to $470.** TERRY identified the pathology in the abstract ("a bigger pass means cheaper, not better") and then recommended the structure exhibiting it; PROME independently presented Will a table ranked by the same ratio with the worst trade at the top. **A ratio gate does not merely fail to rank — it systematically REJECTS a whole structure class**, and here that class was the good one. Second-order: leg (b) also moved **30.0% → 34.0% → 38.0% within one hour** on a 1.7-point spot move (a sticky quote on the long leg), so on a narrow denominator the verdict is not a stable property of the structure at all.

**How to apply (gate variant):** before adopting any threshold of the form `cost ≤ X% of <span>`, **measure the fixed-cost floor in the numerator and express it as a share of each candidate denominator.** If that share varies materially across the structure classes the gate must compare, the gate is width-/scale-biased and cannot be used to rank — only to veto, and even then it will veto the wrong class first. Fixes are (a) net a friction allowance out of the numerator, (b) state the floor on mid with a separate slippage cap, or (c) scale X with the denominator. **And re-measure at the moment of action:** a ratio with a small denominator amplifies ordinary quote noise into verdict flips. **STUE instance (2026-08-13) — the REGIME-BREAK variant, where the denominator changes OBJECT and inverts a conclusion twice in one day.** Student-loan 90+ delinquency printed **10.60%** against a 2015–19 average of **11.12%**, read as "back to normal / below pre-pandemic." **It is not the same ratio.** The NY Fed measure divides by *all* student debt, including balances that structurally **cannot** be delinquent; the exposed share collapsed — **active repayment 53.8% → 38.5%** while **forbearance rose 10.0% → 29.5%** (SAVE-litigation parking). Normalised to the in-repayment base on the publisher's own internally-consistent series: **7.97% → 10.95% = 1.37×**, cross-checked at **1.31×** by applying the exposure share to the other publisher's numerator. **The headline said "slightly better than normal"; like-for-like it is ~37% worse.** ⚠️ **Conservative, because the base EXCLUDES the defaulted stock — which grew 5.2M → 9.0M in the two quarters immediately before the reading, stripping the worst loans out of the denominator right before it was measured.**

⚠️ **The instructive part is how many baseline checks it survived.** In one session I asked *"compared to what?"* (found a below-baseline result), then *"was that comparison period itself normal?"* (retracted it — the window was a for-profit-boom plateau). **Both are denominator-adjacent questions and BOTH missed it**, because both interrogated the *reference period* while assuming the *ratio* was stable. **The third question — "is this ratio measuring the same object in both periods?" — is the one that mattered, and it reversed the answer a third time.** A regime break (payment pause, forbearance program, moratorium, reporting suspension) is precisely when a denominator silently changes composition, and precisely when "vs pre-COVID" comparisons proliferate.

**How to apply (regime-break variant):** (1) **Before comparing any rate across a policy break, decompose the DENOMINATOR by exposure** — what share of it is even *capable* of showing the numerator's event? If that share moved, the raw comparison is invalid, not merely noisy. (2) **Prefer the publisher's own exposure-normalised series** (here: delinquency as a share of loans *in repayment*) over the headline; if none exists, build it and **cross-check by a second route with an independent numerator** — agreement at ~1.3× vs ~1.4× is what makes it reportable. (3) **Check which way the exclusions bias you** — if the worst cases exit the base (defaults, charge-offs, write-offs), the adjusted figure is a floor. (4) **Watch for the inverse trap in pre-registered signals:** a companion row here fired its band on "reservoir drains," registered as *reassuring* — but the reservoir drained **into default** (forbearance −1.9M *and* performing repayment −1.6M, default **+3.7M**), i.e. the maximally adverse reading. **A band colour is not a reading; record the DESTINATION of a flow, not just its direction** ([[finding_rising_stock_flat_inflow_means_slower_outflow]]).
