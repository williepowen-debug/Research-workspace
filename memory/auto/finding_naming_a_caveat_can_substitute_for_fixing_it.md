**Disclosing a defect is not remediating it, and disclosure is the more dangerous of the two failure modes because it looks like the safe one.** When you write "⚠️ note, my instrument had this bug" into a deliverable, the sentence discharges the *felt* obligation. The reader — and you — bank it as handled. But the bug's **consequence** is a separate step that nobody has taken, and the surrounding report now carries a credential of self-awareness it has not earned. **An undisclosed bug gets caught on the merits; a disclosed-but-unfixed one gets waved through on the author's apparent rigour.**

**Concrete (BOND, 2026-09-10).** A background poller was keyed on "does a row exist for this operation date." The **announcement** row existed hours before the event ran, so the poller exited on its first fetch (~13:31) and saved a payload whose result fields were all null. At ~15:40 BOND quoted that ~2-hour-old capture as current state and published **"offer-to-cover is NOT computable — the offered figure is null at the primary,"** with a `re-test` date and an explicit warning not to over-read a related figure. **All of it was false**: the field carried $10.489B, and re-fetching the same endpoint would have shown it. A peer (RED) caught it within the hour.

**The tell that this is the disclosure failure and not simple carelessness:** BOND *had already written down* that the poller fired on the announcement row. The diagnosis was correct, present, and in the shipped document. **What was missing was the one-line inference from it — "therefore the capture is stale, re-fetch" — and naming the bug is exactly what made that inference feel unnecessary.**

**Two operational rules:**
1. **When you disclose a defect in your own instrument, write the CONSEQUENCE in the same breath, as an action taken, not a property noted.** "My poller may have fired early" is a property. "My poller may have fired early, so I re-fetched at 15:40 and confirmed" is an action. If you cannot write the second sentence, you have not finished the work — and the first sentence should not ship alone.
2. **A captured tool payload is not live state.** Background jobs, saved outputs, and `tool-results/` files record what was true at capture time. **Re-fetch before quoting anything time-sensitive from one**, and prefer pollers that exit on the CONDITION you care about (a populated result field) rather than on a proxy for it (a row existing).

**Related but distinct:** [[finding_a_flag_resolved_in_the_wrong_direction_launders_the_defect]] — there the flag was fired and resolved *backwards*; here it was fired, recorded accurately, and simply **never acted on**. Both launder the defect; only this one does it with the author's own honest sentence.
