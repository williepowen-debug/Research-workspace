**A record about an action is not the action.** This holds for other agents' records *and for your own* — the second is the one that gets through, because you trust your own commit messages.

**Two instances, 2026-07-27, both PROME's, ~5 hours apart.**

1. **Someone else's record.** CREED's memo said a REGINALD freeze-banner ask was *"still pending"* — 23 days open. PROME relayed it to Will and cited the root Data Hygiene rule at it. **REGINALD had satisfied it 18 days earlier** (`da82a095`, 7/09), and the banner was *better* than the ask required. The stale claim lived in **CREED's own records** (`LAST_COMPLETION.md`, `STATUS.md`), written 7/4 and never refreshed. Three hops, one unchecked predicate.
2. **My own record.** The HEARTBEAT re-base commit stated a VIX-COT decay finding was **"Routed to VIOLET."** No such packet existed. I had written the intent in a commit body and never created the artifact. Found only because I checked VIOLET's inbox before writing a follow-up — **while VIOLET was booting to mark a live position with a mandatory exit two days out.** The two available framings of that datum pointed in *opposite* directions, so the missing half was decision-relevant, not cosmetic.

3. **A third party's record of their own intent, relayed.** *(Added 2026-07-28, PROME again.)* VIOLET's 7/27 close memo said it had **"asked HENRY for a fresher flip."** PROME relayed this into SCRATCH, STATUS and HEARTBEAT as *"VIOLET asked HENRY; HENRY has not answered"* — manufacturing an owed-item against HENRY. **No VIOLET→HENRY packet ever existed** (verified 7/28: not in HENRY's inbox or processed, not in VIOLET's outbox — the "ask" was prose in a memo to PROME). HENRY checked its inbox before acting on PROME's directive, found no ask, said so, **and delivered the answer anyway** because the deliverable was right independent of the request. New edge: **the requester's *stated intention to ask* is even weaker than a requester's "still pending" — it records a plan, not a send.** When relaying "A asked B," verify the packet in B's inbox first; otherwise write "A intends to ask B" and route it yourself.

4. **Your own record of a FIX — the declaration that stops anyone looking.** *(Added 2026-07-28, BRENT. n=4, and the most expensive shape.)* BRENT's USO call spread **filled 7/24**; STATUS and TRADE.md carried it as PENDING until 7/27. On finding that, BRENT wrote a hygiene note **declaring the contradiction fixed** — and had corrected only the **execution-log row**. The TRADE.md header, a TRADE.md narrative line, and **`NEXUS_BRIEF.md:61`** all still said *"pending fill."* NEXUS reads that brief **in place of** BRENT's STATUS at its boot, so **a live money-committed position was reported to a consuming agent as un-filled for four more days**, now under cover of a written statement that the problem was solved. Found 7/28 by a DAEDALUS architecture audit — **not** by BRENT's own closeout sweep, which had read the note and moved on.

   **The generalisation: "declared fixed but only partly fixed" is worse than "open."** An open defect still attracts attention. A *declared-fixed* one is actively defended by its own record — the note is the newest, most authoritative artifact, it is written in the voice of the person who did the work, and it converts every future reader (including the author) from *checking* to *trusting*. **Same mechanism as the three cases above, one turn worse: the record doesn't just substitute for the action, it immunises the gap against discovery.** Cousin to [[finding_freshness_check_cannot_catch_a_fresh_lie]] — there a *fresh* file is false; here a *fix note* is partial. Both defeat the check that would have caught them.

   **Apply:** when you declare something fixed, **enumerate the surfaces and re-grep for the defect string**, don't fix the instance you happened to be looking at. `grep -rn "<the wrong string>"` over your whole directory costs seconds and is the entire check. And **scope the claim to what you verified** — "fixed the execution-log row" is true and safe; "fixed the PENDING contradiction" was neither.

**Why it survives every reader:** these records are *assertions in the voice of the system of record.* A commit message is written by the person who would have done the thing, at the moment they intended to; a requester's status file is written by the person who wanted it. Neither is updated by the event that would falsify it. Nothing in the pipeline re-checks them, so they propagate at full confidence.

**How to apply:**
- **Before re-raising an aged cross-agent ask:** `git log` the **target path**, not the requester's note. The requester's record of an ask is not evidence the ask is open.
- **Before citing your own routing/delivery as done:** `ls` the **recipient's inbox**. "Routed", "flagged to", "notified" in a commit body are intents until an artifact exists at the destination.
- **Highest-risk moment:** a *crashed or interrupted session*. Intent-language survives in commits; the artifact-creating step is what gets lost. After any unclean shutdown, re-verify every delivery the dead session claimed.
- **Cheap tell:** if the only evidence for X is a sentence written by whoever was supposed to do X, it is unverified.

**n=6 — THE SELF-DIRECTED FORM: "I verified it" is itself an unverified claim (BRENT, 2026-08-02).** All prior instances are about a record of an action *someone was supposed to take*. This one is narrower and harder to see: **the asserted action is the VERIFICATION ITSELF.** BRENT froze a pre-registration and wrote that its baseline *"−7.75% … **reproduces on my own OHLC**."* It did not. The figure had been back-solved from an **intraday 12:58 PM quote lifted out of BRENT's own earlier prose** rather than the session close; the true value was **−8.70%**. **The four words "reproduces on my own OHLC" were doing all the evidentiary work and nothing behind them had run.** Aggravating detail that makes the pattern legible: it was written **inside the same document where BRENT was correcting another agent for an intraday-as-close error and correcting its own vol figure for the same class** — i.e. the verification language got *more* confident precisely where the surrounding text was about verification failure.

**Why this form evades every existing guard:** a delivery check can ask "does the artifact exist?", and an audit can ask "was the step run?" — but a *verification claim* has no artifact and leaves no trace. It reads as the strongest kind of sentence in a document (a first-person, self-audited confirmation) while being the cheapest to fabricate by accident, because re-reading it **feels** like checking it.

**Apply:** never write **"verified" / "reproduces" / "confirmed" / "checked"** unless the command that produced it **ran in the current session** — if you are recalling that you checked, you did not check. **And prefer to paste the derived number's inputs** (dates + both endpoints), because an input pair is falsifiable by the next reader and an adjective is not. Corollary found in the same session: the error surfaced only because BRENT went to **pull data**, never from re-reading — **so when a claim is load-bearing, re-deriving is the check and re-reading is not.**

**n=7 — THE INVERSE DIRECTION: an authorized action whose record lives only in an ephemeral channel reads, to an auditor, as an unauthorized one (PROME, 2026-08-15).** All prior instances are a record without the action. This is the action without the durable record. Will ruled a rules-file change in-session ("All approved as recommended", item 4 of a presented batch); PROME delivered the ruling to the RESIDENT owner agent by SendMessage; the owner encoded correctly, citing "Will ruled 2026-08-15 via PROME." **Same evening, an independent reviewer (DAEDALUS) audited the encode and found the citation nearly unverifiable** — the authorizing side held only an ephemeral message plus one clause inside a HEARTBEAT amendment; the queue row and the ruling artifact where a verifier actually greps carried nothing. The reviewer's honest best-read was "most likely a record-lag, not fabrication" — but it escalated the question to the operator, and a colder reviewer would have flagged the encode itself. **The ruling was real; the thin record made the legitimate edit look like the defect this whole memory warns about.**

**Why the resident case specifically breeds this:** when the owner is DARK, a ruling forces a committed inbox packet (the only delivery channel), which doubles as the record. When the owner is RESIDENT, SendMessage is faster and feels equivalent — but a message authorizes without recording. The speed of live orchestration is exactly what strips the paper trail.

**Apply:** a ruling delivered by message to a live agent still gets its artifact-of-record written the SAME HOUR, exactly as if the owner were dark — queue-row annotation, ruling-artifact addendum, or committed packet, whichever surface a future verifier would grep. Symmetric corollary for reviewers: before flagging an "unrecorded" authorization, check the ephemeral-adjacent surfaces (amendments, commit bodies of the same hour) — overstating "no record exists anywhere" spends credibility the finding didn't need.

**n=8 — THE OWED-ROW FORM: a record that an action is OWED outlives the action's completion and manufactures duplicate work (PROME, 2026-08-17).** Mirror of the original: instead of a done-record without the action, an *owed*-record persisting after the action. PROME's forum-4 §6 slate carried item-15 as "WALTER correction owed"; at the 8/17 reconciliation PROME searched WALTER's registry/inbox, found nothing, declared the correction **"NEVER DELIVERED,"** sent a duplicate packet, and **publicly owned a 7-day delay that did not exist** — the correction had been delivered 8/10 (PROME's own packet in `AGENTS/WALTER/inbox/processed/`, FALCON's consumed copy + KB-FALCON-091). The duplicate packet then propagated: WALTER wrote a duplicate correction banner off it before finding the 8/10 original and reverting byte-identical. Two failure surfaces compounding: the ledger row was never closed when the action completed, and the reconciliation search checked the *requester-side* surfaces but neither the TARGET artifacts nor PROME's own `processed/` delivery record (same miss-shape as the 8/16 search-floor limb on [[finding_bare_since_date_drops_same_day_commits]]). WALTER's independent check initially missed the existing marker too — its grep window truncated at 3000 chars with the marker past it — so BOTH desks' searches under-reached before the artifact settled it.

**Apply:** before declaring anything NEVER-DELIVERED (or re-sending it), check three places in order: the target's `processed/` (consumption is the strongest evidence), your own `processed/`/outbox-delivered (your delivery record), and the downstream consumer's artifacts (a consumed correction leaves lineage). A "corrections owed" ledger row must die the session its correction ships — an owed-row is a standing instruction to future sessions to redo the work. And ownership claims cut both ways: retract an owned-delay as loudly as it was owned when the delay turns out not to exist.

**n=9 — THE DATA-SERIES FORM: the underlying DATA is not a record of whether the GATE fired, and reading it as one manufactures a FALSE MISS (WALTER, 2026-08-19).** The other limbs are *a record substituted for the action*. This is *the raw data substituted for the record*, and it fails in the opposite direction — it invents a gap rather than hiding one.

WALTER pulled the EIA Cushing series, saw **18,599 MBBL under a registered 20M boundary**, and was one step from reporting an unfired trigger to Will. **The boundary had fired 2026-06-24, run ACTIVE ~7 weeks, been re-fired by WALTER itself on the 7/17 re-cross (`SIG-W-20260723-001`, IMMEDIATE), and been formally rescinded by BRENT on 8/12** — on two consecutive prints back above 20M, cross-confirmed on a second instrument.

**Three things the series could not say and the gate's record could:** it had already fired · **the re-fire convention SUPPRESSES fires on continued state**, so five sub-threshold weeks are only TWO qualifying events, not five · and **WALTER was not the primary firer at all** (BRENT-primary, WALTER-fallback-only-on-a-5-day-lag). **One step from reporting a miss on another desk's correctly-executed gate.**

**The same day, same desk, the sign flipped and it bit the other way:** a Trepp **special-servicing** figure of 16.58% sat above a `>15` **delinquency** band and looked like a FIRE — stopped only by REGINALD's `value_basis` cell reading *"NOT special-servicing rate."*

**Apply: a number next to a threshold tells you nothing until you read the gate.** False-MISS and false-FIRE are one error class with the sign flipped, and one move prevents both — **consult the gate's own record and its stated basis, never the data alone.** Before reporting any gate as unfired, check: has it fired? does its re-fire convention suppress continued state? **and is it even yours to fire?** Cousin of `[[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]` (the wrong-by-ARTIFACT form).

**n=10/11 — THE APPEND-FORM: appending a retraction is not retracting, and the record being in the RIGHT FILE is exactly why it passes inspection (VULCAN, 2026-08-21; DAEDALUS falsifying test same day).** VULCAN retracted a claim, banked the retraction in KB + struck STATUS in the same commit — then, six minutes later, rewrote the fleet-facing NEXUS_BRIEF *with the retraction in hand* and APPENDED a new row instead of amending the killed row above it. The killed claim ran 116 minutes live, 30 of them post-retraction; a full read found six more unstruck superseded claims in the same file (n=7, one file, one day). **Appending reaches the bottom of the file and FEELS like retracting; amending means going back up, and nothing forces that.** Two riders from the same incident: ① address a retraction to the ORIGINAL row's named recipients, copied from its To field — never to a description of what readers might have done with it (two same-day violations: "anyone who took my cohort-split read" when the recipients were VIOLET and LIQUID by name); ② VULCAN's first post-mortem blamed an ordering RULE and the commit graph exonerated it — the flattering diagnosis ("the process did it" = nobody's fault) reached for first; check the timeline before banking a process-blame post-mortem (kin: `[[finding_a_charitable_reading_of_your_work_is_the_one_to_check]]`).

**⚠️ The falsifying test result — the class is SURFACE-GEOMETRY-CONDITIONAL, not fleet-general and not a VULCAN habit (DAEDALUS, same day, hand-checked SAM 26-tokens/0-strikes + BOND 20/0):** the fleet's dominant NEXUS_BRIEF form retracts by PREPEND — newest block first, kill-on-sight correction at top, block-scoped supersession banners ("everything below this block is superseded where it conflicts") — so the reader meets the correction BEFORE the stale claim by construction, with zero strikethroughs. **The failure requires a surface where corrections land AFTER (in read order) the claims they kill**: append-at-bottom briefs, append-only logs, TSV ledgers. **Apply:** on any correction, the fix must sit UPSTREAM in read order of the claim it kills, or the claim is amended/struck in place — prepend-form surfaces satisfy this free; append-form surfaces need the ordering rule *amend the original FIRST, append the retraction SECOND*. **Do NOT adopt the retraction-tokens-vs-strikethrough ratio as a check — it false-flags the healthiest desks** (prepend-and-banner form scores 26/0 while being immune).
