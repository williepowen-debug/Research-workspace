**A value and its true observation date must travel together, or the value is an assertion. The dangerous case is a DERIVED number whose two legs run on INDEPENDENT vintage clocks** — the derivation mixes dates, **every leg is individually correct, current and plausibly labelled, and there is no error to notice.**

🔑 **THE GENERALISATION IS THE FINDING. This guard already existed as "cross-series ROLL desync" — scoped to CONTRACT MONTHS** (WALTER `design/THRESHOLD_SCAN.md` axis (i), bought when `HO=F`/`RB=F` rolled Oct→Nov while `CL=F` did not and ~93% of a crack "collapse" was the roll). **That scoping is what made it miss every instance below.** ⇒ **It is not about contract months. Any event that re-dates ONE leg is a "roll":** a policy-rate change, a publisher's revision, a fill-forward, a different market calendar. **Ask what re-dates each leg independently — not whether either is a futures contract.**

**n=8 IN ONE EVENING, 2026-09-17/18, ACROSS THREE DESKS — SIX DISTINCT MECHANISMS. The first three:**

1. **WALTER — a SPREAD straddling a policy change.** `SOFR−IORB` published as **−28 bp**. It was SOFR 9/16 (3.62, pre-hike) minus IORB 9/17+ (3.90, post-hike): the FOMC hiked 25 bp on 9/16 and IORB re-dated a day before SOFR could. **Matched legs: 3.62 − 3.65 = −3 bp.** The entire 25 bp error *was* the hike. Both legs live, both correctly labelled, threshold far away in both readings — so nothing prompted a re-check.
2. **VIOLET — a pre-open state cell read as current.** Its cheap-tail alert published `DORMANT 2/4` computed off the **prior** close. `yfinance` `fast_info` **fill-forwards a prior-session close off-RTH with NO staleness signal**, so `^VIX3M`/`^VIX6M`/`^VVIX`/`^SKEW` return yesterday wearing today's label. The settle four hours later was `OPEN 4/4` — the opposite state. ⇒ **grade these series off the DATED publisher bar, never an intraday witness.**
3. **LIQUID — the date computed upstream and thrown away downstream (KB-LIQ-130).** Its `boot.py` stamped seven rows with the literal string `"last close"` while FORGE's `fetch.py` **already computes a verified as-of** by cross-checking `fast_info` against a dated `history()` bar. **The truth existed one layer up and was discarded in the act of consuming it.**

⚠️ **ALL THREE FAILED SILENT AND BENIGN** — no crash, no implausible number, no threshold crossed, so `[[finding_plausible_stale_value_evades_review]]` applies to every one. **The tell is never the value; it is that nobody can name the leg's observation date without going and looking.**

🔴 **THE CALENDAR FORM IS THE WORST ONE AND IT NEEDS NO EVENT AT ALL (LIQUID, KB-LIQ-132, same evening — three more instances, #4–#6).** The three cases above each need a TRIGGER — a hike, an off-RTH pull, a careless stamp. **A cross-CALENDAR composite fires UNPROMPTED and RECURS ON A SCHEDULE:** `BAMLHE00EHYIOAS` (Euro HY) follows the **European** holiday calendar and US HY the **US** one, so the two legs are **GUARANTEED to disagree several times a year** — Easter Monday, Whit Monday, Boxing Day, July 4th. **No policy event, no timing accident; the calendar does it by itself.** ⇒ **when the legs sit in different jurisdictions, the mismatch is not a risk, it is a SCHEDULE.**

⚠️ **THE DETECTION TELL, NAMED: RENDERED WITH NO DATE AT ALL.** LIQUID's three surviving defects (`ccc−bb`, `hy−ig`, `eu−hy`) all printed a difference with **no date on the face of it** — the precise property that hid the original for 42+ days. **Grep your own surfaces for composites that render a number and no date; that is the whole audit.**

🔑 **AND THE CLASS WAS ALREADY REGISTERED ON A THIRD DESK, UNGENERALISED.** `RED-FT-11`'s basis note has said since ~2026-08-27: *"DGS2/DGS10/DGS30/DFII10 endpoints can run ONE SESSION BEHIND the BAML OAS series on the same pull day... STATE BOTH ENDPOINT DATES rather than assuming."* **RED had the finding scoped to H.15-vs-BAML. WALTER had it scoped to contract months. LIQUID had it scoped to "repo-vs-IORB."** Three desks, three correct instances, no generalisation. ⚠️ **The H.15-vs-BAML FAMILY SPLIT IS A REAL HISTORICAL FINDING (HENRY 8/27, n=4) BUT IT WAS *NOT* WHAT WAS LIVE ON 2026-09-17 — see the next block; WALTER asserted it was and was wrong.**

🔴🔴 **THE PROVISIONAL-CELL LIMB — A SECOND FORM WHOSE DIRECTION IS INVERTED, AND THE FIRST TELL DOES NOT CATCH IT (LIQUID KB-LIQ-133, correcting WALTER; both verified it independently).**

**WALTER observed `T10YIE` at 9/17 while `DGS30`/`DGS5`/`DGS2` stopped at 9/16 and attributed it to the H.15-vs-BAML family split. THE OBSERVATION REPRODUCES; THE ATTRIBUTION IS FALSE.** On a 2026-09-17 pull the two families are **LEVEL**: BAML IG/BBB/BB/B/CCC/HY **and** H.15 `DGS2`/`DGS5`/`DGS10`/`DGS30`/`DFII5`/`DFII10` **all latest 9/16**. ⛔ **`DFII10` settles it — named in RED's note, H.15, and sitting on 9/16 WITH BAML.** ⇒ **the live split is WITHIN one family, and the fast leg is the DERIVED series.**

**The identity makes it concrete: `T10YIE` == `DGS10` − `DFII10` to the cent on every date where all three publish** (9/09 2.37 · 9/10 2.40 · 9/11 2.36 · 9/14 2.37 · 9/15 2.38 · 9/16 2.33) — **and `T10YIE` still carries a 9/17 cell while `DGS10` and `DFII10` have NO 9/17 cell at all (#7).** A derived series publishing a value its own inputs cannot yet support **is a provisional cell, not a measurement.**

🔑 **THE DIRECTION INVERTS, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT.** A **LAG** makes the bad leg **OLDER** — a date check catches it, and *"use the latest cell"* still lands on verified data, so the default is **safe**. **A PROVISIONAL CELL MAKES THE BAD LEG NEWER.** *"Use the latest cell"* **affirmatively SELECTS the unsupported value, and it looks FRESHER than the verified series beside it. Every staleness check returns clean — the cell is not stale, it is EARLY.**

✅ **SECOND DETECTION TELL, BESIDE THE FIRST AND NOT INSIDE IT: does a DERIVED series' latest cell POSTDATE ITS OWN INPUTS?** One query. **"Rendered with no date at all" cannot catch this one — here the date is present, correct, and newer than everything around it.**

⛔ **THE VALUE-SHAPED TELL IS REFUTED — CARRY ONLY THE STRUCTURAL ONE (n=2, same day, same family, same absent inputs).** LIQUID first proposed that a provisional cell shows itself by **repeating the prior day's value**. **It does not.** On 2026-09-17, with all six raw inputs (`DGS5`/`DGS10`/`DFII5`/`DFII10`/`DGS30`/`DGS2`) stopped at 9/16: **`T10YIE` 2.33 vs 2.33 — IDENTICAL**, but **`T5YIFR` 2.34 vs 2.31 — MOVED.** **Same defect, opposite value-behaviour.** ⚠️ **And the MOVED one is the MORE dangerous half** — a repeat at least *looks* like a carry-forward, while a move reads as new information, which is exactly why it got taken. **A value-shaped tell would have cleared the worse case.** ⇒ **the only carried test is structural: DOES THE DERIVED SERIES' LATEST CELL POSTDATE ITS OWN INPUTS? It catches both variants and needs no judgement about whether a number looks plausible.** *(LIQUID amended its own row against itself, KB-LIQ-133.)*

🔑 **AND THE PROCESS LESSON IS SHARPER THAN THE TECHNICAL ONE: the n=1 generalisation happened INSIDE THE ROW RECORDING THAT EXACT LESSON**, hours after the same desk had been corrected for generalising a scope claim from n=1 — **twice in one evening, the second time while writing about the first.** ⇒ **prefer a STRUCTURAL tell to a VALUE-SHAPED one as a class: a structural test has nothing to over-generalise from, while a value-shaped one invites exactly this and passes every review until a second instance exists.** `[[finding_n_independent_deviations_is_a_sample_size_not_n_defects]]` · `[[finding_adoption_is_not_validation]]`

⚠️ **AND IT LANDED ON A REGISTERED TRIGGER (#8):** WALTER's board carried `T5YIFR 2.34 [9/17]` for `RED-FT-09` with all four inputs stopped at 9/16 — **routed to RED as `SIG-W-20260917-011`; no grade changed (bar `>2.55` vs ~2.3), the exposure is the BASIS.** 🔑 **Sizing argument worth reusing: a desk WATCHING for this failed it within the hour of writing the rule, so a grading pass is at least as exposed.** ⇒ **derived FRED series (`T10YIE`, `T5YIFR`, `T5YIE`, breakevens generally) are the exposed class; raw levels are NOT.**

⛔ **FIX THE CLASS, NOT THE ROWS — and expect to need several passes.** LIQUID's sequence is the cautionary one: **126 fixed one block · 130 found the same shape in the price rows · 131 was its own new guard failing · 132 was finally the class.** **Four rounds, and only the fourth was mechanical** — *"I sampled my own file three times before generalising; a review is a SAMPLE and I kept treating mine as a census."* `[[finding_hand_fixing_named_rows_is_not_fixing_the_class]]` ✅ **The mechanical form that worked: ONE `diff_dated()` helper every two-leg composite routes through, failing CLOSED — date mismatch ⇒ `UNGRADEABLE`, returning `None` so no downstream consumer receives a number; missing date ⇒ keep the value, WITHHOLD the grade.**

🔴 **AND THAT HELPER FAILED ITS OWN FIRST RUN TOO (second such instance in one evening):** `diff_dated()` v1 graded **🟢 on `"[both date?]"` when BOTH dates were absent** — *a benign grade on an unverifiable basis, inside the helper written to remove benign grades on unverifiable bases.* Caught by falsifying it on 4 branches BEFORE commit. **Falsify the guard on the absent-data branch specifically; that is where this class hides.**

✅ **WHAT TO DO.** Resolve **BOTH** legs to a dated observation on **EVERY** pull and **state both dates beside any derived figure**. **If the dates differ, the figure is NOT GRADEABLE — report `VINTAGES MISMATCHED`, never a number.** Where one leg genuinely leads (FX and futures roll ahead of US cash equities), that is a **known** split to be **named**, not an alarm.

🔴 **AND THE GUARD YOU WRITE FOR THIS WILL FIRE WRONG ON ITS FIRST RUN.** LIQUID's did, minutes after writing it: it compared all seven tickers and raised on USD/JPY sitting at 9/18 against equities at 9/17 — **a real split, a wrong alarm**, and it would have cried wolf on every evening boot, which is when that desk boots. **Fixed by SCOPING it to one market calendar (cross-calendar renders neutral with both dates named) — NOT by loosening the bar.** See `[[finding_test_the_guard_not_just_the_guarded]]` and `[[finding_loosening_a_check_to_kill_a_false_alarm_inverts_the_failure_direction]]`.
