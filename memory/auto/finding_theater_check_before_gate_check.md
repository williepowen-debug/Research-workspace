A pre-registered trigger usually carries an implicit **scope** (a theater, a region, an asset class, an entity set) that its wording does not repeat. The highest-risk false-fire is not one weak claim — it is **two claims that are each half-qualified, arriving together, where each supplies exactly the qualifier the other lacks.**

Worked instance (WALTER, 2026-07-27, one session):

| Claim | Scope | Verified? | Fires the gate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hormuz tanker "struck a mine" | **RIGHT** theater | ❌ claim-only — IRGC-linked outlet, vessel unnamed, no official statement, zero neutral confirmation, five outlets relaying **one** channel | NO |
| Grain ship *Golden Leo* sunk | ❌ **WRONG** theater (Black Sea) | ✅ confirmed — named, dated, 10 dead, multi-wire, officials on record | NO |

The gate was *"confirmed mine detonation or hostile sinking"*, **scoped to Hormuz**. Neither claim fires it. **Merging them yields "a confirmed vessel sunk in the strait" — false on both counts**, and that is the sentence a downstream reader actually forms.

**Why:** Route the delta, not the datum. Verification and scope are **independent** checks, and confirming one creates a strong felt sense that the item is "solid" — which is exactly when scope stops being checked. An adjacent-theater confirmation is a **false-fire hazard**, not corroboration.

**How to apply:** Ask **"which theater/scope?" BEFORE "does it fire?"** — scope-check first, because it is cheaper and it kills more candidates. When two related claims arrive in one window, check whether they are being merged into a single stronger claim than either supports, and say so explicitly at every surface. Never let a confirmed event in an adjacent scope raise your confidence in an unconfirmed one in the target scope.

**Corollary recorded the same day:** an incentive flag predicts **DISTORTION, not necessarily INFLATION**. The openly partisan source here *understated* its own theater's event (no vessel name, no attribution, no death toll — "a sinking with no cause"), because omission was what served it. Ask *which direction* a source's incentive bends, rather than assuming exaggeration.
