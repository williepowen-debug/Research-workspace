A research run carrying a directional prior — a thesis to test, a kill condition to evaluate, a hypothesis someone commissioned — produces an **asymmetric verification effort**. A datum that fits the prior reads as *finished* and the search stops; a datum that cuts against it reads as *a problem* and gets a second pass. The result is that **the confounds you never check are disproportionately the ones that would have deflated your finding.**

The tell is countable and it is available before you ship: **line up every confound you did catch and check their directions. If they all point the same way, your search was directional, and the ones you missed point that way too.**

**The case (DEWEY, 2026-08-28, CARL-DR-2).** The commission asked whether a charged-off consumer cohort was deteriorating. I was looking for deterioration. **Three separate figures looked like strong deterioration evidence and all three had mundane explanations I found only because I checked:**

1. A subprime lease-to-own book contracting **−17%** read as "underwritten subprime credit is shutting down." Management attributed it to **weakness in the furniture industry**, and it was **−2%**, not −15%, once off-balance-sheet loans held by a bank partner were included.
2. Pawn receivables **+19% and +13% same-store** at two operators read as "the informal layer is absorbing distressed borrowers." **Gold was +22.1% YoY** and jewelry was **69%** of one operator's collateral — the mechanical revaluation effect *exceeded* that operator's entire same-store growth.
3. Debt-buyer recoveries "**+25% above forecast**" read as a behaviour signal. Normalized by collections it was **6.8% vs 6.3%** — mostly book growth.

**Every one of the three deflated the thesis I was testing. Not one of the confounds I caught cut the other way.** That asymmetry is the finding: it is not plausible that the world handed me only deflationary confounds, so the ones I did not look for were deflationary too — and the honest posture is that the verdict is weaker than the surviving evidence makes it look.

**⚠️ The inverse is the more dangerous form, and it appeared in the same run.** Where evidence cut *against* my emerging verdict, I produced an explanation that dissolved it (a survivorship argument that turned the strongest counter-evidence into supporting evidence). **That move deserves more suspicion than any confirming datum**, because it is the one place where the prior does work rather than merely surviving. I flagged it as inference and put it first in Counter-Evidence; that is the minimum, not a fix.

**How to apply:**
1. **Before shipping, list the confounds you checked and their directions.** All-one-way is a search artifact. Say so in the report rather than letting the surviving evidence carry unearned weight.
2. **Spend the marginal check on the datum that FITS.** The one that cuts against you already got a second pass — that is exactly why it is not where your error is.
3. **A commodity price, a sector-specific demand story, an accounting reclassification, and a denominator that grew** are the four explanations that most often sit under a number that looks like distress. Check them by name.
4. **When you explain away your best counter-evidence, that explanation is the report's load-bearing weakness.** Name it as such, first, and give a reader the falsifier.

Both are the same mechanism: the reading that costs nothing to accept is the one that enters the record unchecked.
