When stating where an authority, ownership, or scope boundary falls, **read the granting document.** Do not infer the boundary from adjacent evidence — a behavioral record, a neighbouring file path, a canon line that travels near the claim, or a conservative-sounding default.

**n=3 in two days (2026-08-21 → 2026-08-22), all three PROME, all three caught by someone else:**
1. Told DAEDALUS that `PROME/tools/` was its grant lane. It wasn't — verified wrong at three surfaces; DAEDALUS refused the territory.
2. Passed two tariff scope claims to spawning desks labelled "PROME-verified." They were canon-carried, not verified — tier inflation; both desks caught it.
3. Told WALTER (and Will) that "PROME spawns read-only subagents only; domain-desk launches are Will's." False. `AUTONOMY.md` Tier 1 grants follow-up spawns in an already-approved workstream FREE and Tier 2 makes a new-direction domain spawn *proposal-required* — a cost gate, not a prohibition; `ORCHESTRATION_PLAYBOOK` §Model-tiering says domain agents spawn as subagents **instead of** Will launching sessions. **Cause: read SCRATCH's spawn-LEDGER line — "PROME spawned READ-ONLY VERIFIERS ONLY", a description of what I did — as a statement of what I may do.** Will challenged it directly.

**Why:** a boundary claim is self-authenticating in a way a number is not. Nobody re-derives it, because it sounds like the speaker is describing their own constraints and therefore has no incentive to be wrong. **Instance 3 shows the error is not directionally safe:** understating your own authority looks like discipline, so it draws *less* scrutiny than a land-grab would — and it silently kills a capability the fleet already granted and paid for. A refused grant is as much a defect as a claimed one. The three instances also differ in direction (over-claimed twice, under-claimed once), which is the tell that the failure is the *method*, not a bias.

**n=4 — THE ATTRIBUTION FORM: a figure inherits the SOURCE named nearest it, and the mislabel then survives every re-sourcing attempt (CREED, 2026-08-28).** The first three are authority boundaries. This one is **data provenance**, and it shows the method failure is not about permissions at all — **it is about what a document's authority actually covers.**

A **$160B+ 2026 multifamily maturity wall** sat on two desks' rails for four months labelled *"Trepp / CRE Daily"*, flagged PROVISIONAL, with a dated kill on it and repeated failed re-sourcing. **The sentence is attributed, by its own source article, to a SPONSOR quoted in it — the co-founder of a real-estate operator — not to Trepp.** It sits in a trade article whose **headline credits Trepp for a completely different figure** (a delinquency rate). **The data-provider label attached to the adjacent number.** ⚠️ **A web summariser reproduced the identical error live, in front of the desk running the check**, returning *"This data comes from Trepp"* for that same sentence — so the mislabel is **reproducible on demand and is a property of the artifact**, not a transcription slip by any one reader.

**Why it defeats re-sourcing specifically, which is the useful part:** every attempt searched **the named provider's publications**, and the figure was never there. **A wrong attribution does not fail loudly — it fails as an absence**, and an absence reads as "not published yet," "paywalled," or "we didn't look hard enough." Four months of honest effort produced no signal that the *label* was the defect. ⇒ **[[finding_record_of_an_action_is_not_the_action]] n=9's shape, one level up: the raw absence is not a record of whether the claim was ever made.**

**The tell that cracked it:** the figure always travelled with a **+50% YoY** growth rate, while the named provider's own comparable total that year was **DOWN 9%**. **A metric and its supposed source moving in opposite directions is an attribution test that costs one lookup** — cheaper than any search, and it should be run *before* the search, not after it fails.

**Apply:** when you cannot re-source a figure after real effort, **stop searching the named source and go read the artifact where you first met it** — check whether the attribution in that artifact actually covers the sentence you took, or only a neighbouring one. **A named source in a headline, a byline, a chart caption, or an endnote source-LIST covers what it covers and nothing adjacent.** Corollary for publishing: when you quote a figure out of an article, record **who the article says said it**, not what the article is about — those differ exactly often enough to matter, and the difference is invisible once it is in your own rail.

**How to apply:** before writing any sentence of the form "X is Y's lane / X may not do Z / that authority is mine-or-theirs" — **name the document that grants it and open it.** If you cannot name one, say "I don't have the grant in front of me" rather than defaulting to the conservative reading. Specifically: a LEDGER of past behavior (spawn ledgers, commit records, "we've always…") is evidence about practice and says nothing about permission; proximity of a path or a canon paragraph is not ownership. Related: `[[finding_record_of_an_action_is_not_the_action]]` (the inverse — a record of doing isn't the doing; here a record of doing isn't a rule about doing), `[[finding_adoption_is_not_validation]]`, `[[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]]` (points inward: verify the claim that makes you retract *or* self-limit), `[[finding_a_charitable_reading_of_your_work_is_the_one_to_check]]`, `[[finding_guidance_is_not_the_instrument]]`.

**n=5 — THE UNNAMED-TRIGGER FORM: the grant was read, recited, and still not exercised (PROME, 2026-09-05; WQ-184).** Fourteen days after instance 3 put the tiered spawn grant into the auto-injected `PROME/CLAUDE.md`, three desks (MIDAS · TERRY · FERT) held registered dated DOCKET rows through three PROME sessions in one day, each session carrying *"the spawn nod"* as an item **open for Will**, until Will typed *"go ahead and spawn all three."* PROME had spawned HOMER (8/31) and REGINALD (9/1) on due rows with no nod the week before. **The reading was correct this time — PROME could quote the tiers — and the application still failed, because the grant named the LANE ("follow-up in an already-approved workstream") and nothing named the TRIGGER: which inputs count as an approved workstream.** A registered row naming a desk is one; nobody had written that sentence, so at every due row the conservative default re-emerged and the grant read as "ask." Codex logged the cause as UNKNOWN; DAEDALUS (FERT F-2) called it a spawn-driver question; the fix (WQ-184, ruled the same night) was one sentence: *a registered dated row is the nod* — with precedence stated over the triage outcome that had been sending the same input to "propose to Will."

**What it adds to the rule:** reading the grant is necessary and not sufficient. **A grant that names a lane without naming its trigger will be under-exercised at every input that is not obviously inside the lane** — and "obviously" is exactly what a conservative reader never grants itself. When you find yourself asking permission for something, check two documents, not one: the grant, AND whether any sentence says *this input* is inside it. If none does, the defect is in the canon (write the trigger), not in your caution — and asking again next week is the wrong fix.


## BOND, 2026-09-17 — read the LETTER's SCOPE CLAUSE before counting arrears

Same class, arrived at from the obligations side rather than the authority side.

A per-op deliverable to another desk had been docketed **once**, and a RESOLVED row cannot drive the next occurrence, so the recurring duty had no carrier. The coordinator correctly registered the class defect (*"do not date a class"*). **But the first question at the wake was not "how many reads do I owe?" — it was "which of the events that ran are inside the letter AT ALL?"**

Two operations had run since the last read and **neither was in scope**: the governing letter's step-up covered two named sectors only, and both events fell outside them. **Arrears were ZERO and the gap was purely structural.** A desk that had counted "events since the last read" would have reported two missed obligations and routed two packets the recipient could not use.

**⇒ (a) READ THE SCOPE CLAUSE BEFORE THE ARREARS COUNT — a count keyed on the wrong population is confidently wrong in BOTH directions.** **(b) Key a recurring carrier to the ISSUER'S schedule, not to the feed:** the feed only shows events announced ~2 days ahead, so a carrier keyed on it cannot see the CLASS, only its next instance.

## ZHAO, 2026-09-18 — n=6, the DOCUMENTED-BUT-UNINSTRUMENTED form: the lane was accepted in prose and never in the ledger

n=5 was a grant that named the lane but not the TRIGGER. **This is one step worse and one step earlier: the lane was named, accepted, and written into canon — and no instrument was ever built, so there was nothing a trigger could have fired on.**

Will proposed the assignment on **2026-08-18** (*"Shouldn't China 10Y go to Zhao?"*). A scope paragraph went into `AGENTS/ZHAO/CLAUDE.md` on **2026-09-02**, carefully drafted — it even settles the ZHAO↔BOND boundary (*"what is Beijing doing"* vs *"how is the CGB market clearing"*) and carries a substantive analytical caveat about not reading a low nominal CGB yield as haven demand. **By every documentary test the lane was accepted.**

**As of 2026-09-18 the desk had logged ZERO data points on it — no `VX.tsv` row, no `KB.tsv` row, no threshold.** The only trace was one line on a `STATUS.md` dashboard reading `1.710% [STALE 7/31]`. **And because it was never a ledger row, the desk's own staleness checker could not see it**: `boot.py` reads `VX.tsv`, so a number living only in dashboard prose is structurally exempt from every freshness alarm the desk owns. **31 days, and the instrument that would have complained did not know the lane existed.**

**The cost was not hypothetical.** Inside that window the **China–US 10Y yield gap set an all-time record** (317bp on 2026-09-10, Bloomberg series to 2002). ⚠️ **But note the honest accounting, because it sharpens the lesson rather than dramatising it: the stale value was 1.710% and the live one 1.678% — a −3.2bp drift over seven weeks. The number was never wrong.** ⇒ **The failure was not staleness. It was that nothing was WATCHING** — no bands, so no threshold could be crossed; no ledger row, so no alarm could fire; no KB row, so no reader would ever travel to it.

**What it adds to the rule — the test to run when you accept a lane:**

- **Prose acceptance and instrument acceptance are different acts, and only the second can fire.** A scope paragraph — however well drafted, however correctly it settles boundaries with a neighbouring desk — **creates an obligation and no capability.** Writing the boundary well is the part that *feels* like doing the work, which is exactly why it substitutes for it (`[[finding_naming_a_caveat_can_substitute_for_fixing_it]]`, same shape one level up).
- **The self-audit question is mechanical: for each lane you claim, name the ledger row that carries it.** If you cannot, you hold the lane in documentation only. **Run it over the whole scope section, not over the lane you happen to be thinking about** — the un-instrumented lane is by construction the one that never comes to mind, because nothing ever surfaces it.
- **A value living only in a dashboard/prose surface is invisible to ledger-keyed freshness checks.** This is the general defect: *every* guard has a population, and a figure outside that population is not "passing" the guard, it is un-scanned. `[[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]`, `[[finding_scan_keyed_on_naming_reads_local_form_as_absence]]`.
- ⚠️ **Direction check, because this class is not directionally safe (see instance 3): the failure here is UNDER-exercise of a granted lane, and it drew no scrutiny for a month precisely because a quiet desk looks like a disciplined one.** Nothing complains when you fail to build.

**Corollary worth a fleet pass, offered as a question rather than a claim:** if one explicitly-granted, canon-documented lane sat 31 days with no instrument, the base rate of *lanes accepted in prose but never instrumented* is probably not zero elsewhere. A desk can only audit its own. Flagged to PROME 2026-09-18.
