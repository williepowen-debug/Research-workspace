A rationed payout produces a percentage that is a **share of the amount requested**, not a **price**. "Investors will be returned approximately 45% of their requested capital" (5% offer ÷ 11.2% demand, paid **at NAV**) is a fill rate with **no haircut**. Stored as "returning 45 cents on the dollar — forced liquidation pricing," it becomes a fabricated distressed print.

BROCK carried exactly that in `KB-BRK-103`, rated **A1/EMPIRICAL/CONFIRMED, for ~4 months**, while the correct reading of the same event sat in `KB-BRK-061` on the same date. It nearly resolved a registered Stage-3 trigger (`BRK-25`, "first arms-length sub-90¢ print") TRUE on a number that never existed.

**Why:** the misread is invisible because the number is *plausible* and it *confirms the thesis* — a distressed print is what a bear is hunting, so it is never re-read. Both readings are the same digits and both sound bearish. This is the `[[finding_plausible_stale_value_evades_review]]` mechanism applied to meaning rather than age, and the denominator half of `[[finding_ratio_gauge_denominator_branch]]`.

**How to apply:**
- When a percentage could be a **share of a quantity** or a **price**, write the denominator *into the fact*: "45% of requested capital," never a bare "45%." A number without its denominator does not enter a ledger.
- **Read one paragraph further in the source you are already citing.** The same shareholder letter reported NAV/share −1.2% and total return +1.0% — a direct contradiction of a "55-cent haircut." The refutation was inside the document, not somewhere else.
- Any datum that *would satisfy a registered trigger* gets a primary re-read **before it is stored**, not before it is used. Storage is where confidence ratings get attached and audit stops.
- Rationing language to treat as fill-rate-not-price by default: "pro-rata," "% of requests honored," "% of requested capital," "satisfaction." Price language: "marked at," "traded at," "cents on the dollar" **in a transaction**.
- On correction, mark the bad row **refuted in place** rather than deleting it, and trace downstream reach explicitly — the false figure had propagated into one research brief and stopped there, and knowing *where it stopped* is what made the correction safe to close. See also `[[finding_verification_correction_downstream_propagation]]`.
