**"Register a threshold" is a hypothesis, not a task. Test it before you build it, and be willing to conclude that it should not exist.**

Two numbers decide it: the **unconditional base rate** (how often is this condition true at all?) and the **separation** (how much more often is it true in the state you are trying to detect than outside it?). **A trigger with low separation is a description of the world wearing threshold clothing** — when it fires it tells the recipient nothing.

**Incident (LABOR, 2026-08-07).** One pass base-rated six candidate thresholds against 36 years of monthly data. Three results:

- **Level bars were never triggers.** Two live escalation thresholds keyed to an unemployment *level* — one at 4.7%, one at 5.0% — were true in **65%** and **58%** of all months since 1990, separating the target state by only **+12.9pp** and **+13.8pp**. The *change* form of the identical series separated **+50.7pp**. **The series was never the problem; the LEVEL construction was.** Replacements built on a drawdown separated **+53 to +63pp** at base rates of 8-12%.
- **Two of three requested thresholds were killed by measurement.** One would have keyed on a series that *lags* the one already triggering (peak correlation at +1 month; a signal unique to it was a false positive ~9 times in 10). The other keyed on a series whose declines separate the target state by **+0.0pp** at one bar and **−4.0pp** at another — *it fires more often outside the state it was meant to detect.*
- **The same measurement prevented a scoring error.** The largest single sector loss in that month's data sat at the 5.7th percentile of its own history and was the obvious candidate to escalate a tracked vector. Because that series carries no information about the target state, **scoring it would have imported noise** — so the vector was held. **An unusual level is not a signal; only a level that discriminates is.**

**Incident 2 — the base rate was computed CORRECTLY and was still wrong, because the WINDOW was a structural anomaly (STUE, 2026-08-13).** A domain's headline metric was compared against its own pre-pandemic average and came out *below* it — a thesis-killing result, produced by exactly the discipline above. **Will asked one question: "maybe there were reasons the rate was higher those years."** There were. The chosen 2015-19 window was **the peak plateau of a decade-long doubling** (6.19% → 11.40%), driven by a cohort (for-profit college borrowers, ~10% of enrollment and ~50% of defaults) that had since **halved**. Against 2003-07 the same number read **+3.94pp**; against 2015-19, **−0.52pp**. **Same figure, three baselines, opposite conclusions.** A companion claim — "16% below the pre-2020 trend" — was withdrawn outright: that trend's slope *was* the same one-time cohort accumulating, **and it was already decelerating when it was fitted**, so extrapolating it forward six years was invalid. ⚠️ **The chosen window happened to be the one that made the finding strongest.** That is the tell.

**Incident 3 — base-rate BOTH DIRECTIONS of a line at registration, or you ship a one-way instrument without knowing it (RED, 2026-08-20).** A registered kill line (equity ^SKEW <140 sustained 4td) had fired legitimately — the sub-140 state obtains only **11.5%** of sessions, a genuine event. When the level re-crossed, the natural symmetric re-arm (>140 s=4) was measured before registration: **54-65% of all sessions — the index's MODAL state.** The mirror of a sound event-detector was a descriptor, so **the line was structurally one-way: it could only ever debit one side of the book and never credit it back** — the exact ratchet class RED had filed against another desk's kill-rule amendment, sign-flipped. Worse, the fire's own precondition premise ("no re-cross") had been conditioning on an 11.5%-frequency state persisting indefinitely — near-certain to die regardless of regime, and nobody had base-rated it at registration. The repair was not the mirror but an event-grade line at a level the index rarely holds (≥150 s=4 = 7.5%, rarity-symmetric with the kill), with the original kill line as its exit — a two-way pair. **And the joint form mattered more than either marginal: conditional on the companion instrument's calm state (VIX<16), the "alarming" configuration obtained 92% of the time, while the configuration that had actually validated the thesis was a 2.0% joint rarity.**

**Incident 4 — I base-rated MY OWN PROPOSAL and never base-rated THE RULE I WAS REPLACING (CARL, 2026-08-27).** Re-speccing a full-thesis kill rule, I measured six candidate forms of the new leg over 94 quarters and killed my own first draft on the numbers (direction-only fires **43.5%** and is not silent through the GFC build; the survivor fires **5.4%** and is). Textbook application of everything above — **and the audit was one-sided.** The coordinator's verification found the number I had never computed: **the INCUMBENT rule I was replacing base-rates at 25.0%** (23/92 windows), with post-2015 fires driven by exactly the denominator artifact the re-spec existed to repair. **I had built the entire case for replacement on a single quarter's anecdote and never asked how often the old rule misfires.** Here the asymmetry ran *against* me — measuring the incumbent would have made my case stronger, and I under-sold it. **That is luck, not method.** The general failure is that **the challenger must pass a test the incumbent is silently grandfathered past**, and the usual direction of that error is the dangerous one: a weak replacement ships because nobody scored what it replaced.

⚠️ **Second half of the same session, same disease on a different surface:** the kill rule's old leg keyed on a **share**, which falls when its denominator grows even as the numerator rises — and a fleet spec being piloted that same week carried a *"the brief's share of the pair rose ⇒ narrative relocated"* test that fired on a pure denominator move (the brief had not changed by a single byte). **A ratio was asked to carry a claim about a numerator, twice, in one week, on two unrelated surfaces.** When you base-rate a ratio threshold, base-rate what its denominator does independently, or you have measured nothing.

**How to apply:**
0. **Audit the WINDOW before trusting the base rate.** *"Compared to what?"* is the right question; **"and was THAT period normal?" is the same question one level deeper, and it is where the error lives.** Before shipping any base-rated comparison: (a) **plot the full history, not the window** — a plateau at the top of a long climb looks identical to a stable norm if you only see the plateau; (b) **name what drove the reference period** and ask whether that driver is still present; (c) **report the result against 2-3 candidate windows** — if they disagree, the disagreement *is* the finding and no single number should be published; (d) **apply the strongest scrutiny to the window that most flatters your result.** ⚠️ **Never extrapolate a trend whose slope was produced by a one-time cohort washing through** — and check whether the slope was already decelerating when you fitted it. **Sometimes the honest output is "no clean baseline exists," which is a real answer, not a failure.**
1. **Compute base rate + separation before shipping any threshold — and compute the EXIT/mirror's base rate in the same pass.** Under ~20pp separation, it is a descriptor. Do not ship it. **A kill/fire line whose mirror is the modal state is one-way by construction** — either find an event-grade line for the reverse direction or record explicitly that the instrument can only ever move the book one way.
2. **Prefer deltas and drawdowns to levels** unless the level *is* the decision boundary (a stop, a covenant, a margin call). Levels drift with regime; changes do not ([[finding_escalation_line_needs_delta_not_level]]).
3. **Record the NO with its numbers.** A killed threshold is a permanent saving, and the written record stops the same idea being re-proposed next quarter by someone reading the same suggestive print.
4. **Base-rate conjunctions JOINTLY, never by multiplying legs.** A compound measured 8.2% against a naive product that was nowhere near it ([[finding_compound_gate_jointly_unsatisfiable]]).
5. **Check every new spec against the live print: if it fires the day you wrote it, suspect yourself.** Not firing today is weak evidence you did not reverse-engineer it from the data in front of you.
6. **When a discredited gauge is still what the audience watches, demote rather than delete** — keep publishing it, move the trigger elsewhere, and say plainly that it no longer fires anything.
7. **Never re-point a LIVE prediction onto a replacement gauge you just built, in the direction that makes it fire.** Fix the instrument for the future; let the open call resolve on the letter it was written with ([[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]], [[finding_claim_outlives_its_discredited_instrument]]).

8. **A threshold set against a contaminated window is not "wrong by a bit" — it can be uninformative in the same way a level bar is.** The 2026-08-13 case: a `>10%` trigger the series had cleared in **31 consecutive quarters** before the anomaly. It was near-certain to fire for reasons unrelated to the state it was meant to detect. **Re-derive against a composition-adjusted baseline; do not re-cut against the contaminated window.**
9. **And the corollary that cost three months there: `INTEGRATED` means TRANSCRIBED, not REASONED ABOUT.** The observation ("back to pre-pandemic") had been **routed to the fleet in a signal headline, dispositioned `INTEGRATED`, and transcribed verbatim into the receiving agent's own log** — three months before anyone noticed it contradicted a live threshold. **Nothing in a normal disposition workflow asks whether an incoming fact CONTRADICTS a standing threshold.** When you log a fact, ask that question explicitly; and when you think you have found something novel, **grep your own ledgers first — it may already be written down** ([[finding_record_of_an_action_is_not_the_action]]).

10. **Base-rate the INCUMBENT and the CANDIDATE in the same pass, or you have measured a difference you cannot interpret.** "How often does my new rule fire?" is half the question; "how often does the one I am replacing fire?" is the half that gets skipped, because the incumbent feels like the neutral baseline rather than a claim. It is a claim. **Score both or you cannot say the change is an improvement** — and note which direction the asymmetry runs, because a replacement that ships un-scored against its predecessor is the common and dangerous case.
11. **When the threshold is a RATIO, base-rate the denominator's own behaviour separately.** A share/rate/percentage trigger can fire, or fail to fire, entirely on denominator movement while the quantity you care about does the opposite. Ask explicitly: *if the numerator were frozen, could this threshold still trip?* If yes, it is not measuring what its name says ([[finding_ratio_gauge_denominator_branch]], [[finding_normalization_choice_picks_opposite_winners]]).
