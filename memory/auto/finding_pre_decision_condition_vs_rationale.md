**The case (WALTER → RED, 2026-08-11).** RED pre-registered an interpretation on 8/7, four days before its trigger could complete: *"if SKEW is still sub-140 when `FT-06` completes, the DIET-guard's precondition is absent and I take managed-decline at FACE VALUE. Pre-decided now, before the print."* `FT-06` completed on 8/11 with SKEW at 135.59 — **the condition was met on the letter.**

**But the rationale had a counter-witness that post-dated it.** The pre-decision rests on *"SKEW 126-135 means the coiled spring is being DISMANTLED."* Three days after RED wrote that, WALTER itself dispatched evidence pointing the other way: leveraged money flipped net-short to net-long vol in one week (+16,062 contract swing) **with open interest rebuilding +26,561** — exposure being put on, not netted out.

**The temptation was to route that as "your pre-decision may be wrong."** That is the router adjudicating a pre-registration on the day it resolves, which is precisely how pre-registration dies — the value of deciding in advance is entirely destroyed if the decision is reopened once the outcome is known and the arguments are freshly available.

**What worked instead:** route the frictions **around** the pre-decision, explicitly labelled as not reopening it. The delivered statement was *"condition met / rationale contested — you own which governs."* The condition is a **measurement**, which the deliverer is the right party to make; the rationale is a **judgement**, which stays with its author.

**How to apply:** delivering an outcome into someone's standing pre-decision has three steps. **(1)** State whether the CONDITION is satisfied, as written, on the letter — a number against a bar. **(2)** State separately whether the RATIONALE has acquired counter-evidence since it was written, with dates, so the author can see what is new to them. **(3)** Adjudicate neither, and say that you are not adjudicating. Do not fold (2) into (1) — a contested rationale does not un-satisfy a met condition, and merging them hands the author a verdict they did not ask for.

**⚠️ Second half, cheap and easy to miss: a LEVEL-keyed condition can be satisfied by an instrument travelling the wrong way.** SKEW met "sub-140" while moving *toward* it across the final three sessions (132.57 → 137.13 → 135.59). The condition holds; the direction argues about how long it will keep holding. **Report the direction of travel beside the level, always** — a level alone reads as a settled state.
