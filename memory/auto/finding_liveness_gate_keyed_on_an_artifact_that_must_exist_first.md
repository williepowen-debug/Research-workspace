**The pattern (Kernel Gate C Sitting 2, RED verifier seat, 2026-09-01 → 09-02):** root carve-out ④ grants a desk commit only while a LIVE activation packet names the desk; the activation pins each submission by path + sha256. For the FIRST commit of a new file those two rules are circular — the packet needs the bytes, the bytes need the packet. On 9/1 RED read it as STOP (correct on the letter; the custodian was also absent, so nothing could be minted). On 9/2 the custodian resolved it by sequence: desk authors the file UNSTAGED (inert bytes in its own dir, not a submission), custodian mints the activation pinning path+hash, desk verifies the packet at the artifact on every leg, THEN commits the one pathspec. All five legs true at the commit instant; the record states the circumstance rather than laundering it.

**Why:** a gate keyed on liveness of an instrument that is itself derived from the gated act is a bootstrap defect in the RULE, not the party — and the two available "fixes" are asymmetric: relaxing the gate (key on ruled window bounds, the runbook's successor-scope wording) trades a loud STOP for a silent grant a desk cannot observe at act time; sequencing keeps the gate intact and moves the cost to the custodian being present. The divergence between the two keys stays a Will-gated canon item (DAEDALUS 9/1); satisfying the letter once is not evidence the divergence is harmless.

**How to apply:** when a control refuses your first-ever artifact because a pin/registration of that artifact must already exist — (1) do not relax or reinterpret the gate; (2) look for the inert-existence step (draft/unstaged/staging path) that lets the registering party pin bytes before the governed act; (3) verify the registration at the artifact on EVERY leg before the governed act, not on the custodian's word; (4) write the sequence into the record. If no custodian can mint, STOP is the right answer and the defect belongs to the rule's author, not the refusing desk.
