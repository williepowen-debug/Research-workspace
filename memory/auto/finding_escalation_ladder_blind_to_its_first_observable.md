**Instance (n=1) — SHADE, 2026-08-28 (Delaware Life, DL-041):** the six 7/27 escalation markers were all regulator / rating-agency / issuer acts. The Truist + Fifth Third **distribution PAUSE** — a counterparty decision needing no finding, filing or committee — had no rung, and in this case it came before any official act. SHADE inserted it as **marker (0)**.

**The hypothesis, stated as one:** a "what happens next" list enumerated from the actors who must FILE inherits those actors' latency; a private counterparty choosing not to transact (a pause, a haircut, a line not renewed) *may* be an earlier observable. Whether it *usually* is — across credit, insurance, banks — is **not established by one case**; the first draft of this memory said "precedes every official act" and "almost every stress", which was one instance written as a law (RAV review 8/28, accepted).

**Two things the instance does NOT conflate, and the ladder must not either:** the counterparty's first **action** (which may be private and unobservable to us) vs the first **publicly observable evidence** of it (a distributor's product list changing, a filing that mentions it, a desk hearing it). A rung is only useful if it names the observable, not the act.

**How to apply:** when registering an escalation ladder, ask *"what might a counterparty DO before anyone has to write anything down — and what would we actually SEE?"* Consider private-counterparty behaviour as a candidate rung (0); record it as a candidate until a second instance grades it.
