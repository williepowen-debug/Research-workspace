**A scope rule answers "who can SEE the whole population?" — not "who is better at deciding?" Almost every allocation of authority in this fleet is a visibility claim wearing a hierarchy's clothes.**

⛔ **The flattering misreading is the dangerous one.** "The coordinator judges better" is false, and believing it produces two failures at once: the junior seat defers where it should push back, and the senior seat stops checking its own basis. **The visibility reading produces a question instead of a posture — *what can that seat see that I cannot, and is it actually looking?***

## Worked case (PROME ↔ WALTER, 2026-09-11 evening) — and the author checked his own motive

The fleet's memory index tripped its 75%-of-cap flow rule. Canon (Will-ruled 2026-07-28): **any agent FLAGS, only PROME DEMOTES.** WALTER flagged, having contributed two of the rows, and explicitly did not propose which rows should go.

PROME first explained this as the coordinator making the better call. **Then it checked why it had actually held `finding_silent_blank_evades_review` hot — and the reason was that VIOLET's blank-cell finding had crossed its desk FORTY MINUTES EARLIER.** Not foresight. **Position.** WALTER could see its own two rows; PROME could see the whole evening. Choosing which rows fire unpredictably mid-work is a judgement about the WHOLE INDEX against the WHOLE WEEK, and WALTER could not have made it well from where it sat — not because it judges worse.

⭐ **A rule that survives its author checking their own motive is worth more than one that does not.** The check is cheap and almost never run: *name the specific thing you saw that they could not.* If you cannot name it, you were claiming competence, not visibility — and the rule may be wrong.

## The general form — four live instances from one evening, all the same shape

| Rule | Looks like | Actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **WQ-206 aged-action grant** | PROME's discretion over another desk's backlog | PROME can enumerate LIQUID's WHOLE inbox; WALTER sees its own dispatch. PROME DECLINED the drain on the population evidence (0 of 28 items action-class) |
| **The doorbell gate** | a routing desk asking permission | WALTER sees ROUTING, PROME sees the FLEET; neither alone can price "does this desk's accumulated state justify a touch?" |
| **Read-cap perimeter** | a per-file byte budget | a cross-agent read is invisible at BOTH ends because neither desk can see the population — the reason the failure is silent |
| **DOCKET L334 (info-backlog study)** | a threshold proposal | literally a question about whether ANYONE can see a population that has no instrument — which is why the answer is "measure it," not "pick a bar" |

**⇒ When a scope rule confuses you, do not ask who outranks whom. Ask: WHAT POPULATION IS THIS DECISION ABOUT, AND WHO CAN ENUMERATE IT?** The seat that can enumerate it is the seat the rule names, and if NOBODY can, the rule is not a rule yet — it is an unbuilt instrument (that is exactly what L334 is).

## How to apply

1. **Before deferring to a seat, name what it can see that you cannot.** Can't name it ⇒ you are deferring to rank. Push back or ask.
2. **Before exercising an authority, name the population you can enumerate and they cannot.** Can't name it ⇒ you may be asserting a lane rather than reading a grant (`[[finding_scope_boundary_asserted_from_proximity]]`, which fired TWICE in this same evening in OPPOSITE directions — WALTER claiming reach a grant did not give, PROME in August declining reach a grant did give).
3. **Write the VISIBILITY BASIS into the rule when you author one.** "Only PROME demotes" is obeyed; "only PROME demotes, because only PROME sees the whole index against the whole week" is *understood*, and can be falsified when it stops being true.
4. ⚠️ **A visibility claim EXPIRES.** If a flagging desk gains an instrument that enumerates the population, the rule's basis is gone and the rule should move, not persist on precedent. Re-derive the basis, do not inherit it.
5. **When no seat can see the population, stop allocating and start measuring.** Authority questions with no enumerable population are instrument gaps in disguise.
