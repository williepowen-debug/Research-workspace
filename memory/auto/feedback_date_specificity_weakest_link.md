When a falsifiable prediction is both (a) low confidence (<~50%) and (b) about an event-driven outcome (a cut, a filing, a disclosure, a resolution), the **specific Resolve_Date is the most fragile clause**. The thing you're least sure about is *when*, not *whether*. Pinning a tight date at low confidence means the prediction can resolve FALSIFIED on timing while being directionally correct on substance — a calibration loss that teaches nothing about the underlying thesis.

**Why:** OTTO-26 predicted a PSEC dividend cut at the Feb 20 earnings (40% conf). The cut DID happen ($0.045 → $0.035) — but on May 7 (Q3 FY26 earnings), ~2.5 months later. Falsified on date, directionally correct on substance. The 40% confidence was honest about *whether*; the tight Feb-20 date added a second uncertain clause the prediction couldn't support. Related: [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]] (separating what-fires from when/why it fires).

**How to apply:** When drafting a date-specific prediction at <50% confidence, choose ONE to pin tight, not both: either widen the Resolve_Date to a window that comfortably brackets the catalyst (e.g., "by end of next earnings cycle" not "at the Feb print"), or keep the date but raise it to a near-certain event. Don't pin a tight date AND a low confidence — that's two bets stacked, and the date is the one that breaks. At resolution, score date-falsified-but-direction-right as a *calibration-on-timing* loss, distinct from a thesis miss. Transferable to any agent maintaining a prediction book (BROCK/HENRY/SAM/REGINALD).
