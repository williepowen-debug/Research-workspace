When presenting a "two-way read" or scenario branches, **grade each branch's near-term direction explicitly** — not just its mechanism. Symmetric-sounding language ("also bullish, via different mechanism") can hide directional asymmetry, especially when scenarios resolve at different time horizons.

**Why:** Caught Jun 3 2026 on BRENT EIA WPSR synthesis. SPR floor scenarios were framed as:
- Throttle (base case): -1.1-1.3 mbpd supply removed → bullish
- Drain-through (tail case): backstop finite → "also bullish, via different mechanism"

Will's correction: the drain-through is **near-term BEARISH** (incremental SPR supply continuing past expected throttle + administration visibly capping the tape) / **medium-term bullish** (backstop exhausts faster). The symmetric framing presented a directional event ("Jun 10 print") as a one-way trade — same shape as the Cushing-floor falsification earlier the same morning, just with the bias hidden under "two-way read" headers rather than a deterministic projection.

**How to apply:**
- When writing scenario branches, **separate time horizon from direction**. Each branch needs both axes (near-term: bullish/bearish/neutral; medium-term: bullish/bearish/neutral).
- If both branches are claimed bullish (or bearish) via different mechanisms, treat that as a red flag and re-examine. Genuinely two-way reads have actual directional disagreement, not just mechanism diversity.
- Same scrutiny applies to "either-way wins" framing on any binary catalyst (auction result, FOMC decision, OPEC outcome, treaty signature, central-bank meeting, etc.).
- Related to [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]] (separate threshold-hit from mechanism-intact) — both lessons preserve directional rigor under uncertainty-framing pressure.

**Cross-agent applicability:** SAM (BOJ/MOF policy scenarios, JGB auction reads), HAWK (kinetic-vs-diplomatic branches), HENRY (FOMC reaction-function), LIQUID (credit-event paths), CARL (consumer-confidence paths), MARCO (tariff/trade-policy branches). Any agent doing scenario-tree reasoning under uncertainty.
