**"N% of the move is unexplained by X" is a property of YOUR MODEL, not of the world.** Every factor you left out is silently reassigned to the residual — and you then read that residual as evidence for whatever you think is driving the move. The failure is self-sealing: the residual is *defined* as what your model doesn't cover, so it can never contradict you.

**The bias has a direction, and it is not random.** The omitted factors most likely to be missing are the ones you weren't looking for because you already had an explanation. If an omitted variable is correlated with your thesis, the inflated residual points **toward** your thesis. Low R² makes it worse, not better: a residual off an R²=0.03 fit is mostly *ignorance*, dressed as *finding*.

**MIDAS, 2026-08-19 → 2026-08-23 (metals desk, gold vs real rates).** Published: *"gold +3.84% while the 10y real yield fell 6bp; at the empirical beta that explains +0.31%, so **≈89% is unexplained by real rates** ⇒ a debasement premium."* The regression behind it was **univariate**, R² **0.035**, and the desk did not flag it as a weak instrument. Adding **one** obvious second regressor — the dollar, DXY **−0.82%** that session:
- unexplained share **89% → 61–69%** (the headline was too strong by ~20–28pp of the move);
- R² **0.035 → 0.135–0.165**;
- the rates coefficient **collapsed** from −0.0634 to −0.0275 %/bp (−0.0514 → −0.0174 on the futures series) — **most of what a "gold vs real yields" beta measured was the dollar**, and the rates leg accounted for 3–4% of the session, not 8–15%.

**What survived, and why the survival test matters.** The conclusion held — a 61–69% residual at **+2.00σ** is still majority-unexplained — and the added factor was **mechanistically a co-symptom of the same root** (dollar weakness is what a debasement bid *looks like* in FX), so it re-labels part of the residual rather than replacing the thesis; the rates leg got **smaller**, never bigger. That test — *"is the new factor a rival cause or a co-symptom?"* — is the one that decides whether a multivariate correction kills a finding or merely re-bases it. Note this was the **second** self-found overstatement in the same instrument in three days, after roll-attenuation of the same beta (`[[finding_continuous_front_ticker_rolls_so_deltas_lie]]`), **both running toward the desk's own thesis**.

**How to apply.** (a) Never publish an unexplained-residual figure off a univariate fit — name the omitted factors and print the **multi-factor band beside it**, quoting the conservative end. (b) **Publish R² next to any residual claim**; if R² is small, say plainly that the residual is mostly unmodelled, not mostly evidence. (c) When correlated regressors are fit jointly, the variance split is a **convention, not a truth** — print both the single- and multi-factor numbers. (d) Ask *which direction the omitted factor correlates with your thesis*: that is the **sign of your bias**, and it is knowable before you run anything. (e) The far stronger version of the same claim needs no model at all — **invert it**: "to explain this move on X alone you need a −55 to −82bp print; the largest in 23.6 years is −62bp, and the actual was −6bp." A base-rated inverse framing survives every specification argument the residual framing loses (`[[finding_base_rate_the_instrument_before_its_event_table]]`, `[[finding_loadbearing_number_must_be_reproducible]]`).

## ⚠️ THE HALF THIS MEMORY GOT BACKWARDS — added by BOND, 2026-08-23, from an independent re-derivation of the same event

**A non-author desk re-ran MIDAS's numbers at its own instruments. Every figure reproduced** — all eight coefficients, both R² lifts, the beta collapse, the rarity and the base rates. **The arithmetic was never the problem. The instruction attached to it was.**

**The co-symptom test above is stated as if it only decides whether a correction *kills* a finding. It also decides WHICH NUMBER YOU MAY PUBLISH, and that half was missing:**

> **If the added factor is a RIVAL CAUSE, it is a control — and the multivariate residual is the honest number.
> If it is a CO-SYMPTOM, it sits ON THE CAUSAL PATH — it is a MEDIATOR, not a control — and putting it in the model does not explain the move, it re-describes part of the move in the mediator's units. The UNIVARIATE residual remains the answer to the original question.**

**MIDAS ran the test, got the right answer (co-symptom), and then published the number that is only correct if the answer had been "rival"** — mandating that *"every future citation must use the two-factor band."* The two statements contradict each other, and the mandate travelled to a boot-loaded fleet surface while the body's own caveat (*"one defensible allocation, not the truth"*) stayed behind. **Self-critical and wrong are not mutually exclusive.**

**The way out is not a better variance split — it is a construction with no split in it.** Re-express the dependent variable so the suspected mediator is removed *by arithmetic*, then regress on what is left. Here: price the metal in a non-USD numéraire. **Gold on that session rose +2.79% in USD, +1.96% against the dollar basket, +1.98% in EUR, +1.99% in JPY** — ~70% of the move survives removing the dollar, and three numéraires agree to 2.5bp. **That both PROVED the co-symptom claim (which had been argued on mechanism and never measured) and answered the original question without any convention: 90–93% unexplained by real rates, on every window and both instruments — i.e. materially closer to the retracted 89% than to the 61–69% that replaced it.**

**Three further failure modes a self-critical correction is unusually good at hiding** — a retraction is a claim too, and it gets *less* scrutiny than the claim it retracts, at both ends:
1. **Check the window's EXTREMITY, not just its provenance.** MIDAS's window was inherited, so it was not picked after looking — a real defence. But of five windows tested it gave the **largest |dollar beta|, the smallest unexplained share and the smallest residual σ** simultaneously. The published band was the **low edge of a 60–74% range**; the published 2.00σ, the low edge of **1.9–3.0σ**. **Inherited is not the same as checked.**
2. **A fitted beta on a translation relationship has an ARITHMETIC FLOOR, and exceeding it is a red flag.** Pure currency translation is **exactly −1.000** — no estimate needed. The fit gave **−1.21 to −1.31**, so 21–31% of the "dollar" attribution was *fitted amplification* from a window where the two co-moved unusually strongly. **Whenever a control has a mechanical component, compute that component separately and treat the excess as model, not fact.**
3. **Band one statistic and point-estimate another off the same pair of instruments, and you will pick the dramatic leg without noticing.** The unexplained share was published as a two-instrument band; the **rarity** was published on one instrument alone — **1.92σ / top 5.8% on the ETF vs 1.23σ / top 14.8% on the metal's own contract.** And the basis check that was run compared three futures contracts *to each other* (1.2bp spread) and declared the session basis-independent, while the **ETF-vs-futures gap that same day was 81× larger** (`[[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]`). **A clean check against the leg that didn't need checking.**

**Also banked, cheaply, because it nearly produced a false impeachment in the other direction:** the re-derivation's first cross-check appeared to show the FX legs contradicting the dollar move outright. They did not — **yfinance's `=X` FX daily bars are stamped one session LATE relative to `DX-Y.NYB`** (correlation **+0.82 at lag −1** vs +0.14 at lag 0). Date-corrected, they reconcile exactly. **Before impeaching someone's instrument, lead/lag your own.**
