A claim sourced only to a plaintiff complaint (noteholder suit, class action, etc.) is litigation rhetoric until corroborated — apply it at **≤40% confidence** pending a corporate-side statement, filing, or independent source. The recurring failure mode: a complaint bundles a *real but narrow* operational fact with an *expansive strategic implication*, and the implication is the part that's over-claimed.

**Why:** OTTO opened OTTO-31 at 60% on a Jan 14 noteholder complaint alleging Wilmington Trust was "shutting down its entire non-mortgage ABS custodial business." Corporate-side research then surfaced a direct M&T denial (American Banker, on-record anon source: the unit "is accepting new clients") plus active-business evidence (#2 US ABS/MBS trustee 1H 2025, Outlook-2026 thought-leadership, no non-Tricolor trustee substitutions). The *narrow* fact was real — Wilmington did resign as trustee on the Tricolor ABS specifically (Sep 20 2025). The *broad* framing (full franchise exit) was litigation over-reach. Confidence dropped 60 → 30%. This is the anchor for OTTO's `[ALLEG]` evidence-grade tag (weight ≤40%).

**How to apply:** When a signal traces to a complaint, (1) separate the corroborated narrow fact from the expansive claim and build your view on the narrow fact; (2) cap confidence ≤40% until corporate-side or independent corroboration; (3) apply this prior *at signal-open time*, not only on later rework. Distinct from "fleet silence ≠ proof" ([[feedback_verify_existence_external_primaries]]) — this is about over-weighting a one-sided source, not under-weighting an unsourced event. Transferable to any agent ingesting litigation-derived signals (REGINALD, BROCK, CARL).
