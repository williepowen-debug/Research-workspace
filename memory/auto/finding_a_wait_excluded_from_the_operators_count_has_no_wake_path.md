**The finding (PROME, 2026-09-10, Will *"I am wondering if/when we act on the decisions?"*):** the Decision Deck made ruling cheap (20 taps in a day) and exposed the back of the pipe: a ruling becomes action only when the owner desk runs, and desks are dark by default. The three wake rules (dark-owner doorbell, WQ-184 dated rows, WQ-206 aged actions) all key on a row that NAMES the desk with a date or an ACTION line. The deck's third group — rows that ⛔ wait on another desk's draft, proposal or levels — is deliberately excluded from Will's count so it does not nag him, and by the same construction it matches no spawn rule. Measured that night: WQ-74 waited 20 days for oil exit levels that TERRY had delivered on 9/9 and Will had declined on 9/10 under a DIFFERENT number (WQ-200) — nobody re-read the older row; WQ-216 (OSPREY/HAWK taxonomy) had waited since 8/20 and WQ-219 (BROCK's draft) since 8/13 behind dark desks.

**Why:** "excluded from the count" and "has a wake path" were assumed to be independent properties; they are the same property seen from two sides. Anything removed from the operator's attention must be given to SOME clock, or it has none. A wait is also a place where a delivery can land under another identifier: the older row keeps waiting for an event that already happened.

**How to apply:** every ⛔-waits row gets a dated DOCKET row naming the BLOCKING desk as owner (reconsider-by = registration + 7d) so the existing spawn driver is the one wake mechanism — never a fourth spawn clause. At every reconcile, check each wait against the blocker's newest artifact (the delivery may have landed under a different row) before extending it. On the deck, a wait pill names the blocker and days dark. Registered as WQ-221 (2026-09-10) for Will's word.
