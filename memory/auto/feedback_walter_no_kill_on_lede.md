When Will pastes or makes available the full body of an article, read it before finalizing KILL/ROUTE classification. Don't trust the lede to predict the body's signal density.

**Why:** On 2026-04-14, WALTER killed a Seeking Alpha KRE piece based on lede-only read (stale, low-credibility author, consensus framing). Will then pasted the full body, which contained three hard data points that were thesis-relevant counter-evidence: KRE YTD -1.94% vs XLF -12.56% (regionals outperforming by ~10pts), FLG as KRE's #1 holding with a recent Fitch upgrade (direct position relevance — we have FLG puts), and $936B CRE-maturity aggregate with $59.5B office split. The named-aggregator stats (Morningstar, Redfin, BEA, IEA) raised credibility of specific claims even though the author synthesis was weak. Reversed KILL → filed as PRIORITY counter-evidence to RED.

**How to apply:**
- If Will pastes only part of an article and promises more, wait for the full paste before classifying.
- If the full body is available, skim all sections — especially "By the numbers," tables, and citations — for data points BEFORE deciding kill.
- Distinguish author-synthesis (often weak) from named-aggregator data (often citable). A low-credibility author can still surface a real Morningstar/Redfin/BLS stat.
- Even on KILL decisions, extract named data points into kill_log notes so they're recoverable.
