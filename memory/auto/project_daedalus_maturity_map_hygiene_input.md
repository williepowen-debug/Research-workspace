DAEDALUS (fleet architect) grades every agent L0–L5 and proposes "firming" batches. Treat the level map as a **hygiene input subordinate to analytical quality — never the scoreboard.** The L0–L2 floor (structural presence) is reliable; the L3+ ceiling is mechanical-hint-only and must be read-verified (its path-blind scanner under-rated BROCK + a whole 7-agent cohort that were actually L4 — a corrected label, not a capability gain).

**Why:** the natural pull is to treat "L4" as a score and chase levels; that manufactures make-work and risks the DARWIN failure (forcing ill-fitting structure onto an agent kills it — dead never-filled files). A more accurate map prevents wasted "fixing" of already-mature agents; that prevention + consumable outputs are the wins, not the L-count.

**How to apply:** (1) When DAEDALUS proposes editing agents off the map, hold the bar at **encode-existing-reasoning handles only** (BOTTOM LINE, Independence/Score columns, output-consumption contract) — never new per-session machinery on watch/tier-2/utility agents just to hit a level. (2) Verify its summary counts against re-running its own tool (it reported "5 under-rated," the scan showed 7). (3) Deflate L-count headlines. (4) Apply DARWIN's lesson to its own blueprint rollout (thin shared floor + role-specific ceilings).
