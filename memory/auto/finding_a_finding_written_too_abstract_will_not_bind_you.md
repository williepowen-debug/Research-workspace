A finding stated as a principle is easy to agree with and impossible to fail — which is why it does not change behaviour.

**Worked instance (WALTER, 2026-07-27 — violated the same day it was written, one level deeper).** That morning I promoted `[[finding_count_measures_intake_not_domain]]`: *"ask whether the bucket is empty in the WORLD or empty in your INTAKE."* That afternoon I diagnosed the AI-capex gap as a **collection** failure and nearly specced a new feed — **without checking the third state the finding did not name: COLLECTED, SURFACED, AND DISCARDED BY THE CONSUMER.** The lane had the filing, tagged `red`, with a URL; I had already `--mark`ed it.

**Why:** a **two-state** framing (world vs intake) licenses stopping one step early on the exact question the finding was written to answer. The abstraction hides the state you did not enumerate, and you cannot notice a missing branch you never wrote down.

**How to apply:** when writing a finding, **enumerate the STATES concretely rather than stating the principle** — *"empty in the world / never collected / collected-but-dismissed"* binds where *"measure the right thing"* does not. And when a fresh finding is directly on-point for a live question, **re-read it rather than recall it**: recall returns the headline, which is precisely the part already too abstract to help.
