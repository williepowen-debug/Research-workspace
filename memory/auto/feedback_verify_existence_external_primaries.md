When a claim asserts a real-world event occurred (especially post-knowledge-cutoff / future-dated), verify its EXISTENCE against independent external primary sources — do NOT treat absence in our own fleet's files as proof it didn't happen.

**Why:** In the 2026-05-31 produce-attribution work, the deep-research harness claimed a $3.17B Florida freeze (Dec 2025–Feb 2026). I called it FABRICATED because no fleet agent (MARCO/CARL/REGINALD) had logged it — then built a confident mechanistic story for *why* it was fake. Will pushed back: "just because our fleet hasn't logged it doesn't mean it didn't happen." A direct WebSearch confirmed the freeze was REAL (USDA disaster declaration, WGCU/FlaglerLive/FreshPlaza). The fleet had simply missed it — nobody owns ag-weather. Two symmetric errors: a research harness over-asserts events that don't exist (fills voids with plausible archetypes); I over-asserted an event didn't exist because it wasn't in our records. Same root failure — substituting a confident narrative for an actual check.

**How to apply:** (1) Internal records adjudicate *consistency*, never *existence* — they're incomplete by design (domain seams like ag-weather). (2) To settle existence, fetch independent external primaries (gov declarations, multiple real outlets), not the suspected source and not our own files. (3) Hold the same skepticism in BOTH directions — distrust a harness's confident "verified ✓" AND distrust your own confident "fabricated." (4) When uncertain, say "uncorroborated," not "fabricated."
