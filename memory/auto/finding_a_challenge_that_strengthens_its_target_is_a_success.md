An adversarial commission that fails to overturn its target reads like a wasted run. It usually is not — and scoring it that way corrupts the next one.

**Worked instance (WALTER, 2026-07-25).** PROMPT-19 refuted **2 of my own 4 hypotheses outright**, found the 3rd real-but-immaterial, and only the 4th moved anything. Yet the same run: struck a widely-cited Urban Institute citation **fleet-wide**, falsified the parent's own load-bearing sentence, **corrected 3 of the parent's citations**, surfaced a statutorily-compelled quarterly data surface nobody was reading, and **saved one live watch from erroneous closure**.

**Why:** "did it overturn the conclusion?" is a single-bit score on a multi-output process. A challenge that leaves the conclusion standing **on better foundations** has moved the evidence base, which is the durable half. Scoring on overturn-rate teaches the next commission to hunt for reversals, which is how adversarial work turns into contrarianism.

**How to apply:** report the shape explicitly — *"conclusion survives, evidence replaced"* — and enumerate what moved (citations struck, surfaces found, watches saved) rather than filing a null. **Corollary that generalises past adversarial work:** when you author a re-test, **the parent's citations are inputs to be checked, not facts to be carried** — this prompt inherited **three** citation errors from the parent report (a circular number, a superseded Mortgagee Letter, and a fiscal-year category error).
