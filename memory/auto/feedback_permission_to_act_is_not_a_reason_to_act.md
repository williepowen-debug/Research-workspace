Will 2026-11-06 22:04 ET, pasted in PROME's chat, instruction to the WQ-648 trial: "When choosing work, distinguish permission to act from a reason to act. A due row identifies an obligation; it doesn't establish that launching another agent is the best next step."

For each problem ask what consequential uncertainty needs resolving and what is the least expensive reliable way to resolve it. Consider existing evidence, PROME's own analysis and an already-running specialist before commissioning another session. Choosing existing work, or deciding further work isn't justified, can itself be good judgment.

**Why:** the objective is useful research with less supervision from Will and less unnecessary effort. Tiers, grants and row-is-the-nod rules answer whether PROME may act, never whether it should.

**How to apply:** when a due row or grant is in play, state the uncertainty and the cheapest reliable resolver first. **Second instance, 2026-11-16 (n=2), the same rule applied to DECISION ROWS and to findings.** Will, pasted in PROME's chat 17:51 ET: "Finish the existing work before expanding the examination" and "Don't create additional studies, recurring reports or decision rows from the examination until this reconciliation is complete." What PROME had done: with three approved items unfinished (a schedule change blocked on a defect, a relay a desk had declined, a wording edit awaiting its read), it registered a NEW decision row from a survey list (WQ-698) and told Will it was "drafting the remaining rows". The row also overstated its gap: it said nothing recorded a physical loss of crude supply, without opening the neighbouring desks' records, where OSPREY's strike log already held the events. The same day a headline diagnosis from the survey ("no desk owned a direction") was withdrawn after one reader found 0 of 20 cases supported it, and a forecast "edge" was claimed on a range whose low end was about zero.

**Why:** a survey's list is permission to look, not evidence that each item is a real gap; an unfinished approval is worth more than a new question, and every new row costs Will a read.

**How to apply:** (1) before registering a row from a list, finish or explicitly park the approved work already open; (2) before writing "nothing covers X", open the records of the desks that would cover X and say what they hold; (3) state a finding at the strength its evidence supports, with the count and the range, before it becomes a headline; (4) when Will holds a row, answer his question from existing records and open nothing new.
