When you import another domain's signal into your convergence matrix, the staleness check ("is this still true?") is the wrong first question. The first question is the **sign check**: *does this signal move MY thesis's target metric in the direction MY thesis claims?* A signal can be fully confirmed and intact yet be orthogonal-to-counter for your thesis.

**Case (LABOR, Jun 16 2026):** LABOR's convergence matrix scored ICE enforcement (5🔴🔴) and H-2A bottleneck (4🔴) as bearish-employment convergence — +9 points of a 57/85. But those are MARCO labor-*supply* signals, and a migration-driven supply cut keeps unemployment *low* / wages *up* — the **opposite** of LABOR's demand-weakness (rising-claims/rising-U-3) thesis. Re-verifying them (Orc's instinct) would have *confirmed* them and left the 9 points in place; ICE had actually *strengthened* (funding signed into law). The defect wasn't staleness, it was sign: real signals scored on the wrong side. Honest re-grade pulled them to a CROSS-DOMAIN CONTEXT table and kept only the narrow same-direction slice (worksite-disruption → on-site layoffs, scored 2). Score dropped 57/85 → 48/80.

**Rule:** for every cross-domain vector in a convergence/score table, write down which way it moves your thesis's headline metric. If it moves it the wrong way (or sideways), it is context, not convergence — pull it out of the score. Especially watch supply-vs-demand inversions (a supply shock and a demand shock move quantity the same way but PRICE/slack opposite ways). Sibling of [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]] and [[finding_catalyst_vs_consequence_conflation]] — all three are "the signal is real but you're crediting it wrong." Transferable to any agent running a convergence matrix (CARL/REGINALD/BROCK/HENRY/NEXUS).
