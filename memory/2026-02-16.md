## Late Session: Lender Strategies Research (21:30-21:48 UTC)

Will asked: WHY do elevated delinquencies not convert to charge-offs?

### The Conversion Puzzle
- **GFC:** 7% delinquency → 10.5% charge-off (unemployment 10%)
- **Now:** 7% delinquency → 4.2% charge-off (unemployment 4.2%)
- **Key insight:** Delinquent + employed = eventually catches up. Delinquent + lost job = charge-off.

### Fed Research on WHY Delinquencies Rose (Without Unemployment)
From Fed and Fed researcher papers:
- **MAJOR factor:** Real debt levels increased (not just spending, actual balances grew)
- **MAJOR factor:** Credit extended to riskier borrowers (loose 2021-22 standards)
- **MINOR factor:** Unemployment
- **MINOR factor:** Interest rates
- **"Credit score migration"** — pandemic stimulus artificially boosted scores, lenders couldn't distinguish true from inflated creditworthiness

### Lender Strategies Identified (Charge-Off Avoidance)

1. **Hardship Programs** — Reduced rates, fee waivers, modified payment plans
2. **Forbearance** — Temporary payment pause, full or partial
3. **Account Re-Aging** — OCC rules: 9-month minimum account age, 3 consecutive payments required, can only re-age once per period
4. **Auto Term Extensions** — Up to 84 months to reduce monthly payments
5. **Debt Restructuring** — Consolidation into new terms

### Why This Matters for Thesis
Current elevated delinquencies are a **normalization problem** (debt + loose lending), NOT an employment problem. Employment cracking would be ADDITIVE — would change conversion rates from "eventual cure" to "permanent loss."

**CACC conditional trade:** Only works if claims >250K sustained triggers employment-driven charge-offs.
