## Executive Summary

**Thesis PARTIALLY VALIDATED:** Insurers see fraud before public disclosure. FAF and FNF both announced major fraud prevention investments in **September 2025** — 5 weeks before Stupin/Cantor went public in October.

**Key Insight:** Operational capex (tech investments, new endorsements) is the leading indicator, not claims reserves which lag.

## Fidelity Bond Coverage Analysis

### WAL vs ZION Divergence

| Factor | WAL | ZION |
|--------|-----|------|
| Claim Basis | "Forgery" — unauthorized signatures | Cannot claim forgery |
| Exclusion Issue | Loans carve-out with collusion exception | "Authorized Signer Defense" blocks |
| Potential Recovery | Up to $25M | None |

WAL can claim under "forged signatures" or "counterfeit documents" provisions since title policies were allegedly manufactured/fake.

## New Industry Standards

### ALTA 49 Endorsements (August 2025)
- **ALTA 49:** Forgery coverage in new owner's policy
- **ALTA 49.1:** Existing owner protection for forward-looking forgery

### ALTA Best Practices v4.2
- Specialized staff training to detect impersonation
- Stronger notary/signing-agent controls
- Additional third-party verification steps
- Defined fraud response protocols

### Technology Investments

| Company | Technology | Purpose |
|---------|------------|---------|
| FAF | Intellicheck AI | Early transaction ID verification |
| FNF | CLEAR biometric | Prevent impersonation |

## Key Takeaway

**Operational response (tech investments, endorsements, best practices) is a more timely signal than claims data.** The September 2025 investments should have been a signal that something was building.

**Leading Indicator:** Watch for title insurer capex announcements on fraud prevention tech.
