# Theme Park Attendance & Convention Travel as Leading Indicators for L&H Unemployment Claims

**Research Date:** March 19, 2026  
**Analyst:** LABOR subagent  
**Status:** Preliminary — sourced from public earnings calls, industry reports, and news

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Theme park attendance and convention/business travel are plausible **3–8 week leading indicators** for leisure and hospitality unemployment claims. The mechanism: attendance softness → reduced staffing hours → layoffs/non-recalls → UI claims filing. The lag exists because parks manage labor through seasonal adjustment and variable scheduling before resorting to separations. Key 2026 risk: Disney has explicitly flagged international visitation headwinds, Las Vegas had a slow January, GBTA data shows ~29% of corporate travel managers expect volume declines, and spring break is the NOW-or-never window for summer booking signals.

---

## (A) DISNEY, UNIVERSAL, SEAWORLD — ATTENDANCE SENSITIVITY TO GAS/MACRO SHOCKS

### 2008 Recession Case Study
**Source: TEA/ERA Attraction Attendance Report 2008**

- **Magic Kingdom (WDW):** Attendance flat at ~17.1M vs. prior year — the headline was "resilience" but this masked a Q4 2008 collapse that occurred after regional parks had already closed for the season, so year-round destination parks "took the fourth quarter hit"
- **SeaWorld Orlando:** Down **2.9%** in 2008
- **Universal Islands of Adventure:** Down **2.4%** in 2008
- **Epcot:** Up ~1%, seen as outperformer given its different visitor profile

Key quote from ERA Senior VP John Robinett (2009): *"It is typical of destination parks to be impacted more by a recession than regional parks, because they are located farther away from their markets, and cost more to visit. The negative growth didn't actually occur until the fall."*

**Implication:** Destination parks are a lagged recession indicator themselves — they show weakness after regional activity turns. The Q4 2008 hit was sudden once the financial crisis crystallized consumer uncertainty.

### 2022 Gas Price Spike Case Study
**Source: OC Register / Citi Analyst James Hardiman, July 2022**

- Summer 2022: National average gas peaked at ~$5.01/gallon (June 2022)
- Wall Street analysts flagged "lagging attendance and spending levels at Disney, Six Flags, SeaWorld, and other U.S. theme parks" despite predictions of pent-up post-COVID demand
- Citi Analyst James Hardiman (July 8, 2022) downgraded SeaWorld Entertainment, Cedar Fair, and Six Flags citing "the month of June particularly problematic"
- Wells Fargo analyst Steven Cahall said Disney stock had "seen the wheels come off" in 2022, projecting lower spending, hotel revenue, and operating income extending into 2023
- Six Flags and Cedar Fair showed the earliest weakness (June 2022) — they rely more heavily on drive-market, lower-income visitors most sensitive to gas prices
- Disney appeared more resilient initially due to pre-booked vacations but forward guidance deteriorated

**Pattern:** Gas prices hit Six Flags/SeaWorld/regional parks first (lower-income, drive-market guests), then ripple to Disney (higher-income, longer-planned trips) 4–8 weeks later.

### Disney Q1 FY2026 Earnings — February 2, 2026 (Most Recent)
**Source: Disney Q1 FY2026 Earnings Call Transcript, Motley Fool; InsidetheMagic.net; CNBC**

**The candid disclosure:** Disney rarely admits to attendance challenges explicitly. Their Q1 FY2026 commentary was notable for its directness:

- **CFO Hugh Johnston** flagged "**international visitation headwinds at domestic parks**" as a significant factor limiting Q2 FY2026 operating income growth
- Q2 FY2026 guidance: Only "**modest**" segment OI growth — a conspicuous downgrade from prior quarters' strength
- Johnston acknowledged Disney has "**less visibility**" on international visitors because they stay in Disney hotels less frequently than domestic guests
- Domestic hotel bookings at WDW were described as strong and up 5% for the full year, weighted toward the back half — suggesting front-half softness
- The parks recorded $10B+ in quarterly revenue (a record) but forward guidance was notably muted

**What Disney flagged but didn't say explicitly:**
- The "international visitation headwind" is partly attributed to: strong dollar, US border policy anxieties, reduced Canadian and European travel sentiment (anti-US economic sentiment post-tariffs)
- InsidetheMagic.net (Feb 3, 2026): *"Disney used plain language to describe the situation, which suggests the impact is significant enough to warrant transparency with investors."*

**Historical context (Disney Tourist Blog, FY2025 full year):**
- Domestic attendance down 1% FY2025 — first decrease since COVID closure
- CFO attributed entirely to Hurricane Milton (Q4 2024 impact ~$120M on operating income)
- But on an adjusted basis (removing hurricane effect), attendance was essentially flat to slightly down vs. a "normal" baseline
- Disney ran "historically aggressive" discounts in summer 2025 to fill resorts — a demand management signal, not just pricing strategy

## (B) GEOGRAPHIC CONCENTRATION — EMPLOYMENT AT RISK

### Orlando / Orange County, FL
**Source: Visit Orlando / Tourism Economics Report; ClickOrlando.com (Aug 2025)**

- Tourism and hospitality (direct + indirect) accounts for **42% of all jobs in Orange County** and **35% in Osceola County**
- Total supported employment: ~**468,000 jobs** across the Central Florida region (~37% of all regional employment)
- WDW is the largest single-site employer in FL with ~77,000 direct employees
- Central Florida tourism generated **$94.5 billion** in economic impact in 2024 — a record
- These numbers mean a 5% attendance decline translates to potential exposure of ~20,000+ jobs before multiplier effects on supporting businesses (hotels, restaurants, retail)

**Key vulnerability:** The 37–42% concentration makes Orange County L&H claims extremely sensitive to demand shifts. There is NO other county in the US with comparable theme park concentration at this scale.

### Anaheim / Orange County, CA
**Source: CSUF Economic Impact Study; OC Business Journal (June 2025); Anaheim Chamber of Commerce**

- Disneyland Resort is **Orange County's single largest employer** with ~36,000 direct employees
- Disneyland supports **102,000 jobs** in Southern California (direct + indirect)
- Disneyland alone accounts for ~**3.6% of ALL jobs in Orange County** (CSUF study)
- Orange County has ~160,000 tourism industry jobs total — Disneyland is ~23% of that
- $16.1B in annual economic impact on Southern California

**Anaheim specificity:** Unlike Orlando, Anaheim is partially buffered by proximity to LA metro, but the Disneyland Resort area is the dominant employer. A 10% attendance drop would cascade through the Anaheim Resort District (hotels, restaurants, retail) rapidly.

### San Antonio, TX
- SeaWorld San Antonio + Six Flags Fiesta Texas are major employers in the region
- SeaWorld San Antonio employs ~4,000 directly (seasonal peak higher) but is a meaningful part of Bexar County's L&H sector
- San Antonio's L&H sector is more diversified than Orlando — convention center (Henry B. Gonzalez), River Walk, military installations. Theme park dependence lower (~8–12% of L&H employment) but still material for summer seasonal hiring signals

## (C) CONVENTION / BUSINESS TRAVEL DATA — 2026

### Las Vegas — Slow Start to 2026
**Source: Travel and Tour World; LVCVA data (March 2026)**

- January 2026: Gaming revenue on the Strip fell **11% year-over-year** (broader measure) / Strip total gaming win -6.55%
- Baccarat underperformance was the primary driver (high-roller Chinese visitor sensitivity to US-China tensions)
- **BUT:** Convention attendance up **7%** in January — business travel segment was resilient
- Hotel RevPAR jumped **23%** in February and ADR +8% — suggesting a rebound
- Spirit Airlines slashed 23 routes to Las Vegas (Harry Reid International) — this reduces feeder capacity for lower-income leisure visitors

**Assessment for 2026:** Las Vegas convention channel was holding in early 2026. The risk is not cancellation of existing bookings but *failure to book Q2–Q3 conferences* as corporate uncertainty grows.

### GBTA Poll — Corporate Travel Managers' Outlook (March 2026)
**Source: GBTA Poll, published March 2026**

This is the most directly actionable data point for the convention/business travel channel:

- **29% of global travel buyers** expect their company's business travel volume to *decline* in 2025, with an average projected decline of **21%**
- **27% of buyers** predict a **20% decrease** in business travel spending (potential $88B decline off $1.63T baseline)
- **37% of travel suppliers** expect business travel-related revenue to decline (average -18%)
- Overall industry optimism collapsed: only **31%** of professionals remain optimistic for 2025 — down from **67%** in November 2024 poll

Drivers cited: US tariffs, entry restrictions, detainment risk from border policies, reduced federal employee travel (DOGE effect), and anti-US sentiment reducing inbound international business travel.

**Quote from GBTA CEO Suzanne Neufang:** *"Productive and essential business travel is threatened in times of economic uncertainty or in an environment of additional barriers and restrictions. This undermines economic prosperity and damages the many sectors that rely on global business travel."*

**CWT/GBTA Global Business Travel Forecast 2026 (Sept 2025):**
- Baseline: stabilization, not growth
- Downside scenario: recession or stagflation "could significantly reshape the pricing environment"
- Forecast growth cut from +8.1% to lower range as uncertainty materialized

### Federal Government Convention Travel (DOGE Effect)
- Federal employee business travel has been directly curtailed — agencies required to justify all travel
- Government agencies represent a non-trivial share of convention attendance (DOD, HHS, USDA all run large annual conferences)
- Agency conferences being cancelled or downsized — anecdotal but widespread in Washington-area hospitality press
- Washington Marriott Marquis, Washington Convention Center, and comparable venues seeing softness in government-related bookings per industry reporting

## (D) THE GAS PRICE THRESHOLD — $4/GAL AS BREAKPOINT?

### AAA Definitive Survey Data
**Source: AAA — "$4 Gas the Tipping Point for Most Americans" (conducted March 2022 during $4+ national average)**

This is the closest thing to an authoritative industry answer:

- **59% of Americans** said they would make changes to their driving habits or lifestyle if gas reached **$4/gallon**
- **80% of changers** said they would opt to drive less
- For vacation specifically: **42% said they would NOT change travel plans regardless of gas price** — meaning **58% ARE price-sensitive**
- At **$5/gallon**: **75% of Americans** said they would need to adjust their lifestyle

**Current gas price context (March 2026):**
- National average as of the AAA search: **$3.884/gallon** — approaching but not yet at the $4 threshold
- December 2025 average was only $2.89 — prices have risen significantly YTD

**The $4 threshold research:**
- The AAA $4 tipping point is well-established in industry practice — not a single academic paper but a recurring survey finding across multiple years
- Refinement: the threshold is REAL WAGES-adjusted, not nominal. In 2022, $4 gas hit middle-income families harder than in 2008 because wage stagnation had persisted. In 2026, with inflation eroding real wages, a $3.90–$4.00 average may function like a higher nominal threshold from earlier cycles.
- Drive-market parks (within 200-300 miles) are more affected than fly-in destinations — this is why Six Flags and regional parks showed weakness first in 2022

**The current risk:** At $3.88 and rising seasonally into summer, gas is approaching the threshold. If crude oil moves on Middle East tensions or supply cuts, $4+ is not far. For families already strained by inflation, the psychological $4 level matters even if they ultimately don't cancel.

## (E) SEASONAL TIMING — WHEN DOES DAMAGE SHOW UP IN CLAIMS?

### The Critical Booking Window
**The Iron Rule of Theme Park Labor:** Parks staff for expected attendance 4–8 weeks out based on reservation data.

- **Spring Break (NOW — mid-March to mid-April):** This is the real-time test. Spring break attendance is already occurring. If parks are seeing soft walk-up traffic and lower-than-expected reservation fulfillment, that signals something.
- **Summer booking lock-in:** Families with school-age children typically book summer vacations **by April**. April is the decision point. A family that hasn't booked by April 15 is likely not going to Disney in June.
- **Hotel reservation leading indicator:** Disney's own bookings data (forward reservations at Disney hotels) is the best proxy — Disney said WDW bookings were up 5% for FY2026 but "weighted toward the back half." This means Q2 (April–June) is the weak period.

### Employment Impact Timeline
Based on 2008 and 2022 precedents:

| Event | Lag to L&H Employment Impact |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| Attendance drop signal (reservation data) | T+0 (internal knowledge only) |
| Reduced seasonal hiring / non-recalls | T+2 to T+4 weeks |
| Increased part-time/hour reductions | T+3 to T+5 weeks |
| Layoffs / formal separations | T+4 to T+8 weeks |
| UI claims filed | T+5 to T+9 weeks |
| UI claims appear in weekly data | T+6 to T+10 weeks |

**If** spring break demand is soft (observable now, third week of March 2026):
- Reduced seasonal hiring decisions: April
- UI claims beginning to appear: **late April to early May**
- Peak claims impact in data: **May and June** (if damage is real)

**For summer:**
- Booking decisions: April (NOW)
- Non-recall of seasonal workers: May
- UI claims signal: **June to July**

### 2026-Specific Complication: Epic Universe (Universal)
Universal's Epic Universe opened in 2025, creating a new theme park supply dynamic in Orlando. This may shift some attendance from Disney to Universal but also brings net new visitors. For employment purposes, Universal is adding jobs — so L&H employment totals may be resilient even as Disney-specific attendance softens. The net effect on claims is unclear but the composition shifts.

## (F) CONNECTION TO FL WAVE 1 UI EXHAUSTION (MAR 24, 2026)

### The Amplification Mechanism

Florida's Wave 1 UI exhaustion (estimated ~March 24, 2026) represents the class of workers who filed initial claims in late 2025 / very early 2026 (during FL's 12-week benefit window). These exhaustees are:

1. Looking for work OR have returned to work in roles potentially in L&H
2. If they returned to L&H jobs in early 2026 and those jobs are now softening, they may be on the verge of second-round claims
3. If they haven't found work, they are completely falling off the benefits cliff — not counted in continuing claims, creating a **false improvement in claims data**

### Combined FL L&H + Exhaustion Scenario

**If tourism demand is softening simultaneously with Wave 1 exhaustion:**

- **Exhaustion suppresses continuing claims** (these claimants disappear from UI rolls) → headline continuing claims look better than reality
- **New L&H softness generates fresh initial claims** → initial claims spike from tourism-adjacent workers
- These two forces can **cross and mislead** — continued claims dropping while initial claims rising = data appears mixed but the underlying condition is deteriorating

**The key dates:**
- **March 24:** Wave 1 exhaustion hits
- **Late April:** Summer booking window closes; parks make seasonal staffing decisions
- **May 1–15:** L&H initial claims from non-recalls and reduced hours would begin appearing
- **May–June:** The combined FL signal would be: low continuing claims (exhausted population gone) + rising initial claims (new L&H layoffs) = **net negative for FL labor market, possibly obscured by headline data**

### Florida's Specific Vulnerability

- Orange County (Orlando) = 42% L&H employment concentration
- Walt Disney World = ~77,000 direct employees, largest single-site employer in FL
- Universal Orlando = additional ~20,000+
- SeaWorld Orlando = ~5,000 (seasonal)
- Hotel, restaurant, retail workers supporting these parks: multiplied by ~2.5x on top of direct employment
- FL L&H sector employed ~1.35M workers as of recent data (FRED: FLLEIH series)
- A 3–5% contraction = 40,000–67,000 job losses concentrated in Central Florida

**The exhaustion-tourism confluence creates an amplified signal:**
- Wave 1 exhaustees who returned to L&H may RE-FILE if tourism-adjacent jobs thin
- This creates a "second wave" of claims from the same demographic cohort
- FL's 12-week window is short by national standards — workers exhaust quickly and reenter the labor market, making FL claims especially volatile as leading indicators

### Verdict

Theme park attendance and convention travel are **valid leading indicators** for L&H UI claims with a **4–8 week lag**. The 2022 case study provides the cleanest precedent — analyst downgrades and park operator warnings in July 2022 preceded actual L&H claims movement by 5–8 weeks.

**2026 has multiple simultaneous pressure points:**
1. Disney explicitly flagging Q2 headwinds from international softness
2. Corporate travel demand under structural pressure (tariffs, DOGE, anti-US sentiment)
3. Gas prices approaching psychological $4 threshold
4. Spring break decision window closing NOW; summer booking window closing in April
5. FL Wave 1 UI exhaustion (Mar 24) removing continuing claimants from headline data just as new L&H softness may begin generating fresh initial claims

**Bottom line for LABOR thesis:** If spring break and April booking data show continued softness, L&H UI claims in FL (particularly Orange County) would be expected to begin rising in **May 2026 initial claims data** — 4–6 weeks after the booking window closes and parks make seasonal staffing calls. The FL exhaustion cliff on March 24 will temporarily suppress continuing claims, potentially masking the true deterioration. Watch initial claims in FL, not continuing claims, as the clean signal.

## DATA GAPS / UNCERTAINTIES

- **Disney does not publicly release granular reservation data** — forward booking "up 5% FY2026" weighted to back half is self-reported management guidance, not auditable
- **Gas prices:** Current $3.88 is close to but not at the $4 threshold; additional tariff pass-through or crude oil moves could push it over
- **Epic Universe effect:** Universal's new park may absorb displaced Disney visitors, keeping total Orlando L&H employment stable even if Disney-specific attendance softens
- **International tourism composition:** Disney flagging international softness means some job losses may concentrate in hospitality (hotels, restaurants catering to international guests) rather than direct park employment
- **No 2026 large-scale convention cancellation data found** — the risk is more about organic weakness in corporate meeting booking than explicit cancellations
