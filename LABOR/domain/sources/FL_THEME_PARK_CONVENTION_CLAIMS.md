## 2. THE 2008 ANALOG — CLAIMS TIMELINE AND MAGNITUDE

### Oil Price / Macro Shock → FL Claims Sequence (2008)
| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Jan 2008 | Oil ~$90/bbl; Orange County U/R ~3.8% |
| Apr 2008 | Oil ~$110+; national L&H employment starts softening |
| Jul 2008 | Oil peaks $147/bbl; theme parks still showing surface resilience |
| Aug–Sep 2008 | "Destination parks took the Q4 hit" — TEA/ERA report notes regional parks weakened first |
| Oct 2008 | Lehman + oil shock combined → FL hospitality claims spike begins |
| Jan–Jun 2009 | Orange County U/R rockets from ~6% to peak ~10.8% |
| 2009 full year | Orlando tourist arrivals -4.7% YoY; SeaWorld -2.9%, Universal Islands -2.4% attendance |

**Key finding:** Oil peaked July 2008 → FL hospitality claims spiked September–October 2008 → **lag was ~2-3 months**. By mid-2009, Orange County unemployment had nearly **tripled** from the 2007 trough (3.5% → 10.8%).

**National context (BLS MLR, March 2009):** National L&H employment "peaked in December 2007 and fell by a monthly average of 12,000 jobs during the first 8 months of 2008; job losses accelerated to an **average of 38,000** in [the last 4 months of 2008]." FL, with its Orlando concentration, was disproportionately exposed.

**Why 2026 may differ (worse):**
- 2008 shock was financial (Lehman) + oil. The hospitality damage was partly cushioned by initial fiscal support.
- 2026 oil shock is supply-driven (Scenario D / Yanbu / Qatar LNG) with no immediate fiscal offset
- FL Wave 1 UI exhaustion (Mar 24) removes the safety net buffer that existed in 2008-09
- FL's 12-week max UI (shortest in US) means the 2026 cohort exhausts by June 24 from a March filing — right as the hospitality wave would hit

## 4. TRANSMISSION MECHANISM — GAS → CLAIMS SPEED

### Full Causal Chain with Timing
```
Gas $4+ (breaking NOW ~Mar 22-23)
    ↓ [immediate]
Consumer travel hesitation — drive-market families revise plans
    ↓ [2-4 weeks]
Spring break attendance softness visible in park turnstiles
    ↓ [0-2 weeks after signal]
Parks make reduced seasonal hiring decisions (non-recalls)
    ↓ [2-4 weeks]
Seasonal workers not rehired file UI claims
    ↓ [1 week]
UI claims appear in weekly FL data
    ↓ [1 week to publish]
Claims data released publicly

TOTAL LAG FROM GAS BREAKPOINT: ~6-10 weeks
```

### Summer Channel (Larger Magnitude)
```
April booking window closes (no-book by Apr 15 = no Disney in June)
    ↓
Parks announce reduced summer staffing (May 1-15)
    ↓
Layoffs / non-recalls processed (May)
    ↓
UI claims spike in FL data: JUNE 2026
```

**If oil shock started Feb 28 and the 3-6 month lag holds:**  
- Spring break channel → May claims spike  
- Summer booking channel → June-July claims spike  
- **Peak FL hospitality claims window: May–July 2026** ✓ (consistent with thesis)

### Why Theme Parks Are Special (vs Generic L&H)
1. **Binary employment model:** Parks either recall seasonal workers or they don't — no gradual reduction. A non-recall is a mass layoff event without a formal announcement.
2. **Geographic concentration:** Orange County absorbs the entire shock in a single claims district.
3. **Low-income, no-savings workforce:** FL theme park workers average $14-18/hr. Most have <1 month savings. File UI almost immediately on separation.
4. **FL 12-week exhaustion compresses the timeline:** A park worker laid off in June exhausts by mid-September — entering poverty/migration before the trade/hotel sector even stabilizes.

## 5. EXHAUSTION AMPLIFICATION — THE HIDDEN MULTIPLIER

**The Mar 24 Wave 1 exhaustion cliff interacts with the tourism shock:**

1. Wave 1 exhaustees who found L&H jobs in Jan–Feb (the cyclical "good news" in early 2026 claims data) are now working in exactly the sector about to soften
2. If they lose those L&H jobs in May–June, they **cannot refile** immediately under FL rules (must have earned sufficient wages after exhaustion) — or face an extended waiting period
3. **The result:** These workers fall completely off all UI measures — not counted in initial claims, not in continuing claims — creating a **silent poverty pool** that appears as "labor force exit" in headline data
4. Wave 1 exhaustion **depresses continuing claims** (exhaustees drop off rolls Mar 24) while fresh L&H layoffs **spike initial claims** — the two signals point in opposite directions for ~4-6 weeks, creating interpretive confusion

**Data implication:** Watch FL **initial claims** (not continuing claims) starting May 2026 as the clean signal. Continuing claims will be structurally suppressed.
