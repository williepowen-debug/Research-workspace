# Michigan October final: sentiment 48.0, revised up from the 46.3 preliminary and flat on September; year-ahead inflation expectations 4.7% and long-run 3.5%, both up a second month to the highest since May; durable buying conditions plunged 18% on rates; Michigan publishes a special report on the risk of entrenched inflation expectations

**Final results (University of Michigan, 10:00 ET 10/23), read at primary:**

| Index | Oct final | Oct prelim | Sep 2026 | Oct 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer sentiment | 48.0 | 46.3 | 48.1 | 53.6 |
| Current conditions | 46.7 | 44.7 | 50.9 | 58.6 |
| Expectations | 48.8 | 47.3 | 46.3 | 50.3 |
| Year-ahead inflation expectations | 4.7% | 4.7% | 4.6% | |
| Long-run inflation expectations | 3.5% | 3.5% | 3.4% | |

Director Joanne Hsu: the current-conditions decline "was driven entirely by buying conditions for durables, which plunged 18% on the basis of higher interest rates and rising concerns about the affordability of such purchases." Sentiment is 10% below a year ago and 15% below February, before the Iran conflict. Year-ahead expectations compare with 3.4% in February; long-run 3.5% is above the 2024 range of 2.8%-3.2%; both rose for a second straight month to their highest since May.

**Special report, same day (opening section read):** Michigan says that after five years of above-target inflation, expectations "have ticked up in recent months, exposing risks that elevated inflation expectations could become entrenched", likening the pattern to the 1960s-70s, when consumers pulled purchases forward to beat higher prices and "robust precautionary spending" made expectations self-fulfilling. It calls entrenchment "not inevitable" but says the risks "are present".

**Read, not a grade:** a headline that looks steady hides two things the Fed watches on 10/28: rising long-run expectations, and the same "precautionary" buying S&P Global named in this morning's PMI (SIG-W-20261023-010). Affordability is crushing durable-goods intentions at the same time.
