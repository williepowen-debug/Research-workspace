## 2. Why the ARITHMETIC is fine and the INFERENCE is not

**The level is checkable and I have no reason to dispute it.** The problem is what is being concluded from it — **three failures, and the chart supplies the evidence for the third itself:**

**(a) 🔑 THE DENOMINATOR IS NOT THE SAME OBJECT.** M2 in 2000 and M2 in 2026 sit on opposite sides of a structural break: post-2008 IOR, two QE regimes, the 2020 M2 explosion (~+25% in a year), the 2022-25 first sustained M2 *contraction* since the 1930s, and — per the very item routed alongside this one — **a Fed balance sheet that is expanding again via reserve-management purchases.** **A ratio is only a valuation gauge if the denominator measures the same thing across the comparison. Money-supply definitions and velocity do not survive that test over 26 years.** *(Standing class: `finding_ratio_gauge_denominator_branch`.)*

**(b) THE CHART'S OWN HISTORY REFUTES THE IMPLIED SIGNAL.** Read the series as drawn: the ratio spent **2009 through ~2021 rising almost monotonically off the 0.07 trough**, and it crossed most of its own historical range without marking anything. **A gauge whose only two touches of a level are 26 years apart has n=1 as a sell signal.** *"Same level as the peak"* is a coincidence of scaling until someone shows the level has predictive content — and one prior instance cannot.

**(c) IT IS A LEVEL CLAIM MASQUERADING AS A TIMING CLAIM.** Even granting the level, **nothing here says WHEN.** The 2000 analogue took the ratio *through* this level on the way up before it broke. **A threshold with no sustain rule, no confirmation condition, and no falsifier is not tradeable** — and *"marked the peak"* is retrospective labelling of a single observation.
