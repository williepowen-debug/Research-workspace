## Body (as reported)

**Analyst team:** Daan Struyven / Yulia Zhestkova Grigsby / Alexandra Paulus / Filippo Cuscito. Timestamp: 2026-07-20 21:30 EDT.

**Core framing:**
1. **Lower Gulf flows → higher prices.** Persian Gulf flows below **45% of pre-war**; Brent futures curve modestly above GS's $80/75 forecasts for Q4'26/2027 (their base case still assumes de-escalation in Q4'26).
2. **Upside price risks tilted.** Two named upsides:
   - **Shipping disruptions in Hormuz — and potentially the Red Sea.** Yanbu pipeline flows up **5mb/d since war start → >6mb/d**, offsetting part of the Hormuz decline.
   - **Damage to energy infra from Middle East + Russia-Ukraine wars.** The 42% average production hit after 4 years is the base rate; often reflects infrastructure damage, underinvestment, or tight sanctions.
3. **Lower stocks vs greater flexibility.** Upside scenario: Brent **>$120 in Q4'26**, avg **$100 in 2027** if Hormuz remains disrupted through 2027 (Gulf output fully recovers only by Dec-27, supported by pipeline extensions). Q2 inventory draw >3mb/d. But global visible stocks are down only **0.3mb/d YoY** and simulated price upside is less high than early-war estimates → GS now assumes greater demand elasticity + more adaptable Mideast supply + higher market tolerance for low inventories before aggressive demand destruction.
4. **China stabilized crude markets.** Net-seaborne imports **−4.7mb/d YoY in June** on weaker refinery runs + products demand (retail gasoline sales volumes −21% YoY); shift from 2025 stockpiling to destocking (~1mb/d in June).
