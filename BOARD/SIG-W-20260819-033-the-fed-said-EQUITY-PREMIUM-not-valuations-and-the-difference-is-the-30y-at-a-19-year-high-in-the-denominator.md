**The tweet:** *"Federal Reserve reveals U.S. stock valuations are 'near dot-com bubble levels.'"*

## 2. 🔴 THE SUBSTITUTION, AND WHY IT IS NOT PEDANTRY

**The equity premium is a SPREAD** — the excess expected return of equities over the risk-free rate. **Valuation is a LEVEL** — CAPE, P/E, price-to-sales.

**A spread has two legs, and only one of them is the stock market.**

**🔑 The 30-year Treasury printed 5.31% on 2026-08-17 — a 19-YEAR HIGH, the second observation ≥5.31 in 4,910 sessions since 2007** (`SIG-W-20260819-009`, verified at FRED). **⇒ A compressed equity premium in this environment can be produced by the RISK-FREE LEG RISING rather than by equity prices rising.** Those are opposite stories: one says stocks are euphoric, the other says bonds got cheap and stocks did not reprice against them.

**The Fed's sentence does not tell you which**, and **the tweet's rewrite silently picks the first.** `[[finding_spread_metric_blind_to_common_mode]]` — **report the component LEVELS beside any spread**, always.

⚠️ **Second, smaller substitution: "near … levels" vs what the Fed wrote.** The Fed said the premium *"has only been **lower** … during the dot-com bubble"* — a **ranking**: dot-com is the *sole* period that was lower, so today is **above** it on this measure. *"Near dot-com bubble levels"* blurs at/above/below into a vibe. **The Fed's sentence is more precise than its paraphrase, and more precise in a direction the paraphrase loses.**
