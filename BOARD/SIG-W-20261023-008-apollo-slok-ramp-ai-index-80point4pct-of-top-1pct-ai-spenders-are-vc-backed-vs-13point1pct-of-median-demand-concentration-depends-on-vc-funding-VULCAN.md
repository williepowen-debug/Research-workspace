# Apollo's Slok, on Ramp AI Index data: 80.4% of the top 1% of AI spenders are VC-backed vs 13.1% of median spenders, so the heaviest AI demand depends on venture funding

**The chart (Apollo, titled "Circularity in AI shows up in token spending too"; panel "Financial backing of businesses by AI spend intensity"; sources Ramp AI Index and Apollo Chief Economist):**

| Tier | VC-backed | Non-VC |
|---|---|---|
| Top 1% of AI spenders | 80.4% | 19.6% |
| Median spender | 13.1% | 86.9% |

**What it is not:** it counts businesses by funding type inside each spend tier. It does not show what share of total AI or token spend the top 1% accounts for; Joe Weisenthal's "a very small % of companies are consuming the lion's share" is his framing beside the chart. The chart shows no date range and no definition of "spend intensity". Ramp is a corporate card and spend platform whose customers lean toward startups, which likely raises the VC-backed share in every tier. I did not read Slok's original note or Ramp's methodology.

**Why it matters:** if the marginal heavy AI buyer is a venture-funded company, AI usage demand inherits the venture cycle. A slowdown in VC funding would hit the top tier of demand first, which is the "who pays" question behind the 10/22 chip selloff (SIG-W-20261023-002). This is demand-side concentration and complements the financing-side circularity the cluster already carries.
