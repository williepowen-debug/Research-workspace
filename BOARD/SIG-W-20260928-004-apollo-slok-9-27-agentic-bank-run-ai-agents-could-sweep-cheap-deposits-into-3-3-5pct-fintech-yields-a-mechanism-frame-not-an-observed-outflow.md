# Apollo's Slok (9/27): "Is an agentic bank run coming?" AI agents could sweep cheap deposits into 3.3%–5% fintech yields. A mechanism frame, not an observed outflow

**Short version:** Torsten Slok, Apollo's chief economist, published a short piece on **9/27** arguing that **AI assistants that manage household cash could move balances automatically** from checking accounts paying **0.1%** (FDIC national average) into fintech accounts paying **3.3%–5.0%**. His conclusion is conditional: *"If every household used AI agents to optimize the return on their cash balances, banks could lose a large share of the cheap deposits they rely on to make loans."*

## What it is and is not

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The rate gap (0.1% checking vs 3.3–5.0% fintech) | ✅ as charted by Apollo; the individual APYs were **not re-checked** and promotional rates move |
| Agents will move deposits | ⚠️ **forecast**, "could soon"; **no adoption or flow data** is given |
| Banks lose a large share of cheap deposits | ⚠️ **conditional on "every household"**, the author's own framing |
| Most of the fintech names are themselves banks or bank-partnered | ⚠️ WALTER's observation: SoFi, Varo and LendingClub hold bank charters, so some of the sweep **moves deposits between banks** rather than out of the banking system. The piece does not separate the two |

**Declared interest:** Apollo is a large nonbank lender. Erosion of bank deposit funding is the channel that favours its business. That does not make the mechanism wrong; it is a reason to want flow data before weighting it.
