## Signal

David Beckworth (Mercatus Center, Macro Musings podcast) Apr 8 2026 Substack: **the Fed's "Overton window" is shifting from supply-driven ample reserves toward a demand-driven operating system** like BoE / RBA / ECB / Riksbank / RBNZ / BoC. Lays out six load-bearing observations:

1. **Logan (Dallas Fed) Aug 25 2025** — supports ample reserves but argues the Fed should minimize its financial footprint via "ceiling tools and temporary operations" rather than outright holdings.
2. **Bowman (Vice Chair Supervision) late Sept 2025** — preference for reserves "closer to scarce than ample"; argues lower reserves improve market functioning + reduce Fed footprint.
3. **March 3 2026 cluster (per Beckworth: Bowman + Bessent + Hill simultaneously)** — argues the post-2008 liquidity framework encourages liquidity hoarding and weakens the Discount Window; proposes that DW capacity should count toward bank liquidity requirements (LCR). **Verify-research correction:** Bowman + Bessent gave separate speeches March 3; Hill spoke at ABA Summit March 9-11 (different venue/date); the DW-counts-LCR proposal came from Bessent (via Under Secretary McKernan), not Bowman. Two-of-three on date is correct, framing of "joint forum" is wrong.
4. **Miran (Fed Governor) March 26 2026** — coauthored paper "A User's Guide to Reducing the Federal Reserve's Balance Sheet" (FEDS 2026-019) lays out a menu of policy options that could shrink the balance sheet by **$1.2-2.1T** (Beckworth shorthand "$1-2T" undershoots upper bound).
5. **Logan + Schulhofer-Wohl April 2 2026** — paper distinguishes "returning to scarce reserves" from "shifting the demand curve inward"; preferred path is lean ample-reserves with banks needing fewer reserves, system defined by spreads not pre-positioned quantities.
6. **Warsh — incoming Fed chair** — Trump nominated Jan 30 2026; confirmation hearing Apr 21 2026; **vote pending** as of Apr 24-26 (Tillis hold removed after DOJ dropped Powell investigation). Publicly supportive of balance sheet reduction + "regime change" in operating framework. Chair-designate, not seated.

Beckworth argues four reasons demand-driven is warranted: (1) revival of interbank lending, (2) reduced liquidity fragility (Rajan/Acharya — QE-induced runnable liabilities don't unwind on QT, creating ratchet effect), (3) stronger bank lending to real economy (Diamond/Jiang/Ma + Hogan — abundant reserves crowd out lending by raising marginal balance-sheet cost; QE reserves financed by runnable deposits → less maturity transformation), (4) greater Fed independence (avoids political balance-sheet capture for student-debt or fiscal goals).
