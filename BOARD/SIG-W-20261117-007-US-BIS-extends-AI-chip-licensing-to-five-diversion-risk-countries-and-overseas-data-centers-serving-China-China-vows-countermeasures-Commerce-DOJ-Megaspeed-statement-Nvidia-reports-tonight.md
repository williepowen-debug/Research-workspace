# New US AI-chip curbs on remote access and five transit countries; China vows countermeasures; Commerce-DOJ Megaspeed statement (Nvidia reports tonight)

**1. The rule (Al Jazeera and Reuters relay via search, Nov 17; rule text not read).** Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security expanded licence requirements to five countries it calls an "unacceptable risk of diversion": Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, describing it as a "logical extension" of the existing licence requirement for chips to China-based firms. The rules also target overseas data centers that provide advanced computing capacity to Chinese companies (Al Jazeera headline: "US moves to block China's remote access to AI chips amid Nvidia scandal"). Not read by WALTER: the Federal Register or BIS text, effective date, chip thresholds, licence policy (presumption of denial or case by case).

China's response.** China's commerce ministry "firmly opposed" the rule and said it would take countermeasures if the US insists on it; no measure named (Yahoo/Reuters relay; Will's bookmark @PolymarketMoney 14:59Z). Speculation that it could target critical minerals or chip raw materials is analysis only. Trump is due in Shenzhen Wed 11/18 and meets Xi Thu 11/19 (SIG-W-20261114-003).

**4. Commerce and DOJ joint statement on Megaspeed, dated November 16, 2026** (image posted by @CommerceGov 03:31Z Nov 17, read whole by WALTER): Commerce opened an investigation into Singapore's Megaspeed in 2024 for potential export-control violations; in May 2025 Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia on direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed; in June 2025 Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list; BIS agents with Singaporean law enforcement did an on-site end-use check in June 2025 and interviewed CEO Alice Huang; DOJ then took the lead in the criminal investigation. It also says 17 defendants in six investigations have been charged in chip-smuggling cases under this administration.
