## Full research packet (verbatim, as delivered by Will 2026-06-19)

# South Florida "Financial-Distress Tidal Wave": Verify-First Assessment — Is Bank-Loss Transmission Close?

*Merged from two independent research passes. Where the two passes differed on a specific number, the better-sourced / more-recent figure is used as headline; the three substantive discrepancies were subsequently verified against primary filings and are documented in the "Verification notes" at the end (all resolved June 19, 2026).*

## TL;DR

- **The "tidal wave" framing is overstated, but the consumer side is genuinely deteriorating.** Florida bankruptcy filings are rising and accelerating — the Middle District of Florida is confirmed **#2 nationally** and the Southern District **#6** for the 12 months ending March 31, 2026, up year-over-year across Chapters 7, 11, and 13 — yet on a per-capita basis Florida (~190/100k statewide; Southern District ~222, Middle ~199) sits only modestly above the national rate (~168–173) and far below the "bankruptcy belt" (Alabama ~520/100k). The top-six ranking is largely a population-and-migration artifact; the YoY growth rate is the genuinely notable signal, and it is principally a **consumer** story (business cases are only ~5–6% of district filings).

- **Insurance reconciles as "easing wholesale, still-high but decelerating retail."** June 1, 2026 Florida property-cat reinsurance fell 15–25%, the FHCF rate fell ~9.51% statewide, Citizens cut personal-lines rates and is down to ~293,404 policies (June 12, 2026; from a ~1.41M peak), yet the typical Florida homeowner still pays nearly 3x the national premium. Crucially, the **commercial/condo segment is not easing as cleanly**: Citizens commercial lines were filed at **+10.4%** after capping, and all-in commercial budgets stay painful as savings on property-cat get redirected into liability/umbrella. Both the "easing" and the "still expensive" claims are true at different layers.

- **Bank transmission is NOT yet visible in the prints.** Q1 2026 disclosures for SouthState, Seacoast, BankUnited, Valley and Amerant show benign-to-improving net charge-offs (9–11 bps), contained NPAs, **stable-to-growing deposits** (no funding run), and only selective, collateral-backed reserve pressure. The weight of evidence points to **slow demand-normalization, not acute crash** — probabilistically ~70% normalization / ~30% acute crash over Q2–Q3 2026, with the Iran energy shock the dominant tail risk.

## Key Findings

**1. Bankruptcy data is real, accelerating, and consumer-led — but the per-capita story deflates the "tidal wave."** For the 12 months ending March 31, 2026, national filings rose 11.9% to 591,850. The Middle District of Florida is #2 nationally (27,925, up from 21,927) and the Southern District is #6 (16,326, up from 14,387), with both districts rising across all three consumer chapters. Florida's statewide nonbusiness filings rose 22.2% to 44,496. But Florida's per-capita rate (~190/100k statewide) is only modestly above the national ~168–173 and roughly comparable to several mid-sized states. **Verdict: the attorney's "top-six" claim is literally true but the framing is a population artifact; the YoY acceleration is the genuine signal — and it is a household-insolvency story, not a corporate-court avalanche.**

**2. Insurance: both claims are true at different layers, and the commercial layer is the one to watch.** Wholesale/reinsurance is clearly easing (June 1, 2026 renewals down 15–25%; FHCF −9.51%). Retail homeowner premiums have turned flat-to-down for the first time in years (Citizens multiperil −8.8%, blended personal lines −2.6% after capping) but remain ~3x national. **Commercial residential/condo master pricing is the exception: Citizens commercial lines were filed at +10.4% after capping — on a +18.8% uncapped actuarial indication** — and all-in commercial insurance budgets remain elevated even as property-cat eases. The capped figure understates the true indicated need, making this the most plausible insurance channel into condo/CRE stress.

**3. Bank transmission: not yet visible, and funding is stable.** Across all five named banks, Q1 2026 credit metrics are benign: net charge-offs 9–11 bps, NPAs well under 1% of loans at the cleanest names, reserves flat-to-down. Where non-performers ticked up (Seacoast's two commercial credits; Amerant's NPA line), management cites collateral coverage and "no credit loss expected." Equally important, deposits were **stable or growing at every bank** — there is no liquidity-run signature. No bank flagged condo-master-loan or HOA distress directly.

**4. Business bankruptcy: elevated nationally but not a Florida-specific CRE feedback loop.** National business filings rose 11.4%, but commercial Chapter 11 filings actually *fell* ~7% YoY in May 2026, and Florida business cases are only ~5–6% of district volume. Spirit Airlines (Florida-HQ) liquidated May 2, 2026 — but the proximate cause was the Iran-driven jet-fuel spike, not Florida demand, and it filed in SDNY. Florida office and retail vacancy are *tighter* than the national average, so the CRE-vacancy feedback loop is largely absent at the metro-aggregate level — though that does not preclude pain in older condo/habitational stock.

**5. Macro overlay: the Iran energy → core-CPI spillover is forecast/narrative, not yet supported by data.** May 2026 CPI ran +4.2% YoY (highest since April 2023), but energy alone drove >60% of the monthly gain; core was only +0.2% MoM / +2.9% YoY and core goods actually fell 0.1%. Multiple economists explicitly noted energy is "not bleeding into core." Sean Snaith / UCF's thesis is framed conditionally; his own Florida housing read is "prices flattened," no 2009-style crash. Treat the spillover as a live forward risk, not a realized fact.

**6. Single most diagnostic metric:** a **synchronized** jump in already-elevated criticized/classified CRE and relationship credits — across **more than one** Florida-concentrated bank at the same time — that **converts into realized net charge-offs and specific reserve builds**. Today those balances are migrating but staying current and collateral-backed; the crash tell is when collateral values fail and losses actually print across institutions simultaneously.

## Details

### 1. Bankruptcy data (per-capita normalized)

**National baseline (AOUSC, primary source).** For the 12-month period ending March 31, 2026, the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts reported total U.S. bankruptcy filings rose 11.9% to 591,850 (from 529,080). Business filings rose 11.4% to 25,960; non-business 11.9% to 565,890. By chapter: Chapter 7 = 369,702 (up ~15.3%); Chapter 11 = 9,941 (up ~12.4%); Chapter 13 = 211,700 (up ~6.3%). Chapter 7 (liquidation) is rising fastest — consistent with consumer/small-business stress rather than large-corporate reorganization.

**District ranking (AOUSC F-2, 12-mo ending March 31, 2026).** Central District of California is #1. **Middle District of Florida is #2 nationally at 27,925** filings (up from 21,927 in the period ending March 31, 2025), and **Southern District of Florida is #6 at 16,326** (up from 14,387). Florida uniquely has two districts in the national top six. The chapter detail confirms broad-based acceleration:

- **Middle District:** total 21,927 → 27,925; Chapter 7 16,611 → 21,563; Chapter 11 452 → 524; Chapter 13 4,847 → 5,821.
- **Southern District:** total 14,387 → 16,326; Chapter 7 7,805 → 9,167; Chapter 11 309 → 375; Chapter 13 6,257 → 6,769.

*(An earlier pass, unable to fetch the March 2026 district-level F-2 directly, used the December 2025 12-month figures as a proxy — Middle ≈26,279, Southern ≈15,780. Those sit cleanly between the March 2025 and March 2026 windows, corroborating the trajectory; the March 2026 figures above are the headline.)*

**Per-capita normalization — where the "tidal wave" loses force.** Florida recorded the second-most total filings nationally (44,496, up 22.2% from 36,407), behind California. But on filings per 100,000 residents, Florida is ~190 statewide — only modestly above the national ~168–173, and below Maryland (~203). At the district level, the Southern District runs ~222/100k and the Middle District ~199/100k, both above the national rate but well short of the genuine per-capita leaders: Alabama, Mississippi, Tennessee and Georgia (the "bankruptcy belt," each well above 450/100k, Alabama exceeding 500). Florida's YoY *change* (+22.2%) ranks third nationally for growth, behind only low-base North Dakota and Alaska.

**Incentive flag (high importance).** The originating claim comes from a bankruptcy attorney whose business volume scales directly with filings. The verifiable AOUSC data supports the *direction* (filings genuinely up and accelerating, FL districts genuinely high-volume) but not the alarmist *magnitude*: on a population-adjusted basis, Florida is elevated-but-normal, not a national outlier. The best verified version of the claim: **yes, South and Middle Florida are very high-volume and accelerating; no, that alone does not prove an acute, Florida-specific cascade.**

### 2. Insurance reconciliation

**Wholesale/reinsurance — clearly easing (high confidence, corroborated across brokers and the official regulator).** At the June 1, 2026 Florida-heavy renewal, Guy Carpenter reported risk-adjusted property-cat decreases of −15% to −20% across many layers; Gallagher Re reported "very consistent softening in the 20–25% range… an average 22.8% reduction" across its portfolio; Aon and Howden Re reported decreases reaching 25%. Florida's regulator independently reported the 2025 ARDC showed average risk-adjusted reinsurance costs down ~0.46% YoY (versus the huge 2022→2023 increase), and FHCF rates approved in June 2025 fell **9.51%** statewide. Drivers: a ~77% domestic combined ratio in 2025 (vs. 92% in 2024), policyholders' surplus up ~45%, a benign hurricane season, record reinsurance capital, and growing cat-bond issuance (~$4.3B Florida-focused in 2026). *Incentive flag: brokers earn on placement and benefit from a "thriving market" narrative — but the softening is corroborated across four brokers, the cat-bond market, AND the official OIR/FHCF direction, so confidence is high.*

**Retail homeowner premiums — flat-to-down for the first time in years, but still extremely high.** For policies effective June 1, 2026–May 31, 2027, Citizens *filed* a **statewide personal-lines change of −2.6% after capping** (the first personal-lines cut since ~2015); OIR then went further, *tripling* the decrease to roughly **−8.7%/−8.8%** on approval as litigation costs kept falling. Within the filing, **463,096 personal-lines policies (59.2%) receive an average decrease of 11.5% (−$359)**, with South Florida deepest (Miami-Dade −13.0%, Broward −12.6%, Palm Beach −11.7%). State Farm, USAA, GEICO, Allstate and Progressive filed decreases of roughly 7–10%. However, levels remain punishing: Insurify's 2026 report projects Florida at **$8,458** by year-end 2026 versus a **$2,948** national average — nearly three times national, with Miami-Dade and Keys coastal premiums far higher. (Underlying driver: Citizens' indicated actuarially-sound HO3/HW2 premium fell ~43% — from $6,347 to $3,617 — post-SB 2-A, as litigation and reinsurance costs dropped.) So the *trend* has turned while the *level* remains ridiculously expensive. **Both claims reconcile.**

**Commercial property / condo & multifamily — the segment that is NOT easing.** This is the most thesis-relevant layer, and the verified filing settles it decisively. Citizens' 2026 rate filing shows **Commercial Lines +10.4% (proposed, after glide-path capping) on a +18.8% uncapped actuarial indication** — i.e., the underlying indicated rate *need* for the commercial book is +18.8%, suppressed to +10.4% only by the statutory cap. By peril, the proposed commercial changes are +9.6% multi-peril (uncapped +21.3%) and +10.9% wind-only (uncapped +17.2%). This is the **opposite of "flat to down"** and runs directly counter to the personal-lines cut. Commercial-market commentary (Marsh McLennan Agency) adds that property-only pricing has eased ~10–20% in places, but owners are redirecting those savings into more expensive liability and umbrella coverage, and multifamily underwriting is still tightening (aging systems, water loss, crime, lender requirements). Layered on top: HB 1021 (condos) and HB 1203 (HOAs) tightened milestone-inspection/SIRS compliance, and Citizens is now barred from renewing non-compliant associations — a structural squeeze on the worst condo risks. **Net: for condos, apartments and shopping centers, all-in insurance cost is still rising, and the capped +10.4% understates the true +18.8% indicated need — the most plausible idiosyncratic Florida channel into bank books, and arguably stronger evidence for the condo-stress thesis than first assumed.** *(Caveat: Citizens' executive summary reports "Commercial Lines" as a single line and does not separately isolate commercial-residential / condo-association master policies from commercial non-residential; the +10.4% / +18.8% is the blended commercial figure. The earlier "commercial-residential flat to down" characterization is not supported at this aggregate level.)*

**Supply relief is real.** Citizens' policy count has fallen from its late-2023 peak (~1.41M) to **336,000 in March 2026** and then to **293,404 by June 12, 2026** — clear evidence of improving residual-market supply and relief for at least some insureds. *Incentive flag: Citizens/OIR and the state administration have a political incentive to declare reforms "working." The fairest reading is not "problem solved," but "the cost spiral has broken at the wholesale margin while the retail level — and the commercial/condo all-in cost — is still high enough to pressure households, condo boards, and commercial owners."*

### 3. Bank transmission (Q1 2026 prints)

Across the five most Florida-exposed regionals, the latest quarter shows **contained normalization, not loss transmission** — and, notably, deposit stability everywhere.

**SouthState (SSB).** Large Florida-HQ regional with broad Southeast diversification, so less of a pure South Florida vehicle. ROA 1.37%, ROTCE 17.6%; loans $49.5B (+7% annualized), deposits +5% annualized. NPAs 0.66% of loans + OREO; net charge-offs just **9 bps**; provision $10.8M; ACL plus unfunded-commitment reserves **1.32%** of loans; classified assets $2.5B (3.6% of total assets) with 88% in accrual and 99% current. CEO John Corbett explained CRE special-mention/classified migration as a function of underwriting to a 3% rate shock overtaken by a ~5% move — "little to no concern about loss content" (problem-loan weighted-avg LTV 56%, 98% current). Watch: weaker low-income consumer, small business/SBA.

**Seacoast (SBCF).** A pure Florida franchise — the cleanest stress barometer in the group. NPLs rose to **0.75% of loans ($95.0M, up from $76.3M)** — but driven by just two commercial credits with "collateral values well in excess of balances outstanding," so "no credit loss is expected." Criticized/classified loans **2.82% of loans, flat sequentially**; ACL 1.39%; net charge-offs **11 bps**; provision just $0.8M. CRE ≈60% of loans; ~$21B assets post-Villages acquisition; construction & land development carries an above-average 1.21% reserve. Funding strong: organic deposit growth +7% annualized, noninterest-bearing deposits +29% annualized, deposit cost down to 1.54%.

**BankUnited (BKU).** The clearest example of "visible exposure, but not visible contagion." Headquartered in Miami Lakes. Credit improved sequentially: **criticized and classified loans fell $146M (−12%)**, **NPLs fell $98M (−26%)**, and the **NPA ratio improved to 0.79% from 1.08%**; CRE non-accruals fell to ~$75.5M (from ~$109M) and C&I non-accruals to ~$175.4M (from ~$238M). Allowance for credit losses **$208.8M, or 0.87% of total loans** *(verified — see Verification notes)*, down from $219.8M / 0.91% the prior quarter, with ACL-to-NPL coverage rising from ~59% to 75.9%. The allowance fell despite higher specific reserves because net charge-offs ($36.1M, an elevated ~37 bps trailing 12-month) and improved asset quality more than offset them; Q1 provision was $25M, including an $8M qualitative overlay for geopolitical uncertainty. CRE portfolio $6.9B (47% Florida, office at 20%, weighted-avg office DSCR 1.78, LTV 64.2%); CET1 12.2%; NIM 2.99%; EPS $0.83. Funding solid: non-brokered deposits +$277M QoQ / +$1.4B YoY.

**Valley National (VLY).** More a CRE-concentration story than a Florida-crisis story. NIM 3.17%; net income $163.9M; loans $50.8B, deposits $52.9B; ACL/loans 1.18% (down from 1.19%); non-accrual loans 0.85% of total, with **non-accrual CRE falling** modestly; CRE concentration ratio edged down to ~**329% of risk-based capital**; CET1 10.91%. The print is not showing a Florida-driven break.

**Amerant (AMTB).** The purest South Florida read (21 of 23 banking centers in South Florida, 2 in Tampa) and the bank most visibly *working through* criticized credits. Net income $17.9M ($0.44 EPS, up from $0.07); provision rose to **$7.8M (from $3.5M)**, reflecting proactive de-risking (exiting large-exposure, out-of-footprint and criticized loans; dedicated portfolio-management team stood up). **Classified loans fell 9.7% to $320.3M (from $354.8M)** on payoffs and sales. But the non-performing and watch buckets ticked *up* (all verified against the 8-K): **NPAs rose to $191.6M from $186.9M; NPLs rose to $176.1M from $171.4M; special-mention loans rose to $148.2M from $136.5M.** Gross charge-offs were $9.1M (net charge-offs 0.45% of average loans); the ACL ratio was 1.21%, up from 1.20%. So Amerant is a genuinely mixed signal: classified balances resolving down while non-performers and special-mention edge up — a bank working through stress, not yet overwhelmed by it. Deposits still grew (+2.0%); CET1 11.84%; total loans ~$6.8B.

**Verdict:** Florida stress is genuinely NOT yet visible in the prints. The only upticks are modest, collateral-backed, and explained by rate-driven reclassification or isolated credits rather than loss content; net charge-offs remain at multi-decade lows (9–11 bps) and deposits are stable-to-growing. *Incentive flag: bank managements and sell-side analysts are structurally biased toward "benign credit / no loss expected" right up until losses crystallize. The independent checks are realized net charge-offs (very low) and specific reserve builds against CRE/condo credits (the early tell).* **Critical caveat for the condo thesis: public filings rarely isolate "condo-master" or association-master loans — they are buried inside CRE, C&I relationship books, or specialty portfolios. The absence of a disclosed condo shock does not mean the channel is absent; it means the best public proxies remain the broader ones (criticized/classified CRE, special-mention migration, reserve builds, deposit stability) — and those do not yet show a synchronized Florida bank-loss event.**

### 4. Business bankruptcy

National business filings are up (25,960, +11.4% 12-mo ending March 2026), and Subchapter V small-business elections jumped (+36% in May 2026 per ABI/Epiq). But commercial Chapter 11 filings actually *fell* ~7% YoY in May 2026 (684 vs. 739), arguing against a runaway corporate-distress wave — and in the Florida districts, business cases are only ~5% (Middle) and ~6% (Southern) of total filings. The Florida surge is principally a **consumer** story first.

The salient Florida anecdote — Spirit Airlines (Dania Beach HQ) — liquidated May 2, 2026 (wind-down approved in SDNY). Its proximate trigger was the Iran-driven jet-fuel spike: counsel told the court that rising fuel costs after the U.S./Israel strikes on Iran "engulfed Spirit entirely," with fuel expenses up roughly $100M "in March and April alone"; Reuters called it "the industry's first casualty linked to the Iran war." So it is an **energy-shock casualty, not a Florida-CRE-driven event,** and should not be double-counted as Florida-district business distress. Darden (Orlando) is closing/rebranding 28 Bahama Breeze locations.

Crucially, the CRE-vacancy feedback loop is largely absent at the metro-aggregate level: Miami posted the lowest office vacancy among the 25 largest U.S. office markets (12.5%, down from 15.7% a year earlier) vs. a 17.6% national rate; Tampa 15.1%; and Florida retail vacancy is near historic lows (Miami ~2.8%, Tampa ~3.0%, Orlando ~3.4% vs. ~4.2% national). *That said, low aggregate vacancy does not rule out South Florida pain in office, condos, or older habitational stock — a metro-by-metro / segment-level vacancy series was not built in this pass, so the "no doom-loop" read is an aggregate statement, not a segment-level all-clear.* **Net: Florida business distress is elevated only in line with the national trend plus a few salient anecdotes — not a self-reinforcing local CRE spiral, on the evidence available.**

### 5. Macro overlay — Iran energy → CPI spillover

**Energy conditions.** The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since February 28, 2026. Brent averaged ~$117/bbl in April and ~$107 in May 2026, then fell to roughly $77–92 by mid-June on U.S.–Iran ceasefire optimism (EIA forecasts ~$105 June/July, easing to ~$79 in 2027 once flows resume).

**Spillover thesis vs. data.** May 2026 CPI rose 0.5% MoM and 4.2% YoY (highest since April 2023), per BLS. But BLS attributed *over 60%* of the monthly gain to energy (+3.9% MoM, +23.5% YoY); core CPI was only +0.2% MoM and +2.9% YoY, and *core goods fell 0.1%*. Transportation services fell 0.6% — "a potential indicator that high energy costs were not filtering into other areas." Chris Rupkey of Fwdbonds noted "the inflation risks for core consumer goods are in retreat for now." The spillover the Snaith/UCF narrative warns about is, as of the latest data, *not yet present* — it remains a forward risk, not a realized fact. (Energy-price pass-through is economically plausible and historically common; "supported by data" and "built into a forecast" are different claims, and the realized data sits on the latter side.)

**Snaith / UCF credibility.** Sean Snaith directs UCF's Institute for Economic Forecasting and sits on the WSJ Economic Forecasting Survey, the Philadelphia Fed's Survey of Professional Forecasters, and the USA Today panel; he was named one of the nation's most accurate forecasters by Bloomberg in 2008 and California's most accurate in 2007, and is widely regarded as a credible, accessible communicator. *Calibration: his own substance is measured, not alarmist — he forecasts GDP slowing (2.1%→1.8%→1.7%), explicitly does NOT predict recession, and on Florida housing says "prices have flattened," a 2009-style crash being "highly unlikely." His thesis is a conditional forecast ("if the Iran conflict continues"). At the same time, no audited long-run public scorecard of UCF's forecast errors versus peers on this specific inflation channel was found in this review. Net: give the UCF thesis **moderate credibility as informed macro forecasting**, but do not overweight it as a back-tested empirical early-warning system for South Florida distress.*

### 6. Bottom line — the single most diagnostic metric

**The cleanest discriminator over Q2–Q3 2026 is a *synchronized* jump in already-elevated criticized/classified CRE and relationship credits — across more than one Florida-concentrated bank at the same time — that converts into realized net charge-offs and specific reserve builds.** Today the system shows the signature of normalization, not crash: non-accruals and criticized balances are migrating, but they are staying current and collateral-backed, NCOs are at multi-decade-low levels (9–11 bps), reserves are flat-to-down, and deposits are stable-to-growing. The acute-crash scenario requires that collateral values fail so the criticized balances actually charge off — i.e., NCOs spike and banks build specific reserves against CRE/condo credits, simultaneously across institutions. A secondary diagnostic is the *mix* of Florida consumer filings: a sharp acceleration in **Chapter 7 (liquidation) per-capita, specifically in FLM/FLS**, would signal genuine household insolvency rather than a Chapter 13 "payment-plan" filing culture.

**Calibrated judgment: the weight of evidence points to slow demand-normalization, not acute crash — roughly 70% normalization / 30% acute crash over the next two quarters.** Supporting normalization: benign bank credit, stable deposits, easing wholesale insurance, dramatic Citizens depopulation, tight Florida CRE vacancy at the aggregate, falling oil post-ceasefire, contained core inflation, and per-capita bankruptcy that is elevated-but-not-extreme. The 30% tail is dominated by the Iran energy shock: if the Hormuz closure persists and Brent re-spikes toward $110+, the jet-fuel/transport channel (which already killed Spirit) plus a delayed energy-to-core CPI pass-through plus a higher-for-longer Fed could convert the currently benign criticized-loan migration into actual losses. The bearish case remains alive — especially through condo economics, high all-in ownership costs, and pockets of criticized-credit migration — but the "tidal wave" framing is ahead of what verified bank-loss transmission data currently show.

## Recommendations

1. **Track the criticized-to-charge-off conversion quarterly.** For SSB, SBCF, BKU and AMTB, watch Q2 2026 (reported ~late July) and Q3 2026 (~late October) for: (a) net charge-offs breaking above ~25–30 bps annualized, (b) specific reserve builds against CRE/condo-association credits, and (c) classified loans moving *out of* accrual status. Any two of these three flipping — **across more than one bank at once** — would materially raise the acute-crash probability.
2. **Use Chapter 7 per-capita filings in FLM/FLS as the consumer tripwire.** A sustained acceleration in Chapter 7 (not Chapter 13) filings per 100k in the two Florida districts — pulling the district per-capita rates decisively above ~230/100k — would indicate genuine household insolvency rather than population-driven volume.
3. **Treat the Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz situation as the master variable.** Benchmark: Brent sustained above ~$100/bbl into Q3 2026 (vs. the post-ceasefire ~$77–92) would revive the jet-fuel/transport distress channel and raise the odds of energy-to-core CPI pass-through. A durable reopening toward Brent ~$79 (EIA's 2027 path) reinforces the normalization base case.
4. **Watch the commercial/condo insurance layer specifically.** It is the segment NOT easing cleanly (Citizens commercial +10.4%; multifamily underwriting tightening; SIRS/HB 1021–1203 compliance squeeze). Monitor special-assessment delinquencies, Citizens non-renewal of non-compliant associations, and commercial-master rate filings — the most plausible idiosyncratic Florida channel that could surface before it hits bank prints.
5. **Discount incentive-conflicted sources explicitly.** Down-weight the bankruptcy-attorney "tidal wave" framing (volume-incentivized), the Citizens/OIR "thriving market" narrative (politically incentivized), broker "thriving market" reinsurance commentary (placement-incentivized), and bank-management "no loss expected" assurances (structurally optimistic). Up-weight realized, hard-to-game metrics: net charge-offs, FDIC call-report data, AOUSC per-capita filings, deposit flows, and BLS core-CPI components.

## Caveats

- **Data recency/lag.** Bank data is Q1 2026 (filed April–May 2026); Q2 2026 prints are not yet available. Some bankruptcy figures from third-party aggregators (SmartAsset, BankruptcyWatch) are used for per-capita and weekly context and may differ slightly from final AOUSC tables. Per-capita population denominators are approximate for judicial districts, which do not map cleanly to census units.
- **Spirit Airlines is an energy-shock casualty, not a Florida-demand casualty,** and was adjudicated in SDNY — it should not be double-counted as Florida-district business distress.
- **The insurance "easing" is loss-experience-dependent.** All broker commentary is explicitly conditioned on a benign hurricane season; a major 2026 landfall would reverse the wholesale softening quickly.
- **The macro tail is genuinely two-sided.** The energy-to-core spillover is unproven in the data *today* but is a live forward risk; a renewed Hormuz escalation is the single fastest path from "normalization" to "crash."
- **The "no CRE doom-loop" read is an aggregate statement.** A metro-by-metro / segment-level vacancy series (office vs. older condo/habitational stock) was not built; aggregate tightness does not rule out segment-specific distress.
- **Condo-master exposure is not cleanly disclosed** by any bank in the set; broader CRE/multifamily/habitational criticized-credit indicators remain the best public proxies.
