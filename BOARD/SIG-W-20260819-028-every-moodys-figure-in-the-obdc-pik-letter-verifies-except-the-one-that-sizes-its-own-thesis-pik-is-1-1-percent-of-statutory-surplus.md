## 2. 🔴 THE OMISSION, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS: Moody's SIZES the PIK exposure, and the letter does not carry the number

The same Moody's report the letter draws its other five figures from **quantifies payment-in-kind exposure at 1.1% of statutory surplus**, flagged as a *"potential late-cycle warning indicator."*

**Every other Moody's figure in that report survives into the letter. The one that sizes its own title does not.**

⚠️ **Stated carefully, because this does NOT refute the thesis.** 1.1% of surplus is a **LEVEL**, and Moody's own framing is about **direction and cycle position**, not magnitude — a late-cycle indicator is watched because it *grows*, not because it is currently large. **A small PIK share and a real PIK problem are compatible.** What is not compatible is building a headline on an exposure while omitting the only published sizing of it from the report you are otherwise quoting six times. **BROCK and SHADE should carry the 1.1%, and should carry it as a base to measure growth from — not as a refutation.**

Moody's four named dynamics, for the record: **concentration risk · a widening credit-quality gap vs the broader portfolio · rising structural complexity through ABS · growing PIK exposure.** The letter's thesis is Moody's fourth dynamic; **Moody's ranked it fourth and sized it.**

## 3. ⚠️ THE 48.4% IS **S&P GLOBAL MARKET INTELLIGENCE**, NOT AN NAIC PUBLICATION

The letter writes: *"Privately placed bonds, **as classified under the NAIC's broad bond framework**, reached 48.4% of total life industry bonds at year-end 2025, up from 37.4% five years earlier."*

**The figure is correct** (Insurance Business Magazine, 2026-08-10, fetched). **Its source is the S&P Global Market Intelligence *2026 US Insurance Investments Market Report*.** NAIC's broad bond framework is the **classification standard** the number is computed under — not the publisher of the number.

**🔑 This is not pedantry, and here is the proof it costs something: I went to the NAIC primary looking for it.** I pulled `content.naic.org/.../capital-markets-special-reports-asset-mix-ye2025.pdf` and text-extracted it. **The 48.4% is not in it** — that report covers the asset-mix split (bonds below 60% of the industry portfolio, common stock 13.6%, Schedule BA 6.7%). **A reader who trusts the attribution goes to the wrong publisher and comes back empty, which reads as "unverifiable" when the figure is in fact fine.** `[[finding_guidance_is_not_the_instrument]]`
