# CRE pricing winners-vs-losers spread hits a RECORD high — industrial +88.5% vs office +36.6% (pre-pandemic), blended median a record $129/SF (CREED)

**One line:** Per Altus Group Q1 2026 (via CRE Daily), the gap between CRE's top performers and weakest links is the widest on record: **industrial prices +88.5% vs pre-pandemic, office +36.6%** — Altus calls it a **"structural repricing," not a temporary recovery gap.** Overall CRE prices +1.4% QoQ / +8.7% YoY (industrial +11.8% / retail +10.4% leading); **every property type except hospitality hit new pricing highs.** Median transaction price a record **$129/SF** (>2× the 2009 $56/SF bottom). Per-SF medians: multifamily $150 (highest) / retail $142 / hospitality $138 / office $137 / industrial $110 (cheapest). Altus: macro risks (rates, oil, AI-office) "haven't dented pricing" yet but "could emerge later this year."

> **GRADE: SKIP-VERIFY 0.80, cluster_mediating.** A genuinely two-sided, counter-thesis datum CREED should reconcile against its "selective recognition ACCELERATING, not broad cascade yet" base case. **Bull-CRE:** transaction pricing is resilient — record highs across nearly every type, the broad-distress narrative hasn't shown up in marks. **Bear/bifurcation:** the office-vs-industrial spread is at a *record* (the structural repricing Altus names), AND the headline "record $129/SF blended median" is exactly a [[blended_index_masks_bifurcation]] artifact — it averages booming industrial/retail/MF over a distressed office tail. The deeper caveat: **transaction-median pricing is selection-biased** — it reflects what CLEARS, not what's stuck; distressed office that can't transact near par (601W Chicago / BXMT-default-type assets, SIG-626-012) is under-represented in a median of completed deals. So "office +36.6% vs pre-pandemic" is a *transacted-office* number, not a mark on the frozen distressed tail.
