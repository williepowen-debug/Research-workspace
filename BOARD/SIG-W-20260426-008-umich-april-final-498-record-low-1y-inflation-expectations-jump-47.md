## Signal

**UMich Consumer Sentiment Index April 2026 FINAL: 49.8** (released Apr 24 2026, last Friday of month, on schedule). Above preliminary 47.6, above consensus 48.0, but **below prior all-time low of 50.0 (June 2022, data back to 1978)** — barely. Down 3.5 points from March final 53.3.

Component breakdown:
- **Current Conditions: 52.5**
- **Expectations: 48.1**
- **1-year inflation expectations: 4.7%** (up from 3.8% in March — **largest one-month jump since April 2025**)
- **5-year inflation expectations: 3.5%** (highest since October 2025)

This is a refinement of SIG-W-20260410-001 (UMich preliminary 47.6 dispatched IMMEDIATE Apr 11 paired with CPI 3.3% YoY hot print under "stagflation lock" framing). The preliminary anchored a more severe collapse; the final revised up.

## Caveats

- **Refinement of SIG-W-20260410-001, not new signal.** Original IMMEDIATE dispatch on preliminary stands; this is final-print update with two corrections (less dire headline, more dire inflation-expectations component).
- **Final 49.8 vs preliminary 47.6 = 2.2-point upward revision.** That's larger than typical preliminary-to-final revision (often <1 point). Suggests survey-period heterogeneity — early-April panel saw higher stress than late-April panel.
- **Record-low headline holds by 0.2 points** (49.8 vs 50.0 June 2022). Statistically distinguishable but tight. Communications should preserve "barely-record-low" framing rather than dramatic.
- **1Y inflation expectations are survey-derived**, not market-derived. Persistence is the question — single-month jumps can mean-revert. Watch May print.
- **5Y at 3.5% (highest since Oct 2025)** is a slower-moving series; rising = re-anchoring concern, not noise.
- **What's NOT in this signal:** wage-growth components, partisan breakdown of sentiment (UMich publishes Republican/Democrat/Independent splits), spending intent measures.
