# Uninsured flood-exposed homes = a growing, FL-heaviest mortgage-credit-risk channel — climate flood-map expansion meets the NFIP take-up gap (CORAL)

**One line:** National Mortgage News (6/26, on a Moody's RMS report) frames a structural channel — flood risk is expanding *beyond* the homes/areas historically recognized as flood-prone, while most newly-exposed homes carry NO flood insurance, so a flood event turns into mortgage delinquency/default. The exposure is geographically concentrated, and **Florida is the single most-exposed state.**

> **GRADE: CONFIRMED 0.85, PRIORITY.** This is a real, primary-backed transmission channel (climate flood-map expansion → flood-insurance gap → mortgage credit risk), not a paywalled headline. It's **structural / slow-burn, not an acute trigger** — and the headline $-figures are *modeled tail-scenario* exposure, not realized loss (route the channel + the mechanism, not the big number as if incurred).

## Verified key datapoints (WALTER verify-research, 6/27)
- **$375B uninsured flood exposure** at the 1-in-100-yr level (**65% protection gap**); **>$1T at 1-in-500-yr** (>70% gap); ~$472B by 2050 under intermediate emissions — *Moody's RMS US Inland Flood HD model, 5/26/26.*
- **65% of the protection gap sits in <2% of US counties**, clustered in **FL, LA, SC, TX** — same source. (Concentration = the FL/CORAL relevance.)
- **Flood risk beyond FEMA maps:** First Street finds **~14.6M properties at substantial risk, ~68% above FEMA's 8.7M** (~26M at some risk over 30 yrs). This is the subhead's "homes not expected to be high-risk are becoming so."
- **Insurance gap:** NFIP ≈ **4.7M policies / ~3.7M buildings**; **77% of at-risk homes OUTSIDE FEMA zones carry NO flood insurance** (Neptune/Insurance Journal, Aug 2025); Philadelphia Fed: **~70% of annual flood losses uninsured (~$17.1B/yr).**
- **Mechanism (strong, peer-reviewed):** post-Hurricane-Harvey, flood-damaged Houston properties saw mortgage **delinquency +205%** (90-day-DQ entry ~50× normal); in non-mandated (uninsured) zones, damage materially raised 180-day delinquency/default/modification odds — *Fannie Mae loan-level + FHFA WP24-09.* The article cites ~10,000 post-Beryl Houston mortgage delinquencies in one month + CFPB's ~440,000 homes NFIP under-counts as at-risk.
- **Florida:** **~1.7–1.8M NFIP policies ≈ 18% of all US policies (most of any state), ~$440B coverage**; ~15–20% of FL homeowners uninsured for flood; NFIP underpins ~40,000 FL transactions/month.
