# UN official warns a food crisis in parts of Asia and Africa could follow if the US and Iran do not agree on the Hormuz fertilizer plan within weeks; no fertilizer deal 'failed'

Jorge Moreira da Silva, UN Under-Secretary-General and head of UNOPS, who oversees the UN Hormuz fertilizer taskforce (not FAO or WFP), told Reuters in Bangkok on October 14 that failure by the US and Iran to agree on the UN plan within weeks COULD trigger a food-security crisis in parts of Asia and Africa: 'It's absolutely crucial to get the deal in the next few weeks. Otherwise, we will miss the planting season.' The mechanism was drafted after the taskforce formed in March but is not activated. It could start 7-10 days after a US-Iran agreement: an Oman coordination centre would register vessels, UN monitors would verify loading at Gulf ports and ships would be tracked, moving about five ships a day. There was no discrete fertilizer negotiation that failed; broader indirect US-Iran exchanges have stalled since the June interim arrangement collapsed. The White House, State Department and Iran's embassy did not comment. Figures with basis: before the war Hormuz carried about one-third of globally traded urea and nearly half of seaborne sulphur (Reuters); IFPRI says Gulf fertilizer exports remain 'largely' halted; an ICC August scenario put cereals up to +81% and oil crops +66% if the disruption continues (a scenario, not a forecast). The circulating tweet turned a forward conditional into an accomplished failure.
