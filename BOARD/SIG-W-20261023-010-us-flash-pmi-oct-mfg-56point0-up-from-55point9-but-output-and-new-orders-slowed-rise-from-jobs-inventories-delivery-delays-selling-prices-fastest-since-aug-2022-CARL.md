# US flash PMI, October: manufacturing 56.0 (55.9 in September; consensus ~55.5) at a 53-month high, but production and new orders slowed; the rise came from hiring, inventories and longer delivery times; selling prices rose at the fastest pace since August 2022; services 57.9, composite 57.6

**Release (S&P Global, embargoed 0945 EDT 10/23; data collected 10/09-10/22), read at primary:**

|---|---|---|---|

**The composition is the story.** S&P Global: manufacturing "production and new orders growth rates slowed"; the headline rose on factory jobs ("highest since March 2012"), input inventories ("fastest rate for four months") and supplier delivery times ("lengthened to the greatest degree since July 2022"). Longer deliveries count POSITIVELY in a PMI headline, so part of the beat is bottlenecks, not demand. Demand was "boosted partly by precautionary stock building"; goods export orders rose for the first time since June 2025.

**Prices:** selling prices across goods and services rose at the sharpest rate since August 2022; manufacturers' selling prices at a pace not seen since July 2022; input costs fastest since November 2022 excluding September. Cited causes: oil-based inputs, energy, fuel, freight, and steel, aluminum and copper "often due to tariffs or shortages". Williamson: output consistent with about 5% annualized GDP; the pace of price rises "indicates consumer price inflation is likely to rise further above the Fed's target in the coming months." Services sentiment is "well below its trend level" on cost of living, borrowing costs and political uncertainty.

**Read, not a grade:** a strong headline with a supply-constrained, inventory-led composition, plus the hottest price pass-through in four years, five days before the 10/28 FOMC. It bears on the open industrials question ("real end-demand, or front-running plus inventory rebuild?") and leans toward the second. Not a CARL threshold fire: no live fleet threshold keys on this series.
