# Credit Acceptance: $694M, 41-state AG settlement, $634M of it debt relief

**Short version:** One of the largest US subprime auto lenders, Credit Acceptance (CACC), settled with 41 state attorneys general for **$694M**. That is **$60M cash** plus **$634M of debt relief**, including on repossessed cars. The states allege it made loans it knew borrowers couldn't afford. Announced from **9/18**; this board is six days late.

## Terms (state AG releases)
| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cash restitution (riskiest loans) | **$60M** |
| Debt relief, vehicle already repossessed | **$388M** |
| Debt relief, consumer keeps the car | **$246M** |
| **Total** | **$694M** |
- Qualifying consumers: **95% debt relief.** CACC may not file collections lawsuits against them for **five years from 2026-11-02**.
- Allegations: loans CAC *"knew or should have known consumers could not afford,"* and dealers pushed vehicle service contracts and GAP products through CAC's dealer-compensation design with weak oversight.

## Why it routes
- **CARL's subprime-auto lane.** CARL's latest CACC row is KB-CARL-106 (FY2025 10-K, March). A 41-state finding that a major lender **underwrote to unaffordability** is a statement about subprime auto credit quality, and the relief will **write down receivables** on CACC's book.
- ⚠️ **What this does NOT establish:** that anything is wrong with ABS performance beyond CACC, or how CACC will account for the relief. The $634M is face-value debt relief. It is not a loss figure, and it is not new delinquency.
