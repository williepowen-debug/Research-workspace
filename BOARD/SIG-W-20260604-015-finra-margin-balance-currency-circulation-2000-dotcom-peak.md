**Mechanism — FINRA Margin Balance vs Currency-in-Circulation:**
- **FINRA Margin Balance** (numerator): total customer debit balances in margin accounts at FINRA member firms (i.e., aggregate retail+institutional borrowed-to-buy-securities position). Published monthly by FINRA.
- **Currency in Circulation** (denominator): Fed-reported physical-cash-in-economy; grows ~5-7%/yr with M0 expansion.
- **Ratio normalization** removes the secular growth in absolute margin debt (which always trends up) and gives a "margin debt relative to monetary base" metric.
- **0.54 means margin debt is 54% the size of currency in circulation** — extreme leverage in the system relative to the monetary base.

**Why the 2000 dotcom peak is the relevant analog:**
- 2000 peak of 0.54 preceded SPX -49.1% peak-to-trough Mar 2000 - Oct 2002.
- 2007 peak (0.41) was LOWER than 2000 even though SPX peaked higher in absolute terms — because absolute margin debt levels are not comparable, only ratios.
- **Reaching the 2000 peak in 2026 = retail+institutional leverage relative to the monetary base is back at the dotcom-bubble extreme.**
