**What the abstract says, in four parts:**
1. **Remote access:** new restrictions on exports, reexports and in-country transfers to **overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau**. Data centers, foreign foundries and chip exporters must do due diligence on customers, including indirect ownership or control by D:5 or Macau persons. Commercial recommender systems are excluded.
2. **Five diversion-risk countries:** a licence is now required to export advanced semiconductors to **Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil** (and any country the Secretary adds), except for chips destined to companies headquartered, or with an ultimate parent headquartered, in the United States, if they meet reporting requirements. Commerce names possible consequences for countries that tolerate evasion: import restrictions, new licence requirements, a downgraded export-control status, Entity List designations.
3. **Foundries and packagers:** fewer pathways to overcome the presumptions for chip designers located in destinations that need a licence for advanced chips.
4. **AI Diffusion Rule** formally rescinded (never enforced; BIS announced the rescission in May 2025).

**For the owners:** VULCAN (Nvidia's 10-Q is still pending; Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand data-center demand sits under the new licence line) and ZHAO (China-headquartered access through overseas data centers).
