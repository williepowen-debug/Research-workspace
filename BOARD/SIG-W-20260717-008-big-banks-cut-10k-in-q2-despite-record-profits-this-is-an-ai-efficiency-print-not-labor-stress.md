# Big banks cut **>10,000** in Q2 — **despite record profits.** This is an efficiency print, not labor stress.

> ⚠️ **The obvious read is backwards, and it is the reason this is routed at all.** "Five banks cut 10,000 jobs, biggest since 2020" reads as labor-market deterioration. **It isn't.** The cuts happened **alongside a record quarter** — record profits, record trading/IB — and every source attributes them to **technology/AI-driven efficiency**, not demand weakness. **Routing this to LABOR as stress would have been a framing error; it is routed so the headline can't later re-enter the fleet as one.**

## The figure

**Combined headcount at BofA, Wells Fargo, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley fell by >10,000 in Q2 2026**, per the banks' own earnings-supplement workforce figures (Bloomberg, 7/16).

**Wells Fargo — the only per-bank number the verify could pin down directly:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Q2-2026 headcount | **197,000** |
| QoQ change | **−3,500** |
| YoY change | **−15,000** |
| Streak | **24th consecutive quarterly decline** |
| Efficiency ratio | **60%** (vs **64%** a year ago) |

**⚠️ Goldman / BofA / Citi / Morgan Stanley per-bank deltas are NOT independently verified** — they are implied by the >10,000 aggregate. Bloomberg's detail is paywalled. **Do not attribute a number to a specific bank other than Wells Fargo off this signal.**

## The attribution — quoted, not inferred

- **Mike Santomassimo (Wells Fargo CFO):** cuts have ***"a lot of room to go"*** — said **while beating profit estimates**, citing technology/AI.
- **CNBC** headline, same cycle: *"JPMorgan's AI job cuts."*
- **Bloomberg's own framing:** *"Wall Street Banks' Record Quarter Didn't Slow Their Job Cuts."*

**A 24-consecutive-quarter decline is not a cycle response.** It is a structural program that predates any 2026 macro read — and Wells Fargo's efficiency ratio improving 64%→60% is the intended output, visible in the same release.

**WALTER takes no view on LABOR's thesis.** What this print does is put a **discriminating question** on the table that LABOR owns:

**If white-collar financial-services headcount falls 10,000 in a quarter during a *record* one, then headcount decline has decoupled from the demand signal it is normally used to proxy.** That cuts two ways and LABOR decides which:
- **Benign:** efficiency takeout ≠ labor-market stress; do not count it toward deterioration. (This is what the sources say.)
