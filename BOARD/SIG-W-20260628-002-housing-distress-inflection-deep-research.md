## Key Finding

US residential distress is **inflecting on year-over-year rate-of-change but still normalizing on absolute level** — an early-stage rise off a post-moratorium *suppressed* base, not a 2008-style break. The single place where every distress thread coheres is **Florida + the 2022-and-later Sun Belt FHA/VA thin-equity vintage**; elsewhere the threads point in *different* directions (zombies are a Midwest/Rust-Belt phenomenon, not a Sun-Belt one). Crucially, the **most recent monthly print (May 2026) fell −5% MoM**, so the "surge" framing is a Q1-vintage artifact that has already softened.

---

## Evidence

### The core tension is semantic, not factual
ATTOM (the primary data provider behind nearly all these headlines) and the "shifting dynamics" reading **describe the same rising series**. ATTOM's own Q1 release is literally titled "Foreclosure Activity Rises in Q1 2026 **As Market Continues To Normalize**," and CEO Rob Barber frames it as "market recalibration [rather] than widespread homeowner distress" while simultaneously saying the rise "could signal shifting housing market dynamics" [PRIMARY: ATTOM Q1 2026 Foreclosure Market Report, Apr 16 2026]. "Normalization" here means *rising back toward pre-pandemic norms from artificially suppressed lows* — it does not negate "rising." Any read citing only one framing is incomplete.

### Thread 1 — Foreclosure activity: up YoY, but the latest print eased
| Metric | Q1 2026 | YoY | QoQ | **May 2026 (latest)** | YoY | MoM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filings | 118,727 | **+26%** | +6% | **40,355** | +14% | **−5%** |
| Starts | 82,631 | +20% | +7% | 27,304 | +13% | −4% |
| Completions (REOs) | 14,020 | **+45%** | +2% | 4,092 | +6% | — |

[PRIMARY: ATTOM Q1 2026 Foreclosure Market Report, Apr 16 2026; ATTOM May 2026 U.S. Foreclosure Market Report, rel. Jun 11 2026]. The NMN "foreclosures surged 26% annually" headline matches Q1 filings YoY exactly — but it is **Q1-vintage**. The freshest print (May, the latest available as of 2026-06-27) is **+14% YoY and −5% MoM**: an eighth straight month of YoY gains, but a sequential *cooling*. Both starts (front of pipeline) and REOs (back) are rising YoY, so the pipeline is filling and clearing faster — genuine "shifting dynamics." But the +45% REO is off a depressed post-moratorium base (14,020 vs a pre-pandemic Q1 norm of ~30–50k), so the percentage is **base-effect-inflated**; absolute volumes remain below historical peaks. ATTOM's own characterization is "gradual year-over-year growth," not a surge.

### Thread 2 — Negative equity: multi-year high, but off a historically tiny base
1.1M+ borrowers ended 2025 underwater = **~2.1% of mortgages**, up from ~696k (1.3%) a year earlier; another 3.2M (7.9%) hold <10% equity [PRIMARY: ICE/Black Knight Feb 2026 Mortgage Monitor; INSTITUTIONAL corroboration: CalculatedRisk recap]. ICE calls this "**the highest level since early 2018**" (~8 years).

**Correction to the source headline:** the NMN "underwater mortgage rate hits a **4-year high**" teaser **misattributes the metric**. The "four-year high" in the ICE report refers to **affordability** (best since early 2022, after the early-Jan rate decline cut the typical P&I payment ~7% to ~$2,091) — *not* the underwater rate, which is an 8-year high. (Workflow adversarial vote confirmed this attribution 2-1.) The direction of both is the same, but the label is wrong.

Negative equity is concentrated in **FHA/VA loans originated 2022+** — the thin-equity, bought-near-the-top cohort. FHA non-current rate is **>13% (≈3× the market average)**, with early-payment defaults at the highest since 2009 — the mechanism that converts thin equity into actual foreclosure.

### Thread 3 — Zombie/vacant foreclosures: essentially flat → NOT a distress signal
8,312 vacant homes in the foreclosure process in Q2 2026 = **3.4% of the 245,376 properties in foreclosure**, up trivially from 3.3% in Q1 2026 *and* 3.3% a year earlier [PRIMARY: ATTOM Q2 2026 Vacant Property & Zombie Foreclosure Report, rel. May 21 2026]. The "zombies rose in 38 states" headline describes tiny QoQ moves on tiny absolute bases (e.g., Georgia "+98%" = 51→101 homes). 8,312 zombies out of 104.9M residential properties is a rounding error. **Discard this thread as a distress signal.**

### Thread 4 — Distressed-sales share: rising marginally, explicitly normalizing
Bank/REO dispositions ≈ **1.6% of all home sales in Q1 2026**, up from ~1.3% the prior period and ~1.5% a year earlier; the data provider frames it as "continued normalization back toward pre-pandemic levels," and REO buyers paid 67.3% of retail value (vs 64.6% a quarter earlier, 68.6% a year earlier) [NEWS/INSTITUTIONAL: National Mortgage News, "Distressed sales numbers show signs of market normalization" — attribution to underlying provider is soft]. Small share, modest rise, "normalize" side of the ledger.

### Thread 5 — Property taxes rising into falling values (the cleanest consumer-squeeze thread)
$396.8B in property taxes levied on 89.6M single-family homes in 2025 = **+3.7% YoY** (from $382.7B); the average bill rose **~3% to $4,427** even as the average estimated single-family value **fell −1.7% to $494,231** [PRIMARY: ATTOM 2025 Annual Property Tax Report, rel. ~Apr 2026]. The effective tax rate rose to 0.90% (from 0.86%) — **highest since 2020**. Taxes rose in **40 states + DC** and fell in 10 (mostly the West). Steepest hikes: Delaware +18%, Maryland +11.6%; metros Memphis +34%, Baltimore +27%, St. Louis +11%. Highest effective rates: NJ 1.58%, Vermont 1.4%, Connecticut 1.36%. **This is the thread that bridges to PROMPT-3 (muni-fiscal):** municipalities raise levies to fund services regardless of values, squeezing the homeowner from a second direction.

### Thread 6 — "31 of 50 riskiest markets in 4 states" — verified exact
ATTOM's Q1 2026 Housing Impact Report ranked 580 counties on **four measures: foreclosure activity, affordability (cost % of wages), underwater rate, and local unemployment**. Of the 50 most-at-risk counties: **12 Florida + 9 California + 5 Illinois + 5 New Jersey = 31** [PRIMARY: ATTOM Q1 2026 Housing Impact Report / PRNewswire 302791284]. The five riskiest counties: Charlotte (FL), Butte (CA), Charles (MD), Shasta (CA), Cumberland (NJ). Primary drivers this quarter: **unemployment + foreclosure**. (Least affordable, separately: Kings County NY at 108.6% of local wages, Santa Cruz CA 97.1%, Marin CA 91.1%.)

### Geographic synthesis — the threads cohere only in Florida
- **Foreclosure rate:** FL worst state in May 2026 (1 in 2,110 units); Lakeland & Punta Gorda the top-2 metros in Q1; Tampa #3 in May.
- **Negative equity:** the *only* major metros >10% underwater are **Cape Coral FL (10.1%) and Lakeland FL (10.8%)**; 7 of the 8 most-underwater majors are FL/TX.
- **Risk-county count:** FL leads with 12 of the 50.
- **Net:** Florida is the one geography where foreclosure, negative equity, and composite risk all point the same way → **CORAL escalation is warranted on the evidence.** The 2022+ FHA/VA Sun Belt vintage is the transmission cohort for both CARL (housing-deflation timing) and REGINALD (FHA/Ginnie residential-collateral trajectory).
- **Counter-geography:** zombies/vacancy are a **Midwest/Rust-Belt** story (Cedar Rapids IA 13.2%, Youngstown OH 11.4%) — a *different mechanism* (legacy-process abandonment). Do **not** merge into one national "distress map."

## Counter-Evidence (mandatory)

**The case that this is NORMALIZATION, not a distress inflection — and it is strong:**
1. **Absolute levels remain below pre-pandemic.** Foreclosure volumes are roughly half of 2019; ATTOM repeatedly states "volumes remain below historical peaks."
2. **The big percentages are base-effect artifacts.** +45% REO YoY is off a moratorium-suppressed 2025 base, not a high one.
3. **The latest monthly print already eased** (May −5% MoM). The Q1 "surge" may be rolling over; we won't know until full Q2 ATTOM data (not yet released).
4. **National negative equity (~2.1%) is historically tiny** — vs ~23% at the 2009 trough. National equity is "historically strong"; the stress is a thin geographic/vintage sliver.
5. **Zombies and distressed-sales share are flat-to-normalizing**, not inflecting.
6. **The data provider itself chooses "normalize."** ATTOM's editorial framing is consistent across multiple reports — this is not cherry-picked.

**The case that this IS an inflection:** YoY direction is unambiguous and broad (8 straight months, both pipeline ends rising); negative equity ~doubled in a year and is concentrated in a clearly identifiable at-risk cohort (2022+ FHA/VA) with FHA non-current >13% and early defaults at a post-2009 high; the geographic concentration is real and intensifying in FL. 

**What would disprove the inflection read:** a second consecutive MoM decline in the June/Q2 ATTOM foreclosure print (turning the May dip into a trend); FHA non-current rates stabilizing; the FL-metro underwater share ceasing to climb. **What would confirm it:** Q2 foreclosure starts re-accelerating MoM; negative-equity share rising again in the next ICE monitor; FL foreclosure rate widening its lead.

**Two adversarially-killed over-claims** (kept out of this report by the verification pass): (a) "Q1 starts indicate *accelerating new distress rather than normalization*" — refuted 0-3 as a framing overreach; (b) a stale Cotality Q1-2025 underwater figure (2.1%/1.2M/+17% YoY) — refuted 0-3 as superseded by the end-2025 ICE print. Neither is used here.
