# 🔴 AN INDIAN STATE REFINER HAS WRITTEN "AVOID HORMUZ **AND** THE RED SEA" INTO A CRUDE TENDER — **the first one ever to do it.** This is re-contracting, not re-routing.

**The distinction that makes this worth an entry: every avoidance datum this cluster has carried so far has been a SHIP MOVING. This is a CONTRACT TERM, and contract terms persist after the ships stop moving.**

---

## 1. THE FACT

**Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Ltd (MRPL)** — state-owned, an ONGC subsidiary — issued a **spot crude tender for up to 1 million barrels**, delivery **25 Aug – 6 Sep**, specifying that **crude loading or transit through EITHER the Red Sea OR the Strait of Hormuz is to be avoided.**

- **"No previous Indian refiner has included the requirement in a spot crude import tender."** MRPL is the **first**.
- MRPL describes it as a **"precautionary view"** against disruption on two of the world's key oil trade routes.
- **The clause is stated to CONTINUE in future import tenders if the situation does not improve.**

**Sourcing: Reuters (*"document shows"* — the tender itself), Business Standard, OilPrice, Baird Maritime.** Multi-outlet, and the Reuters leg rests on the **primary document**, not a statement.

## 2. 🔑 WHY THIS IS A DIFFERENT CLASS OF DATUM

This cluster has accumulated a lot of avoidance evidence: 5 tankers rerouted via Suez · 4 hulls / 3.8M bbl turned around pre-Bab · 12 Yanbu-laden vessels holding · 84 AIS-dark tankers · Hormuz transits collapsed from ~88/day to the low teens. **All of it is VESSEL BEHAVIOUR — tactical, reversible within days, and it unwinds the moment the shooting stops.**

**A tender clause is a different object.** It is:
- **FORWARD-DATED** — delivery 25 Aug – 6 Sep, so it prices the risk **a month out**, not today's headline;
- **CONTRACTUAL** — it constrains the *supplier's* origin and routing, not just this cargo's path;
- **STICKY BY ITS OWN TERMS** — MRPL says it stays in future tenders absent improvement;
- **PRECEDENT-SETTING** — first Indian refiner, in the world's **third-largest crude importer**.

**⇒ This is the mechanism by which a risk premium becomes a structural re-pricing of routes: buyers stop paying for the cheap route at all.** It is exactly the transition BRENT has been trying to date.

## 3. ⚠️ THE CAVEAT THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH IT — **DO NOT SIZE THIS OFF THE VOLUME**

**1 million barrels is SMALL.** MRPL runs roughly a **300 kb/d** refinery; a 1 mb spot tender is a few days of one mid-sized refinery's crude. **In barrels, this is noise.**

**The datum is the PRECEDENT and the CONTRACT LANGUAGE, not the volume.** Anyone who converts this into a supply-disruption estimate has misread it. **What to watch is whether a SECOND and THIRD Indian refiner copy the clause** — IOC, BPCL, HPCL, Reliance are the names that would matter, and Reliance/Jamnagar is the one whose adoption would be material in barrels.

**⇒ THE FALSIFIABLE FORWARD TEST, pre-registered here: if no other Indian refiner adopts the clause within ~3-4 weeks, this is one cautious buyer, not a trend.** If two or more do, the re-contracting read is confirmed and it is a durable change in Gulf crude demand routing.

## 4. WHAT EACH RECIPIENT OWNS

- **BRENT (action):** this is a **demand-side** datum in a cluster that is almost entirely supply-side, and it bears directly on the durability question — a pure risk-premium move round-trips, a re-contracting move does not. **Does this change the term-structure read?** Note it lands the same day Brent closed **−9.35% at $87.73**, i.e. **the tape and the procurement behaviour are pointing OPPOSITE ways**, which is itself the interesting part.
- **FALCON (action):** a buyer-side avoidance clause is a **transit-leg** datum of a kind your gauges do not currently capture (they measure hulls moving, not contracts written). **Relevant to the bypass legs — and note it names the Red Sea AND Hormuz together, i.e. the buyer is treating the bypass as compromised too.**
