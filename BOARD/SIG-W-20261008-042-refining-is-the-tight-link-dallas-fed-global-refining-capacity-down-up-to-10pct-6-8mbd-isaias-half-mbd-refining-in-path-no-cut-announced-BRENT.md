# Refining is the tight link: the Dallas Fed puts global refining capacity down as much as 10% (6–8 mb/d) since late spring; Isaias has ~0.5 mb/d of Gulf Coast refining in its path, but no refinery has announced a cut

**1 — Dallas Fed, published 10/8 (primary, read).** Jesse Thompson and Garrett Golding, "Refinery outages pumping up fuel prices more than cost of crude oil" ([dallasfed.org](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/1008)).
- Global refinery capacity has fallen *"by as much as 10 percent"*; the fuels market *"has sustained as much as 6 mb/d to 8 mb/d of reduced capacity since late spring"*. The causes listed: the Hormuz closure, Red Sea attacks, damaged Middle East refineries, Ukrainian strikes on Russian refining, and Chinese and Russian export restrictions.
- Diesel, gasoline and jet cracks *"have exceeded levels last seen in 2022"* (the text gives no $/bbl; the levels are in Chart 1 only). Inflation-adjusted retail diesel has *"never exceeded gasoline by as much."*
- Outlook: U.S. fuel prices *"will likely remain unusually elevated relative to crude prices"* even after Hormuz flows normalize.
- SPR: 284 mb in September, the lowest since 1982, draining ~1.2 mb/d, which the authors put at under six months to the official 80 mb minimum. Global inventories ~8 bn bbl in late September, ~450M bbl below prewar.
- Data through late September; global scope, no PADD split. This is not the Q3 Dallas Fed Energy Survey, which is a different publication.

**2 — Isaias refining exposure (Will's X bookmark: ZeroHedge 10/8 15:17 ET; Reuters via BOE Report).** The offshore shut-ins are already on the board (`-035`: 62.89% of Gulf oil, BSEE 11:00 CDT). New here: Energy Aspects puts **~0.5 mb/d of refining capacity in the storm's path**, including Chevron's Pascagoula, MS plant (369,000 b/d), which ZeroHedge calls at risk only on *"any abrupt westward shift"* of the cone. **No refinery shutdown or rate cut had been announced** in any source read (latest: ZeroHedge 15:17 ET). Landfall is expected late Friday into Saturday (LA/MS/AL/FL Panhandle).

**3 — The Jefferies "fuel supply event" line is weaker than the headline makes it.** It comes from Kaumil Gajrawala, a **consumer-staples** analyst, in a note sent earlier on 10/8. It rests on the **~25%** shut-in figure, not 63%, and ends in a Coca-Cola (KO) buy call: *"A small disruption now has outsized price consequences."* Read it as a staples analyst's framing, not an energy desk's read.
