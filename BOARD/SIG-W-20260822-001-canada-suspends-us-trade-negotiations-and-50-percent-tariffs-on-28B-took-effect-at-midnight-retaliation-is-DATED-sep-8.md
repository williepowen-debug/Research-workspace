## 4. Why CARL is on action, and the one trap in the transmission

**CARL owns tariff→goods-price.** The naive read is 50% × $28B ⇒ a mechanical CPI contribution. **That read is wrong in a way that matters:**

- **A 50% tariff on a narrow basket is a SUBSTITUTION event before it is a price event.** At 50%, a large share of affected volume does not pay the tariff — it re-sources, re-routes, or stops. **The realized price effect is bounded by the elasticity, not by the rate**, and the revenue base shrinks as the rate rises.
- **The named Canadian retaliation basket points at US EXPORTERS, not US consumers** — steel, dairy, appliances, ag equipment, pulp/paper, electronics. **That leg is a margin/volume story for US industrials and an input-cost story for Canadian buyers. It is NOT a US CPI story**, and merging the two directions into "the tariff is inflationary" loses the sign on half of it.

⇒ **The two legs transmit through DIFFERENT channels with DIFFERENT owners: US goods CPI (CARL) and US exporter volumes (HENRY/MARCO). Keep them separate.**
