# GFS Pride: cause still unresolved, the IRGC mine claim named no ship, and the IMO counts it as the war's second loss, not a 'first mine sinking'; Cape Dao reportedly sinking; Houthi source tells Aramco shareholders to sell

1) GFS PRIDE (follows SIG-W-20261014-002). Cause is unresolved. Singapore's MPA says 'unidentified projectile', and Reuters maritime-security sources also say projectile. The mine claim came from the IRGC Navy public-relations office, no individual named, via Tasnim, Mehr and Fars on the morning of October 14. It described a container ship on an 'unauthorised and dangerous' route that 'struck a mine that exploded' and sank, carrying more than 2,500 containers and 'contraband', but it did not name the ship; Maritime Executive identifies GFS PRIDE from the IRGC images (2,824 TEU, 39,446 dwt, built 2008). Marisks (via CNBC): 'no independent evidence' a mine caused it. No CENTCOM statement on the ship was found, the owner was unreachable, and there is no UAE statement. UKMTO reported two unnamed container ships (about 50,000 t and 19,000 t) with 'minor damage' around 16:40-17:00 GMT October 13, which does not cleanly match a vessel that sank. The circulating 'first vessel sunk by a mine in the war' appears in none of the primary texts; the IMO counts GFS PRIDE as the SECOND vessel lost since February 28 (the first being Safeen Prestige, per gCaptain). Do not count a mine detonation on this claim. The '10 days AIS off' claim is unverified. New: the bulk carrier Cape Dao (Antigua and Barbuda flag, UAE-managed; struck September 23 about 15 nm NE of Khasab, one seafarer killed) is reportedly sinking after a failed salvage (Vanguard, single source). That would be a further loss if confirmed; FALCON adjudicates against its owner count. 2) Aramco threat. An unnamed 'military source' quoted by the Houthi-run Saba agency (YPA, October 14) said: 'We advise Aramco shareholders to dispose of their shares so they do not incur losses in the coming days.' It is not attributed to Yahya Saree; 'hand over' and 'heavy losses' in the circulating version are not in the text. It is a threat statement naming no target or date. Context: the Saudi Energy Ministry said an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran was 'targeted, resulting in a limited fire' late October 13, with no casualties, while Reuters company sources describe a ballistic-missile hit on an HQ office tower with 'extensive damage' and operations continuing; that tension is unresolved (SIG-W-20261013-008, -1014-002).
