## Core Finding

Even with full US military control of Hormuz, oil flows cannot resume quickly. Mine clearance takes weeks, insurance reinstatement is indefinite, and Iran retains asymmetric disruption capabilities. This validates our 4-6 month closure base case — and may be conservative.

## Operational Timeline (US "Takes Over")

| Phase | Duration | Key Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Air defense suppression | 3-7 days | Iranian ballistic missile retaliation |
| Anti-ship missile suppression | 7-14 days | Mobile launchers survive, saturation attacks |
| Mine clearance (initial corridor) | 1-3 weeks | Re-mining by Iran |
| Full operational control | 3-6 weeks | Sustained asymmetric harassment |

Even "militarily clear" ≠ "commercially safe" — insurance companies won't cover until well after military declares area safe.

## Iran's Asymmetric Arsenal

- 5,000-6,000 mines (can deploy in hours, takes weeks to clear)
- Anti-ship missiles: Noor/Qader (200-300km), Khalij Fars (300km, Mach 3-4), Abu Mahdi (1,000+km, AI-guided)
- 3 Kilo-class submarines + midget submarines
- ~3,000 fast attack craft (swarm tactics)
- Mobile launchers disguised as civilian trucks
- Strategy: "manufacturing sustained friction" — attrition, not decisive battle

## Assessment: Posturing vs Real

**Genuine military planning:**
- Two carrier groups (Lincoln + Ford arriving mid-March)
- 150+ military cargo flights
- 50+ additional fighter jets deployed
- Pentagon moving personnel out to reduce exposure

**Posturing/negotiation:**
- Trump: "war is very complete" (contradicts Iranian resistance)
- $20B insurance scheme (alternative to military)
- "Death, fire, fury" rhetoric (negotiation pressure)

**Verdict:** Both happening simultaneously. Real preparation for limited objectives (securing lanes) + posturing for Iran concessions.
