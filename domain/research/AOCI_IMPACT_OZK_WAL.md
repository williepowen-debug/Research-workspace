## 1. The Regulatory Change

**Source:** Fed press release, March 19, 2026 ([confirmed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20260319a.htm))

Three proposals issued simultaneously. Proposal 2 is the operative one for regionals: it "would require certain large banks, subject to a transition period, to reflect unrealized gains and losses on certain securities in their regulatory capital levels" — i.e., eliminate the AOCI opt-out. Comments due **June 18, 2026**.

**Critical scope question:** "Certain large banks" historically = Category III ($250B+ assets or $75B+ WSTF) and Category IV ($100B+ assets or $50B+ WSTF). The $49.5B aggregate hit across "21 regionals" (Risk.net) implies these are all $100B+ banks. OZK ($40.8B) doesn't qualify. WAL ($92.7B) is borderline, likely qualifying by ~2027 at current growth rate.

**Street's misread:** Proposal 1 (Basel III endgame) *reduces* capital requirements for large banks and *simplifies* requirements for smaller ones. This is what the street is cheering as "net positive." But this capital relief belongs to large banks (JPM, BAC, C), not Cat IV regionals. For sub-$100B banks, the net effect of the full package is approximately neutral to modestly negative — and it's all 2027-2028 timeline at earliest.

**OZK detail:** 88.5% interest-bearing is the highest ratio in the group — OZK funds itself almost entirely with retail CDs and structured deposits, not DDA. ## 6. The "Modeled Relief ≠ Deployable Capital" Problem

Will's framing here is essential. Even for banks that benefit from the capital rewrite, the market's front-running is probably wrong on timing.

**The slow-burn reality:**
1. **Comment period:** Jun 18, 2026. Final rule takes 12–18 months post-comment.
2. **Effective date:** Earliest 2027–2028, more likely 2028–2029 given historical pace.
3. **Phase-in:** Additional multi-year transition for major changes.
4. **CCAR interaction:** Banks won't deploy modeled capital relief until CCAR stress tests incorporate the new framework. That's another 1–2 annual cycles after effective date.
5. **Discretionary buffer maintenance:** Even after rules finalize, management teams will hold excess capital above the new minimums. No CFO shrinks the buffer to zero on day one.

**Net result for timing:** Modeled capital relief from Proposal 1 (the positive for big banks) is a **2029–2030 story** for being fully deployable. The street is pricing it in Q1 2026.

**For our OZK/WAL thesis specifically:**
- The capital relief from Proposal 1 flows primarily to JPM, BAC, C, WFC — not to Cat IV regionals
- The capital drag from Proposal 2 (AOCI inclusion) arrives for Cat IV banks starting ~2028 and is modest for WAL (-55bps over 5 years)
- **Neither mechanism is relevant to Jun 2026 puts**
- The capital rewrite narrative is **neutral to slight negative** for OZK/WAL specifically (minor future drag on WAL, no relief for either)

The market's "banks are good because capital rules are loosening" narrative gives us **relative richness on the call side** and **cheap insurance on the put side** — the crowd is wrong about both the beneficiaries AND the timing.

## 8. Thesis Implications

### The Capital Rewrite Is a Wash for OZK/WAL
Bilateral noise: they don't benefit meaningfully from capital relief (wrong size) and they don't suffer meaningfully from AOCI inclusion (minimal exposure, borderline scope). The rule change is priced wrong by the street — it's good for large banks, not meaningful for these two — but that mispricing is a mild tailwind for our puts, not a direct catalyst.

### What Actually Drives the Bear Case

**OZK — the real story:**
- CRE construction book = 57%+ of loans ($18B+), primarily bridge/construction/transitional
- Vintage risk: 2021–2022 originations at cap rates that no longer work
- Credit quality: watch NCO acceleration, NPL builds, provision step-ups in 2026
- NIM fragility: 88.5% IBD means floating-rate liability structure; if short rates fall, asset repricing from floating CRE doesn't save NIM because loans will be paying down (not refinancing)
- AOCI: **Irrelevant.** -$25M, out of regulatory scope.

**WAL — the real story:**
- Rapid growth ($55B → $93B in 3 years) = compressing vintage quality, potential credit vintage risk
- Deposit concentration: fintech/startup ecosystem still matters; any credit cycle shock hits this cohort
- AmeriHome: mortgage origination volumes and MSR fair value tied to rate environment
- CRE + construction exposure growing alongside the balance sheet
- AOCI: **Minor future drag.** -$344M, approaches scope by 2027. Not a 2026 catalyst.

### Jun 18 Comment Deadline vs. Jun Put Expiry

These dates align superficially but are causally disconnected. The comment deadline does not trigger capital impacts; it merely closes the public input window. A final rule requires additional rulemaking steps with an 18–24 month timeline from here. Our Jun puts expire before the ink is dry on the final rule. There is NO capital mechanic that runs through Jun 2026.

The deadline *could* create sentiment pressure via:
- Media cycle coverage of regional bank capital concerns
- Industry comment letters quantifying aggregate AOCI exposure → focus on the dirty names (KEY, HBAN, RF) rather than OZK/WAL
- Broad "bank stocks risky" narrative bleeding into our names

Use that as an opportunity if puts cheapen on the "capital relief is good for banks" narrative — the sentiment story is wrong about OZK/WAL, and it creates asymmetric timing.
