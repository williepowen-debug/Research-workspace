## (a) The answer, in plain words

1. **No. The synergies from this deal are uncapped.** Clause (f) of "Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA" adds back run-rate cost savings and synergies "related to the Transactions" (the WBD deal and its financing). It has no percentage cap, no dollar cap, no time window, and no "reasonably identifiable and factually supportable" test. Amendment No. 1 did not change the definition (FILED; the April and October text is byte-identical after whitespace normalisation, INFERRED check).
2. **The 25% cap applies only to other deals.** Clause (g) covers synergies from *other* acquisitions. Those must be "reasonably identifiable, factually supportable" and expected within 24 months, and they are capped at 25% of EBITDA measured *after* the add-back, with costs to achieve outside the cap (FILED). The borrower determines the figures "in good faith", and they are "conclusive absent manifest error" (FILED).
3. **The covenant is a net figure and covers only the bank lenders.** It is the "Consolidated Total Net Leverage Ratio", ≤ 5.50x. Cash netting is uncapped: all unrestricted cash of the parent *and all subsidiaries* counts. The first-lien net ratio must stay ≤ 3.25x. First test: the quarter ending 12/31/2026 (INFERRED from "first full fiscal quarter ended after the Closing Date"). The covenant protects only the revolver and term A lenders. Term B lenders are hit only after those lenders accelerate (FILED, §5.14(d), §6.1(c)).
4. **What the uncapped clause is worth (INFERRED, inputs below).** Back-solving Debtwire's 4.51x gives covenant EBITDA of about $18.7B, of which about $12.7B is before synergies. On the repo's $86.6B face debt less an estimated $5.0B of cash:
   - all $6.0B of synergies credited: **4.37x**
   - if a 25%-after-add-back cap applied (it does not, for this deal): **4.82x**
   - realized synergies of $1.75B only: **5.65x**
   - no synergies: **6.43x**
5. **So what.** The 4.51x is the real covenant figure, and the borrower can hold it down by definition. Breaching 5.50x needs about $14.8B of covenant EBITDA (INFERRED). With $6.0B of synergies credited, that means pre-synergy EBITDA would have to fall about 30%, from about $12.7B to about $8.8B. Alternatively, the company would have to credit less than about $2.1B of synergies. As a stress gauge the covenant is weak. TERRY's "filed covenant leverage ≥ 5.00x" re-open condition can be met only by a large EBITDA miss, or by a debt or cash change. ⚠️ **The EBITDA inputs are back-solved from a secondary estimate (Debtwire 10/14, via LIQUID), not filed.** The company gave lenders a closing-date Compliance Certificate with its own TTM EBITDA and ratios (FILED, §5.1(g)). That certificate is not public.
