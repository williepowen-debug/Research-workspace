## 1. Episodes (8 selected; MicroStrategy/Strategy excluded, see 1.9)

### 1.1 Telecom vendor financing, 1999–2001 (Lucent; Nortel; Cisco not researched)
- **Trigger:** Lucent Jan 6 2000 flat revenue, stock -28%, ~$64B market value erased (VERIFIED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent). CLEC credit market closed through H1 2001.
- **Insider/affiliate edge:** the vendor itself lent to customers. Lucent commitments to extend credit: ~$8.4B (Dec 31 1999), ~$7.2B (Mar 31 2000), ~$6.7B (Sep 30 2000, of which ~$1.3B outstanding, ~$3.3B undrawn), ~$5.7B (Dec 31 2000), plus ~$1.4B customer-debt guarantees at Sep 2000 (VERIFIED via SEC 10-K405/10-K/A FY2000 surfaced by search: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095012300011855/y43690e10-k405.txt). Loans+guarantees $5.5B at Jun 30 2001 (VERIFIED, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095011701500911/a31155.txt).
- **Issuer outcome:** customer Winstar fully reserved on bankruptcy (Lucent Q2 FY2001 ~$700M provision); One.Tel insolvent (~$700M reserve on two projects, Q3 FY2001); customer-financing/uncollectible provisions $1,950M for 9 months to Jun 2001; three financings were ~60% of FY2001 expense (VERIFIED, same SEC links, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095011701501896/a31752.txt). Customers: default inside 12 months of the stress.
- **Funder outcome:** Lucent peak $84, mkt cap $258B; trough $0.55 Oct 2002; headcount ~165,000 -> 30,500; merged into Alcatel Dec 1 2006 (VERIFIED, Wikipedia above). Nortel mkt cap C$398B (Sep 2000) -> <C$5B (Aug 2002), ~$16B write-downs in 2001, ~US$3B revenue booked improperly 1998–2000, Chapter 11 Jan 14 2009 (VERIFIED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nortel). Nortel vendor-financing exposure figure: SEARCH-NOT-FOUND.
- **Marked bottom?** No (funder equity fell another ~99%).

### 1.2 Enron related-party vehicles, 1999–Dec 2001
- **Trigger:** Skilling resigns Aug 14 2001; restatement Oct 16 2001 (earnings -$613M, equity -$1.2B); SEC probe Oct 22; stock $20.65 (VERIFIED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enron_scandal).
- **Insider edge:** LJM1/LJM2 run by CFO Fastow, funded ~$390M from banks/investors + $22M Merrill; Fastow took ~$30M; Raptors held >$1.2B of assets including Enron common stock (VERIFIED, Wikipedia). Powers Committee: vehicles let Enron transact with an entity it could not use at arm's length; counterparty to hedges was effectively Enron itself; earnings inflated by almost $1B Q3 2000–Q3 2001 (VERIFIED via Enron 8-K surfaced: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001024401/000090951802000089/big.txt). Late "rescue": Dynegy $1B (Nov 2) / $1.5B injection with merger agreement (VERIFIED, Wikipedia).
- **Issuer outcome:** junk Nov 28, Dynegy walked Nov 28, Chapter 11 Dec 2 2001 (VERIFIED). Restatement-to-bankruptcy = 47 days.
- **Funder outcome:** the insider vehicles' principal (Fastow) was removed Oct 25 2001 (VERIFIED); his later conviction/plea is INFERRED (background). Dynegy lost the merger; its own later Chapter 11 (2011) INFERRED.
- **Marked bottom?** No. Note: in this episode "insider capital" was an accounting device, not new outside cash.

### 1.3 Sponsor support of SIVs, Aug–Dec 2007 (Citi, HSBC)
- **Trigger:** CP market near-illiquid early Sep 2007; Moody's review of Citi SIVs (VERIFIED, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_investment_vehicle and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000831001/000114420407067635/v097312_ex99-1.htm).
- **Insider edge:** HSBC consolidated Cullinan + Asscher, ~$45B of assets, Nov 26 2007 (VERIFIED, https://www.hsbc.com/-/files/hsbc/investors/results-and-announcements/stock-exchange-announcements/2007/november/sea-071126-sivs.pdf). Citi consolidated seven SIVs, ~$49B of assets (down from $87B Aug 2007), Dec 2007; Citi said "no direct funding requirement" while Risk.net reported ~$35B of funding still needed (VERIFIED, https://www.risk.net/derivatives/structured-products/1519933/citi-takes-49bn-siv-debt-balance-sheet).
- **Issuer outcome (SIVs):** Cullinan liquidated 2008; Victoria Finance defaulted Jan 14 2008; Standard Chartered withdrew from Whistlejacket Feb 11 2008 (receiver); Sigma, last SIV, collapsed Oct 2 2008 (VERIFIED, Wikipedia SIV page). Sponsored-and-consolidated SIVs avoided a sudden default but were absorbed, not rescued.
- **Funder outcome:** Citi agreed to acquire SIV assets for $17.4B (Bloomberg headline 19 Nov 2008, via Wikipedia; VERIFIED as a headline only). Citi's later TARP/guarantee support: INFERRED (background), not retrieved. HSBC survived (INFERRED).
- **Marked bottom?** No: support came at the start of 2008's losses.

- **Marked bottom?** No — the "rescue" was at ~$8B-class valuation and was later wiped.

Larry Ellison, Alwaleed, a16z, Sequoia, Qatar Holding; Musk cut his Tesla-backed margin loan from $12.5B to $6.25B and put in ~$20B cash (VERIFIED, Wikipedia). - **Issuer outcome:** no default. ## 3. Base rate and its limits

Coding: "insider capital preceded default/restructuring within 24m" = Y if the capital-seeking entity (or its vehicles) defaulted, was liquidated or wound down inside 24 months of the support event.

|---|---|---|

| Enron | Y (47 days) | N |

| Adani | N | Y (GQG, a non-affiliate) |
| Evergrande | Y | N |

- **Default/restructure within 24m: 4 of 8 (50%); 5 of 8 if WeWork is counted at 49m.** Wilson 95% interval for 4/8: ~22%–78% (INFERRED arithmetic). Marked the bottom: **1 of 8 (12.5%), and 0 of 7 if "insider" means affiliated capital only** (GQG was an unaffiliated buyer).
- **Selection bias (large):** all eight made headlines, i.e. they were chosen on outcome visibility. Episodes where affiliate support quietly worked (sponsor rights offerings, parent-guaranteed bonds that were repaid) do not make news and are absent. Failures are over-represented; 50% is NOT an estimate of the population rate. Also: the episodes mix four different things (vendor lender, related-party vehicle, sponsor support, promoter/owner injection), several of which are not "voluntary new money" at all (Enron; Adani promoters were sellers).
- **Neutral denominator (not built here):** all issuers that lost market access in a defined window (e.g. a pulled/failed offering, bonds yielding above ~1000 bp over Treasuries, or CDS >1000 bp) in a past cycle, split into those that received insider/affiliate capital within 90 days and those that did not, with 24-month default as the outcome. Comparison to the Moody's/S&P speculative-grade 2-year default rate by rating cohort is the control. I did not retrieve those default studies: SEARCH-NOT-FOUND.
- **Honest reading:** conditional on being newsworthy, insider support was a coin flip for survival, and almost never the bottom for the insider. The only clear "bottom" was a third party buying from a forced seller.

## SUMMARY FOR PROME
- **Base rate:** 4 of 8 hand-picked episodes (50%; Wilson 95% interval ~22–78%) saw the capital-seeking entity default, liquidate or wind down within 24 months of insider support; 5 of 8 counting WeWork at 49 months. Insider capital marked the bottom in 1 of 8 (GQG-Adani), 0 of 7 for affiliated capital only.
- **Denominator caveat:** all 8 made news, so failures are over-represented; 50% is not a population rate. A neutral denominator = all issuers that lost market access in a window (pulled deal, CDS/bond spread >1000 bp), split by insider capital within 90 days, versus Moody's/S&P 2-year default by rating cohort. I did not retrieve those studies.
- **Two best tells:** (1) Circular collateral: the funder's capacity depends on the same asset the issuer sells or pledges (Enron stock in the Raptors, Lucent selling gear to customers it lent to, SIV sponsors' own ratings, SoftBank's Arm/OpenAI pledges). (2) Who sets the price: capital priced by an unaffiliated buyer after a drawdown (GQG, +36% in about 5 months) separated "opportunity" from capital dictated by the funder (SoftBank-WeWork) or state-directed (Evergrande ~0.3% of liabilities).
- **Today matches:** Ellison liquidity includes Oracle stock (~1.16B shares reported) while Oracle has record CDS (~288–308 bp, Oct 15–21 2026); CoreWeave CDS record ~1,194.5 bp (Oct 15) with Nvidia as supplier/buyer/equity holder; Paramount's S&P BBB- first-lien rating rests on an Ellison deleveraging pledge; SoftBank is borrowing against Arm and OpenAI stakes (Arm loan $25B, bond coupon 8.5%).
- **Today does not match:** Oracle is investment grade with an operating business; Paramount's HY and loan books were oversubscribed (orders ~$15.6B and $11.5B); ~$24B of Paramount equity is from Gulf SWFs (non-voting), not family only; no restatement or off-book related-party vehicle reported.
- **Premise check for PROME:** I found no source placing Musk or SoftBank as Paramount/WBD equity backers; found Gulf SWFs (PIF, QIA, L'imad), LionTree and Kushner's Affinity. Treat the Musk/SoftBank link as unsupported until sourced.
- **Could not reach:** Cisco and Nortel vendor-financing totals; Chinese cross-guarantee mechanics; Icahn pledged units and 2023 new-money amount; Ellison's exact Twitter cheque and the equity friends' mark path; primary SEC filings; CNBC Oct 2026 pages (HTTP 403); Moody's/S&P default-study denominators; Paramount close confirmation; SoftBank current LTV.
- **Method warning:** search summaries are second-hand; reported figures conflicted (e.g. Oracle CDS 75 / 144–215 / 288–315 bp across sources; Twitter bank loss $0.5B–$2B), shown above as ranges.
