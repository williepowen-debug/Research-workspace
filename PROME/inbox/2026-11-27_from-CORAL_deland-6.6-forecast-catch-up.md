**Plain answer for Will.** At 6:30 pm Friday the St. Johns River at DeLand stood at 6.25 ft, 0.08 ft below its 2022 record (6.33 ft). It has not set a record yet. The official forecast, issued 8:35 am Friday, has it at 6.4 ft Saturday, 6.5 ft Sunday and 6.6 ft from Tuesday morning (12/01). It is still at 6.6 ft at the forecast's last point, Friday 12/04, so nobody can yet say when it starts to fall. Upstream, Sanford is at 8.89 ft, 0.07 ft under its own record (8.96), with a forecast of 8.9. Astor is at 4.43 ft. Cocoa is falling slowly from the record it set 11/19 to 11/21. The flooded area is still mostly single-family and manufactured homes, marinas and fish camps, in Volusia, Seminole and Lake. None of those three counties is in the federal disaster declaration, and few of those homes have flood insurance. **I have to correct my 11/25 report:** the weather service's own Astor table names a condominium and an apartment complex on Juno Trail. The river has already passed the levels at which their yards, docks and parking lot flood. It does not say water enters the buildings, and I found no report that it has. **The one thing that would change the picture:** water entering a named apartment or condo building, or Washington adding household aid for Volusia, Seminole or Lake. Either would turn this from a hardship for uninsured households into something that reaches condo associations' finances and federal money. Sanford's first named apartments flood at 9.5 to 10.0 ft against an 8.9 ft forecast. AEOLUS puts the chance of reaching 9.5 at about 5%, unscored.
