1. **United Fidelity probably had a special FDIC permission to keep using brokered deposits after its 2023 order, and lost it on October 30.** Nobody publishes that permission, so this is a strong inference from its own regulatory reports, not a document. If it is right, October 30 started a new funding squeeze: about $1.0B of brokered money cannot be renewed (half of it matures within a year), and perhaps $0.7B more held through a deposit-sharing network.
2. **Its regulator's clock:** a capital plan is due **Monday December 14**. If none is filed, or the plan is rejected, the bank falls under the rules for "significantly undercapitalized" banks, which let the regulator order a sale or a recapitalization. Dividends, management fees and growth are already barred. Nothing new is public from the OCC since October 13; its next monthly release is around **Thursday November 19**.
3. **Its holding company's bondholders stand behind a possibly larger claim.** To get the plan accepted, the owners must guarantee it, up to about $296M. In a bankruptcy that guarantee would likely rank ahead of the roughly $114M of outside notes (my reading of the law; I did not open the statute today). The two listed funds that hold the paper have under 1% of their assets in it.
4. **None of my 14 banks is in this position**, so there is no read-across to them.
5. **Home Loan Bank borrowing is high but not a stress sign.** Four big, healthy borrowers explain over half of this year's $127B rise: PNC, Citigroup, Wells Fargo and Apollo's insurer Athene. The typical smaller borrower did not borrow more. The third-quarter dip was one district, Cincinnati, where U.S. Bancorp repaid at least $15B. No escalation, and **no trade idea from either item.**
