## C. Investment implication (the WQ-696 read)

- **What the evidence implies:** the losses stay with private credit funds and are spread thin across listed vehicles. Cable One's default would transmit a mark-to-market loss to listed closed-end funds and two listed BDCs, each at 0.65% of net assets or less. Much of it is already marked: the 2030 notes at 37 cents in July, the MBI loan at 49 cents in September. For the listed banks it would be a credit loss of at most 0.32% of equity at a total loss, and about 0.1% on a realistic first-lien recovery (INFERRED). Car-Mart's losses fall on private Silver Point and Beach Point funds. **No listed company carries a loss large enough to support a trade.**
- **Horizon:** Cable One's grace period ends about 12/16 (inferred). Car-Mart's standstill ends 11/18. Quarter-end marks fall at 12/31, with BDC 10-Ks in late February to early March.
- **What would invalidate this:** a filing showing a larger holder; a listed company exposed through a guarantee or a financing arrangement not seen here (for example a bank warehouse line to a Silver Point fund named alongside Car-Mart); or a listed insurer's Schedule D showing a large position.
- **What remains unknown:** insurer and CLO holdings; current per-bank revolver amounts; holdings after 7/31 for the funds (and after 6/30 for RA and the BlackRock funds); the investors behind the private lenders; the current price of the 2030 notes (no free quote).
