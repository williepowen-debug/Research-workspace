**What it covers, as the press relays FEMA (one secondary text, not the primary):**
- **Help for households** (FEMA's Individual Assistance: grants for temporary housing and repairs, and the low-interest federal disaster loans that come with it) in **six counties: Brevard, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach, St. Lucie.**
- **Help for local governments** (Public Assistance, debris removal and emergency protective measures only, no permanent rebuilding named) in **Brevard, Hardee, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach.**
- Incident period: "severe storms and flooding" beginning **October 31, 2026**, continuing. Hazard mitigation money: not mentioned. Disaster number: not yet known.
- **Not covered, as reported:** the St. Johns River counties now at record or major flood (Seminole, Volusia, Lake, Orange) and Broward, which took the 11/17 to 11/19 rain. Those were in a second, broader request filed 11/24 that is still pending. Because the incident period is "continuing", they can be added by amendment; the Hurricane Isaias declaration was amended within two days (10/27 to 10/29).

**What it changes, if the reported text holds:**
- **Households in the six counties** can get FEMA grants of up to **$46,400 for housing repair and up to $46,400 for other needs** per household (FEMA's 11/06/2026 notice), but only for losses insurance does not cover, and the cap is not what a typical household receives. They can also borrow from the SBA up to **$500,000 to repair a home and $100,000 for belongings** (federal rule 13 CFR 123.105); that is debt, not a grant. A household in a mapped flood zone that takes repair money must then carry flood insurance on that address, and the duty passes to the next owner.
- **Mortgages:** FHA's 90-day foreclosure pause applies in a declared disaster area. HUD's past practice ties it to the counties with household aid. VA asks lenders to pause but does not order it. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac let servicers grant disaster forbearance; the declaration matters only for borrowers in the household-aid counties who have not been reached (Fannie's first three months without contact). A borrower whose own insurance claim was paid already qualified at Freddie. So the declaration delays some foreclosures in six counties and leaves the St. Johns counties where they were.
- **Insurers:** nothing changes. Home policies, Citizens' included, do not cover rising water, and federal flood-policy claims are paid by contract with or without a declaration.
- **Local governments:** FEMA repays at least **75%** of eligible debris and emergency costs, after the fact and after review. That is permission, not money paid; counties front the cash. The only local spending action found is Palm Beach County's **$2 million** homeowner flood-relief program, **postponed 7 to 0 to December 1** (CBS12, 11/17). No county borrowing or fee action found.

**What it does not establish:** how much of the loss is uninsured (still a HYPOTHESIS; household aid tests it later, see below), any money actually paid, any change in home values, any loss at any bank, insurer or property trust, or anything about Seminole and Volusia.
