**Bottom line.** A river flood of record lowers the price of the homes that actually flooded, by a lot at first and for years where buyers learn something new about risk; it barely moves a county or metro index. Florida's November floods land on counties that were already flat to slightly down while the nation rose about 1%, at a 7.28% mortgage rate, with home insurance near three times the national average and the condo market already split by the post-Surfside law. The housing-finance channel matters more than the price channel this time: **with no federal declaration, the automatic borrower protections (FHA's 90-day foreclosure stop, Fannie Mae's disaster forbearance) are not switched on**, and many flooded homes sit outside the mapped flood zone, where few owners carry flood insurance. Expect a local volume drop and a delinquency bump in the flooded ZIPs over the next two quarters, not a statewide price break caused by the flood.

## 1. What the evidence says a flood does to home values

| Study | Setting | Effect (as the paper states it) | How long | Limit | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ortega and Taşpınar 2018**, J. Urban Econ. 106 | NYC sales 2003–2017, Sandy 2012 | **Damaged homes −17 to −22%** right after, partly recovered; **undamaged flood-zone homes −8% by 2017** | Penalty on undamaged zone homes still there ~5 years on | Authors say part may be the 2012–13 flood-insurance repricing (Biggert-Waters), not belief alone | VERIFIED |
| **Gibson and Mullins** (IZA DP 13553, 2020; later JAERE) | NYC, Sandy and Biggert-Waters | Flooded homes −4.5% (minimal) to −5 to −7% (average depth); **unflooded homes newly mapped into the floodplain −11%**; premium increases −3 to −5%, imprecise | n/a | Working-paper version read | VERIFIED |
| **Bin and Landry 2013**, JEEM 65(3) | Pitt County NC, Fran 1996 and Floyd 1999 | No floodplain discount before Fran; **−5.7% after Fran, −8.8% after Floyd** | **Gone about 5–6 years after Floyd** | One county; abstract | VERIFIED (abstract) |
| **Atreya, Ferreira and Kriesel 2013**, Land Econ. 89(4) | Dougherty County GA, 1994 flood | Floodplain prices "fell significantly" (size not seen) | **Gone 4–9 years after** | One county | duration VERIFIED, size NOT FOUND |
| **Beltrán, Maddison and Elliott 2018**, Ecological Economics 146 (meta-analysis, 37 studies, 364 estimates) | US/intl | Inland 100-year floodplain **−4.6%**; inland 100-year **−2.9% before a flood → −6.9% after**; **inland 500-year zone +0.3% → −5.2% after a flood** | Post-flood studies average 2.5 years after the event | No reliable inference from coastal studies (amenity confounding) | VERIFIED |
| **Hino and Burke 2021**, PNAS 118(17) | All US sales, two decades | National floodplain discount **−2.1%** (CI −4.2 to +0.1); **−4.1% in states with all three disclosure rules**; floodplain homes **overvalued $43.8B** at 5% | Not tracked | Disclosure result significant only without Florida in the comparison group | VERIFIED |
| **Gourevitch et al. 2023**, Nature Climate Change 13 | US flood-exposed homes | Overvalued **$121–237B**; worst in coastal counties without disclosure | n/a | Florida ">$50B" from an EDF press release only | range VERIFIED, FL SECONDARY |
| **Keys and Mulder 2020**, NBER WP 27930 | Florida coast most exposed to sea-level rise | 2013–18 **sales volume −16 to −20%** vs less-exposed areas while prices kept pace; 2018–20 relative **prices ~−5% from peak** | **Volume first, prices ~5 years later** | Working paper | VERIFIED (abstract) |
| **Kousky, Palim and Pan 2020**, J. Housing Research 29(S1) | Harvey 2017, Fannie Mae loans | Damage → more 90-day delinquency; where insurance was required, damaged loans **prepaid** (claims paid off); where it was not required and few held it, damage → **more modifications and 180+ day delinquency or default over 2 years** | 2 years | Point estimates not read (Fannie PDF 403) | direction VERIFIED |

**Insured vs uninsured, mapped vs outside the map.** Homes that **actually flooded** (Ortega–Taşpınar, Gibson–Mullins) take a large first hit, −5% to −22% depending on depth and damage, and partly recover. Homes merely **in a mapped zone** carry a few-percent discount that appears after nearby floods and fades in 4–9 years in three of four tracked cases (NYC's had not faded by 2017). **Outside the map** is where a river flood of record bites: Beltrán's 500-year zone goes from no discount to −5.2% after a flood; 63% of Sandy-flooded NYC properties were outside the 1% floodplain (Gibson–Mullins); 73.8% of Harvey's Fannie loans were outside it with no policy, and the uninsured damaged loans did worst on credit (Kousky–Palim–Pan). **The insured damaged home repairs or prepays; the uninsured damaged home becomes a delinquency and, later, a discounted sale.** Risk Rating 2.0: the 18% cap spreads the premium shock over years; the only price-capitalization estimate found (an unopened thesis, −0.70% of value per +$100 a year outside the map) is SECONDARY, and **no study measures Risk Rating 2.0's effect on prices.** Hino–Burke report 16% of Florida single-family homes inside the mapped floodplain vs 3.6% nationally (study figure, not a HOMER series). **Gap:** no study I could open measures sale prices of uninsured flooded homes outside the map directly. ## 3. Housing finance: what the agencies require after a disaster (national rule; Florida counts are CORAL's)

| Rule | What it does | Switched on by | Source (version) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FHA foreclosure moratorium | 90 days, no new foreclosures, in-process ones paused; credit reporting suspended for performing borrowers; formal forbearance up to repair time or 12 months of arrears | **Presidentially declared major disaster only** ("automatic" per ML 2025-07) | HUD PDMDA fact sheet 10/18/2023; ML 2025-07 (03/06/2025) — VERIFIED |
| FHA pre-endorsement due diligence | Mandatory inspections rescinded; lender "reasonable due diligence"; repairs before endorsement unless habitable with escrow | Declared area with Individual Assistance | ML 2025-19 (06/27/2025) — VERIFIED |
| FHA 203(h) disaster loans | no-down-payment loan for a destroyed or badly damaged primary home | Presidential declaration | HUD page — SECONDARY |
| Fannie disaster forbearance | up to 3 months without contact milestones, 12-month cap | **FEMA-declared area eligible for Individual Assistance** | Servicing Guide D2-3.2-01 (04/08/2026) — VERIFIED |
| Fannie property-condition check before delivery | lender must decide whether condition changed; inspection or new appraisal as needed; uninsured or structural damage repaired first | **Any disaster, no declaration needed** | Selling Guide B2-3-05 (09/03/2025) — VERIFIED |
| Fannie servicer damage assessment | assess damage "whether or not it is in a FEMA-declared area" | Any disaster | Servicing Guide D1-3-01 — VERIFIED |
| Freddie counterparts (Guide 8404 etc.), VA | not read (JS-rendered site; no 2026 VA circular found) | — | NOT FOUND |

**Today's state:** OpenFEMA at 21:47Z 11/23 lists no Florida declaration after DR-4955 (Hurricane Isaias, 10/27, Panhandle, Individual Assistance **false**). So: **origination-side checks are on (any flooded property needs a condition check before a GSE sale closes, which slows closings in the basin); borrower-side automatic relief is off** until a declaration with Individual Assistance. Flooded borrowers have ordinary hardship options only. A declaration would date FHA's 90 days from the declaration, not from the flood.

**Condo eligibility (the post-Surfside interaction).** Fannie Selling Guide B4-2.1-03 (version 11/04/2026, VERIFIED): a project is ineligible for "any unfunded repairs costing more than $10,000 per unit that should be undertaken within the next 12 months"; a special assessment tied to an unremediated critical repair, an evacuation order for unsafe conditions, a structural inspection within 3 years showing critical repairs, or **failing a mandatory state or local inspection** each make it ineligible. Freddie's FAQ (5701.3) matches in substance (failed mandatory inspection = ineligible; safety assessment needs repairs fully completed), without a $10,000 figure. Limited/streamlined reviews ended for applications from 8/03/2026 (my 8/12 grade; Fannie LL-2026-03 per secondaries). ⚠️ **The 15% reserve rule effective 1/04/2027 is SECONDARY only** (Fannie primary 403; Freddie's FAQ still says 10%); do not cite it as confirmed. Florida law (VERIFIED at the 2026 statutes): milestone inspections at 30 years for buildings of 3+ stories (Fla. Stat. 553.899), structural integrity reserve studies by 12/31/2025 with no waiver of reserves for budgets adopted from 12/31/2024 (718.112(2)(g)). **The interaction:** a building that fails its milestone inspection or carries an unfunded critical repair above $10,000 a unit is out of GSE financing, so its units sell for cash or non-QM; the flood adds repair bills to ground-floor and garage structures in the basin, but the basin is mostly single-family, so the condo channel stays driven by the law, not the water. **Share of Florida buildings affected:** only SECONDARY counts, not comparable (1,438 FL buildings on Fannie's unavailable list, April 2025 press; ~28–35% of the national list in other reports). ## 4. Insurance to affordability (arithmetic is mine; premium levels are CORAL's)

PMMS 7.28% (Freddie, week of 11/19). A 30-year loan costs $6.842 a month per $1,000 borrowed. Example: $360,000 loan (a $400,000 home, 10% down), principal and interest $2,463 a month.

| Annual premium increase | Monthly payment | Share of P&I | Extra income needed at a 43% debt-to-income cap | Loan the same buyer can carry falls by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +$1,000 | +$83 | 3.4% | +$2,326 a year | $12,179 |
| +$2,500 | +$208 | 8.5% | +$5,814 | $30,449 |
| +$5,000 | +$417 | 16.9% | +$11,628 | $60,897 |

**Read:** each $1,000 a year of premium does to a buyer roughly what a $12,000 price increase does, which is about 3% of a $400,000 home. Fully capitalized, a permanent $1,000 premium increase is worth about $13,700 of value at a 7.28% discount rate. Premium levels: Florida about three times the national average in 2026 consumer-site estimates (Insurance.com $8,471 vs $2,872; Insurify $8,292) — **SECONDARY and assumption-dependent; CORAL owns the Florida premium figure.** Florida holds 1,767,045 NFIP policies, 38.9% of the national total (NAIC deck citing NFIP, April 2026 data, VERIFIED in the PDF).
