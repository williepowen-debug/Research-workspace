It is one shock, in oil, and it has not transmitted — and on VIOLET's evidence it has not been transmitting for three and a half months. Brent sat at $113–117 for a week with oil volatility near the top of its range, while equity vol fell through the cluster (VIX 18.39 → 16.91, 10/26–10/28; ⚠️ 10/29 PROVISIONAL 17.20 on Cboe's 16:15 ET delayed quote, up on the day — HENRY's 16.50 was a 16:06 ET vendor pull; the archive final supersedes both), the S&P rose, and high-yield spreads tightened every day from 10/22 to 10/28 (340 → 314 bp). The only equity footprint of "energy" was the opposite sign to a cost shock: refiners fell hard on 10/27 (VLO −9.2%, MPC −7.4%, PSX −7.0%) while airlines rallied, which is what falling diesel looks like. The oil-vol canary that fired on 10/28 has fired on 28 days since July; across that whole stretch VIX stayed between 14 and 21 and the S&P rose 3%. The gas and power legs were real but brief: a touch, not a settle, and a fifteen-minute spike. Working read, not truth: an oil market carrying a high, persistent volatility premium that the rest of the market has been looking through since summer. The one dated test still ahead is tomorrow's credit cells.
