## Comparison design

| ETF | Comparison and limitation |
|---|---|
| KRE | Regional banks, modified equal weighting; changing constituents and weights over history |
| KBWB | Banking index with modified capitalization weighting, tilted more toward larger banks; NOT a pure large-bank-only control, overlaps KRE |
| XLF | Broad financials: banks plus insurance, capital markets and other financial businesses; not a bank substitute |
| IWM | Small-cap equity comparison; contains banks, so not independent of KRE |
| SPY / RSP | Capitalization-weighted vs equal-weight S&P 500; breadth/size/sector composition all differ |
| QQQ | Nasdaq-100, technology-heavy but not a pure technology-sector fund |
| HYG / LQD | High-yield / investment-grade corporate bonds; returns combine rates, spreads, income and other effects |
| IEF / TLT / SHY | Intermediate / long / short Treasury ETFs; different durations, not literal yield-curve spreads |
| VNQ | Listed real-estate equities; not a direct measure of banks' CRE loan losses or property appraisals |

## What would strengthen or weaken each explanation

No new thresholds or standing assignment are created.

- Regional-bank-specific improvement: sustained KRE outperformance of BOTH KBWB and IWM, alongside bank-specific deposit, funding and credit evidence. Price ratios alone cannot supply that evidence.
- Broad rotation/rate relief: continued RSP-versus-QQQ strength, Treasury price strength and participation from property/small-cap equities. These can benefit bank valuations without resolving loan recoveries.
- Bank stress re-emerging: KRE loses relative ground to KBWB and IWM while credit spreads widen and owner evidence identifies funding pressure or realized loan deterioration.
- General market stress: banks, small caps and credit weaken together; Treasury direction depends on whether growth fears or inflation/rate pressure dominates.

**PROME judgment:** bullish evidence for near-term bank prices remains real, but the cross-asset evidence is mixed. It supports a plausible explanation for the rally without requiring a clean bank-credit bill of health. Most useful ongoing comparisons are KRE/KBWB, KRE/IWM, RSP/QQQ, actual Treasury yields/curve, and actual credit spreads. Existing owners retain their domains; no automatic tracker build, new prediction, trade proposal or grade follows.
