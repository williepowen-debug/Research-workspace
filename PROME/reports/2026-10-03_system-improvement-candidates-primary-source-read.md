## Appendix A — Opus answer, verbatim
# One addition: put the low tail on hold until its problem happens again

**The addition:** a new disposition in §4 (b), called **DORMANT ON RECURRENCE**. At the WQ-379 ruling, ranks 19–29 come off the queue: L523 · L361 · L431 · L485 · L486 · L459 · L423 · L561 · L504 · L440. L366 stays ranked because its residue includes a "silent fail-open branch." Each dormant row gets one cell naming its **re-arm symptom**: how the defect would show itself if it came back. For L523 that would be "a Deck Decided entry ends mid-sentence." If the symptom turns up in an ARGUS ledger, a reader report or a closeout, the row comes back at its old rank with the new evidence. If it never does, the row is tombstoned at the 10/25 review.

**Why it adds something.** §5 only works on the drain side. It shows inflow beating drain (21 overdue rows on 9/25, 26 now, about 0.9 changes a day) and answers with more capacity, the DAY unit. Criterion 5 sweeps residue ledgers but never asks whether a hardening row has earned its slot. Dormancy turns the low tail into a set of watches. A defect that never comes back costs nothing. One that does comes back with the proof of consequence that criterion 1 asks for. The cut takes 29 slots down to 19, so the realised clearing time drops from about five weeks to about three without touching the ceiling. The 10/25 review also gets a measurement: how many dormant rows re-armed.

**Cost.** Ten one-line symptom cells at the WQ-379 sitting, plus one sentence in the ARGUS prompt telling it to check findings against dormant re-arm symptoms. It builds on an existing control (WQ-229), so it uses none of R1's ceiling.

**What could make it wrong.**
1. **Defects that are silent by nature.** A defect that fails open produces no symptom, so dormancy would bury it for good. Criterion 2 has to *exclude* a row from dormancy, not just rank it higher.
2. **Matching on wording** (`finding_scan_keyed_on_naming_reads_local_form_as_absence`). A recurrence described in other words looks like no recurrence. The cell should name what to observe, not a phrase to search for.
3. **Conflict with how DOCKET treats overdue rows.** §4 says reclassified rows "are not waived." Dormancy is a conditional waiver, and Will has to rule it as one.

## Appendix B — Sonnet answer, verbatim
# Addition: a "burn-down receipt" row in section 5 (the DAY unit), a pre-registered kill test for the backlog itself

**The addition.** Before Will rules WQ-379(c), add to section 5 a pre-registered falsifier: a per-slot "consequence receipt". For each ranked slot, record one line BEFORE it is worked: the specific observed failure it prevents (e.g. L580: "six Deck taps sat 3.5 h unconsumed 10/1"), plus one measurable "did it recur" check dated 14 days after the slot closes. At the 10/25 review (L445), each completed slot is scored RECURRED / NOT-RECURRED / NOT-TESTABLE. Slots scoring NOT-TESTABLE are demoted to tombstone candidates at the next review. The DAY authorization in (c) becomes conditional: ranks 1-5 are done on the DAY, but ranks 6-29 stay locked until the receipts from ranks 1-5 show at least a stated share NOT-RECURRED.

**Why it is accretive.** The plan ranks by criteria in section 1 (consequence, fail-open guards, encode owed, size, sweep), but every criterion is judged at ranking time by the author. Nothing in sections 2-5 measures whether a repair worked. The plan's own evidence is the problem: the section 5 arithmetic shows the backlog grew while 7 process changes were used, and section 2's L361 and L370/L450 are repeat-defect rows, which means repairs have not been closing classes. Section 4's tombstones prune the backlog by supersession only. This adds the missing feedback edge: the backlog is pruned by outcome, so the 29-slot, five-week queue shrinks if the process is not paying off, and the 10/25 review gets a measured unit (the DAY vs sitting question left open in section 6) rather than PROME's unreplicated tallies (section 7).

**Cost.** Roughly 30 minutes now to write 5 receipt lines for ranks 1-5, then about one line per later slot. The recurrence checks are mostly greps over ORCH_LOG and DOCKET at the review. It adds no tool, so it should not itself consume an R1 process-change slot (the plan should say so, since the R1 reading is Will's).

**What could make it wrong.**
- Many of these defects are rare or dangerous-direction (L601 withheld state passing silently, L370 fail-open guards); "did not recur in 14 days" may reflect rarity, not a fix, so a NOT-RECURRED score would be falsely comforting. Counter: allow a seeded counterexample test as the check where recurrence is unobservable.
- Gating ranks 6-29 on receipts could stall repairs whose consequence is real but slow, replacing a ceiling with a second bureaucracy (the paperwork reflex PROME/CLAUDE.md warns about).
- The author scores their own receipts; without an independent reader, this is another self-certified control.
