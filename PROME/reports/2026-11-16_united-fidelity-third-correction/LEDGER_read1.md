## Verdict

The rule readings are substantively right at the primary text: Part 6 does cover a federal savings bank (6.1(c), 6.2), the deemed-notice date is the date the Call Report "is required to be filed" (6.3(b)(1)), the Q3 2026 report was due Friday 2026-10-30 (30 calendar days after 9/30), the 337.6 category keys on that same Part 6 notice date (337.6(a)(3)(iii)), an undercapitalized bank may not accept, renew or roll over any brokered deposit with no waiver (337.6(b)(3), (c)), and the plan date of Monday 2026-12-14 computes correctly from 6.5(a)(1). But the corrected brokered-deposit sentence is **safe only with a wording change**. One finding is BLOCKING: the bank's own consent order (Art. IV(3), already in this folder's report B) has barred it from being "well capitalized" since Oct 2023, so it has been at most adequately capitalized for three years and has needed an FDIC waiver to roll ANY brokered deposit since then. The correction deletes the waiver UNKNOWN as if settled; it is settled for today, but whether the bank HAD a waiver before 10/30 is the unknown that decides whether 10/30 starts a new funding bar or merely continues a runoff already in force (brokered deposits have fallen every quarter from $2,525.9M at 12/2023 to $1,006.5M at 9/2026, per report F). Seven further findings are NON-BLOCKING: a cite to a paragraph that does not exist (6.4(e)), an exit route ("unless the filing is amended") the rule text does not contain, an OCC "change the category" route that can only move a bank down, the waiver cite pointing at (b)(2) instead of (c), CET1 missing from the "one measure only" list, unstated conditions on the 10/30 date and the "cannot be rolled" scope, and a Review-state sentence that says a result "is recorded" before it existed.

**Counts: 1 BLOCKING, 7 NON-BLOCKING.**

## Counterexamples of my own

1. **(B1) The bar may not be new.** The correction reads as "on 10/30 the bank lost the ability to roll brokered deposits." Under 6.4(b)(1)(i)(E) and consent order Art. IV(3), the bank has been ineligible for "well capitalized" since Oct 2023, so 337.6(b)(2) has applied since then: no brokered deposit could be accepted, renewed or rolled over without an FDIC waiver. The steady fall in brokered deposits from $2,525.9M (12/2023) to $1,006.5M (9/2026) fits a no-waiver runoff as well as a managed reduction. If there was no waiver, the funding constraint the bullet presents as the "live" one has been in force for three years and 10/30 changed nothing for brokered funding. The earlier text's "any FDIC waiver UNKNOWN" was pointing at exactly this; the correction removed it.
2. **(N1) A phantom paragraph and a one-way door.** 6.4(e) does not exist in the current rule; the rule's own 6.3(b)(3) still cross-references it, which is presumably how it entered REGINALD's reading. The real reclassification paragraph, 6.4(d), only treats a bank "as if [it] were in the next lower capital category", and 6.3(c) only covers events that would place the bank "in a lower capital category". So "unless ... the OCC changes the category" lists routes that cannot relieve the bank; the only way out of undercapitalized is better ratios recognised at a later notice event.
3. **(N6, unchecked adjacent rule) The bank's own CD rates.** 337.6(a)(5)(vi): the term deposit broker "includes any insured depository institution that is not well capitalized ... which engages ... in the solicitation of deposits by offering rates of interest ... which are significantly higher than the prevailing rates". The bank advertises 4.20% to 4.45% APY against a 1.76% national average. Report A says that is under the 337.7 national rate cap (6.20%), which likely answers this, but nobody in the diff's chain checked how "significantly higher" is tested for an undercapitalized bank. Not a defect in the diff; a known unknown next to it.
