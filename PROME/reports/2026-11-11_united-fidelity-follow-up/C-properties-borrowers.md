# United Fidelity Bank: the loans, borrowers and properties behind the Q3 2026 multifamily nonaccrual ($2.14B) and charge-off ($362M)

Researcher: read-only public-source agent for PROME. Read date for every source: 2026-11-11. Budget: about 50 tool calls.
Tags: VERIFIED (read at the source) · INFERRED · SEARCH-NOT-FOUND (describes my search only, not proof of absence) · UNKNOWN.

## Five-line answer

1. **Public sources do not name the impaired loans, borrowers or properties.** No document ties the $2.14B to specific deals or to Pedcor-affiliated borrowers, so the affiliate question stays UNKNOWN. However, the bank's own regulators and filings describe a model in which the bank **itself, through wholly owned subsidiaries, develops, owns, finances and invests in** the affordable-housing projects it lends to, and sells completed projects to partnerships "owned and operated by related parties". VERIFIED: OCC CRA evaluation 2024-01-08; SEC Reg A filing 2021-10-22.
2. **HMDA loan-level data (from the federal home-lending disclosure system) shows the book that probably became nonaccrual.** From 2021 to 2023 the bank originated **256 multifamily loans totalling $4.18B**, every one conventional, held rather than sold, with a balloon payment, almost all interest-only (250 of 256), and at a median loan-to-value of 85%. Coupons were low and fixed-looking: medians of 2.78% in 2021 and 3.35% in 2022. The loans were spread across 41 counties in IN, FL, NC, CO, OH, GA, TN, SC and TX, with **$598M in Florida**. On 237 of the 256 loans, 100% of the units are income-restricted. VERIFIED from the data; the link from these originations to the nonaccrual loans is INFERRED.
3. **Why now: the only explanation on record is the bank's own Call Report note.** It says the loans "were current" before being placed on cash-basis nonaccrual, so the trigger was a collectability judgment, not missed payments. Two other facts are evidenced, but nothing documents them as the trigger: (a) the affiliate insurer that "wrote a fair amount of credit derivatives to support" the bank stopped in Q3 2024 (AM Best, 2026-03-25); (b) the June 2026 Call Report put about $3.09B of loans at a remaining maturity or repricing of 1 to 3 years, and the part of that bucket that disappeared by September matches nonaccrual plus charge-offs. Refinance or balloon risk, appraisal markdowns and examiner pressure are HYPOTHESES only.
4. **Other lenders: no document shows another lender, bond trustee, FHLB or participant on any specific impaired loan.** Every named tie to an outside party is at the Pedcor-sponsor level: U.S. Bank as tax-credit investor on one 2024 Ohio Pedcor deal, and HUD/GNMA on the bank's older loans that were converted to bonds. None is at loan level. The evidence leans toward an **isolated, mostly in-house concentration**: 134 of the 139 second-lien loans sit in census tracts where the bank also made a first lien, and the credit protection came from a sister company. That reading is INFERRED, not proven.
5. **Recovery evidence is close to none.** No public appraisal, occupancy, sale, foreclosure, receivership, lawsuit or tax-credit compliance item was found for any 2025–2026 Pedcor or UFB property. The ending allowance of $6.3M against $2.14B of nonaccrual loans is consistent with the loans already being written down to collateral value (INFERRED).

**Assessment (INFERRED):** at loan level, no outside lender, participant or trustee is documented on any of the impaired loans. Three facts point toward an isolated, in-house exposure, not proof of one:
- every HMDA multifamily loan was retained;
- most junior liens sit beside the bank's own first liens;
- the bank and its subsidiaries are developer, fee-earner and equity holder, with $1.03B of real-estate venture investments and a $146.7M development-fee receivable payable from project cash flows.

Sharing is documented only at the sponsor level (tax-credit investors such as U.S. Bank) and in unnamed SBLC participations.

**Tension to keep open:** if the borrowing partnerships were "affiliates" under Regulation W, $2B+ of credit to them would far exceed section 23A limits, which are a fraction of roughly $0.73B of June total capital. The impaired borrowers are therefore probably either outside the affiliate definition (unrelated third parties, or partnerships controlled through the bank's own subsidiaries) or inside a structure the public record does not show. **Whether the impaired loans are loans to Pedcor-related entities remains UNKNOWN.**

## Open unknowns

- The identity, sponsor and affiliation of the borrowers behind the $2.14B, and whether they are Pedcor partnerships, VCC/PHC/PCDC-related entities or third parties.
- Whether the HMDA 2021–2023 loans are the nonaccrual loans. The 120-month 2022–2023 loans do not obviously fit a 1–3 year maturity bucket unless they reprice or were modified.
- Collateral values, occupancy, DSCR and tax-credit compliance for any property; any guarantees (none found).
- Who took the $166.4M of conveyed SBLCs, and whether any impaired loans are pledged to the LOC issuer.
- How the $1.03B real-estate venture investments and the $146.7M fee receivable overlap with the impaired loans. If they are the same projects, the bank's loss exposure extends beyond the loans.
