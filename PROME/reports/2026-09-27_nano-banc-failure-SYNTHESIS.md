## 1. The question, answered
**Is this the CRE transmission chain reaching the bank layer, or a fraud-born one-off — and what does the FDIC's loss say about Southern-California CRE marks?**

**Answer (working model, four desks concurring, none dissenting):** a fraud-born, litigation-dominant capital collapse (with credit deteriorating underneath it) that was then RESOLVED as a commercial-real-estate loss. Two different things killed the bank, in sequence:
1. **The capital collapse was litigation-DOMINANT, alongside credit deterioration — not litigation instead of credit.** FY2025 net loss −$75.3M; the three largest charges were legal fees $46.3M, provision $16.7M and a deferred-tax write-off $12.8M (they sum to $75.8M against a $75.3M net loss, so this is the composition of the loss, not an exact earnings bridge — CATO NB2) [REGINALD §2c, Call Report RI-E]. Equity $126M [6/24] → $39M [6/26] → **$5.66M (0.82% of assets) on 9/22/26** [DFPI possession order Exh. A via DEWEY]. The state's seizure order finds CAPITAL ONLY (equity below the 3% statutory floor); "self-dealing" is press-release language and no order names a borrower. The regulators' orders name BOTH causes, in sequence: the 2021–22 Fed and DFPI orders were about governance and insider/affiliate dealing (Reg O credit to insiders, 23A/23B), the 3/6/2026 DFPI order about capital, CRE concentration and "unsecured loans to finance real estate" [DEWEY §1a–§1b, §2b, §5(a), all PRIMARY]. The conduct came first, by three to four years; the noncurrent wave of spring 2025 coincides with the fraud being EXPOSED and litigated (Nano's own notices of default 5/20/2025; the arbitration awards of Feb/May 2025), not with the conduct itself [DEWEY §5(c), medium confidence].
2. **The FDIC's loss is the assets.** With equity gone, the insurance fund bears the marks on a book that was 73% CRE including CRE-finance loans booked outside the real-estate lines [REGINALD §2a]. **THE figure: ≈$120M of loss beyond the bank's own last books, ≈17% of $690.9M (9/22 base; range $110–120M)** [REGINALD §3 — the one shared figure; CREED and WAL cite it]. ⚠️ It is the DESKS' scenario (equity consumed + the FDIC's ESTIMATED DIF cost), not an FDIC-reported asset mark (CATO NB1). ⚠️ Two MEASURES, never paired across bases: the FDIC's own measure (DIF cost ÷ assets) is **15.5% on 6/30 assets = 16.5% on 9/22 assets**; REGINALD's implied-total-loss measure (equity consumed + DIF cost) is **21% on 6/30 = 17% on 9/22**. Cite 17% (9/22) as the desks' figure and 15.5%/16.5% as the FDIC's; "15.5% → 17%" is NOT one number rising. Retained pool ≈$215M ⇒ a loss on that pool of **~51–56% ON THE ZERO-ADJUSTMENT SCENARIO ONLY — NOT a ceiling and NOT a bound (REGINALD §3, the figure's owner, rejects both words; CREED uses 'ceiling' — the owner's word governs)** — no deposit premium, no purchase discount to Sunwest, receivership costs inside the estimate — and **unallocable** until the bid terms post: a premium paid by Sunwest or unpaid junior creditors RAISE the true loss (and so the ceiling), a purchase discount LOWERS it (CATO NB1: e.g. +$10M premium −$5M costs ⇒ ≈58%). Cite it as a labelled scenario or not at all.

**So: not systemic by size** ($736M, one branch, no depositor loss, acquirer found; five of the six 2026 failures are under $300M, aggregate ≈$1.4B) — **and a pattern tell by anatomy.** Nano's balance sheet carried two markers REGINALD's instruments already measure, and both were visible in public filings well before closure (§2).
