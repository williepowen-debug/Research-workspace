## The question
Seven failures in 2026 against 0 to 4 in every other year since 2000 that was neither a crisis nor its aftermath (Will's point, 00:16 ET: the crisis years are not a fair comparison). Four explanations, to be tested against each other:

| # | Explanation | What would show it | What would kill it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Regulators are closing known problem banks faster | Shorter time from first order to closing; more capital directives; loss share of assets flat or falling | Longer time to closing; fewer formal actions |
| H2 | More banks are getting worse, regulators unchanged | The pool of weak banks (thin capital, bad loans against capital) growing since 2022 | A flat or shrinking pool |
| H3 | Regulators are doing less, so problems are caught late and cost more | Fewer formal actions and exams, staff cuts, looser standards; loss share of assets rising; inspector-general reviews citing slow action | Action counts steady; loss share flat |
| H4 | Chance at this sample size | Nothing above moves | Any of the above moving clearly |
