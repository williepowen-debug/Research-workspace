## Why the short thesis is broken

### Important caveat — Memo Item 3 / C&I hiding-place framework

This review does **not** invalidate REGINALD's Memo Item 3 / "C&I as Convergence Hiding Place" framework. That framework is real: CRE can be economically CRE while reported inside C&I, and Call Report Memo Item 3 / RCON2746 is the cleaner place to test it.

For ZION specifically, the Q1 10-Q confirms one disclosure-negative move — **leasing balances of ~$374M were folded into C&I** — and REGINALD's Q1 analysis correctly flags this as part of a broader Q1 cohort reclassification pattern. That means the concern was not imaginary: ZION did make C&I less transparent at the exact moment analysts are starting to ask about NDFI/warehouse/fund-finance buckets.

But I did **not** find evidence in the extracted 10-Q or ZION sub-agent files that a large block of bad CRE was newly transitioned into C&I. Current ZION C&I stress metrics are not flashing: C&I nonaccruals fell to $83M from $93M QoQ, C&I 30-89 day past dues fell to $36M from $75M, and C&I modifications were $65M vs CRE term modifications of $139M.

So: **Memo Item 3 remains a watch item and should be verified in the Call Report. It is not, on current evidence, enough to keep ZION as a conviction July short.** If ZION's Call Report shows a WAL/OZK-style hidden-CRE number, this recommendation should be reopened.

This is the opposite of what we need for a July put. The bank is not showing near-term recognition pressure.

### 2. CRE is not giving us the break

Deeper REGINALD/sub-agent review does show why ZION originally felt suspicious:

- RENO tagged ZION as Nevada-exposed with **440% CRE/equity** and no visible CLN hedge, making it more vulnerable than WAL if Las Vegas/Nevada stress transmits.
- CREED flagged ZION as a Western/secondary-market CRE repricing candidate — Denver/Salt Lake/Phoenix-style office risk, not just gateway-market CRE.
- REGINALD's municipal research found ZION has real conduit/municipal lending exposure, where private borrowers can hide behind public-sector labels.
- Q1 analysis flagged the leasing-to-C&I reclassification as **disclosure-negative**.

That is real thesis context. The problem is timing/evidence: most of it points to H2 2026 / 2027 transmission or Call Report verification, not a live July put catalyst.

CRE is still the largest optical risk, but the Q1 details are not bearish enough:

- Total CRE: $13.658B, +2.0% QoQ.
- CRE nonaccruals: $42M vs $73M QoQ — improving.
- Office: $1.595B, down from $1.675B; nonaccruals $37M vs $67M — improving.
- Multifamily: $4.091B, 30% of CRE; no nonaccruals, no delinquencies; criticized ratio 16.6% vs 17.5%; classified 13.6% vs 15.0%.
- Construction/land: $2.271B; growth exists, but no clean delinquency/nonaccrual signal in the extracted 10-Q metrics.

There is a latent maturity/refi issue, especially multifamily with ~46% maturing in 12 months, but ZION is not the weak-link expression. OZK/WAL/KRE remain better places to carry bank thesis risk.

### 3. NDFI is low, flat, and newly disclosed

10-Q confirms NDFI is not a hidden growth bomb:

- Total NDFI: ~$2.034B, 3.3% of total loans, roughly flat from $1.997B at Q4.
- NDFI credit quality: criticized 0.7%, classified 0.7%, nonaccrual 0.5%, ACL/NDFI 1.40%.
- Business credit intermediaries are the largest NDFI sub-bucket at $974M; includes finance companies, direct lenders, private debt funds, equipment leasing companies, BDCs/SBICs/senior loan funds.

This confirms REGINALD’s local post-call read: ZION is the cohort floor, not the NDFI stress carrier.

### 4. Funding/liquidity is strong, not stressed

- Deposits: $76.9B, +2% QoQ.
- Customer deposits excluding brokered: $73.1B, up from $71.8B.
- Brokered deposits: $3.795B / 4.9% of deposits, slightly down.
- Available liquidity: $46.9B vs $35.0B estimated uninsured deposits = 134% coverage.
- FHLB borrowings outstanding: reduced to zero in the liquidity table; available FHLB capacity $17.4B.
- Total borrowed funds down 44% YoY; short-term FHLB advances drove the decline.

No funding squeeze. No liquidity catalyst.

Additional evidence found:

1. **Fraud/accounting evidence cuts against ZION as a short.** The ZION subtree exists mainly to benchmark WAL: ZION charged off ~83% of Stupin/Cantor exposure immediately, used EY, led disclosure, and called collateral effectively irretrievable. That is bad for WAL comparison, not bad for ZION.
2. **Muni/conduit risk is real but small in current credit metrics.** Q1 10-Q says ZION extends credit to private commercial / 501(c)(3) borrowers through pass-through municipal structures, and the ~$2M municipal nonaccruals relate to those private commercial entities. That confirms the conduit-risk mechanism, but not a live deterioration signal.
3. **Multifamily maturity wall is the best latent CRE concern.** $4.1B multifamily is 30% of CRE, with ~46% maturing within 12 months. Basis Multifamily acquisition adds strategic exposure to agency MF origination/servicing. This is a Q2/Q3 watch item, not July conviction by itself.
4. **Management is openly saying CRE activity is returning and pricing is competitive.** Transcript says they are seeing increased CRE activity and “as much pricing pressure in CRE as they've seen for some time.” That is a cycle-risk flag, but it pairs with improving current credit metrics.
5. **Accounting/presentation changes are a pattern, not a smoking gun.** Besides leasing into C&I, ZION also changed derivative/cash-collateral netting presentation, reducing gross balance-sheet optics by ~$100M. Worth watching, but not hidden bad CRE evidence.
6. **The 10-Q text does not contain `MI3`, `Memo Item 3`, or `RCON2746`.** The Call Report remains the necessary verification source.
7. **Several details are actually anti-short:** no collateral-dependent loans measured at fair value, no Q1 fair-value losses on those loans, NPA decline driven by term CRE improvement, $215M expected Visa gain in Q2, buyback authorization, and FHLB borrowings reduced to zero.

Net from the deeper folder-tree sweep: **ZION is not evidence-free, but the evidence is mostly “latent watch / WAL comparator / Call Report reopen,” not “keep July OTM put.”**

### 6. Capital/AOCI is the only real bearish leg, but it is not enough by itself

AOCI remains real:

- AOCI loss: -$1.935B.
- Includes $1.5B pre-tax / $1.2B after-tax unrealized losses on securities previously transferred AFS → HTM.
- CET1: $8.05B; CET1 ratio 11.6%.
- Proposed rules could include most AOCI in regulatory capital.

But as a trade catalyst, this is too broad and regulatory-timing dependent. ZION-specific fundamentals are improving, and the bank authorized another $225M buyback for the remainder of 2026. If we want AOCI/regional-bank downside, KRE/WAL/OZK runway is cleaner than a single ZION July put.
