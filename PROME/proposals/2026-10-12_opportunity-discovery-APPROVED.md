## How to answer “what is priced in”

Use dated issuer guidance, earnings expectations with provenance, a stated valuation/scenario calculation, or option payoff/cost where appropriate. Specify which is observed and which is an assumption. Guidance is not consensus; a price rally is not proof of full pricing; option breakeven is not the market's probability forecast. Work backward from the business/price outcome needed to deliver a worthwhile return after costs, then evaluate the evidence and time available. If a supportable comparison is unavailable, label no demonstrated mispricing and identify whether a bounded source check can improve it.

Use plain-language reasons, not a new scoring system: thesis contradicted; no demonstrated mispricing; timing mismatch; unsuitable instrument/liquidity; insufficient evidence; portfolio/capital constraint. Multiple reasons can apply. State whether the next useful step is stop, a different expression, a named evidence check, or waiting for an existing event. A cheaper instrument fixes only the instrument problem.
