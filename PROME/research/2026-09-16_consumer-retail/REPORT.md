DERIVED: control = total − motor vehicles/parts − gasoline stations − building materials/garden − food services. August $421.968bn versus July $416.300bn and June $418.141bn. This reconstructed nominal aggregate is not BEA real PCE or a GDP forecast.

August added $9.485bn: nonstore $3.567bn (37.6%), gasoline $1.848bn (19.5%), restaurants $1.246bn (13.1%), autos $0.814bn (8.6%). The remainder, net, accounts for 21.2%. Excluding gasoline AND nonstore, sales still rose 0.72%. Nonstore is not synonymous with pure e-commerce, and channel totals do not identify household income groups.

Sequential recovery is less dramatic: August total is 0.70% above June; control 0.92% above June. Autos remain 1.22% below June; building materials 0.36% below. Strong rebound, modest two-month net growth; neither a collapse nor evidence that +1.2% is a sustainable monthly pace. Twelve of thirteen broad top-level sectors rose; that is descriptive breadth, not twelve statistically significant gains. Department stores declined within the broader positive general-merchandise category.

## Inflation and timing

[BLS August CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_09112026.htm), released September11: headline +0.4%, gasoline +3.9%, core goods +0.1%. This supports the interpretation that gasoline receipts partly reflect higher prices while the broader retail rebound cannot be dismissed as gasoline inflation. Census measures establishments, CPI measures consumption items: station sales include nonfuel goods. Do not label sales/gasoline-CPI arithmetic an exact fuel-volume measure. Similarly, headline retail minus headline CPI is not official real consumption because category weights and coverage differ.

August precedes the September Petroline shutdown and subsequent pump-price squeeze. It establishes the starting strength consumers carried into that event, not the event's effect. Real PCE, income/saving, employment and subsequent credit performance are the needed confirmations.

## Transmission interpretation — INFERRED, not an owner re-grade

- **Consumer → credit → banks:** near-term evidence weakens the claim that aggregate spending is already contracting. CARL STATUS September11 framed July as a material adverse print and possible spread of weakness into the control leg. This release challenges that inference directly. It does not resolve CARL's lower-income stress, delinquency or registered kill rules; aggregate sales cannot distinguish healthier consumers from debt-financed spending.
- **Inflation → policy → duration:** resilient demand gives less growth-based reason for easing. Directionally hawkish information; actual market repricing and the policy decision were not measured here. Strong sales do not identify term premium versus expected policy rates, and do not authorize a duration add.
- **Energy:** consumers had more spending resilience entering September than a July-only read implied. The competing risk remains that energy absorbs future discretionary budgets. Measure September rather than treating today's backward-looking strength as proof that transmission failed.
