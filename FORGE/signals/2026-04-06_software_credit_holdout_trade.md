# Signal: The Holdout Trade — Software Credit Restructuring

**Source:** Credit Weekly (Apr 5, 2026)  
**File:** Credit_Weekly_The_Holdout_Trade.pdf  

---

## Core Claim

The most contrarian trade in software credit isn't buying the dip — it's **waiting** (the "holdout" trade). Sponsors are using coercive LMEs to strip collateral, leaving negotiating lenders worse off than holdouts who get paid at par.

---

## Key Mechanics

### The LME Playbook
1. Sponsor requires NDAs before sharing restructuring details
2. NDAs bar lenders from communicating with peers (siloing)
3. Sponsor cuts side deals with large holders first
4. Smaller lenders approached last with worse terms, take-it-or-leave-it

### Vibrantz Technologies Example
- **Sponsor:** American Securities
- **Tactic:** NDAs to silence coordination, different deals for different groups
- **Result:** Large creditors got first-out paper; smaller lenders got worse terms with 90% already signed

### Recovery Data (Fitch)
| Path | Recovery |
|------|----------|
| LME → eventual filing | ~41¢ on first lien |
| Straight Chapter 11 | ~68¢ |

**Harvard Law finding:** Majority of coercively restructured firms file anyway. LME buys time, doesn't fix balance sheet.

---

## The Holdout Theory

If just enough lenders hold out, the post-LME stub may be small enough that company pays holdouts at par with cash on hand — no negotiation, no haircut.

**Risk:** Requires coordination breakdown (sponsors actively preventing this via NDAs).
