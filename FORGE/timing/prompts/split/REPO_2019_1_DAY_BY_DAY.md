# Reconstruct the September 2019 Repo Crisis Day by Day

## What I Need

A detailed chronological reconstruction of the September 2019 repo crisis — what happened each day, why, and what the Fed did in response. Then extend through the December 2019 near-miss and into early 2020.

## Context

On September 17, 2019, the overnight repo rate spiked from ~2% to 10%. The Fed injected $75B the next morning, eventually expanding to $500B+ by year-end. Lorie Logan (then NY Fed) called it "structural." The conventional explanation is Treasury settlement + corporate tax payments on the same day drained reserves — but JPM hoarding $350B in excess reserves while smaller institutions couldn't borrow suggests deeper structural issues.

I need the granular timeline because Fed's Perli said on March 26, 2026 that reserve ampleness metrics have returned to Q1 2019 levels — the exact conditions that preceded the crisis. March 31, 2026 has similar confluence risks (Japan FY-end, US Q-end, 20Y settlement, tax payments) but with zero RRP buffer and a private credit crisis overlaid.

## Questions

1. September 16-20, 2019: what happened each day? Repo rate, Fed actions, which institutions were borrowing vs lending, market reactions.

2. What was the actual proximate cause? Beyond "Treasury settlement + taxes" — was it JPM pulling back from repo lending? Reserve distribution vs aggregate level? SLR/LCR constraints preventing banks from lending even though they had excess reserves?

3. What happened in the weeks after (October-November 2019)? Did stress persist? How large were ongoing Fed repo operations?

4. The December 2019 near-miss: what happened at year-end? Did the pre-positioned term repo and T-bill purchases prevent a repeat, or was it close?

5. By early 2020 (pre-COVID), was the structural problem solved? Or did COVID stimulus just mask it?

6. What are the best primary sources? Fed staff papers, NY Fed Liberty Street blog posts, BIS working papers, academic research on the 2019 repo crisis mechanics.

## What I Want Back

A chronological narrative from September 16, 2019 through February 2020. Include: daily repo rates, Fed intervention amounts, key institutional behaviors, and the structural diagnosis. Cite specific papers and data sources.
