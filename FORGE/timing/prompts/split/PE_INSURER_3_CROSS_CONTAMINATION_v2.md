# What Happens When Athene Can't Issue New FABNs?

## What I Need

A scenario analysis: Athene's FABN program effectively shuts — spreads blow out or investors refuse to buy. Not a downgrade-triggered run (FABNs are bullet, non-puttable). A market access closure. Map the cascade from funding squeeze to forced asset sales to cross-contamination.

## What We Already Know (treat as confirmed)

- **Athene wholesale funding: $69B** — $30.4B FABNs (via Athene Global Funding SPV), $21B FHLB advances (largest single insurer borrower), $18B FABR (repo-style, presumably shorter-dated)
- **$35B authorized FABN capacity**, currently at $30.4B = 87% utilized
- Funding agreements were **38-44% of Athene's new business inflows** in recent quarters — this is not optional funding, it's the engine
- **~1/5 of investments are loans to affiliated Apollo funds** (circular). $21B of a $50B industry increase in private ABS is Athene-affiliated paper
- **Surplus ratio 2.4%** vs 7.2% industry average
- FABNs rank **pari passu with policyholder claims** — senior to general creditors. This means policyholder stress and FABN stress hit the same priority bucket
- FHLB was the backstop in 2007-08 (absorbed 3/4 of FABS contraction). But FHLB pulled from First Republic in 2023 (SVB precedent)
- **F&G holds billions in net recoverables from Aspida Re and Everlake** — PE-insurers are counterparties to each other
- **FABR ($18B)** is repo-style = shorter duration = more immediate rollover pressure than bullet FABNs
- Fed classifies FABS as "runnable money-like financial liabilities"
- NAIC cannot confirm zero XFABNs outstanding — opacity on whether any puttable paper exists

## The Scenario

It's Q2-Q3 2026. HY OAS has crossed 400. CCC OAS is 1200+. Several more PC funds have gated. NAIC SVO has begun re-rating PE-insurer private placements. Athene's affiliated ABS portfolio is marking down. Rating agencies have put Athene on negative watch (not yet downgraded). FABN investors demand 200bps+ more spread — Athene's spread-lending model goes negative carry. New issuance stops.

## Questions

1. **The FABR channel breaks first.** Athene has $18B in repo-style funding agreements. These are shorter-dated than bullet FABNs. If counterparties refuse to roll FABR, Athene loses $18B in funding before a single FABN matures. Who are the FABR counterparties? Banks? Broker-dealers? Can they pull overnight or is there term structure?

2. **FHLB as sole backstop.** Athene already has $21B in FHLB advances — largest insurer borrower. If FABN + FABR both close:
   - Can Athene increase FHLB borrowing to cover the gap? What's the FHLB's limit for a single member?
   - What collateral does Athene pledge to FHLB? If it's the same affiliated private credit that's marking down, do FHLBs haircut or refuse?
   - The SVB/First Republic precedent: FHLB of San Francisco pulled advances when the banks were in distress. Under what circumstances does an FHLB restrict a member? Is there a formal policy or is it discretionary?

3. **Forced liquidation of affiliated assets.** If Athene must sell to raise cash:
   - The affiliated ABS ($21B+) has no natural secondary market — Apollo originated it, Athene holds it, who buys it at scale?
   - If Athene sells at 70-80¢, does this force marks on Global Atlantic, F&G, and Everlake who hold similar or identical affiliated paper?
   - Is there a "marks are contagious" dynamic where one PE-insurer's fire sale reprices the entire sector?

4. **Apollo parent company exposure.** If Athene is in distress:
   - Does Apollo face covenant triggers, margin calls, or rating downgrades at the holding company level?
   - Apollo manages ~$700B+ in AUM. How much of that is in vehicles that invest alongside Athene in the same assets? If Athene fire-sales, does it mark down Apollo's broader fund complex?
   - Fee revenue: Athene pays Apollo management fees. If Athene shrinks its balance sheet, Apollo's fee income drops. At what point does this impair Apollo's own credit?

5. **Policyholder surrender — the actual "run" channel.** FABNs can't run. But policyholders can surrender fixed annuities (with penalties).
   - What are typical surrender charge schedules? (e.g., 7% year 1, declining to 0% by year 7-10?)
   - At what point in Athene's distress do policyholders start surrendering? Is it rating downgrade? Headlines? Or only actual missed payments?
   - If surrenders spike, Athene must liquidate assets to pay policyholders — and FABNs are pari passu, so FABN holders and policyholders compete for the same general account assets.

6. **Cross-counterparty chain.** F&G holds recoverables from Aspida Re and Everlake. If Athene stress spreads to the sector:
   - Do PE-insurers who are counterparties to each other face simultaneous stress?
   - Is there a scenario where Insurer A's reinsurance recoverable from Insurer B becomes impaired because Insurer B is also in distress?
   - How big is the intra-PE-insurer counterparty web?

## What I Want Back

A cascade map: FABR fails first → FHLB backstop tested → forced asset sales → cross-insurer mark contagion → Apollo parent impact → policyholder surrender risk. For each stage: estimated timeline, dollar magnitude, and whether it's self-reinforcing or self-limiting. Identify the single most dangerous feedback loop in the chain.
