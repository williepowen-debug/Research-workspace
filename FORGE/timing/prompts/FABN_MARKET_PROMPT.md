# Prompt: FABN Market — The "Monoline Moment" for PE-Insurers

## Context

Funding Agreement-Backed Notes (FABNs) are securitized obligations issued by life insurance companies. The insurer issues a "funding agreement" (essentially a GIC — guaranteed investment contract) and packages it into notes sold to institutional investors. This is a wholesale funding channel for insurers — analogous to commercial paper for banks.

PE-controlled insurers (Athene/Apollo, Evermore/Blackstone, Global Atlantic/KKR) use FABNs as a significant funding source. The concern: if the underlying insurer gets downgraded, FABN holders may refuse to roll or demand significantly higher spreads — creating a funding crisis for insurers that rely on this channel to fund illiquid private credit investments.

**Why I think this matters (March 2026):**
- Egan-Jones had BMA recognition revoked; NAIC found private ratings averaged 3 notches too high
- If NAIC SVO systematically re-rates PE-insurer holdings, capital charges spike → surplus drops → rating agencies downgrade → FABN market seizes
- This is structurally similar to the monoline crisis: monolines funded via short-term markets, invested in long-term illiquid structured products, and collapsed when their ratings were cut
- 7+ private credit funds have gated in Q1 2026; underlying asset quality deteriorating (CCC OAS 1,013bps)
- US Treasury meeting with insurance regulators scheduled April 1, 2026

## Questions

1. **How large is the FABN market?** Total outstanding across all issuers. Growth trajectory over the past 5 years. How much of this is PE-insurer issuance specifically vs. traditional life insurers (MetLife, Prudential, etc.)?

2. **Who holds FABNs?** 
   - Money market funds? (If so, which ones and what percentage of AUM?)
   - Bank treasury portfolios?
   - Pension funds?
   - Foreign central banks / sovereign wealth funds?
   - Other insurance companies?
   
   The holder base determines the contagion channel. MMF exposure = potential money market stress. Bank exposure = direct balance sheet hit.

3. **What are the typical maturities?** Is this a rollover market (like 90-day commercial paper) or are these 3-5 year termed instruments? The maturity profile determines how fast a downgrade translates to a funding crisis. If FABNs are short-dated, downgrade = immediate rollover failure. If termed out, there's a runway.

4. **What happened to FABNs during prior stress episodes?**
   - 2008 financial crisis: did FABN spreads blow out? Any issuers unable to roll?
   - 2020 COVID: any disruption?
   - 2023 regional bank crisis: any impact on insurer funding?

5. **Rating sensitivity.** FABNs are typically rated based on the issuer's claims-paying ability rating. What happens at each downgrade notch?
   - AAA → AA: manageable, wider spreads
   - AA → A: some holders forced to sell (MMF eligibility thresholds?)
   - A → BBB: mass forced selling, potential market freeze
   - BBB → BB: complete market access loss
   
   Where are the PE-insurers currently rated? How many notches from a threshold that triggers forced selling by holders?

6. **The "monoline moment" analogy.** 
   - Monolines (AMBAC, MBIA) had ~$2.4T in guaranteed exposure against ~$30B in capital
   - Their downgrade forced banks to recognize losses on all guaranteed structured products
   - The transmission was: downgrade → guarantee worthless → banks mark down → interbank freeze
   - For PE-insurers: downgrade → FABN rollover failure → forced asset sales → private credit marks revealed → bank exposure recognized
   - How does the magnitude compare? Is FABN disruption a $50B problem or a $500B problem?

7. **FHLB as backup funding.** If FABN markets seize, do PE-insurers turn to FHLB advances as backup? PE-controlled insurers already hold ~$160B in FHLB advances. Can they increase this? What are FHLB collateral requirements for insurers? Could FHLB lending tighten at the same time FABNs seize (like repo + commercial paper freezing simultaneously in 2008)?

8. **Regulatory framework.** Who regulates FABNs? SEC (as securities)? State insurance departments (as insurance products)? Both? Is there a gap in oversight where FABNs fall between regulatory jurisdictions?
