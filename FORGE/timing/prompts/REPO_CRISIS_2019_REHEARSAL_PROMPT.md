# Prompt: September 2019 Repo Crisis as Rehearsal for 2026

## Context

On September 17, 2019, the overnight repo rate spiked from ~2% to 10% — a 5x move in the most "boring" market in finance. The Fed had to inject $75B in emergency repo operations the next morning, eventually expanding to $500B+ in term and overnight repo by year-end.

**The Fed's own Lorie Logan (then NY Fed) called it a "structural" not "temporary" problem.** And now, Perli (current NY Fed Markets desk head) said on March 26, 2026 that reserve ampleness metrics have returned to **Q1 2019 levels** — the exact conditions that preceded the crisis.

But 2026 conditions are materially worse:

| Factor | Sep 2019 | Mar 2026 |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Reverse Repo (RRP) | ~$200B+ (buffer existed) | **$0.99B (effectively zero)** |
| Reserves | ~$1.5T (declining from QT) | **$2.8T but concentrated in G-SIBs** |
| SRF | Did not exist | Exists but $74.6B record usage (Dec 31, 2025) |
| MMF assets | ~$3.4T | **$7.86T (record, 2.3x larger)** |
| Treasury outstanding | ~$22T | **~$36T (+64%)** |
| Private credit overlay | None | **$1.7T+ in stressed PC, 7+ funds gated** |
| Fed T-bill purchases | Not yet | **$195B → $352B in 12 weeks (stealth QE)** |
| SOFR-IORB spread | N/A (SOFR was new) | **-0.02bps (calm but fragile)** |
| Oil shock | None | **Brent $107-108, gas approaching $4** |

**Specific March 31, 2026 risks:**
- Japan fiscal year-end (GPIF rebalancing, life insurer repatriation) = dollar selling, yen buying
- US Q1 close (window dressing, balance sheet optimization)
- 20Y Treasury settlement same day
- Corporate tax payments pulling cash from bank reserves
- Zero RRP buffer means no "release valve" if reserves maldistribute

## Questions

1. **Reconstruct the September 2019 repo crisis day by day.** From September 16-20, 2019:
   - What happened each day? (Repo rate, Fed actions, market moves)
   - What was the proximate cause? (Treasury settlement + corporate tax payments is the conventional explanation — but was there more?)
   - Who was borrowing? Who couldn't lend?
   - How quickly did the Fed respond, and was the response adequate?
   - What happened in the weeks AFTER (through year-end 2019)?

2. **The reserve distribution problem.** In 2019, aggregate reserves looked "adequate" (~$1.5T) but were concentrated in the top 4 G-SIBs — JPM alone held ~$350B, far more than needed. Smaller banks and broker-dealers couldn't access reserves.
   - Is the same concentration problem present in 2026? Reserves are $2.8T but how are they distributed?
   - The Fed's Waller has flagged $2.7T as the "problematic" boundary. Are we already there?
   - Have regulatory changes (GSIB surcharges, SLR, LCR) made it MORE or LESS likely that large banks hoard reserves?

3. **What does the 2019 equivalent failure look like with 2026 vulnerabilities?**
   
   In 2019, the repo spike was a plumbing event — dramatic but quickly contained because:
   - The Fed could act freely (inflation was below target)
   - There was no underlying credit stress
   - The economy was healthy
   - The RRP buffer existed
   
   In 2026, none of these are true. Model a scenario where a repo spike occurs on March 31 or April 1:
   - Can the Fed inject liquidity while PCE is above target? What are the political optics?
   - If PC funds are simultaneously facing redemptions, do they sell repo collateral into the spike?
   - If Japanese institutions are repatriating, are they withdrawing dollars from repo markets?
   - Does a repo spike trigger margin calls on leveraged Treasury basis trades (~$1T in hedge fund positions)?

4. **The SRF was designed to prevent a 2019 repeat.** Is it working?
   - $74.6B record usage on Dec 31, 2025 — is this normal quarter-end behavior or stress?
   - The SRF leaked 38bps above its offered rate on Dec 31, 2024 — why? If the facility is unlimited, why doesn't it cap rates?
   - Access limitations: only primary dealers and certain depository institutions can use the SRF. What about money market funds? Insurance companies? Hedge funds?
   - Is the SRF big enough? Its current capacity is technically unlimited (subject to counterparty limits), but in practice?

5. **The December 2019 near-miss.** After the September crisis, the Fed injected massive repo, but stress nearly returned at year-end 2019:
   - What happened in December 2019?
   - Did the Fed's response (term repo, T-bill purchases) prevent a repeat or just paper over the structural problem?
   - By early 2020 (pre-COVID), was the problem solved? If not, COVID stimulus just masked it.

6. **The "dash for cash" parallel.** March 2020 was a different mechanism — a pure liquidity event where EVERYONE sold EVERYTHING to raise cash. Treasuries sold off alongside equities. Could 2026 produce a similar "dash for cash" if:
   - PC funds liquidate to meet redemptions
   - Insurers sell liquid assets (Treasuries) to cover illiquid PC losses
   - Japanese institutions sell USTs for repatriation
   - MMFs face redemptions and sell repo collateral
   - Basis trade unwinds add forced selling
   
   In March 2020, the Fed bought $1T+ in 3 weeks. In 2026 with inflation above target, could they do the same?

7. **Monitoring framework for March 31 and beyond.** What specific indicators should I watch in real-time?
   - SOFR rate and SOFR-IORB spread
   - Tri-party repo volumes and rates
   - SRF daily usage
   - Fed H.4.1 reserve data (weekly)
   - Treasury General Account (TGA) balance
   - GCF repo rate
   - FedWire Funds volume and value
   - DTCC settlement fails
   
   For each: what's "normal quarter-end" vs "2019 repeat" vs "worse than 2019"?
