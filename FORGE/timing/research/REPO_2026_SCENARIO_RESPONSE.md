# Repo Crisis Scenario Model: March 31, 2026
## Executive Summary

A September 2019-style repo spike occurring on March 31, 2026 would unfold into a significantly more severe crisis given the structural deterioration in liquidity buffers. Unlike 2019's contained plumbing stress, the 2026 scenario presents multiple amplification channels that could escalate into a dash-for-cash crisis requiring unprecedented Fed intervention.

**Key Risk Factors:**
- **Zero RRP cushion** ($0.99B vs $200B+ in 2019)  
- **Reserve concentration** at Waller's $2.7T boundary with assets locked in G-SIBs
- **Massive basis trade** (~$1T leveraged 10-20x vs smaller 2019 positions)
- **Inflated MMF sector** ($7.86T vs $3.4T, intermediating ~50% of repo)
- **Stressed credit markets** (CCC OAS 1,013bp) creating correlation risk
- **Fed policy constraint** (PCE above target, limited cutting ability)

## Scenario Narrative: Hour-by-Hour Crisis Timeline

### March 31, 2026 (Monday) - The Perfect Storm

**6:00 AM ET: Market Open**
- Normal conditions: SOFR opens at 3.85% (15bp above IORB)
- SRF showing $45B usage carried over from Friday's quarter-end pressures
- MMFs holding $7.86T in assets, with ~$4T in repo exposure
- Treasury bill outstanding: $352B (peak level, up from $195B 12 weeks prior)

**9:00 AM ET: Japanese FY-End Pressures**
- Japanese banks begin withdrawing ~$200B in dollar funding as FY-end approaches
- USD/JPY crosses 150 as repatriation flows intensify
- Initial repo rate tick up: SOFR rises to 3.95% (+10bp)

**10:30 AM ET: Treasury Settlement Stress**
- $85B in Treasury settlements due (20Y bond auction, bill rollovers)
- Dealer balance sheets already constrained from elevated T-bill issuance
- Primary dealers reluctant to intermediate given regulatory capital charges
- SOFR jumps to 4.15% (+30bp from morning)

**11:45 AM ET: Tax Payment Drain**
- Corporate tax payments drain additional $120B from system (similar to Sept 2019)
- **Critical difference**: No RRP buffer to absorb shock ($0.99B vs $200B+ in 2019)
- Reserve concentration in G-SIBs prevents redistribution to smaller institutions
- SOFR spikes to 4.55% (+70bp), breaching SRF rate of 3.75%

**12:30 PM ET: SRF Overwhelmed**
- SRF usage explodes to $125B (vs $74.6B Dec 31 record)
- **Rate leakage**: SRF trades 35bp above posted rate due to operational limits
- Tri-party repo rates hit 4.75% as private markets seize
- Fed announces emergency SRF capacity increase to $200B

### Crisis Amplification: The Cascade Begins

**1:15 PM ET: Basis Trade Unwind**
- SOFR at 4.75% triggers margin calls on ~$1T leveraged basis trade positions
- Hedge funds leveraged 10-20x face immediate liquidity demands
- Forced selling: $50B Treasury notes dumped in first 15 minutes
- 10Y Treasury yield spikes 15bp as dealers refuse to warehouse risk

**2:00 PM ET: MMF Stress Transmission**
- Money market funds ($7.86T) face massive redemptions as rates spike
- MMFs intermediate 50% of repo market but can't access SRF directly
- Prime MMF "WAM drift" as funds scramble for overnight liquidity
- Govt MMF rates lag behind market, creating investor arbitrage pressure

**3:00 PM ET: Credit Correlation Trigger**
- Private credit stress (CCC OAS 1,013bp) amplifies liquidity hoarding
- 7 gated private credit funds unable to meet redemptions
- Cross-asset correlation spikes as basis trade unwind hits credit markets
- "Dash for cash" psychology emerges: no longer just repo plumbing

**3:45 PM ET: International Contagion**
- European banks withdraw dollar funding ahead of quarter-end
- Asian session faces $300B funding gap as US markets close
- Currency basis swaps widen 100bp+ as dollar shortage intensifies

### Fed Emergency Response

**4:30 PM ET: Emergency FOMC Call**
- SOFR closes at 5.25% (+140bp), matching September 2019 peak
- But unlike 2019: credit stress, international spillovers, zero RRP buffer
- Fed announces:
  - SRF rate cut to 3.25% (-50bp emergency reduction)
  - SRF capacity increased to $500B
  - Swap line activation with major central banks
  - **Cannot cut Fed funds due to PCE above target**

## Transmission Analysis: What Breaks and When

### Immediate Breakage (0-6 hours)
1. **Repo Market Seizure**
   - Private repo markets effectively shut down above 5%
   - Dealer balance sheet withdrawal due to regulatory constraints
   - SRF overwhelmed despite emergency capacity increases

2. **Basis Trade Liquidation**
   - $1T leveraged positions face margin calls
   - Forced Treasury sales estimated at $100-200B
   - Cross-market volatility as hedge funds liquidate across assets

### Secondary Transmission (6-24 hours)
3. **Money Market Fund Stress**
   - $7.86T in MMF assets creates systemic amplification
   - Prime funds face redemption pressure, govt funds strained
   - WAM extension as funds hoard cash, reducing market liquidity

4. **Credit Market Correlation**
   - Private credit stress morphs repo plumbing issue into credit crisis
   - Investment-grade bonds sell off as liquidity premium explodes
   - Corporate funding markets effectively frozen

### Tertiary Effects (24-72 hours)
5. **International Dollar Shortage**
   - Non-US banks unable to roll dollar funding
   - Emerging market stress as dollar strengthens sharply
   - Central bank swap line usage hits crisis levels

6. **Real Economy Transmission**
   - Commercial paper markets seize as MMFs withdraw
   - Corporate credit lines drawn down preemptively
   - Mortgage rate spike as MBS markets dislocate

## Fed Containment Analysis: Can They Stop It?

### Tools Available vs. Tools Needed

**What the Fed Can Do:**
- **SRF Expansion**: Proven effective but limited to primary dealers/banks
- **Reverse Repo Operations**: ZERO capacity ($0.99B current balance)
- **Swap Lines**: Addresses international dollar shortage
- **Discount Window**: Stigma-laden, insufficient for market-wide stress

**What the Fed Cannot Do:**
- **Interest Rate Cuts**: Constrained by PCE inflation above target
- **Direct MMF Support**: No facility for $7.86T MMF sector
- **Private Credit Backstop**: No tools for gated funds/credit stress

### Critical Differences from 2019

| Factor | Sept 2019 | March 2026 | Fed Containment Ability |
|--------|-----------|------------|------------------------|
| **RRP Buffer** | $200B+ | $0.99B | ❌ **Much Worse** - No automatic stabilizer |
| **Reserve Distribution** | Fragmented | Concentrated in G-SIBs | ❌ **Worse** - Can't redistribute easily |
| **Basis Trade Size** | Limited | ~$1T leveraged | ❌ **Much Worse** - Massive forced selling |
| **MMF Sector** | $3.4T | $7.86T | ❌ **Much Worse** - 2.3x amplification |
| **Credit Conditions** | Stable | Stressed (CCC OAS 1,013) | ❌ **Much Worse** - Correlation risk |
| **Policy Space** | Full flexibility | Constrained by inflation | ❌ **Much Worse** - Cannot cut rates |

### Containment Probability Assessment

**Scenario Probabilities:**
- **Contained within 48 hours** (2019-style): **15%**
- **Extended stress requiring new facilities**: **60%**  
- **Full dash-for-cash requiring massive intervention**: **25%**

**Why Containment Is Harder:**
1. **No automatic stabilizers**: Zero RRP provides no buffer
2. **Scale mismatch**: SRF designed for dealer stress, not $7.86T MMF disruption
3. **Cross-asset correlation**: Credit stress prevents pure "plumbing" treatment
4. **Policy constraints**: Cannot signal accommodation due to inflation concerns

## Policy Recommendations

### Pre-Crisis Prevention
1. **Reserve Distribution**: Fed should monitor G-SIB concentration, consider targeted IORB adjustments
2. **MMF Oversight**: SEC/Fed coordination on money fund liquidity stress tests
3. **Basis Trade Monitoring**: Enhanced reporting requirements for leveraged Treasury strategies
4. **SRF Pre-positioning**: Expand eligible counterparties beyond primary dealers

### Crisis Response Framework
1. **Automatic SRF Rate Adjustment**: Pre-announced triggers for rate reductions
2. **MMF Emergency Facility**: Design facility for direct money fund liquidity support  
3. **Cross-Border Coordination**: Pre-arranged swap line activation with major central banks
4. **Communication Strategy**: Clear messaging distinguishing liquidity support from policy accommodation

### Post-Crisis Structural Reforms
1. **RRP Floor**: Maintain minimum $200B reverse repo capacity as automatic stabilizer
2. **Leverage Limits**: Consider basis trade position limits relative to dealer balance sheet capacity
3. **MMF Reform**: Stronger liquidity requirements given 2.3x sector growth
4. **International Coordination**: Enhanced monitoring of non-US bank dollar funding
