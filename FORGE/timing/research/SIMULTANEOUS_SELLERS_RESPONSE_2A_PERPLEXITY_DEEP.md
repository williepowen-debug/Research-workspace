## SECTION 2: Price Impact — Empirical Research

### 2.1 Demand Elasticity Has Collapsed 5x Since 2010

- Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012 JPE): convenience yield avg 73bps (1926-2008). Decomposed: ≤46bps liquidity + ≥27bps safety. Baa-UST spread elasticity to log(Debt/GDP): -1.31 to -1.66.
- **Convenience yield declining:** AAA-UST spread: 67bps (2012-19) → 36bps (2025). 10Y swap spread: +2bps → -26bps. (St. Louis Fed Oct 2025)
- **Somogyi, Wallen & Xu (2025, Harvard HBS WP 26-033, Dec 2025):** Auction demand elasticity fallen **~5x since 2010**. 1992-2010: -0.9 (1% supply increase → ~2bps). 2011-2025: -0.2 (1% → ~9bps). At ~$28T outstanding, 1% = ~$280B → post-2010 = **~3.2bps per $100B at auction** vs 0.7bps pre-2010.
- Foreign holdings: 34% peak (2015) → 24% (2025), shifting marginal buyer toward more price-sensitive domestic investors.
- Chaudhary, Fu & Zhou (2024, Olin Business School, R&R at RFS): 1% demand increase → ~1% price increase (~10bps yield decline). Since 2008, foreign investors far less influential while Fed's role expanded.

### 2.2 Dealer Constraints Amplify ~One-Third

- **Bräuning & Stein (Boston Fed WP 24-07):** When demand shifts occur with 1-SD tighter VaR-limit shock, yields move **26-29% more** than average effect. 1-SD tighter → 2.1% fall in net position, 1.7% fall in turnover, 2.4% increase in bid-ask. At auction: 1-SD tighter → bid-to-cover drops 5%, high yield rises ~1%.
- **Duffie et al. (NY Fed SR 1070, revised Oct 2023):** Volatility explains ~80% of daily illiquidity (R²=79.5%). BUT when dealer utilization rises 40%→80%, illiquidity increases ~3 SD beyond volatility prediction. March 12, 2020: 5.4 SD, dealer utilization 96% (record). Key: constraints are **occasionally binding** — when dealers approach limits, illiquidity spikes nonlinearly.

### 2.3 Realized Price Impact Across Four Episodes

Applying Bräuning-Stein to Somogyi et al. baseline: ~3.2bps/$100B → ~4.2bps/$100B under constrained conditions (normal stress). But realized stress shows far larger:

- **March 2020:** 64bps in 9 days. Total forced selling $300-500B. Simple ratio: **13-21bps per $100B**. NBER WP 30476 (Beltran et al.): $100B foreign official flow → ~125bps on impact (SVAR, endogeneity corrected). Price impact of cash transactions **5-6x normal on March 12** (Treasury IAWG Report).
- **October 2023:** 115bps over 3.5 months to 5.02%. Multi-month repricing (term premium, not concentrated forced selling). TBAC: 5y5y forward +150bps, 2Y only +20bps.
- **Japan 2022:** MOF spent ¥9.18T (~$64B). 10Y rose 3.2%→4.25%, but dominant driver was Fed tightening. Japan's short-duration selling: likely 5-15bps of >100bps move.
- **April 2025:** 63-75bps in 3-4 days. Record Tradeweb volume $472.5B (Apr 9). Estimated forced selling $100-300B → **25-75bps per $100B in stressed conditions**. NY Fed: "nowhere close" to March 2020.

### 2.4 Synthesized Price Impact Table

| Condition | Est. bps per $100B | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Normal/gradual | **3-10 bps** | Somogyi et al. (2025); Fed QE stock effect |
| Moderate stress | **10-40 bps** | April 2025 implied; Bräuning-Stein ~30% amplification |
| Severe stress/dysfunction | **50-125+ bps** | March 2020 realized; NBER WP 30476 SVAR |

⚠️ Wide confidence intervals. Impact depends on: speed of selling, dealer capacity utilization, margin-call feedback loops, maturity composition, concurrent macro shocks. Duffie et al. nonlinearity: transition from adequate→dysfunctional is **discontinuous, not gradual**.

## CONCLUSION: Real Risk Is Concentration, Not Aggregate Volume

**Four of five seller classes overstated 2-10x for Treasury-specific selling.** The fifth (basis trade) is accurately sized but structurally less fragile than March 2020.

Non-basis selling: likely **$10-45B/qtr under current conditions** — meaningful but manageable in $28T market.

**True systemic risk = tail scenario where:**
- Basis trade unwinds ($50-200B) coincide with
- China acceleration ($25-35B) and
- Rising yields temporarily freeze buyer-side capacity

**Three critical findings:**

1. **Somogyi et al. (2025):** Auction demand elasticity collapsed 5x since 2010. Each $100B now moves yields ~3.2bps in normal conditions vs 0.7bps pre-2010.

2. **Duffie et al. (2023):** Dealer capacity utilization crossing ~40%→~80% triggers discontinuous jump in illiquidity. Threshold effect: selling easily absorbed one week becomes market-breaking the next.

3. **The gap between SELLING SPEED (days) and BUYING SPEED (weeks to months) is where the true vulnerability lies.** Buyer-side data shows ~$200B+ deployable capacity at ~5% 10Y from pensions, banks, insurance, SWFs — but this demand arrives over weeks-to-months, not the days over which forced liquidation plays out.
