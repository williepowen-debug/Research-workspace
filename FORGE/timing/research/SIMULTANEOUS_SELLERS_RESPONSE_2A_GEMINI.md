# Systematic Intermediation Constraints and Treasury Market Clearance in Q2-Q3 2026

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Treasury market is rapidly approaching a critical inflection point characterized by a severe structural mismatch between sovereign debt issuance expansion and regulatory constraints on primary dealer intermediation capacity. Outstanding U.S. Treasury securities have grown nearly fourfold relative to primary dealer balance sheets since 2007. Against this fragile intermediation backdrop, a simultaneous alignment of liquidity demands from private credit funds, PE-controlled life insurers, Japanese institutional investors, sovereign reserve managers, and highly leveraged relative-value hedge funds threatens coordinated liquidation waves.

## 1. Validation and Recalibration of Treasury Selling Estimates

### 1.1 Private Credit Funds: $15-25B/qtr
**Validation: Highly Plausible and Consistent with Primary Data**

- Retail/semi-liquid channels now command nearly 1/3 of the $1T US direct lending market (Morgan Stanley)
- Goldman Sachs: gross sales running ~50% lower than baseline years, with accelerating redemptions
- Over 1/3 of private credit agreements in software sector include PIK options — 3x increase in 3 years
- PIK now >20% of BDC net investment income
- Redemption requests reaching 5.2% to >15% of NAV, triggering 5% quarterly gates
- Math: 5% quarterly gates across $1T market = $50B liquidity demands. After cash reserves, $15-25B in Treasury liquidation is accurate.
- Sources: Morgan Stanley PC 2026 Outlook, Markets Media (retail outflows), Investing.com (PIK/stressed debt), AdvisorHub ($5B trapped)

### 1.2 PE-Controlled Insurers: $10-30B/qtr
**Validation: Plausible, Highly Skewed Toward Tail-Risk Events**

- PE-owned insurers: ~14% of industry general account assets but ~40% of financial/ABS private placements (Chicago Fed WP 2025-09)
- Growing reliance on asset-intensive offshore reinsurance structures
- If PC valuations face sudden downward shock or offshore reinsurance structures face regulatory capital calls → forced liquidation of most liquid assets (Treasuries)
- Rating agencies view added risk as manageable under baseline — but synchronized credit event changes calculus
- Sources: BIS Papers No. 161 (life insurance transformation), Chicago Fed WP 2025-09, S&P Global, Moody's

### 1.3 Japan Life Insurers: $12-30B/qtr
**Validation: Accurate, Supported by BOJ Normalization and Historic Precedent**

- Hedged UST return deeply negative vs. domestic JGBs
- TIC: Japanese holdings fluctuated $1.07T (early 2025) to $1.22T (Jan 2026) — significant valuation effects
- **MOF intervention precedent validated at massive velocity:** Sept-Oct 2022 MOF sold ~$60B in USD assets (¥2.8T on Sept 22, ¥5.6T on Oct 21, ¥729B on Oct 24)
- Sources: MOF intervention data, TIC SLT Table 5, Trading Economics (JGB yield), MOF JGB Newsletter

### 1.4 China/Gulf Sovereign: $20-45B/qtr
**Validation: Strongly Supported by TIC and COFER Data**

- China reduced holdings by **$86B between Nov 2024 and Nov 2025** — leading all global counterparts in net selling (Visual Capitalist)
- Holdings fell to **$694.4B by Jan 2026** (from >$1.3T peak in 2013)
- 2015-16 precedent: China sold **~$200B in 3 months** during peak FX defense
- Saudi holdings stable at ~$149.5B but incremental flow redirected to giga-projects/PE/Asian infrastructure

- Sources: TIC SLT Table 5, Visual Capitalist, CFR, State Dept Investment Climate, Argaam (Saudi holdings)

## 2. Empirical Mechanics of Price Impact and Intermediation Constraints

### 2.1 Convenience Yields and Aggregate Demand Elasticity

- Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012 JPE): aggregate demand curve for Treasuries is distinctly downward sloping. Historical convenience yield: ~73bps premium over similar-safety assets.
- **Regime shift confirmed:** AAA-Treasury spread contracted from 67bps historical average to **36bps**. 10Y swap spread: positive → **-26bps** (St. Louis Fed, Oct 2025)
- Elevated deficits + QT + debt glut have deeply eroded convenience premium
- **Market is structurally far less capable of absorbing new supply without demanding significantly higher yields**

### 2.2 The Shadow Cost of Primary Dealer Constraints

- Bräuning & Stein (Boston Fed WP 24-07): shocks to dealer risk-bearing constraints **directly and powerfully** dictate Treasury market liquidity
- When regulatory capital constraints bind → aggressive retreat from market-making → sharp reduction in turnover + immediate widening of bid-ask spreads
- Shadow cost of constraints: **up to 1/3 of dealers' total intermediation margins**
- In primary auctions: tighter constraints → less aggressive bidding → depressed bid-to-cover → higher accepted yields
- In secondary markets: dealers mark down prices rather than warehouse inventory

### 2.3 Non-Linear Illiquidity and Balance Sheet Utilization — THE KEY FINDING

- Duffie et al. (NY Fed Staff Report 1070, 2023): **dealer balance sheet utilization** becomes the dominant variable during stress, not yield volatility
- Market functionality suffers **precipitous, non-linear loss** when intensive intermediation required

**Capacity Utilization → Illiquidity Table (Duffie et al. framework):**

| Dealer Capacity Utilization | Illiquidity (Std Dev Above Mean) | Regime |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | 0.2 | Normal |
| 70% | 0.5 | Normal |
| 80% | 1.0 | Elevated Stress |
| **90%** | **2.5** | **Occasionally Binding Constraints** |
| **96%** | **5.4** | **Severe Dysfunction (March 2020 Peak)** |
| 100% | 6.0+ | Total Intermediation Failure |

- At March 2020 peak: capacity utilization hit **96%** (sample record). Illiquidity spiked to **5.4 standard deviations**.
- Quantile regressions: capacity utilization as explanatory variable → pseudo-R² of **70%**, far outperforming yield volatility models
- **Once dealers exhaust balance sheet space, marginal propensity to intermediate drops to effectively zero**

### 2.4 Quantifying Price Impact: Historical Benchmarks

- Traditional OLS: 19-44bps per $100B (UNDERSTATES)
- **ABFER structural VAR (adjusted for omitted variables): $100B flow shock → >100bps yield impact when dealer capacity constrained**
- March 2020: foreign officials sold $150-200B in 10-day stress window → ~60bps yield spike, BEFORE $173B HF unwind
- Oct 2023 selloff: 10Y from <4.00% to >5.00% — Fed decomposition confirmed driven almost entirely by **term premium expansion**, not rate expectations (FEDS Notes Sept 2024)
- **Rule of thumb:** 10Y-2Y slope rise ≈ 1:1 with term premium rise

**Projected 2026 impact: 75-125bps per $100B of synchronized, unabsorbed selling pressure**

## 3. Marginal Buyer Step-In Levels

### 3.1 US Public Pension Funds
- $7T+ aggregate assets. Historical: ~46% equities, ~23% fixed income
- Funded status has soared: Milliman 100 at **83%** (mid-2025). Corporate DB plans: 105-108%
- Median expected return: 6.50-7.00%
- **Step-in threshold: 10Y at 5.00-5.25%** — at this level, pensions can meet actuarial targets purely through fixed income
- Triggers massive programmatic de-risking: sell equities, lock in long-duration Treasuries
- State Street/Vanguard confirm this reallocation already in nascent stages
- **Capacity: virtually inexhaustible for long-term absorption**

### 3.2 US Commercial Banks
- Treasuries ~11% of large bank total assets
- Attractiveness measured vs. SOFR (cost of wholesale funding)
- **Step-in requires: 10Y premium of 50-100bps over SOFR** (positive carry for profitable HTM accumulation)
- **BUT: SLR handcuffs.** $1B Treasury increase → 4.87bps SLR reduction for G-SIBs (SIFMA)
- 3 of 6 largest BHCs bound by eSLR
- Unless SLR exemption reinstated (would add 5.48pp capacity), **banks remain sidelined regardless of yield**
- Sources: BPI (Treasury market resiliency), Boston Fed CPP 2025, SIFMA

### 3.3 Retail / Wealth Management
- Historically negligible → now formidable force
- Oct 2023 empirical validation: when 10Y crossed **5.00%**, triggered avalanche of retail buying that helped cap yield spike
- In higher-inflation environment (accustomed to >4% in T-bills): accepting 10Y duration risk requires substantial premium
- **Step-in scales at 4.75%, becomes dominant market-clearing force at 5.00-5.25%**
- Sources: TBAC Q3 2024 charge (demand for USTs), TreasuryDirect, US Bank

### 3.4 Friendly SWFs (Norway, GIC, ADIA, PIF)
- Norway GPFG ($1.86T): rigid mandate ~70.6% equities, 27.1% fixed income
- GIC: 51% equities, ~26% fixed income
- **Anti-cyclical, programmatic rebalancers** — do NOT trade on short-term momentum
- Step-in trigger: **relative asset class divergence** breaching internal rebalancing thresholds (not absolute yield)
- When FI allocation drops below policy limits + equity allocation swells past limits → legally mandated rebalancing
- 1-2% rebalancing = **$10-35B per institution** in forced Treasury purchasing
- Sources: NBIM Annual Report 2025, GIC portfolio, Norway White Paper 2025

### 3.5 Foreign Central Banks (ex-China/Japan)
- IMF COFER: USD share 56.77% (late 2025), minor quarterly fluctuations from valuation effects
- Excluding structural sellers: broader CB complex requires deep liquid dollar reserves for trade invoicing + debt servicing
- As yields rise → yield-seeking within conservative reserve mandates
- **Persistent long-term floor, but slow-moving — won't front-run panic**

### 3.6 Insurance General Accounts (non-PE)
- Prioritize strict asset-liability matching
- Require long-duration assets for long-term liabilities
- **Step-in scales above 4.50-5.00%** — yields comfortably exceed guaranteed payout rates
- Substantial capacity but gradual (premium income flow-driven, not shock-absorber)

## Conclusion

**The ultimate floor for Treasury prices, and the definitive ceiling for yields, resides in the 5.00-5.25% zone on the 10-year note.** At this threshold:
- Pension funds can fulfill liability mandates
- Retail investors secure long-term income targets
- SWFs trigger programmatic rebalancing

These provide the massive, inelastic demand required to clear a fractured sovereign debt market.

**But:** banks remain sidelined without SLR exemption, and the path FROM current yields TO that clearing level involves severe non-linear illiquidity once dealer capacity utilization exceeds 90%.
