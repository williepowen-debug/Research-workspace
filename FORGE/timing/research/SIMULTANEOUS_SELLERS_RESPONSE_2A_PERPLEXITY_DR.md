## SECTION 2: Price Impact

### Demand Elasticity Has Collapsed 5x Since 2010

- Krishnamurthy & Vissing-Jorgensen (2012 JPE): convenience yield avg 73bps (1926-2008). Baa-Treasury elasticity to log(Debt/GDP): -1.31 to -1.66
- Convenience yield DECLINING: AAA-Treasury spread 67bps (2012-19) → **36bps (2025)**. 10Y swap spread: +2bps → **-26bps** (St. Louis Fed Oct 2025)
- **Somogyi, Wallen & Xu (HBS WP 26-033, Dec 2025): auction demand elasticity fell ~5x. 1992-2010: -0.9 (1% supply ≈ 2bps). 2011-2025: -0.2 (1% supply ≈ 9bps)**
- At $28T outstanding: post-2010 = **~3.2bps per $100B** vs 0.7bps pre-2010
- Foreign holdings: 34% peak (2015) → 24% (2025), shifting marginal buyer toward more price-sensitive domestic investors
- Chaudhary, Fu & Zhou (2024, Olin/R&R at RFS): aggregate Treasury demand highly inelastic, 1% demand increase ≈ 1% price increase (~10bps yield decline)

### Dealer Constraints Amplify by ~1/3

- Bräuning & Stein (Boston Fed WP 24-07): when demand shifts occur with 1-SD tighter VaR-limit shock, yields move **26-29% more** than average
- 1-SD VaR tightening: 2.1% fall in net position, 1.7% fall in turnover, 2.4% increase in bid-ask spreads
- At auction: 1-SD tighter → bid-to-cover drops 5%, high yield rises ~1%
- Duffie et al. (NY Fed SR 1070): **nonlinear.** Capacity utilization 40%→80% → illiquidity jumps ~3 standard deviations beyond volatility prediction. March 2020 peak: 96% utilization = 5.4σ illiquidity

## SECTION 3: Buyer Step-In Levels

### US Pensions — Most Predictable Buyer
- Corporate funded ratios: **107.1% (Nov 2025)**, $88B aggregate surplus (Milliman)
- Pension Treasury holdings: $1,062B (Q1 2024), 1Y CAGR +12%, 5Y CAGR +7%
- Average discount rate: 5.34-5.43% (Milliman Jan 2025)
- **Step-in: funded ratio >100% AND long-end yields >~5%**
- Oct 2023 evidence: Florida SBA raised FI target 18%→21%, domestic FI +45.7% YoY. NYC Pensions: domestic FI +25.1% YoY. Boeing: $32.6B cash-matched bond dedication (largest in US pension history) at 99.2% funded
- TBAC: cumulative 237bps decline in 30Y yields during 3:30-4:00pm ET since Nov 2016 (systematic pension rebalancing)
- **Capacity: ~$60-130B** ($1.25T assets × 5-10pp shift), deployed over quarters

## Conclusion: The Real Risk Is Concentration, Not Aggregate Volume

Non-basis selling: **$10-45B/qtr base** — meaningful but manageable in $28T market.

**The true systemic risk = tail scenario where basis trade unwinds ($50-200B) coincide with China acceleration ($25-35B) and rising yields temporarily freeze buyer-side capacity.**

Three key findings:
1. **Auction elasticity collapsed 5x since 2010** (Somogyi et al.): each $100B now moves yields ~3.2bps normal vs 0.7bps pre-2010
2. **Duffie et al. nonlinearity**: dealer utilization crossing ~40%→80% triggers discontinuous illiquidity jump. Selling easily absorbed one week becomes market-breaking the next.
3. **Buyer demand robust at ~5% 10Y** with $200B+ deployable capacity — BUT demand arrives over weeks/months, not the days a forced liquidation plays out.

**The gap between SELLING SPEED (days) and BUYING SPEED (weeks to months) is where the true vulnerability lies.**
