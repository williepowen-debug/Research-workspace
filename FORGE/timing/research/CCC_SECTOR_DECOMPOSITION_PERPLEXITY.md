# ICE BofA CCC & Lower US High Yield Index: Sector Decomposition, OAS Attribution, and Systemic Signal Assessment (Q1 2026)

***

## Executive Summary

The ICE BofA CCC & Lower US High Yield Index (BAMLH0A3HYC) is trading at approximately 1,013 basis points OAS as of late March 2026, a level last seen in December 2007. Against broad HY OAS of approximately 342 bps, the ratio of ~2.96x is historically anomalous. However, forensic decomposition of the CCC index's sector composition reveals that this divergence is **substantially driven by concentrated, sector-specific distress** — not broad-based systemic credit stress. The three dominant contributors are: (1) structurally disrupted cable/satellite/media issuers (~24% of the CCC bond index), (2) PE-backed healthcare operators under reimbursement and leverage pressure (~15%), and (3) AI-disruption-exposed software/technology companies (~8-9%). The "software/AI" narrative — which dominates loan market discussion — represents only a *secondary* driver of the **bond** CCC index. The primary driver is cable/satellite TV, a secular death-spiral sector.[^1][^2]

A key methodological point: the $25 billion-to-$32 billion software distress story (Morningstar LSTA) is a **leveraged loan phenomenon**, not a high yield bond phenomenon. BAMLH0A3HYC tracks bonds, and software companies predominantly fund via syndicated loans and private credit — not public bonds. The software distress is therefore partially orthogonal to BAMLH0A3HYC, though credit contagion across instruments is real.

The bottom line for the systemic signal question: **the 1,013 bps CCC OAS is not a replication of December 2007 risk.** The December 2007 level was driven by credit permeating the entire capital structure of the economy through CDO/structured products. Today's CCC stress is concentrated in three identifiable, partially correlated pockets. The 2015-2016 energy sector analog — not the 2007-2008 systemic analog — is far more applicable.

## 2. Sector-Level OAS Contribution Analysis

### 2.1 Which Sectors Are Contributing Most to the 1,013 bps Level?

A direct attribution of OAS by sector within the CCC index is unavailable from public sources without a Bloomberg terminal. However, a framework analysis can be constructed from available data:

**Communications/Cable/Media (~24% weight, extremely elevated OAS):**

- DISH DBS / EchoStar: Completed a distressed debt exchange in November 2024 where S&P lowered Dish Network to 'SD' (selective default). Missed $183 million in interest payments in June 2025. Bonds trading well below par, effective OAS estimated at 2,000+ bps.[^6][^7]
- CSC Holdings / Altice USA: Multiple tranches in the CCC index, among the largest single issuers (~2% of XCCC). European parent Altice France also CCC-rated and in restructuring discussions.[^8]
- Clear Channel Outdoor: CCC bonds, advertising-dependent outdoor media.
- iHeartMedia: Emerged from bankruptcy in 2019, bonds back in CCC territory.
- At 24% weight with average sector OAS likely 1,500-2,500+ bps, Communications contributes an estimated **350-600 bps** to the blended CCC index OAS.

**Consumer Non-Cyclical / Healthcare (~15% weight, elevated OAS):**

- Bausch Health Companies: Multiple CCC-rated bonds across the capital structure, upgraded from CCC to CCC+ by Fitch in April 2025 but medium-term refinancing risk remains high due to expected XIFAXAN revenue decline starting 2027.[^9]
- Community Health Systems: Multi-hospital CCC operator, bonds including 6.875% 2029 and 6.125% 2030 in XCCC.[^8]
- LifePoint Health: PE-backed (Apollo) hospital operator.
- Medical Properties Trust (MPT): CCC-rated hospital REIT facing write-downs on tenant (Steward Health Care) exposure.
- Healthcare services providers carried the highest number of unique defaulters in private credit in the trailing twelve months through January 2026 per Fitch, though their default rate has begun to moderate.[^10]
- At ~15% weight with sector OAS estimated at 1,000-1,500 bps, Healthcare contributes approximately **150-225 bps** to the CCC index OAS.

**Technology/Software (~7.6-9% weight, widening OAS):**

- Cloud Software Group (Citrix + Tibco): The largest single software name, ~2% of the CCC bond index, with 9% coupon bonds due 2029. This is a legacy LBO from KKR/Vista/CPP Investments (2022).[^3]
- McAfee Corp: ~1.6% of XCCC, PE-backed cybersecurity.
- Supporting names: GoTo Group, Rocket Software, Rackspace, Newfold Digital, Virtusa Corp — mostly PE LBO vintages from 2018-2022.
- Combined, technology-labeled software names in the bond index: estimated ~5-7% of the CCC universe.
- At ~7.6% weight with sector OAS estimated at 1,200-2,000 bps (lower end for mission-critical software, higher end for AI-disrupted), Technology contributes approximately **90-150 bps** to the CCC index OAS.

**Financial/PE-backed insurance (~17% weight, relatively lower OAS):**

- The large "Financial" GICS category is populated by PE-backed insurance distribution consolidators: Alliant, Hub International (PAI Partners), Acrisure, USI (KKR), Hightower Advisors, Osaic — all rated CCC due to extreme leverage but NOT operationally impaired in the way cable or healthcare names are.
- These names benefit from sticky recurring revenue (insurance commissions) and have been successfully refinancing.
- Average OAS for PE insurance brokerage CCC names is likely closer to 500-800 bps — elevated for credit risk but not at default-imminent levels.
- At ~17% weight with sector OAS estimated at 500-750 bps, Financial contributes approximately **85-130 bps** to the CCC index OAS — **well below** its weight might suggest, which somewhat offsets the Communications drag.

## 3. Evolution of Technology/Software's Share in the CCC Index (2020-2026)

### 3.1 The LBO Vintage Hypothesis

The CCC bond index's technology/software allocation has grown meaningfully since 2019-2021, driven by a wave of leveraged software buyouts that were subsequently downgraded. The mechanism:

1. **2019-2022 LBO surge**: More than 1,900 software companies were acquired by private equity in deals worth over $440 billion. Major transactions included Citrix ($15B debt package), Zendesk ($4.6-5B), Coupa Software (~$8B), Anaplan ($10.7B), and dozens of smaller LBOs.[^11]

2. **Rating degradation path**: Software LBOs are typically structured at 7-10x EBITDA leverage. These companies enter the high yield or leveraged loan market rated B or B-. As EBITDA growth stalls or interest coverage ratios erode in a higher-rate environment, they migrate to CCC.

3. **From 2020 to 2025**: The ICE "Technology & Electronics" sub-sector grew from an estimated ~5-6% of the CCC index (pre-LBO-wave) to approximately 9.16% by Q1 2024, using ICE classification. GICS "Technology" shows 7.6% as of October 2025.[^4][^3]

4. **Key downgraded software names now in CCC**: Cloud Software Group (Citrix+Tibco post-merger), McAfee, GoTo Group, Rackspace, Rocket Software, Virtusa, Newfold Digital — all PE LBOs from 2018-2022 vintages.

5. **Comparison to 2020**: In 2020, the CCC index was dominated by energy names (shale E&P, oil services) following the oil price shock and COVID. As energy companies resolved (through bankruptcy or oil price recovery), software PE-LBOs backfilled the CCC bucket from 2022 onward.

### 3.2 The Nuance: Software in Bonds vs. Loans

A critical distinction often lost in market commentary: most PE software LBO debt is structured as **leveraged loans** (term loan B, unitranche), not public bonds. The largest software LBO debt packages (Zendesk's $4.6B, Anaplan's debt, Smartsheet's $8.4B LBO) are predominantly private credit or syndicated loans — NOT bonds that would appear in BAMLH0A3HYC.[^11]

The CCC bond index catches only the *bond* tranches of software LBOs. This explains why technology is only 7.6-9% of the CCC bond index, while software is 12-16% of the leveraged loan index. For the systematic distress narrative to fully flow into BAMLH0A3HYC, software companies would need to:[^12][^13]
- Have issued public bonds (not just loans), AND
- See those bonds downgraded to CCC

This creates a **lag** between loan market stress and bond index OAS widening.

## 4. Distressed Ratio by Sector Within CCC (Bonds Below 80 Cents)

### 4.1 Leveraged Loan Market (The Sharper Signal)

The Morningstar LSTA US Leveraged Loan Index provides the most granular distress data by sector:

- As of end of January 2026: **$25 billion** of software-sector loans traded below 80 cents on the dollar, approximately doubling from $11 billion at end of December 2025.[^14][^15]
- By end of February 2026: Software loans in distress grew to approximately **$32 billion**, representing **33% of all distressed loans in the market**.[^16]
- Software holds a ~13% market share of the Morningstar LSTA US Leveraged Loan Index, but ~13% of those loans are now at distressed levels.[^16][^11]
- The weighted average bid of the Leveraged Loan Index (excluding software) was notably higher than the blended index — illustrating software's outsized impact: the LLI weighted average bid dropped to **94.59** at end of February, but excluding software loans, the decline was substantially less severe.[^16]
- In January, software loans declined nearly 3%, their worst performance since 2022, with **47% of software names trading above par at the start of January** but that figure dropping to below **10%** by end of the month.[^12]
- Year-to-date through February 2026, software loans returned **-7.02%**, the worst-performing segment.[^16]

### 4.2 Professional Services and AI-Adjacent Sectors

The distress is not confined to software. Professional services loans declined 2.88% in February 2026, their worst since March 2020, reflecting AI disruption fears spreading to management consulting and IT services adjacent industries.[^16]

### 4.3 CCC Bond Market Distress Indicators

While the bond market lacks the granular loan-level data, several indicators confirm elevated distress:

- XCCC (CCC bond ETF) has returned **-3.74% YTD** through March 30, 2026, and **-4.94% over the trailing 12 months** ending March 31, 2026.[^17][^8]
- The XCCC OAS as of March 30, 2026 is **931 bps** (bid-based, slightly below the FRED BAMLH0A3HYC mid-based reading of 1,013 bps).[^8]
- TCW's March 2026 credit commentary noted that **triple Cs trade at an average price of 70** — the lowest since June 2020.[^18]
- Allianz GI noted that "almost entirely within CCC-rated bonds" were global HY defaults as of December 2025, with a 2026 default rate forecast of approximately 2% — modest in historical context.[^19]

### 4.4 Private Credit Default Rates (Contextual)

While not directly a CCC bond metric, private credit defaults provide context on the severity of underlying credit stress:

- Fitch recorded a **9.2% default rate** in US private credit in 2025, the highest since inception of their private credit monitor.[^20]
- However, Fitch reported **zero software defaults** in 2025 in their PMR (privately monitored rating) portfolio — consistent with the "fear trade, not yet fundamental default wave" characterization.[^20]
- Healthcare services had the **highest number of unique defaulters** in private credit through January 2026.[^10]
- Fitch's US private credit default rate rose to **5.8% for trailing twelve months through January 2026**, continuing its ascent.[^10]

## 5. Ex-Software CCC OAS Estimate

### 5.1 Analytical Framework

Isolating the ex-software CCC OAS requires estimating: (a) software's weight in the bond index, (b) software's average OAS within the CCC index, and (c) the resultant adjustment.

Using the XCCC holdings as of March 2026 and cross-referencing with the GICS Technology sector weight:

- **Software-specific share of CCC bond index**: approximately **4-6%** (technology companies with a direct software/SaaS business model: Cloud Software Group, McAfee, GoTo Group, Rackspace, Rocket Software, Virtusa, Newfold Digital, Ahead DB Holdings). Note this is narrower than GICS "Technology" (7.6%), which also includes non-software tech.

- **Estimated average OAS for software names in CCC index**: approximately **1,500-2,500 bps** (Cloud Software Group bonds at ~9% coupon trade materially below par; Rackspace, GoTo, Newfold are highly distressed).[^3]

- **Weighted OAS contribution of software**: 5% weight × ~2,000 bps average = **~100 bps** of the 1,013 bps total.

- **Ex-software CCC OAS estimate**: approximately **900-930 bps** — roughly 80-110 bps lower than the observed index.

### 5.2 A More Meaningful Cut: Ex-Communications

Because cable/satellite/media is the *dominant* OAS driver in the CCC bond index, stripping communications provides a more impactful analytical lens:

- **Communications sector weight**: ~24% of CCC bond index.
- **Estimated average OAS for Communications names**: ~1,800-2,500+ bps (DISH at near-default prices, Altice France deeply distressed, CSC Holdings stressed, Clear Channel and iHeart operating at elevated leverage).
- **Weighted contribution**: 24% × ~2,100 bps average ≈ **~500 bps**.
- **Ex-Communications CCC OAS estimate**: approximately **700-780 bps**.

### 5.3 Ex-Software AND Ex-Communications

Stripping both the largest OAS contributors:
- Remaining 68% of index at blended ~550-650 bps OAS.
- **Ex-both estimate**: approximately **600-700 bps**.

This 600-700 bps range is elevated versus the long-run CCC median (~850-900 bps on FRED), but would map to stressed-but-not-crisis conditions — consistent with a high-rate environment, a leveraged capital structure overhang from 2021 vintages, and idiosyncratic sector distress in healthcare and industrials.

### 5.4 Key Implication

If you strip out the two sectors with structural (not cyclical) impairment — cable/satellite TV (losing subscribers to streaming, AI-disrupted) and software-specific AI disruption — the CCC OAS is roughly in the 600-700 bps range. This compares to:
- Broad HY OAS: 342 bps
- A "typical" CCC premium over broad HY: 400-600 bps in non-crisis periods
- This would put the adjusted CCC/broad HY ratio at roughly **1.75-2.05x** — still elevated but consistent with normal late-cycle credit dispersion, NOT a December 2007 systemic stress signal.

## 6. Is 1,013 bps a Systemic Signal or Concentrated Sector Noise?

### 6.1 The Case for "Concentrated Sector-Specific Distress"

Several converging lines of evidence support the conclusion that the 1,013 bps reading is primarily a sector-concentration phenomenon:

**Investment-grade and BB spreads are not confirming systemic risk.** BBB corporate OAS sits at approximately 114 bps — tight by historical standards. BB HY OAS is approximately 189 bps. IG A spreads are at ~76 bps. None of these reflect a credit crisis; they reflect an economy where well-capitalized companies continue to access credit at low spreads. If December 2007 systemic risk were present, ALL segments of the credit market would be widening.[^21][^22][^5]

**BB and B-rated HY remain well-behaved.** February 2026 saw BBs return +0.50% (positive), Bs return -0.08% (near flat), and CCCs return -1.25% (significantly negative). This quality stratification — where only the lowest-rated tier is experiencing stress — is the signature of idiosyncratic distress, not systemic contagion. In 2007-2008, the quality stratification ultimately *collapsed* into universal widening.[^5]

**Fitch's forward default guidance is modest.** AllianzGI estimates a 2026 global HY default rate of approximately 2%, "almost entirely within CCC-rated bonds", versus a long-term average of 4.5%. Even with elevated stress in CCC, the systemic contagion is not present.[^19]

**The 2015-2016 energy analog is instructive.** When energy comprised ~16% of the HY index and oil prices collapsed in 2015-2016, the CCC index spiked dramatically (to ~1,800 bps at peak in January 2016) while broad HY widened to only ~800 bps — a ratio of ~2.25x. The rest of the credit market did not implode. Energy's weight in the CCC index was approximately 30-35% at that point. Today's communications sector (cable/satellite/media) plays an analogous role.[^23]

**The software distress story is primarily a private credit/loan story.** The $25-$32 billion of distressed software loans predominantly live in private credit BDC portfolios and CLO structures — not in the public bond market tracked by BAMLH0A3HYC. Fitch found **zero software defaults** in its 2025 private credit monitor, consistent with amend-and-extend dynamics and PE sponsors supporting their portfolio companies rather than formal defaults.[^14][^20][^16]

**The CCC index's financial sector (17% weight) is NOT distressed.** PE-backed insurance brokerage consolidators (Alliant, Hub, Acrisure, USI) are rated CCC purely because of extreme leverage ratios — not because they are operationally impaired. Their sticky recurring revenue streams (insurance commissions) make them more resilient than their ratings suggest. This 17% weight, trading at perhaps 500-750 bps OAS, materially *dampens* the index-level OAS compared to what it would be if the whole index was comprised of DISH-type names.

### 6.2 The Case for Warranted Caution

The above analysis should not breed complacency. Several legitimate concerns merit monitoring:

**CLO CCC bucket mechanics could amplify distress.** Software represents ~10-15% of US CLO collateral on average, with some managers holding 15-20%. When software loans are downgraded to CCC, CLOs face the 7.5% CCC bucket utilization threshold. CLOs breaching this threshold divert cash flow from equity/mezz to amortize senior notes — triggering forced selling of CCC-rated paper that can create self-reinforcing spread widening. This mechanical channel between the loan market and structured products could transmit distress even to the bond CCC index.[^24][^25][^26][^27]

**The 2021 LBO vintage creates a maturity wall.** Approximately 30% of software loan collateral matures by 2028. Companies from the 2021 vintage — underwritten at peak multiples and aggressive leverage — face a refinancing gauntlet in 2026-2028. The EBITDA multiple compression from ~30x (2022) to ~16x today means many of these companies are technically underwater on their leverage ratios. If the AI disruption to software revenue is as severe as bears project, a wave of distressed exchanges and defaults in 2026-2028 could push more names into the CCC bond index.[^24][^11]

**Healthcare structural headwinds are worsening.** The 2025 Medicaid Act and stricter eligibility rules are expected to reduce Medicaid enrollment and increase the uninsured population — directly impairing the revenue model of PE-backed hospital operators (LifePoint, Community Health, Surgery Centers) that populate the CCC healthcare bucket. This is a multi-year headwind that won't resolve quickly.[^10]

**Goldman Sachs credit contagion scenario.** Goldman Sachs modeled a scenario where a 40% recovery rate on distressed software loans produces ~$90 billion in losses — potentially reducing credit to the private non-financial sector by $350-400 billion (~5-6% of new loan flow), equivalent to a 0.2-0.5% GDP drag. This is non-trivial even if not GFC-scale.[^28]

**UBS is significantly more bearish.** UBS raised its baseline private credit default rate forecast to 15% — significantly above Goldman Sachs' worst-case scenario — and warned of potential "serial defaults" and broad transmission risks. This is an outlier view but reflects the severe tail risk if AI disruption proves faster and more comprehensive than currently priced.[^28]

For the purpose of evaluating whether the CCC/broad HY ratio divergence is an early warning of systemic widening (in which case broad HY should converge upward) or a persistent structural premium (in which case CCC normalizes downward), monitor:

## 8. Summary Conclusions

1. **Sector composition of the CCC bond index (~Q1 2026)**: Communications/cable/media ~24%, Financial/PE-insurance ~17%, Healthcare ~15%, Industrial ~13%, Consumer Cyclical ~12%, Technology/Software ~8%, Energy ~5%, Materials ~4%.

2. **OAS attribution**: Communications (cable/satellite — DISH, Altice, CSC) is the single largest OAS contributor, followed by Healthcare (PE-backed hospitals), then Technology/Software. The Financial sector (PE insurance) is paradoxically one of the *smaller* spread contributors despite its large index weight.

3. **Technology/software share growth**: Has grown from ~5-6% pre-2020 to ~7.6-9% of the CCC bond index today, driven by PE LBO downgrades from 2019-2023 vintages. The growth is real but the bond market software exposure (~4-6% when focusing on pure software names) is smaller than loan market narratives suggest (~13-16% in loans).

4. **Distress ratio by sector**: Software constitutes ~13% of the leveraged loan index but ~31-33% of all distressed loans (<80 cents), confirming extreme concentration. In the bond market, cable/satellite names (DISH, Altice) and healthcare (Bausch, Community Health) are the most impaired sectors by price.[^16]

5. **Ex-software CCC OAS**: Approximately **900-930 bps** (adjusting only software). Ex-communications: approximately **700-780 bps**. Ex-both: approximately **600-700 bps**. None of these are "normal" but all remove the December 2007 systemic comparison.

6. **Framework verdict — Concentrated sector-specific, not systemic GFC-analog**: The 1,013 bps reading is **substantially driven by structurally impaired cable/satellite/media names and PE-backed healthcare, with an increasingly significant but secondary software/AI disruption contribution**. Investment-grade spreads, BB spreads, and default rates across the broader credit market are not confirming a systemic 2007-type event. The more accurate historical analog is the **2015-2016 energy sector CCC blowout** — a prolonged period of sector-concentrated elevated stress that eventually resolved through restructuring cycles without systemic contagion. The risk that it *becomes* systemic (via CLO mechanics, BDC redemption cascades, or private credit transmission to banks) is non-zero and warrants active monitoring, but is not the base case.
