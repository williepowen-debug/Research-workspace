### Key Patterns

1. **Ratio always compresses during broad stress.** In every episode, when markets crash, HY widens faster than CCC → ratio drops. The "safe" part of HY gets repriced toward junk.

2. **High starting ratios = sector-specific stress, not systemic (yet).** The 2015-16 energy crisis and pre-COVID both had ratios >2.0, reflecting CCC-specific pain while broader HY stayed calm. Both eventually saw HY catch up violently.

3. **The ratio is a coiled spring.** High ratio = the market is saying "CCC is stressed but HY is fine." When HY finally moves, the compression is explosive. COVID: ratio went from 2.83 → 1.80 in 45 trading days.

4. **CCC leading/lagging is inconsistent.** No reliable pattern of CCC leading HY across episodes. The 10% threshold test shows mixed results.

## What Does This Mean for 2026?

### Today's ratio of 2.96 is unprecedented in the data

- It exceeds the 2020 pre-COVID peak of 2.83 — the previous all-time high
- It's 12% above the 2015-16 energy crisis peak of 2.64
- It's 62% above the GFC starting ratio of 1.82

### The closest analog is January 2020

Pre-COVID: CCC=1001, HY=338, ratio=2.83. Today: CCC=1013, HY=342, ratio=2.96.

**The spread levels are nearly identical.** The ratio is slightly more extreme because HY is marginally tighter relative to CCC.

### What happened next in 2020

Within 45 trading days of the ratio hitting 2.83:
- HY widened from 338 → 1087 (+749bps, **+221%**)
- CCC widened from 1001 → 1962 (+961bps, **+96%**)
- Ratio compressed from 2.83 → 1.80

### Implications

1. **The market is pricing CCC distress but NOT systemic risk.** HY at 342bps is complacent. Either CCC will compress (benign) or HY will catch up (stress transmission).

2. **If HY starts moving, the move will be violent.** Every historical episode shows HY acceleration is non-linear when it finally reprices. The higher the starting ratio, the more explosive the catch-up.

3. **At ratio >2.96, you're in "pre-crash" territory.** The only time the ratio was this high (2020), a crash followed within weeks. The 2015-16 analog (peak 2.64) was already extreme and preceded significant HY widening.

4. **Watch HY, not CCC.** CCC is already telling you there's stress. The question is when HY catches up. The trigger will likely be a liquidity event, not a credit event.

5. **The "benign compression" scenario** would be CCC tightening while HY stays flat — ratio drops via numerator. This happened briefly in late 2015 before everything blew out. Not reliable.

### Critical threshold: If HY breaks above 400bps while CCC stays >1000bps, the ratio compresses mechanically and you're into the transmission phase.
