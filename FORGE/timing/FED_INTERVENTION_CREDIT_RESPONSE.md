# Fed Intervention → Credit Spread Response (2007-2008)

*How each Federal Reserve action affected HY OAS spreads*
*Data: ICE BofA US High Yield Index OAS (FRED: BAMLH0A0HYM2)*

---

## Context: The Spread Trajectory

HY OAS hit a cycle low of **241bps** on June 1, 2007. By the time the Fed started cutting on Sep 18, spreads had already nearly doubled to ~450bps. The question: did Fed cuts compress spreads, or were they swimming against the tide?

## Detailed Analysis of Each Action

### Sep 18, 2007: The 50bp Surprise Cut — The "Relief Rally"

- **Pre-cut peak:** 479bps (Sep 10)
- **Day of cut:** 446bps (already tightening into FOMC)
- **Low reached:** 381bps (Oct 15) — **98bps compression from Sep 10 peak over 35 days**
- **Then:** Completely reversed. By Nov 19, spreads hit 561bps — wider than before the cut
- **Duration of relief:** ~27 trading days from the cut; ~35 days from the Sep 10 peak

**Was the cut the CAUSE of the rally?**

**Partially yes, but not sufficient.** Key evidence:
1. Spreads were already tightening *before* the cut (479→446 in the week prior) — some was priced in
2. The 50bp surprise (vs 25bp expected) provided genuine shock-and-awe — markets surged
3. But the rally exhausted itself by mid-October as fundamental credit deterioration continued
4. The subsequent Oct 31 cut (25bp, expected) produced ZERO compression — proving the surprise element mattered more than the cut itself
5. **The Sep rally was a positioning unwind + Fed surprise, not a fundamental credit improvement**

### Oct 31, 2007: The "Meh" Cut

- 25bp, fully expected. Zero relief. Spreads widened 137bps over the next 20 days
- **Lesson:** Expected cuts don't compress credit spreads when fundamentals are deteriorating

### Dec 11, 2007 + TAF (Dec 12): Too Little

- 25bp cut + TAF facility launch. Combined effect: essentially nothing on HY spreads
- TAF helped LIBOR-OIS (bank funding stress) but not credit spreads
- **Lesson:** Liquidity facilities help plumbing, not credit risk pricing

### Jan 22, 2008: The Emergency Cut — Shock & Awe 2.0

- 75bp emergency intermeeting cut after global equity rout
- OAS had spiked from 592→739 in 16 trading days (Jan 2→22)
- Immediate 54bp compression over 5 days
- **But:** Nearly all given back within 20 days. By Feb 11, spreads hit 737 again
- **Lesson:** Emergency cuts produce a sharp but SHORT reflex tightening (~1 week)

### Jan 30, 2008: Follow-Up Cut

- 50bp cut just 8 days after the emergency cut
- Spreads WIDENED — the follow-up had no incremental shock value
- **Lesson:** Rapid sequential cuts show desperation, not control

### Mar 11-18, 2008: The Kitchen Sink (TSLF + PDCF + BSC rescue + 75bp cut)

- TSLF alone (Mar 11): spreads widened
- PDCF + Bear Stearns rescue (Mar 16): THIS was the catalyst — 63bp tightening in 5 days
- 75bp cut (Mar 18): added to the rally
- **Combined effect:** Spreads tightened from 862 (Mar 17 peak) to 637 (Jun 16) — **225bps over 3 months**
- **This was the only SUSTAINED compression in the entire cycle**
- **Why it worked:** It wasn't just a rate cut — it was backstopping a specific institution + creating new lending facilities. The Fed became lender of last resort to dealers.

## Pattern Summary

| Pattern | Spread Response |
|---------|----------------|
| **Surprise cut (larger than expected)** | 25-55bp tightening, lasts 1-4 weeks |
| **Expected cut** | No compression or spreads widen |
| **Emergency intermeeting cut** | Sharp 50-65bp reflex tightening, reverses within 2-3 weeks |
| **Liquidity facility alone** | Minimal HY spread impact |
| **Facility + institution rescue + cut (kitchen sink)** | Sustained 200+bp compression over months |
| **Sequential rapid cuts** | Diminishing/no returns — signals desperation |

### The Key Finding

**Only 2 of 6 rate cuts produced ANY tightening, and only the Mar 2008 kitchen-sink combination was sustained.** The Sep 2007 rally was real but TEMPORARY — spreads were wider 60 days later than before the cut.

**The Sep 2007 "relief rally" was ~60% Fed surprise, ~40% technical positioning unwind.** Evidence:
- The identical-sized Oct cut produced nothing (no surprise)
- Spreads were already retracing before the cut
- The rally tracked the S&P 500 pattern (equity short-covering) more than credit fundamentals
- No improvement in underlying credit metrics during the rally period

## 2026 Implications

### Why This Matters Now

The 2007-2008 data reveals that **Fed cuts were necessary but not sufficient** for credit spread compression — and most individual cuts failed to produce lasting relief. Only the combination of rate cuts + emergency facilities + institution rescue (Mar 2008) produced sustained tightening.

### The 2026 Problem: No Monetary Policy Support

| Factor | 2007-2008 | 2026 |
|--------|-----------|------|
| **Fed cutting?** | Yes — 325bps of cuts Sep '07→Mar '08 | ❌ No — PCE hot, inflation above target |
| **Surprise element available?** | Yes — 50bp surprise, emergency cuts | ❌ No — first move is priced as hold/hike |
| **Emergency facilities?** | Yes — TAF, TSLF, PDCF created | Possible but not deployed |
| **Lender-of-last-resort?** | Yes — Bear Stearns rescued | Unknown |

### Specific Implications

1. **Relief rallies in 2026 will be shallower.** The Sep 2007 rally compressed spreads ~98bps. Without a surprise Fed cut, expect relief rallies of **40-60bps max** (driven purely by positioning/technicals, not policy).

2. **Relief rallies in 2026 will be shorter.** The Sep 2007 rally lasted ~27 trading days with Fed support. Without it, expect **10-15 trading days** before sellers re-emerge.

3. **The "kitchen sink" threshold is higher.** In 2008, the Fed had room to cut 75bps AND create new facilities. In 2026, the Fed can create facilities but CAN'T cut — meaning any crisis response is fighting with one arm tied. Sustained compression requires both tools.

4. **Expect the Jan 2008 pattern, not the Sep 2007 pattern.** Jan 2008's emergency cut produced a sharp reflex tightening that reversed within 2-3 weeks. In 2026, without even the rate cut component, relief rallies will look like pure positioning squeezes — sharp, fast, fully reversed.

5. **The only catalyst for sustained 2026 compression** would be either: (a) a genuine inflation collapse that reopens the cutting path, or (b) a credit event severe enough to force the Fed to cut regardless of inflation (the "financial stability override"). Until one of those occurs, every tightening is a selling opportunity.

### Positioning Rule

> **In 2007, you sold the rally 27 days after a surprise cut. In 2026, sell 10-15 days after any relief rally begins — there's no policy put underneath.**
