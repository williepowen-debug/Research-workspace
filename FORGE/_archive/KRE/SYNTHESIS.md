## What We Agree On

The stress is real. Both sides now assign eighty-five percent probability that the bearish thesis is directionally correct. Regional banks face genuine pressure from CRE deterioration, BDC contagion, and mortgage stress. The debate was never about whether stress exists. It was about timing and severity.

The capital fortress is weaker than it appears. RED conceded that the 2018 deregulation rollback means most regional banks aren't subject to rigorous stress testing. Self-reported CET1 ratios can't be trusted the way pre-rollback ratios could. SVB proved this precisely — strong ratios right up to failure.

Transmission is faster than historical patterns suggest. The Blue Owl gate transmitted to KRE selling within hours. Repo and SOFR stress appearing now, not in the future, accelerates the timeline. RED moved his June viability estimate from twenty percent to thirty-five percent based on this.

The Fed will intervene. Both sides agree Powell's team will act. The disagreement is whether they act before or after a fifteen percent decline. That timing question is what separates forty-eight percent from fifty percent probability.

## What We Disagree On

The employment firewall. Prome argues Path E, the mortgage stress path, bypasses employment. Underwater homeowners are defaulting while employed because they can't refinance, not because they lost jobs. RED argues employment holding at four point one percent unemployment and two twenty-seven thousand claims provides a buffer that slows transmission. This remains unresolved.

Catalyst identification. RED's strongest remaining argument is that no specific catalyst has been identified to drive an eight to eleven percent decline by June. Prome counters that Blue Owl is the warning sign, Q1 earnings in April is the forcing function, and current repo stress suggests plumbing is already strained. Neither side can prove their timeline is correct.
