# Aluminium & Helium Supply Disruption Assessment
*Date: March 29, 2026 | Trigger: US-Israeli war on Iran, Hormuz closure, direct strikes on Gulf industrial facilities*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**This is significant — not noise.** The Iran war has simultaneously knocked ~9% of global aluminium smelting capacity offline (via Hormuz closure + physical damage to EGA and Alba) and eliminated ~30% of global helium supply (Qatar's Ras Laffan). Aluminium is a large, liquid commodity where 70-80% of Gulf output can reroute via trucking at higher cost — painful but manageable if the war stays contained. Helium is the real crisis: it's a concentrated, non-substitutable input to semiconductors and MRI machines, spot prices have already doubled, and there is no quick backfill. The helium disruption alone could constrain chip production within weeks.

## 2. Aluminium Assessment

### Supply Gap
| Producer | Capacity | Status | Impact |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|
| EGA Al Taweelah (UAE) | 1.6M tonnes/yr | "Significant damage" from Mar 28 strikes | Partial offline, extent unclear |
| Alba (Bahrain) | ~1.5M tonnes/yr (world's largest single-site) | Struck Mar 28, cutting 19% of production | ~285K tonnes/yr lost |
| Other Gulf producers | ~3.5M tonnes/yr combined | Hormuz blockade, unable to ship normally | 70-80% reroutable via trucking |
| **Total Gulf at risk** | **~6.6M tonnes/yr** | **~9% of global 73.8M tonnes** | **~2-3M tonnes effectively disrupted** |

The Gulf accounts for ~9% of global primary aluminium (73.8M tonnes in 2025). The physical damage to EGA and Alba is the key variable — Hormuz-related shipping disruption is partially mitigable via trucking through Saudi/Oman at massive cost premiums.

### Smelter Restart Timeline
This is the critical bull case. Aluminium smelters are among the hardest industrial assets to restart:
- **Orderly shutdown → restart:** 3-6 months preparation + execution
- **Damage/unplanned shutdown:** 6-12+ months. Potlines "freeze" — molten aluminium solidifies in cells, requiring physical demolition and rebuild of cathodes
- **Physical strike damage:** Could be 12-24 months if potrooms or rectifiers are destroyed. The Century Aluminum Iceland case (transformer damage) took ~6 months even with just electrical damage
- **Key point:** Even if a ceasefire happens tomorrow, damaged capacity doesn't come back for 6-18 months minimum

### Can Others Fill the Gap?
- **China** (~44-45M tonnes, 60% of global): Near self-imposed 45M tonne capacity ceiling. Already running at ~97% utilization. Limited room to expand.
- **Russia** (~3.8M tonnes): Rusal could increase marginally but faces sanctions friction and power constraints
- **Canada/Norway/Iceland:** Already at capacity. Century Aluminum restarting Iceland smelter (Line 2 by April 2026) — helpful but small (~180K tonnes)
- **Net assessment:** No quick backfill. Global spare capacity is <2%. This is a structural deficit for at least 2-3 quarters.

### Price Outlook
- **Current:** LME aluminium $3,250-3,450/tonne (Mar 27), highest since 2022 post-Ukraine spike
- **Midwest premium** (US-specific): Record highs, benefiting domestic producers
- **Bear case (ceasefire):** Drops to $2,800-3,000 but stays elevated due to restart lag
- **Base case (war continues 3-6mo):** $3,500-4,000
- **Bull case (escalation/more damage):** $4,000-5,000. The 2022 spike post-Ukraine hit $3,850 with smaller actual supply loss
- **Historical precedent:** The 2018 Rusal sanctions briefly spiked aluminium to $2,700 on threat alone. Actual physical destruction of smelters is unprecedented in modern markets.

### Downstream Exposure
- **Auto:** Ford, GM, Tesla all heavy aluminium users (F-150 body, Model Y structure). Sheet aluminium costs will rise.
- **Aerospace:** Boeing, Airbus — long-term contracts buffer near-term but renewal costs spike
- **Packaging:** Ball Corp (BALL), Crown Holdings (CCK) — beverage cans
- **Construction:** Aluminium prices feed through to commercial construction with 3-6 month lag

## 3. Helium Assessment

### Supply Gap
| Source | % Global Supply | Status |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| Qatar (Ras Laffan) | ~30% | **Offline since Mar 19 strikes.** Helium is LNG byproduct — no LNG = no helium |
| United States | ~30-35% | At capacity. BLM reserve depleted. No spare. |
| Algeria | ~10% | Operating, limited spare capacity |
| Russia (Amur plant) | ~10-15% | Ramping but below nameplate. Geopolitical friction on sales |
| Australia | ~5% | Small scale |
| **Net offline** | **~30% (Qatar)** + shipping disruptions = **effectively 11-30% of global supply lost in 2026** |

AKAP Energy estimate: 30% of Qatar's helium volumes lost in 2026 = ~11% of global supply. But the acute shortage is worse — Ras Laffan was the single largest concentration and contracts are front-loaded.

### Duration
- Helium production is tied to LNG processing. Ras Laffan won't produce helium until LNG restarts.
- LNG facility repair after missile damage: 6-18 months minimum
- **No quick substitute.** Helium is the only element that can cool MRI superconducting magnets to 4K. No alternative exists for many semiconductor processes.

### Semiconductor Impact
- Helium is used in chip fab for: wafer cooling, leak detection, lithography purging, carrier gas
- Major fabs (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) consume significant volumes
- **Korean fabs already drawing down strategic reserves** (per Rare Earth Exchanges)
- Stockpiles: Major fabs typically hold 2-4 weeks of helium supply. After that, production curtailment begins.
- **Timeline to crisis: 4-8 weeks from disruption start (Mar 19) → early-to-mid April semiconductor supply risk**

### Medical Impact
- ~40,000 MRI machines globally, each uses ~1,700 liters of liquid helium
- Helium boils off continuously and needs periodic refilling
- Hospitals already reporting delayed MRI scheduling
- Not immediately life-threatening but creates diagnostic bottleneck, especially for cancer screening

### Price Data
- **Spot helium prices: roughly doubled** since crisis began (Kornbluth Helium Consulting)
- Contract prices lagging but repricing at +40-100%
- Helium is not exchange-traded — no futures market. Pricing is bilateral/opaque.
- Pre-crisis: ~$35-40/Mcf. Current spot: ~$70-80/Mcf estimated

### APD Deep Dive
- **Air Products (APD):** World's #1 helium distributor. Does not disclose helium as % of revenue separately, but industrial gases segment is core business.
- JPMorgan upgraded to Overweight on Mar 20, explicitly citing helium tightening
- APD sources from US, Algeria, Russia — diversified away from Qatar
- **Thesis:** APD benefits from price surge on non-Qatar volumes while competitors with Qatar exposure suffer
- APD FY2025 revenue: ~$12.6B. Helium estimated at 5-8% of revenue (~$600M-1B). A doubling in helium prices could add $300-500M in incremental revenue at high margins.
- **Risk:** APD was already having "helium headwinds" per Q1 FY2026 earnings. The tightening reverses this narrative.

### Other Helium Plays
- **Linde (LIN):** World's largest industrial gas company ($34B revenue). Also upgraded by JPMorgan. Major helium distributor. More diversified than APD.
- **Pulsar Helium (PSRHF):** Micro-cap, pre-revenue helium exploration in Minnesota/Greenland. Pure-play helium but years from production. Speculative.
- **Russia benefit:** Gazprom's Amur gas processing plant is ramping helium. Not easily investable for Western investors.

## 5. Key Risks

### Ceasefire Scenario
- A ceasefire would immediately relieve shipping/Hormuz pressure but **NOT** restore damaged smelter or LNG capacity
- Aluminium: prices drop 15-20% on ceasefire news but remain elevated vs pre-war for 6-12 months (restart lag)
- Helium: Ras Laffan rebuild takes 6-18 months regardless. Helium shortage persists.
- **Net:** Ceasefire is bearish for commodity price but less so than market expects due to physical damage persistence

### Substitution Risk
- Aluminium: Steel substitution possible in some auto/construction uses but takes years to redesign
- Helium: **No substitute** for MRI cooling or many semiconductor processes. Helium recycling systems exist but take months to install.

### China Response
- China could release aluminium from strategic reserves (estimated 1-2M tonnes) to cap domestic prices
- This would suppress global prices temporarily but not address structural deficit
- China unlikely to export reserves given its own consumption needs

### Escalation Risk
- Further strikes on Gulf industrial infrastructure would compound both crises
- If Saudi Aramco facilities are targeted, energy + aluminium + helium all spike simultaneously

## 6. Recommendation

**Yes, this is worth a position. Both disruptions are real, physical, and have multi-quarter duration regardless of ceasefire timing.**

### Priority Trades (ranked by conviction):

1. **APD (Air Products) — Long.** Highest conviction. JPM upgrade provides institutional catalyst. Helium shortage is structural (6-18 months), APD is the primary non-Qatar distributor, and helium margins are extremely high. Not yet crowded.

2. **CENX (Century Aluminum) — Long.** Highest beta to aluminium price + Midwest premium. US-based = shielded from Hormuz. Iceland restart is bonus catalyst. Small cap = more volatile.

3. **LIN (Linde) — Long.** Safest of the three. Massive diversified industrial gas business, helium is an incremental tailwind. Less upside but much less downside.

4. **AA (Alcoa) — Long with caution.** Benefits from LME price but has global exposure including some supply chain risk. More liquid than CENX.

### Sizing Guidance:
- Helium plays (APD, LIN) have longer duration — the shortage persists even with ceasefire
- Aluminium plays (CENX, AA) are more ceasefire-sensitive but have higher near-term beta
- Consider 60% helium / 40% aluminium weighting for durability

### What NOT to do:
- Don't short BALL/F/GM yet — input cost squeeze takes quarters to show up in earnings
- Don't buy PSRHF — too illiquid and too far from production to capture this cycle
