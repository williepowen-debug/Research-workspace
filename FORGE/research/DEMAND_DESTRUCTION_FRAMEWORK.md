## Supply Shock vs Demand Shock — Why 2026 Is Worse

All four sources agree: supply-driven shocks are more reliably recessionary.

**Gemini's unique insight — the "no buffer" effect:**
- In demand shocks (2008), booming economy buffers initial blow (high employment, growing wages, momentum)
- In supply shocks (2026), NO BUFFER. Economy already stressed: negative NFP, subprime auto 7.1%, DOGE layoffs, savings depleted
- Real-time indicators may deteriorate FASTER than Hamilton's model predicts
- Statistical GDP trough still at lag 4, but leading indicators (claims, sentiment, auto sales) may front-load

**Three transmission channels (Gemini):**

1. **Allocative (capital stranding)** — Q2-Q3: auto plants idle, orders cancelled, specialized capital stranded
2. **Income effect (consumer retrenchment)** — Q3-Q4: durable goods collapse, discretionary spending crushed, ripple through supply chains
3. **Monetary policy trap** — Q4+: Fed can't cut (inflation sticky from oil) or hike (recession). C-19 stagflation trap.

**Critical: monetary easing cannot prevent this.** Hamilton's own simulations show that even aggressive accommodative policy cannot circumvent the allocative friction. The reallocation of capital from energy-intensive to energy-efficient activities takes 4 quarters regardless of rate policy.

**RED team concern:** Does demand destruction kill energy longs before credit shorts pay?

**Historical answer: No, in scenarios matching our structural backdrop.**

- 1990 (quick resolution): Oil fell fast, recession mild — both sides wash

**Sequencing conclusion:** Energy stays elevated long enough for credit stress to transmit. Demand destruction is a Q4 2026/Q1 2027 problem. Our energy longs (Jun expirations) sit in the high-oil/low-GDP-damage window.
