# US Munitions Burn Rate — The Hard Physical Clock
**Updated:** 2026-03-22 | **Confidence:** HIGH (consumption data), MEDIUM (exact stockpile levels — classified) | **Purpose:** Campaign duration constraint / trade timing

## Bottom Line

The US burned through years of weapons production in four days. The offensive air campaign can continue for months using cheap gravity bombs — that is not the constraint. The constraint is **interceptors**: the THAAD, Arrow, SM-3, and Patriot missiles keeping US/allied bases alive under Iranian retaliation. These are being consumed 50-200x faster than they can be produced, with no new THAAD deliveries until April 2027. The campaign either ends on Iran's terms (interceptor exhaustion forces withdrawal from forward bases) or transitions to a degraded posture that keeps oil elevated through summer 2026. Every interceptor fired over Iran is one fewer available for Taiwan — and China knows it.

## The 96-Hour Bill

Operation Epic Fury's opening phase (Feb 28–Mar 4) was the most resource-intensive air campaign in modern history.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total munitions expended | **5,197 across 35 weapon types** |
| First 36 hours alone | >3,000 PGMs and interceptors |
| Cost (96 hours) | **$10–16B** (replacement); ~$32B to actually reproduce |
| Day 12 cumulative cost | **$16.5B** (CSIS) |
| Daily cost trajectory | ~$2–3B/day → ~$500M/day as mix shifted to gravity bombs |

Iran's opening salvo forced this expenditure: **800+ drones and 350+ ballistic missiles on Day 1**, over 2,000 drones and 500+ ballistic missiles in the first 100 hours. [Confidence: HIGH — Admiral Brad Cooper confirmed figures]

This is where the war ends. Not bombs — interceptors.

|--------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **Patriot PAC-3** | ~943 rounds | ~2,000 (US) | 620/yr | **18 months** | FY2026: 172 units |
| **THAAD** | >33% of inventory | ~430–490¹ | 96/yr | **3–8 years** | **April 2027** |
| **Arrow-3** | >50% of inventory | ~100 (classified) | ~24–36/yr (est.) | **2.5+ years** | Classified |
| **SM-3** | Significant | ~426 post-June 2025 | ~50–76/yr | **Multiple years** | FY2026: 66 units |

¹ THAAD entered Epic Fury already depleted: 534 delivered through Dec 2025, minus ~150 fired in June 2025 Twelve-Day War, plus ~50 produced since. Last US delivery: **August 2023**. 100 interceptors in pipeline were diverted to Saudi FMS orders.

**FPRI projected exhaustion dates at initial burn rates:**
- Arrow interceptors: ~March 12
- Gulf THAAD: ~March 16
- CNN confirmed: at least one Gulf ally running low on interceptors by Day 4

Iran's declining attack tempo (ballistic missiles down ~90% by Day 15, drones down ~83%) has extended these timelines. Whether this reflects destroyed capacity or deliberate conservation is **genuinely uncertain**. War on the Rocks assessed the pattern as "more consistent with recalibration than capacity constraints." Iran's defense ministry stated March 3 it "has not yet deployed all its advanced weapons." [Confidence: MEDIUM — contradictory signals]

**Gulf partners are a compounding problem.** They fired almost twice as many Patriots as the US. Under FMS rules, Gulf reload orders lag US by 6–12 months. Saudi Arabia's 360-interceptor THAAD purchase now competes directly with US replenishment needs.

## Offensive Munitions — Constrained but Not Binding

The Day 4 "munitions transition" was the tell. General Caine announced a shift from standoff weapons to gravity bombs. Pentagon framed it as tactical success (air supremacy achieved); Washington Post and American Prospect reported it was driven by stock depletion. Both are partially true.

| Weapon | 96h Use | Inventory | Production | Days to Exhaust | Status |
|--------|---------|-----------|------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Tomahawk** | ~375 (319 in 6 days = 10% of stock) | ~2,700–2,800 remaining | 85/yr (surge to >1,000 by ~2028) | 55–70 days at high tempo | **Constrained** |
| **JASSM/ER** | Moderate | ~3,500 pre-war | 550–600/yr (expanding to 810) | ~104 days at high intensity | Under stress |
| **GBU-57 MOP** | 8 (June 2025: 14) | **<6 remaining** | ~0 (line inactive; ~24/yr when active) | **Exhausted** | Cannot hit deep bunkers until Jan 2028 |
| **JDAM** | ~2,000+ | ~100,000+ kits | 35,000–36,500/yr | **Effectively unlimited** | Healthy |
| **GBU-39 SDB** | Increasing | ~30,000 | ~7,000/yr | ~750 days | Healthy |

By Day 14, only **1% of munitions were standoff weapons**. The campaign now runs on $25K–$80K JDAMs instead of $2–3.5M Tomahawks. This is sustainable but represents a 95%+ reduction in per-strike cost — and capability. Without MOPs, remaining deep-buried Iranian facilities are effectively untargetable until 2028. [Confidence: HIGH]

## The Cost Asymmetry — Iran's Strategy Is Working

| Iranian System | Unit Cost | US Interceptor | Unit Cost | Ratio |
|---------------|-----------|----------------|-----------|-------|
| Shahed-136 drone | $20–50K | Patriot PAC-3 | $4M | **100:1** |
| Shahed-136 drone | $20–50K | THAAD | $12–15M | **300–450:1** |

Iran launched ~2,100+ Shaheds in the first 10 days. UAE alone faced 1,422 drones and 246 missiles in eight days. Coalition intercept rate: ~94%. Cost of interceptors in first 100 hours: **$1.7–3.7B**.

Stimson Center: for every $1 Iran spends on a Shahed, the UAE spends $20–28 to intercept it. NYT drone industry executive: ratio could be "60–70:1."

Iran produces **50–100 ballistic missiles/month** and **200–500 drones/month**. Underground production facilities remain largely intact. The attacker's production advantage in drones/missiles vs. defender's interceptor production is approximately **15:1**. [Confidence: HIGH on ratios, MEDIUM on Iranian production rates]

## Seven Single Points of Failure

| Chokepoint | Detail | Impact |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **Ammonium perchlorate** | AMPAC, Cedar City, Utah — sole US source. 600 tons (6.7% annual capacity) needed per 96h of rockets | Every solid rocket motor |
| **RDX/HMX explosives** | Holston Army Plant, Kingsport, TN — sole source. WWII-era. As of Mar 12, **had not received orders to increase production** | Every warhead |
| **Williams F107 engine** | Pontiac, MI — sole source turbofan for Tomahawk, JASSM, JASSM-ER, LRASM | All standoff cruise missiles |
| **Boeing seeker assembly** | Huntsville, AL — binding constraint on PAC-3 regardless of other component availability | Patriot production cap |
| **Gallium** | China: 98% of global production. Banned for US military end-use. Suspension expires Nov 2026 | Radar, guidance, seekers |
| **Rare earths (Nd, Dy)** | China: 90% neodymium, 99% dysprosium. Pentagon holds 2–3 months reserves | Precision guidance magnets |
| **Solid rocket motors** | Only two primes (L3Harris, Northrop) sharing sub-tier suppliers. Disruption at any shared node cripples multiple programs | All missile systems |

Govini analysis: China's mineral export restrictions affect **>1,000 US weapons systems and >20,000 individual parts**. FPRI calculated replacing radar systems destroyed in the campaign's first days requires **more gallium than replacing all 5,197 munitions combined**. [Confidence: HIGH on dependencies, MEDIUM on reserves duration]

## The Pacific Bleed — The Hidden Cost

The Pentagon is **redeploying THAAD batteries from the Indo-Pacific to the Middle East**. Every SM-3 and SM-6 fired against Iranian missiles is unavailable for Taiwan.

- Heritage Foundation (Jan 2026): US forces would exhaust some interceptors after **2–3 PLA salvoes** in a Pacific scenario
- CSIS war games: US runs out of key munitions within **one week** in a Taiwan conflict
- FPRI: "Iran is creating a strategic illusion where the tactical bombing campaign continues, but America's readiness for a larger, second-theater contingency quietly bleeds out"
- Professor Tsang (SOAS): "Could Xi attack Taiwan while US precision munitions are potentially depleted? That is possible."

Undersecretary Colby's pushback ("we're ahead of the problem") is contradicted by the production data. Interceptors needed for Pacific deterrence in 2026 either exist today or they don't. [Confidence: HIGH on stock depletion, MEDIUM on Chinese decision calculus]

## Trade Connection

Three possible paths from here — all keep oil elevated:

1. **Ceasefire/off-ramp** — Oil drops, but Hormuz mine clearance takes months. Strait reopening is not instantaneous.
2. **Shift to less precise weapons** — Civilian casualties spike → political pressure → forced ceasefire. Extended timeline.
3. **Degraded campaign tempo** — War continues at reduced intensity. Hormuz stays closed longer.

**Key variables to watch:**
- Iranian missile launch tempo (conservation vs. capacity constraints — this determines interceptor runway)
- China's Nov 2026 mineral suspension expiry — a ticking clock on production surge plans
- THAAD battery movements from Indo-Pacific — signals of desperation
- GBU-57 MOP restocking (Jan 2028) — deep bunkers are off the table until then
- Gulf ally interceptor requests — competitive with US replenishment

**Duration thesis:** The munitions math supports a minimum 3–6 month conflict at degraded intensity, with no quick resolution pathway. Oil stays elevated through at least summer 2026 under all scenarios. The interceptor constraint, not the bomb supply, is the binding variable on campaign viability.
