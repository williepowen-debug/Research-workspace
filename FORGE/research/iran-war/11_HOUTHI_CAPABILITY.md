# Houthi Military Capability Assessment
**Updated:** 2026-03-22 | **Confidence:** HIGH | **Purpose:** Shipping/oil disruption risk

## Bottom Line

The Houthis operate the most sophisticated military-industrial base of any non-state actor on earth — but it's fundamentally a **final-assembly operation** for advanced systems. They can sustain high-volume harassment (drones, mines, explosive boats) indefinitely without Iran. They cannot sustain precision anti-shipping strikes (cruise missiles, advanced ASBMs) without Iranian component resupply. Harassment alone is sufficient to make Red Sea transit commercially unviable.

## What They Build vs. What Iran Supplies

| Capability | Indigenous? | Iranian Dependency | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|-------|
| Short-range drones | ✅ Fully | None — uses commercial piston engines (~$29K each) | Dozens/month production rate |
| Long-range drones (Shahed variants) | ⚠️ Partial | Iranian turbojet engines, composites | Chinese hydrogen fuel cells now extending range of some variants |
| Basic rockets/artillery | ✅ Fully | None | Ground war capability |
| ASBMs (anti-ship ballistic) | ⚠️ Partial | EO/IR seekers, guidance packages, MARVs | 6+ types identified, all Iranian design lineage |
| Anti-ship cruise missiles | ❌ No | Full systems — turbojet engines (Tolu-4), radar seekers | C-802/Noor variants; entirely Iranian-supplied |
| Ballistic missiles (MRBM) | ❌ No | Complete dependency — rocket motors, guidance | Palestine-1/2 series entirely Iranian |
| Naval mines | ✅ Fully | None | 3,452+ found/cleared since 2018; 17+ models including contact, acoustic, magnetic |
| Explosive USVs (drone boats) | ✅ Mostly | Commercially sourced components | 50-100 units estimated |

Century International study of 140 weapons seizures: **80% of intercepted items were manufacturing inputs** (seekers, engines, guidance modules) — not finished weapons. The pipeline has evolved from smuggling complete systems to sustaining domestic production lines. But the inputs remain irreplaceable.

The Mokha Center's blunt assessment: advanced capabilities are **"nearly 90 percent"** dependent on Iranian-supplied weapons, technology, and expertise. This overstates functional dependency (ground forces and governance are indigenous), but for anti-ship capability specifically — the capability that matters for shipping disruption — it's directionally correct.

## Inventory Estimates (March 2026)

- **ASBMs:** 150–250 units, high readiness
- **ASCMs (cruise):** 80–120 units, sustained production while Iran supplies
- **Attack UAVs:** 500+ units, mass-produced domestically
- **Naval mines:** Thousands available
- **USVs:** 50–100 units
- **Ballistic missiles (various):** 150–250 (CSIS estimate)

Iran's Tasnim News Agency openly confirmed the Ghadr missile was "made available" to Houthis — rare public admission of transfer. The Muhit ASBM is a direct derivative. Chinese hydrogen fuel cells (mislabeled as oxygen cylinders on import) have tripled drone range while cutting heat signatures — first non-state use of hydrogen propulsion globally.

## Supply Chain: Globalized Beyond Iran

Four procurement channels sustain operations:

1. **Iranian maritime** — Dhow transfers to Salif/Ras Issa. Jul 2025: 750 tons seized including Ghadir ASCMs
2. **Omani overland** — Most critical for autonomy. High-value electronics across porous border. Diplomatically costly to interdict. Mar 2025: 800 Chinese drone propellers seized at Sarfayt
3. **Commercial dual-use** — CNC lathes, robotic welders in container shipping. Aug 2025: 44 containers intercepted at Aden; Oct 2025: 2,500 tons of drone manufacturing equipment seized
4. **East Asia network** — Front companies in eastern China procure carbon fiber, resins, industrial machinery. **China has surpassed Iran as largest single source of components** — 35% of seized items vs. 21% from Iran (Century International, 2021-2025 data)

UK government finding: **500 unsearched ships** serviced Hodeidah port in 8 months of 2024 [VERIFY — may be political claim].

## Financial Independence

This is the most underappreciated dimension. Iranian cash transfers ($100–300M/yr) represent only **3–7% of total Houthi revenue.**

| Oil smuggling/resale | $2B+/yr (US Treasury, Jan 2026) |

| Cryptocurrency operations | $900M+ in outflows across sanctioned wallets (TRM Labs) [VERIFY] |

Total peak revenue: **$4–8B/year.** Current (under FTO designation + port strikes): likely $2–4B. Either figure dwarfs Iranian cash support. The dependency on Iran is **technological, not financial.**

## Red Sea Disruption Record (2024 Campaign)

The proof of concept is already complete:
- **150 attacks** on commercial vessels in 2024 (ACLED); 520+ total since Nov 2023
- **4 ships sunk**, 1 captured, 18 mariners killed
- Container shipping through Red Sea fell **90%**; Suez transits down 58%; Bab el-Mandeb transits down 65%
- War risk insurance: **0.7–1.0% of hull value** ($500K–$1M per transit for a $100M vessel)
- Suez Canal revenue fell ~$6B in 2024
- Port of Eilat: 80% revenue collapse → ceased commercial operations Jul 2025
- Cape rerouting: +10–14 days, +~$1M fuel costs per voyage

The Houthis cannot physically close the 16-20 mile wide Bab el-Mandeb. They don't need to. **You don't need to close a strait — you need to make transit commercially unviable.** The Washington Institute assessed they had "effectively turned Bab al-Mandab into an anti-access/area-denial zone."

## Dual-Chokepoint Math

| Chokepoint | Daily Oil Volume | Status (Mar 22) |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Hormuz | 20.4M bbl | Effectively closed |
| Bab el-Mandeb | 6-8M bbl | Threatened, not yet interdicted |
| **Combined** | **~25-30M bbl** | **One-third of global seaborne crude** |

The architectural vulnerability is precise: Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline bypass routes crude to Yanbu on the Red Sea — but those tankers must transit south through Bab el-Mandeb to reach Asian buyers (84% of Hormuz crude goes to Asia). **The workaround for Hormuz depends entirely on Bab el-Mandeb staying open.** Total pipeline bypass capacity maxes at ~5-5.5M bpd — covering only 25-33% of lost Hormuz flows.

170 containerships and 63 VLCCs trapped inside the Gulf. War risk premiums at **5% of vessel value** for Hormuz ($5M per voyage for a $100M vessel). If Houthis activate against Red Sea simultaneously, the entire Arabian Peninsula maritime zone becomes uninsurable at commercial rates.

JP Morgan: disruptions could remove 12M bpd. Oxford Economics: Brent $150-200+ in severe scenarios. Dallas Fed: one-quarter Hormuz closure = WTI $98, global GDP -2.9pp annualized.
