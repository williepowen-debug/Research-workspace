## Research Methodology

**Approach:** Multi-model triangulation. Run the same prompt across multiple AI models (with deep research/search capabilities where available), then cross-reference outputs for convergence and novel data points. Verify key claims against primary sources where possible.

**Models used so far:** Claude (Opus), Perplexity, Grok, Gemini (Deep Research). Claude tends to be strongest on synthesis and analytical framing; Gemini produces the most granular data with best source citations; Perplexity is good for real-time data; Grok is reliable but less detailed.

**Verification standard:** Carnegie Russia piece was verified claim-by-claim against IEA, TASS, Reuters, Forbes, Moscow Times. This became the gold standard for the session — verify before trusting.

## Prompts Used

### Prompt 1: China's Response to the Iran War
**Status:** ✅ Complete (4 models + Gemini)
**Best output:** Gemini (46K chars, most granular data, CIPS daily volumes, Bank of Kunlun mechanism)

```
I'm researching China's strategic response to the 2026 Iran war and Strait of Hormuz closure. I need a comprehensive assessment covering:

1. Energy security response: Is China building strategic petroleum reserves? Redirecting supply chains? What's happening with Chinese crude imports since Hormuz closed (~Mar 4)? How much of China's oil came through Hormuz pre-war (~1/3 reportedly)?

2. Diplomatic posture: Is China mediating? What statements has the foreign ministry made? Are they at the UN pushing for ceasefire? Or staying quiet? What's their relationship with Iran vs their relationship with Saudi/UAE?

3. Yuan-for-Hormuz play: Iran is reportedly negotiating with 8 countries to allow selective Hormuz passage for oil traded in yuan. Chinese-flagged vessels have reportedly passed through. How is Beijing responding to this offer? Are they endorsing it, distancing from it, or quietly enabling it? What infrastructure exists for yuan-denominated oil settlement (Shanghai INE, petroyuan contracts)?

4. De-dollarization acceleration: Is China using this crisis to advance de-dollarization? Any new bilateral currency swap agreements? Changes to CIPS volumes? Central bank gold purchases?

5. US-China tension: Trump called on China to send warships to Hormuz. How did China respond? Are there sanctions risks for Chinese entities buying Iranian oil during the war? How is this affecting broader US-China relations?

6. Economic impact on China: LNG prices (China was a major Qatar LNG buyer), manufacturing input costs, inflation transmission, impact on the already-stressed Chinese economy (LGFV crisis, property sector).

Please cite specific sources, dates, and data points where possible. Distinguish between confirmed reporting and analyst speculation.
```

6. Timeline: How long can Israel sustain this campaign? Interceptor stockpiles, economic cost, public support.

Please cite specific sources and distinguish between confirmed reporting and analyst speculation.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Multi-model triangulation works.** Cross-referencing 6+ models produced much richer analysis than any single model. Each had blind spots the others filled.
2. **Gemini is the sleeper.** Took longest but produced the most analytically sophisticated outputs with the best novel insights (Houthi strategic reserve, LGFV cascade, Bank of Kunlun mechanism).
3. **Verify before trusting.** The Carnegie verification exercise set the standard. Even prestigious sources can be wrong (Carnegie understated Russia's production decline vs IEA data).
4. **Net assessment > confirmation bias.** The Trump steelmanning exercise forced us to stress-test our own thesis. The conclusion (indifference > conspiracy) was more nuanced and useful than either "Trump is stupid" or "Trump is playing 4D chess."
5. **Context management matters.** Three context windows needed for this session. Pre-saving synthesis to files before /clear was essential. The research library structure should prevent data loss in future sessions.
