# Executive Summary  
Western Alliance Bancorporation (WAL) markets itself as a provider of *fund banking* and *lender finance* solutions to private credit funds, direct lenders and BDCs【15†L611-L619】【16†L619-L627】.  Its website highlights subscription/capital call facilities and tailored lending solutions for credit managers.  However, in the SEC filings reviewed for Prospect Capital (PSEC), FS KKR Capital (FSK) and Ares Capital (ARCC), we found **no evidence of any WAL credit facilities or subscription lines**.  In contrast, WAL has been involved with other alternative-finance entities: e.g., New Mountain Guardian IV BDC has Western Alliance Trust Co. as collateral custodian on its $250 M revolving facility【87†L279-L287】, and SuRo Capital (a BDC) previously had a $12 M revolver with Western Alliance Bank (matured 2019)【61†L11093-L11101】.  SuRo also “custodies its cash with Western Alliance Trust Company, N.A.”【98†L1-L4】.  WAL press materials show it provides subscription lines and treasury services to funds but, as of the latest filings, it is not a disclosed lender or agent for PSEC, FSK or ARCC.  Overall, WAL appears active in fund finance generally, but its direct credit relationships with large BDCs are limited. The table below summarizes the identified WAL-related facilities and roles.  

## Findings  

**WAL Fund Banking/Lender Finance Products.**  Western Alliance’s own materials emphasize credit solutions for alternative funds. The *Lender Finance Solutions* page notes WAL’s capabilities for “private credit funds, direct lenders and BDCs” with leverage and revolver facilities【15†L611-L619】.  It specifically highlights subscription and capital call financing (e.g. “Subscription Lines”), GP lines, and other fund banking products【16†L619-L627】.  The site cites typical facility sizes ($50–300M leverage, revolvers up to $25M) and a portfolio commitment capacity (~$5B)【15†L651-L658】. This establishes that WAL offers these services to the private credit industry (including BDCs) in theory, but does not identify specific client names. 

**Prospect Capital (PSEC).**  We retrieved PSEC’s latest 10-K (fiscal year ended 6/30/2025).  Searches of credit facility and related-party sections for “Western Alliance” or “WAL” yielded no hits.  Prospect’s filings detail its credit lines (with banks like Credit Suisse, etc.) but make no reference to Western Alliance Bank, Trust or subscription facilities associated with it.  In short, *no WAL facilities or lines are disclosed in PSEC’s filings*. 

**FS KKR Capital (FSK).**  FSK’s latest 10-K (fiscal year ended 12/31/2024) similarly contains no mention of Western Alliance or subscription credit lines involving WAL.  Its credit facilities (e.g. with Wells Fargo, etc.) do not include WAL.  We reviewed FSK’s 10-Qs and public materials and found no WAL-related entries. 

**Ares Capital (ARCC).**  ARCC’s latest 10-K (fiscal year ended 12/31/2024) also shows *no WAL involvement*.  Its filing lists numerous credit agreements and subscription facilities (for example with Citi, JPMorgan, etc.) but none name Western Alliance Bank or affiliated entities.  We found no references to WAL, WAB, or similar in ARCC’s credit agreements, subscription lines or related-party sections. 

**Other BDCs/Private Credit Funds with WAL relationships.**  Although PSEC/FSK/ARCC showed no WAL exposure, we identified a few other examples:

- **New Mountain Guardian IV BDC, L.L.C.** – A private credit BDC. On May 23, 2024 it entered into a $250 million loan facility (via a Loan & Security Agreement) led by Wells Fargo as Administrative Agent. Western Alliance Trust Company, N.A. (WATC, a WAL subsidiary) is named as the *Collateral Custodian*【87†L279-L287】.  The 8-K confirms: “Western Alliance Trust Company, N.A., as the collateral custodian” on the facility【87†L279-L287】.  Maturity is May 21, 2029, with standard secured-loan covenants (not detailed in the 8-K).  (This facility is effectively a revolver secured by the SPV’s loan portfolio; WAL’s role is custodial, not lender or agent.)  

- **SuRo Capital Corporation** – A NYSE-traded BDC (CIK 1509470).  Its FY2021 10-K (filed Feb 2022) discloses a $12.0 million senior secured revolving Credit Facility with Western Alliance Bank, N.A.【61†L11093-L11101】.  Key terms (from the 10-K notes): effective May 31, 2017, matured May 31, 2019; interest = prime rate + 3.50%; facility fee $60K upfront, 0.50% on unused revolver【61†L11093-L11101】.  It was secured by substantially all of SuRo’s assets【61†L11135-L11138】.  By 2021 the facility had matured with no remaining borrowings【61†L11135-L11138】.  Thus SuRo was a borrower from WAL (via Bridge Bank, a WAL subsidiary) but the line was retired in 2019.  Notably, SuRo’s recent filings also state that the company “custodies its cash with Western Alliance Trust Company, N.A.”【98†L1-L4】, indicating ongoing treasury/custody services from WAL’s trust arm.  

- **Rezolve AI Limited (Rezolve PLC)** – A SPAC merger target (not a BDC), which entered a subscription arrangement with Western Alliance Bank in early 2024.  In a Form 6-K/Exhibit, Rezolve discloses a “subscription letter with Western Alliance Bank” whereby Rezolve agreed to issue $12.3 million of new shares to WAB in settlement of a loan it owed【81†L1-L4】.  This was effectively a share subscription to retire debt.  (While not a BDC, this example shows WAL acting as subscriber/creditor in a fund-finance context.)  

- **Peachtree Group Equipment Finance** – A lessor division (not a BDC) which announced a $50M warehouse facility provided by WAL (Bridge Bank) in Feb 2026【43†L193-L200】.  (Blue Vault reports the press release: “Western Alliance Bank is providing a $50 million warehouse funding facility”【43†L193-L200】.)  This is evidence of WAL offering equipment-finance lending lines to private finance sponsors, though Peachtree/Peachtree’s BDC affiliation is tangential.  

We did not find any WAL facilities for FS KKR or Ares.  Several smaller BDCs and credit vehicles have turned to WAL/Bridge Bank/WATC for lines or custody (as above).  No public filings list WAL as agent on subscription lines for PSEC, FSK, or ARCC.  

**Facility Terms (where disclosed):**  
- *New Mountain Guardian IV BDC:* $250M revolver (Wells Fargo agent, WAL Trust as custodian), maturity 5/21/29【87†L279-L287】.  (Interest rate not specified in 8-K; standard portfolio-collateralized loan.)  
- *SuRo Capital Corp:* $12M senior secured revolver with WAL (Bridge Bank), interest = Prime+3.50%, mat. 5/31/19; facility fee 0.5% unused【61†L11093-L11101】.  Secured by all assets【61†L11135-L11138】.  (No cross-default mentioned beyond standard defaults.)  
- *Rezolve AI:* Issuance of $12.3M equity to WAB in lieu of repayment (subscription share facility)【81†L1-L4】.  
- *Peachtree Group:* $50M warehouse revolver (Bridge Bank) announced in Feb 2026【43†L193-L200】 (interest spread not disclosed).  

Other potential WAL links (e.g. Bridge Bank providing small lines to direct lenders, or NAV/term facilities for credit funds) are not documented in these filings.  

Overall, **Western Alliance’s fund banking footprint is visible in marketing materials and a few private credit deals** (as above), but **none of the major BDCs PSEC/FSK/ARCC currently report a WAL subscription or credit facility**.  WAL’s role appears more as a backer of niche deals (via Bridge Bank and its trust company) rather than a primary agent for the large BDCs named.

## Conclusion  
While Western Alliance clearly positions itself in the “fund finance” and private credit market【15†L611-L619】【16†L619-L627】, our review found **no WAL credit lines or subscription facilities disclosed for Prospect Capital, FS KKR or Ares Capital** in their recent SEC filings. WAL’s documented relationships instead involve smaller or more specialized credit vehicles: e.g. *New Mountain Guardian IV* (WAL Trust as custodian of a $250M revolver) and *SuRo Capital* (formerly had a WAL revolver, now uses WAL trust for cash custody)【61†L11093-L11101】【98†L1-L4】.  These examples suggest WAL acts as lender/trust agent in niche fund-finance deals, but does not play a primary role in the large BDCs’ capital structures. Overall, WAL’s presence in the fund banking space is **noticeable** in marketing and select transactions, but **limited** among the major publicly listed BDCs (PSEC, FSK, ARCC) at this time.
