# Mandatory Cumulative Convertible Preferred (MCCP)

*Extracted from "Short Thoughts: January 12, 2026"*

---

## What It Is

A preferred stock that:
1. **Pays a fixed dividend** (cumulative - if missed, must be paid before common gets anything)
2. **Mandatorily converts** to common stock at a future date
3. **Conversion rate varies** based on stock price at conversion

---

## Why It's Attractive

> "When a common stockholder sees the company issue mandatory cumulative convertible preferred (MCCP) stock, the holder should feel righteous anger. And then **consider switching to the preferred**."

> "MCCPs are not common. The terms are **almost always sweetheart in nature, very generous**."

---

## Example: Shift4 (FOUR) MCCP

**Terms:**
- 6% annual dividend on $100 liquidation preference (~7% yield at $80)
- Paid quarterly ($1.50/share)
- **Cumulative:** Missed payments accumulate, must be paid before common
- **Board rights:** If dividends unpaid, preferred gets 2 board seats
- **Mandatory conversion:** May 2028

**Conversion mechanics:**
| Common Price at Maturity | Conversion Ratio | What You Get |
|--------------------------|------------------|--------------|
| Below $81.81 | 1.2224 shares | More shares (downside protection) |
| $81.81 - $102.25 | Variable | Exactly $100 worth |
| Above $102.25 | 0.9780 shares | 97.8% of further upside |

---

## The Value Proposition

> "The MCCPs are nice because the investor gets **paid a decent rate to wait**, and when the idea works out, **participates in 97.8% of the upside**, through the maturity date."

**Translation:** You get paid 6-7% to wait, with downside protection (more shares if price drops) and near-full upside participation if price rises.

---

## When to Use

1. **Company issues MCCP** - signals management thinks common is cheap
2. **You like the common** - but want income while waiting
3. **You want asymmetry** - limited downside, near-full upside

---

## Red Flags

Burry ultimately exited FOUR despite the attractive MCCP because:

> "Shift4 has a history of shady transactions... I give up after a deep dive on Global Blue, Shift4's most recent and most transformative $2.6 billion acquisition."

> "Silver Lake... has owned the company since 2012, over 13 years, and never got better pricing than 0.29%. Silver Lake is not stupid... If there was an ability to raise prices, it would have been done by now."

> "Silver Lake in 2020 tried to exit through a SPAC. That implies to me that Silver Lake wanted to hide something about the business."

**Lesson:** Good structure can't save a bad business. Do the work on the underlying.

---

## How to Find Them

> "Keep an eye out for new issuances of mandatorily convertible cumulative preferred stock. The Bruker preferred when it was issued was one of the best ideas I had come across in years."
