**What is still true:** Norwegian ran the **first Semi-Annual Sale in its history on 8 July — half off, advertised across its fleet.** That is the company's own announcement. **Truist called it "the most aggressive post-Covid outside of Black Friday promos"** and cut Norwegian to Hold on 23 July. And Norwegian **added 8.9% more capacity** in the spring quarter while posting **much the largest occupancy decline of the three**.

**What was wrong, and it matters.** I argued Truist would not have raised Carnival's target the same day if it thought Norwegian's discounting hurt Carnival. **The article I was quoting says why the target went up: "lower fuel and depreciation assumptions."** Costs, not prices. A cheaper fuel bill lifts a target while a pricing problem pulls the other way — both can be true at once. **So that raise tells us nothing about whether Norwegian's discounting reaches Carnival, and the honest answer is that we still do not know.** **The conclusion I sent TERRY this morning is withdrawn — a bet against Carnival is neither better nor worse than it was yesterday.**

**Three more things I overstated.** The sale is **worldwide** — Alaska and the Mediterranean too — so it shows a promotion that **includes** the Caribbean, not that the Caribbean is where the cutting is concentrated, which is the part the whole story turns on. **My "really only 10–15% off, not 50%" was invented** — the $280 in Norwegian's example is the **free tips and nothing else** ($20 a day, two people, seven days), so it cannot tell us what the fare discount was. And **"the only operator losing occupancy" was false — Royal Caribbean's slipped too, and my own table printed it.** Norwegian's decline is about **nineteen times larger**, which is the claim I should have made.

**One genuinely new thing came out of redoing the arithmetic: Norwegian's average cruise got 12.6% shorter** — from 8.5 days to 7.4 — while it carried **22.75% more passengers**. It grew by running **more, shorter trips**. That matters because shorter cruises carry different daily economics, **so a chunk of any yield move can be mix rather than price.** It is a confound for every yield number we read on the 29th, Carnival's included.

**Nothing here is a trade. No money moved, no level set, both rows still WATCH.** Carnival closed at a 52-week low, **ten cents above our red line**, and a breach is still a measurement, not an action. **Everything still points at Tuesday 29 September** — and the one thing that can settle whether Norwegian's discounting reaches Carnival is **Carnival naming Norwegian, or naming behaviour only Norwegian is doing.** An aggregate yield number cannot settle it in either direction, which is exactly the mistake I made this morning and have now written into the record so it does not repeat.
