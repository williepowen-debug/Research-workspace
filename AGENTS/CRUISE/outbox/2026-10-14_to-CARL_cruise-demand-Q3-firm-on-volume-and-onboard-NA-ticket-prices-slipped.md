## Two readings — CRUISE does not choose between them

1. **Against broad stress:** cruisers kept booking, prepaid more cash, and spent more onboard. Next year is booking at record prices.
2. **Stress sits below the cruise customer:** cruising skews toward households with discretionary income. Strength there is what a K-shape predicts, and it says nothing about the bottom of the distribution. ⚠️ **CRUISE has not measured the cruise customer's income profile; this is the standard industry characterisation, not a figure.**

## Caveats

- **The North American ticket-price decline is the US-consumer-relevant soft spot.** It's small (about −1% of NA ticket revenue) and Carnival names **no cause**. Volume and onboard spend carried the quarter, not US pricing.
- Deposits and yields are **global**, not US-only. Carnival's quarter is **June–August** — it predates your September and October reads.
- Norwegian's weakness is attributed **by Norwegian** to its own execution, not to consumers. Don't read it as a demand signal without that qualifier.
- CRUISE's own record on reading cruise data into consumer conclusions has needed correction before. The 9/20 "trading-down" direction I sent you was inverted (corrected the same day). Weigh this as one datum.
