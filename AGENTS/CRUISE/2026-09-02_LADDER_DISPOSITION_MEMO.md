## 3. The substantive case: the trigger cannot see the mechanism it names

### 3a. The tape does not rank by fuel hedging (KB-CRU-034)

Fuel hedging FY26 — **CCL 0%** (now verified at primary), **NCLH 52%**, **RCL 58%**. If unhedged fuel exposure were the marginal driver of the drawdown, the pain should order **CCL worst → NCLH → RCL**.

| 8/14 → 9/2 close | Move | Hedged |
|---|---|---|
| NCLH $19.01 → $15.57 | **−18.1%** | 52% |
| CCL $28.12 → $23.74 | **−15.6%** | **0%** |
| RCL $305.00 → $265.60 | **−12.9%** | 58% |
| *SPY / XLY / JETS / MAR* | *−1.4% / −2.8% / −10.2% / −6.4%* | — |

The ordering is **inverted at the top** and CCL's excess over RCL is **2.7pp against a 58-point hedge gap**. NCLH's excess is idiosyncratic (funding-gap coverage). Over the ~1-month window the trade press measures, the three declines were **near-identical** (NCLH −16%, RCL −16%, CCL −15%).

⇒ This is a **common-mode sector de-rate ordered by demand tier**, with fuel as a shared input. It is not a CCL-specific unhedged-fuel effect. A trigger that arms *CCL specifically* off a *Brent level* is an instrument that reads clean while measuring something other than what it names. `[[finding_registered_trigger_can_fire_on_an_unnamed_mechanism]]` `[[finding_spread_metric_blind_to_common_mode]]`

### 3b. CCL's own accounting says the prize is second-order (KB-CRU-029)

From the **Sensitivities** table of CCL's Q2 FY26 release (8-K 2026-06-23, acc 0000815097-26-000086, Ex-99.1), impact to adjusted net income:

| | 3Q 2026 | Remainder of 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| **10% change in fuel cost per metric ton (ex emission allowances)** | **$56M** | **$102M** |
| 1% change in net yields | **$60M** | $111M |
| 1% change in adj. cruise costs ex-fuel per ALBD | $27M | $58M |

**A 1% net-yield move is worth more to CCL than a 10% fuel move.** On ~1,377M adjusted diluted shares that is **$0.041 per share per 10% fuel overshoot**, against a guided 3Q adjusted EPS of **$1.35**. A 24% overshoot versus the guided **$812/mt** — fuel/mt around $1,010 — costs roughly **$0.10**.

⛔ **This also corrects a load-bearing figure this desk was carrying.** `workbook/FLOW.tsv` row FL-CRU-06 read *"$145–156M net income hit per 10% fuel move"* — unsourced, inherited from the 3/20 boot thesis, and **~2.7× the company's own current disclosure**. Corrected in FLOW.tsv this session.

### 3c. The asymmetry has already been spent

CCL is **−15.6% in 19 days** and printed a 4-month low of **$23.23 on 9/1**. Whatever the ladder was built to capture, the tape took most of it while the desk was dark. The 8/14 argument that *"the option is cheaper today than the ladder assumed"* no longer holds.

### 3d. And the fuel story is genuinely real — which is the point

None of the above says fuel is fake. CCL's fuel cost per metric ton went **$614 → $793 YoY (+29.2%)** in Q2 FY26 with no hedges, and CCL's own words are *"overcoming extreme geopolitical headwinds and nearly 30 percent higher fuel costs."* The cost line is real and stays live as **VX-CRU-02**. What dies is the claim that it is a **CCL-specific, convex, tradeable** edge triggerable off a Brent level.

Stated up front so the retirement is itself gradeable:

1. **CCL's Q3 fuel/mt prints far above $812 *and* CCL guides FY yields DOWN** — fuel forcing the demand line would restore the convexity claim. Registered as **CRU-08** (80%): if the fuel-attributable EPS hit exceeds $0.10, this memo is wrong and CCL's own sensitivity disclosure needs auditing.

3. **Brent goes vertical (>$120 sustained)** — sensitivities are local, and a large enough move breaks the linear read.

## 5. If Will still wants a cruise trigger — the SHAPE, not the number

**CRUISE proposes no level.** What this session learned about *where* to put one:

- **Key it to the demand tier, not to Brent.** The 8/14→9/2 tape paid demand-tier ordering and did not pay hedge ratios. Candidate observables: an operator's forward net-yield guide direction, booking-pace disclosure, or customer deposits falling sequentially.
- **Grade it against the operator's own published guide, not against peers.** CCL published $812/mt and $1.35 adjusted EPS for this exact quarter. Peer fuel figures are net-of-hedge, from different fiscal quarters at differently-hedged companies — the 8/14 watchlist set its bar that way and it was wrong. `[[finding_cross_entity_comparison_needs_same_perimeter]]`
- **Require two legs, or it is only a cost line.** Fuel *plus* a yield guide-down. Fuel alone is $0.041/share per 10%.
- **Attach a base rate before shipping, and "don't build it" stays a real answer** — which is what §0 recommends.
- **Give any level a drawdown clause.** The 7/17 counterfactual sat 12% underwater before it worked; a level without one is a half-specified instrument.
