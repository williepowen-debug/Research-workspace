## 1 · WHY THE FIRST TEST FAILED, STATED PLAINLY

I wrote a test on an **aggregate level**: Carnival's Q4 constant-currency yield guide near ~0.5% confirms the channel, ≥~1.15% refutes it.

**It cannot identify anything, in both directions:**
- **False confirm:** a 0.65pp drag from *anything* — Europe/Med, Alaska, deployment mix, occupancy, onboard spend, FX, a dry-dock — produces exactly 0.5% with **zero Norwegian effect.**
- **False refute:** a genuine 0.65pp Norwegian drag **offset** by 0.65pp of strength elsewhere leaves 1.15% while the mechanism is real and operating.

**⇒ A single aggregate number cannot separate a mechanism from everything else moving inside it.** The replacement must test **attribution and location**, not level.

## 3 · THE TEST — ATTRIBUTION AND LOCATION, NOT LEVEL

**Read at Carnival's 2026-09-29 release and call. Three legs, recorded separately, never collapsed into one verdict.**

### Leg 1 — ATTRIBUTION (the load-bearing leg)
**Does Carnival attribute yield or pricing pressure to COMPETITOR discounting / promotional activity / industry capacity, in the Caribbean?**
- Sources: the release's outlook language; prepared remarks; **and the Q&A, where analysts will ask directly** (Wells Fargo and Stifel both published this thesis two weeks earlier, so the question is near-certain to be put to management).
- **Record the verbatim quote or the verbatim absence.**

### Leg 2 — LOCATION
**Is the weakness CARIBBEAN-concentrated, or broad / elsewhere?**
- Carnival's stated prior problem was **Europe/Mediterranean**, which it said was *reversing*. **Med-attributed weakness is NOT this mechanism** — it is the older, separate story.
- Sources: geographic commentary in the release and call; any itinerary-level or brand-level colour (Carnival Cruise Line is the Caribbean-heavy brand; Princess/Holland America/Cunard are not).

### Leg 3 — TIMING SHAPE
**Is it CLOSE-IN pricing specifically?**
- The mechanism is **close-in discounting** — a booking-curve phenomenon. Carnival distinguishes close-in from advance pricing in its own language and reports booked position.
- **Advance-book weakness at held prices is a different thing and does not support this channel.**

## 4 · OUTCOMES — THREE, NOT TWO

| Verdict | Requires |
|---|---|
| ✅ **SUPPORTED** | **Leg 1 positive** (Carnival attributes pressure to competitor/promotional activity) **AND Leg 2 Caribbean-concentrated.** Leg 3 strengthens but is not required. |
| ⛔ **CONTRADICTED** | **Carnival affirmatively reports Caribbean pricing as firm or improving**, **or** attributes the yield path to causes that exclude competitive pricing **and** gives no competitor-pressure language under direct questioning. |
| ⚠️ **UNRESOLVED** | **Anything else — and this is the most likely single outcome.** No attribution language either way, or aggregate-only disclosure, or Caribbean not separately addressed. |

**⚠️ "UNRESOLVED" IS A REAL RESULT AND MUST NOT BE READ AS SUPPORT.** Carnival is not obliged to discuss competitor pricing and may simply not. **If the print is unresolved, `FL-CRU-10` stays at Partial with its confidence UNCHANGED — it does not decay toward true for having survived, and it does not get upgraded for a low yield number.**

## 5 · THE TWO FALSIFICATION ASYMMETRIES, DEMONSTRATED

**① Unrelated yield weakness CANNOT falsely confirm.** Support requires **Leg 1 attribution language**, which a Europe drag, an occupancy miss, FX or a dry-dock cannot produce. **A Q4 guide at 0.5% with no competitor-pressure language is UNRESOLVED, not SUPPORTED** — under the old test it would have been a confirm.

**② Offsetting strength CANNOT falsely refute.** Contradiction requires an **affirmative** statement that Caribbean pricing is firm, or attribution that excludes competitors **under direct questioning**. **A Q4 guide at 1.15% with Carnival citing Caribbean competitive pressure is SUPPORTED, not CONTRADICTED** — under the old test it would have been a refute.

**⇒ The aggregate yield number is recorded as CONTEXT and carries no verdict weight on its own.** That is the whole repair.
