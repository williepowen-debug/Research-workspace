## 2. What it means: NCLH's discounting is arithmetic, not mystery

**NCLH added capacity roughly four times faster than Carnival and is the only Big-3 operator losing occupancy.** Its own 10-Q, under its own heading *"Update on Bookings"*, says it *"remains below its optimal booked position"* (KB-CRU-077).

An operator that (a) has grown berths 9–12%, (b) is losing occupancy, and (c) says it is behind on bookings, has **exactly one lever before the ship sails** — price. Cabins are perishable inventory; an empty berth on a departed ship is worth zero forever.

⇒ **This is `FL-CRU-10`'s mechanism with a measured cause.** The desk has carried the Caribbean-discounting channel on **Wells Fargo (9/14) and Stifel (9/16)** — two secondaries that **may share a source** (the desk's own standing caveat). It now has a **primary-derived reason to expect exactly what those notes describe**, computed independently at all three operators.

**⇒ `FL-CRU-10` upgrades from Partial-on-secondaries to Partial-with-an-independent-mechanism.** ⛔ It is still **not** confirmed: no CCL document says NCLH's discounting caps CCL's yields. **What has changed is the quality of the reason to believe the premise**, not the status of the conclusion.

## 3. ⚠️ And it cuts against my own vector — stated because it does

`VX-CRU-03` is titled **"Consumer demand K-shape."** This finding says a material part of the NCLH signal the desk has been reading as *consumer stress* is **NCLH's own supply schedule**.

If NCLH's occupancy loss is driven by adding 9–12% capacity rather than by consumers refusing to cruise, then **NCLH's weakness is less informative about the US consumer than this desk has been treating it.** RCL absorbed +4.87% capacity at flat occupancy **and higher pricing** (KB-CRU-092) in the same quarter, which is not what a broadly failing consumer looks like.

⇒ **The honest read cuts both ways:**
- **Strengthens** the expectation that NCLH discounts (supply overhang is real and self-inflicted) — good for the `FL-CRU-10` channel.
- **Weakens** the inference from NCLH weakness to consumer stress — bad for `VX-CRU-03` as a *consumer* vector.

**Neither vector is re-scored here.** A finding that argues against my own framing gets recorded before it gets acted on.

## 4. ⛔ Four caveats, each of which could change the reading

1. **Occupancy is a VOLUME measure, not a price measure.** It establishes that NCLH has a fill problem. It does **not** by itself prove NCLH cut price. The price leg still rests on NCLH's own "below optimal booked position" plus WF/Stifel. Strong circumstantial, not proof.
2. **⛔ COMPARE THE CHANGE, NEVER THE LEVEL.** RCL 110.2% / CCL 104% / NCLH 102.4% are **brand-mix artifacts** — RCL runs large family ships that sell 3rd and 4th berths; NCLH's Oceania and Regent are premium/luxury with far less multi-occupancy. Reading "NCLH 102.4 vs RCL 110.2" as NCLH being 7.8pp worse is a category error. **Only the YoY delta is comparable across operators.**
3. **CCL's quarter is Mar–May; RCL's and NCLH's are Apr–Jun.** A one-month offset, not adjusted.
4. **⚠️ NCLH's Q1 figures are DERIVED (H1 minus Q2), not disclosed.** Capacity +12.15% and occupancy +2.26pp are my subtraction, not NCLH's statement. Arithmetic on two disclosed periods, but flagged as derived.

## 6. Two incidental finds, both bearing on `CRU-08`

**(a) ⚠️ THE SHARE-COUNT CONFOUND IS BIGGER THAN LOGGED, AND IT RUNS BOTH WAYS.** CCL's 10-Q: *"In December 2025, we settled $1.1 billion principal amount of the 2027 Convertible Notes, resulting in the issuance of **69.1 million shares** of Carnival Corporation common stock and a cash payment of $500 million."*

The desk logged the buyback's ~30M-share retirement as a `CRU-08` confound worth ~$0.030/sh (KB-CRU-081). **It did not hold the 69.1M shares ISSUED in Dec 2025** — more than twice the retirement, in the opposite direction, inside FY26. ⇒ At the 9/29 print, **the share count must be read as a net of both**, and `CRU-08`'s per-share arithmetic cannot be anchored on the buyback alone. **`CRU-08` remains un-re-tuned and inside its window; this is registered as a second disclosed confound.**

**(b) CCL covenant floor, and a coincidence not to over-read.** CCL's most restrictive covenants include *"Maintain minimum liquidity of $1.5 billion"*; CCL holds $6.7B — comfortable, 4.5× the floor. ⚠️ **NCLH's total liquidity is $1.5B** — numerically the same as CCL's floor. ⛔ **This says nothing about NCLH's covenants**, which are its own and were not read. Recorded as a **question to check**, explicitly not as a finding: *what is NCLH's minimum-liquidity covenant, and where does $1.5B sit against it?*
