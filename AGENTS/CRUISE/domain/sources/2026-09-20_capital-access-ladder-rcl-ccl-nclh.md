## 4. Implied cash cost of debt — ⚠️ computed, and it does NOT discriminate. Read the caveats.

| Operator | Annualised Q2 interest | / gross debt | = |
|----------|----:|----:|----:|
| RCL | $944M | $22,836M | **4.13%** |
| CCL | $1,140M | $25,570M | **4.46%** |
| NCLH | $684M | $15,035M | **4.55%** |

⛔ **Do NOT cite this spread of 42bp as a K-shape measure. Three basis defects, each material:**
1. **Capitalised interest.** All three capitalise interest on ships under construction. RCL has **$14.3B** of ships on order and therefore capitalises the most, mechanically *understating* its reported expense relative to peers. The error correlates with the thing being measured.
2. **NCLH's figure is NET of interest income** (`InterestIncomeExpenseNonoperatingNet`); RCL's and CCL's are gross (`InterestExpenseNonoperating`). NCLH's gross is higher, so the true gap is wider than shown.
3. **CCL's quarter is Mar–May; RCL/NCLH Apr–Jun** — a one-month offset.

⇒ **Average cost of debt is backward-looking (it prices the legacy stack). The MARGINAL cost is the discriminator, and that is §1–§2.** This table is recorded so the next reader does not re-derive it and mistake it for a finding.

⚠️ **Tagging trap, recorded:** NCLH tags `InterestExpenseNonoperating` at **$5.8M** for Q2-26. That is a component, not the interest bill — the desk's own KB has NCLH FY interest ~$695M. Comparing $5.8M against RCL's $236M would have produced a 40× false finding from two internally-consistent XBRL cells. **The tag name is not the quantity.** (Instrument-measures-wrong-referent class.)

## 5. Two dated bounds this sweep puts on `FL-CRU-10`

- **RCL Q2 10-Q, verbatim:** revenue rose *"primarily due to an increase in capacity and **higher pricing** in 2026 compared to the same period in 2025"*; and a *"$155 million increase driven by **higher pricing** on both existing ships and new ships."* ⇒ **Through 6/30/26, NCLH's discounting had NOT reached RCL's realised pricing.** No contradiction with WF/Stifel — their claims are about **Q4-26/Q1-27** — but it dates the channel: whatever is happening, it had not shown in a third-party operator's *realised* Q2 numbers.
- **The 4-of-16 financing contingency** is the supply-side tell: NCLH's capacity growth is conditional in a way its peers' is not.
