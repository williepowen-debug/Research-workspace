## 1. ⛔ ACCESS FINDING: no Florida port publishes a monthly cruise passenger series

Checked all three homeports at their own surfaces:

| Port | Cruise data published | Monthly anything? | Verdict |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Port Everglades** | Annual, by fiscal year, `/about-us/statistics/cruise-statistics/` | **Monthly TEUs (cargo) only** — latest July 2026 | Cruise is annual-only |
| **PortMiami** | Annual table, FY2015–FY2025, on `cruise.page` | None found | Annual-only |

⇒ **This is an ACCESS gap, not a research gap** — same class as the war-risk premium (`WQ-230`). No amount of further searching produces a monthly series that is not published. Recorded so it is not re-attempted.

## 3. ⛔ THE TEMPTING INFERENCE, AND WHY I AM NOT MAKING IT

The attractive read is: **record volumes + falling yield guidance = operators filling ships by cutting price**, which would be `FL-CRU-10`'s mechanism confirmed from physical data that cannot share a source with WF/Stifel.

**It does not survive two checks:**

1. **⛔ TIMING — the data predates the stress.** Port FY2025 = **Oct 2024 → Sep 2025**. The yield deterioration is a **calendar-2026** event: CCL's 100bp FY26 CC yield cut runs Mar→Jun 2026 (KB-CRU-080), the WF/Stifel Caribbean-discounting notes are **Sept 2026** (KB-CRU-070). **FY2025 volumes cannot speak to a 2026 mechanism.** Citing them as evidence for the current thesis would be a vintage error of roughly a year.

2. **⛔ CAPACITY — volume growth is partly supply, and unmeasured.** Port Canaveral went to **18 homeported ships**; Port Everglades to **40 ships across 9 lines**. More berths produce more passengers **mechanically**, with no demand implication whatsoever. **Passenger counts cannot separate demand from supply without a berth denominator** — which is exactly Thread C. Until volumes are normalised per available berth-day, "record passengers" is a statement about the size of the fleet, not about how much anyone is willing to pay.

⇒ **Both defects run the same direction: they make the bullish-volume reading look stronger than the evidence supports.** Named rather than used.

## 4. ✅ THE CATALYST — and it is 10 days out, then ~6 weeks

**FY2026 closes 2026-09-30.** That fiscal year — **Oct 2025 → Sep 2026** — is the **first port-volume window that overlaps the stress period**. Historical publication pattern: preliminary counts land ~Oct–Nov, the ACFR in the following spring.

| Date | Event | Why it matters |
|------|-------|----------------|
| **2026-09-29** | CCL Q3 print | Operator's own words on yield (`CRU-09`) |
| **2026-09-30** | **Florida port FY2026 closes** | The physical-volume window over the stress period shuts |
| **~Oct–Nov 2026** | Preliminary FY2026 port counts publish | **First independent physical read on whether ships were filled** |

⇒ **This lands AFTER the print, so it cannot inform the 9/29 trade — it adjudicates the thesis ex post.** That is still worth having: if CCL guides yields down on 9/29 **and** FY2026 Florida volumes come in at or above the ~9M Canaveral budget, the discounting mechanism is confirmed from two independent directions. If volumes *miss* while yields fall, it is demand destruction, not discounting — **a materially different thesis with a different trade.**
