## What the same materials add — and what they cannot tell us

- **Canaveral's attachment route is now closed as a search gap.** The prior September 20 paper inspected agenda titles and explicitly left financial attachments unexamined. This pass read both the September statistical attachment and CFO financial memo. It recovered the full fiscal-year count and month-on-month-year comparison without waiting for the October 28 meeting. The title-level publication gap is no longer the desk's live conclusion.
- **Supply context is visible.** Canaveral's table lists homeport and port-of-call vessels, with materially different vessel allocations across years. Galveston's calls grew 7.38% beside passenger turns 8.79%, and its rows also show changed vessel deployments. Calls do not measure lower-berth capacity days; passenger counts per call would mix ship size, turn timing and transit calls. No load-factor or consumer-demand attribution follows.
- **Port revenue is local port exposure, not cruise fare revenue.** Canaveral's same statistical report shows multi-day cruise revenue **$210.0M vs $181.9M** (+15.47%). Galveston's CFO report labels YTD passenger-service revenue **$38.19M vs $18.67M**. Those are port receipts subject to tariffs, contracts, guarantees and accounting; they do not measure operator realized fares or port-city spending. No economic-impact multiplier, employment estimate or profit model was built.
- **The Galveston budget miss is real and limited.** Growth versus last year coexists with a 3.7% miss against the port's plan. Without a capacity/itinerary and budget-assumption bridge, that is not a demand-destruction verdict. No new gate is inferred.
