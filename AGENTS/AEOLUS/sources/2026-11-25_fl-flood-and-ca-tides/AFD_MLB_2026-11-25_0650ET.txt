.DISCUSSION...
Issued at 300 AM EST Wed Nov 25 2026

Today-Tonight...Showers have already started developing across 
east central Florida early this morning as a trough situated over 
the southeastern Gulf advects moisture across the peninsula. Early
rainfall totals as of 3 AM across the Treasure Coast have 
generally been between 0.5" to 1". This trough is anticipated to 
become better organized through this morning, developing as an 
area of low pressure through the morning hours. Aloft, a strong 
trough digging southward across the southeastern US and the Gulf 
will assist in lifting the surface low across south Florida and 
within vicinity of east central Florida. Since the low has not 
quite yet developed, there still remains some discrepancies on 
where exactly the low will track through today. Regardless, 
increasing rain chances are forecast, with PoPs reaching 70-90% 
across east central Florida. A Flood Watch remains in effect 
across Brevard, Indian River, St. Lucie, Martin, and Okeechobee 
counties. WPC also maintains a Marginal Risk (1/4) of excessive 
rainfall across these same areas. These locations have the best 
chances of seeing rainfall totals of 1-2" and localized higher 
amounts of 3-4". While amounts exceeding 4" cannot be fully ruled 
out, confidence in seeing those totals remains low, especially 
given the uncertainty relative to where the area of low pressure 
will track. If the low tracks farther north, then higher amounts 
exceeding 4" could be realized. Across the remainder of east 
central Florida, rainfall totals of 0.5-1.5" are forecast, with 
higher amounts up to 3" possible. Flooding will be the primary 
concern with activity today, especially across areas within the 
Flood Watch that remain heavily saturated from prior rainfall. 
Unlike previous events, however, this system is anticipated to 
continue moving through the area rather than stalling out for 
several days.

While flooding rain will be the primary concern today, there is a
20-40% chance of storm development embedded within activity. At 
this time, primary storm concerns include lightning strikes and 
wind gusts up to 50 mph. However, as mentioned previously, there 
remains a good bit of uncertainty relative to the exact track of 
the low. If the low tracks more northward, this would put portions
of east central Florida within the warm sector and lead to an 
increase in the risk for severe weather, particularly across the 
Treasure Coast and near Lake Okeechobee. If this were to occur, 
greater instability, cool temperatures aloft, and modest shear 
would support the potential for wind gusts of 60 mph and greater 
as well as a low chance for the development of a tornado or two. 
Again, this is a conditional threat and is dependent on the low 
shifting farther north, so keep an eye on the forecast and be sure
to have multiple ways of receiving weather alerts just in case.

Seas gradually build across the local Atlantic waters with the 
passage of the low, resulting in a high risk of rip currents and 
rough surf along the entire east central Florida coast. Entering 
the ocean is not advised. Additionally, high astronomical tides 
are forecast today, increasing the risk of minor coastal flooding,
especially during the morning high tide cycle. A Coastal Flood 
Advisory is in effect along the coast starting at 5 AM and will 
continue through the remainder of today. 

Thursday-Saturday...By Thursday, the mid-level trough axis sweeps
directly across the Florida peninsula, lifting northeastward 
through the day. The cut-off low positioned across the southeast 
US will gradually lift northeastward towards the Mid-Atlantic on 
Friday, pulling farther away from Florida as some energy lingers 
across the peninsula into Saturday. At the surface, the low will 
push into the Carolinas on Thursday as upper level support lifts 
it away from Florida. High pressure begins to build across the 
southeast US and the Gulf behind the low, extending towards 
Florida through late week and into early weekend. Some lingering 
isolated showers will be possible through early Thursday morning, 
with conditions trending drier for the remainder of Thanksgiving 
Day as the low departs. Friday and Saturday, drier air settles 
completely across the Florida peninsula, resulting in near-zero 
rain chances each day. 

Breezy winds develop areawide on Thursday as the low departs, 
with westerly winds increasing to 15 to 20 mph with gusts up to 30
mph possible. Winds subside to around 10 mph on Friday out of the
west-northwest, becoming light and variable to calm on Saturday 
as the high situates itself directly overhead. Temperatures are 
forecast to be near to slightly below normal Thursday through 
Saturday, with highs in the mid 60s to mid 70s and lows in the 
upper 40s to mid 50s.

Lingering elevated seas will support a continued high risk of rip
currents through at least Thursday. Additional extensions through
the remainder of the week cannot be ruled out. Entering the ocean
is not advised.

Sunday-Tuesday...An elongated streak of vorticity is forecast to 
position itself across the Florida peninsula late this weekend 
into early next week while another mid-level trough across the 
central US drifts eastward. At the surface, the high pressure 
situated across the Gulf and Florida is shoved eastward out across
the western Atlantic as east central Florida becomes sandwiched 
between a stalled boundary across the Bahamas and Cuba and a 
weakening cold front drifting across the southeastern US. As a 
result, moisture is forecast to increase across the Florida 
peninsula, with a return of isolated to scattered showers forecast
from Sunday through Tuesday (30-50%). Storm development cannot be
ruled out with activity, but it remains too early to determine 
exact storm threats at this time. Temperatures are forecast to 
slowly warm through the extended period, with highs remaining 
generally near normal while lows remain generally above normal.

Poor to hazardous boating conditions are forecast to develop 
across the local Atlantic waters today as an area of low pressure 
moves near the waters, resulting in increasing winds and building 
seas. A Small Craft Advisory goes into effect across the offshore 
Volusia and Brevard waters at 4 AM, expanding to the rest of the 
waters at 10 AM. Winds gradually increase to 20 to 30 knots today,
with seas of 6 to 10 feet building throughout the day. 
Additionally, numerous to widespread showers and isolated to 
scattered storms are forecast. Primary concerns with any storms 
that develop include lightning strikes, wind gusts in excess of 30
knots, and heavy downpours. A brief waterspout or two also cannot
be ruled out, though chances remains low. 

Hazardous boating conditions will continue into Thursday behind 
the low with westerly winds 20 to 30 knots and seas 5 to 9 feet. 
Winds and seas begin to subside on Friday, with a return of 
generally favorable boating conditions forecast from Saturday 
through the extended period. Rain and storm chances diminish on 
Thursday as drier air settles across the area, with dry conditions
prevailing through Saturday. By Sunday, rain chances are forecast
to increase once again to 30-60% across the local waters.
