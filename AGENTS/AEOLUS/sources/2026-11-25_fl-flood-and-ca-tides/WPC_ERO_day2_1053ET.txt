Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
1053 AM EST Wed Nov 25 2026
Day 2
Valid 12Z Thu Nov 26 2026 - 12Z Fri Nov 27 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FROM THE DELMARVA
PENINSULA TO THE GREATER NEW YORK CITY AREA...

The powerful surface low tracking along the East Coast will make
weather headlines for the Thanksgiving holiday, with widespread
heavy rainfall expected from Virginia northward to southern New
England. A fierce 850 mb low level jet reaching speeds up to 85
knots (100 mph) will result in intense warm air advection, bringing
PWs up to 1.5 inches or even slightly higher across eastern
portions of the Mid-Atlantic, which is easily 3 standard deviations
above normal for late November. Heavier rainfall bands will be the
result of strong 850-500 mb frontogenesis ahead of the surface
low, with rainfall rates near 1 inch per hour. These rates could
last for a few hours as the core of the warm conveyor belt moves
through, leading to widespread 1.5 to 3 inch totals from the
DelMarVA Peninsula to eastern Massachusetts, heaviest east of the
Interstate 95 corridor. Slightly lighter totals of 1-2 inches are
likely farther inland towards the central Appalachians.

The mitigating factors in this situation are the dry antecedent
conditions across this region and the rapid overall movement of the
storm system. This still favors keeping most of the outlook level
at Marginal Risk. However, a Slight Risk has been introduced from
near Ocean City, Maryland northward to the greater New York City
metro area where the greatest model consensus exists for the
highest QPF and thus highest rainfall rates. The heaviest rainfall
should occur within the 12Z-00Z time period from Long Island and
points south, and between 00-06Z across southeastern New England
before rapidly lifting northward out of the region and weakening.

...Pacific Northwest...

The ongoing moisture surge from the end of Day 1 is expected to
persist into the 12Z-00Z time period, and dropping a little more to
the south to include more of northwestern Oregon. The flow should
generally be SW to NE, and this will tend to elevate rainfall rates
on the windward terrain of the Cascades and the coastal ranges up
to the Olympics. Most of the model guidance is indicating the
potential of 1-3 inches of rain and locally higher, which would be
falling on increasingly saturated ground from rainfall earlier in
the week. The Marginal Risk area remains, but the southern portion
has been trimmed back a little to account for recent trends.
