Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
1053 AM EST Wed Nov 25 2026
Day 1
Valid 16Z Wed Nov 25 2026 - 12Z Thu Nov 26 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS PORTIONS OF
THE COASTAL CAROLINAS AND SOUTH FLORIDA...

...Southeast U.S. Coast...

16Z Update: Forecast remains on track for the upcoming east coast
cyclone with heightened impacts slated to occur over the coastal
Carolina's to the VA Tidewater region later this evening through
tomorrow morning. Latest REFS mean QPF maintains a strong signal
for 5-8" of rainfall in the vicinity of Wilmington up through the
central portion of the Outer Banks with significant rainfall
potential even extending a bit further inland as the low track
maintains headway into eastern NC by tonight. REFS neighborhood and
EAS probabilities are incredibly robust for >3" across all of
eastern NC up to the Hampton Roads area of VA which spells trouble
for the immediate coast when you account for astronomical tide
cycles and persistent onshore flow prompting coastal flooding
across the area. Given the signal for >3" locally across the VA
Tidewater, and with the historical precedence for flash flooding
in the Norfolk/VA Beach area, decided to up the threat to a high-
end SLGT over far southeastern VA to account for the crescendo in
the signal.

Also expanded the SLGT further inland to encompass the Raleigh-
Durham metro as the 12z CAMs and 75-90th percentile NBM have
increased the threat for 3+ inches of rainfall significantly in the
area over the last few model cycles. This likely stems from the
western shift of the low creating a stronger 925-700mb moisture
extension further inland as prevailing east to southeast flow will
advect appreciable moisture into the area during the overnight
period tonight into early tomorrow morning. Latest ECMWF IVT
numbers are insisting on 800-1000 kg/ms advecting into the area
overnight leading to an increase in heavy rainfall all across the
eastern two-thirds of the state by midnight LST. By the early
morning hours, that IVT advection regime will reach its peak
leading to a tongue of 1200-1400 kg/ms of integrated moisture
transport being ushered into the area which will promote rates of
up to 1.5-2"/hr for a few hours before the setup quickly shifts
north. This period will be the time frame where most flash flooding
could occur, likely between the 06-12z time frame, perhaps
lingering into the D2 period initially. In any case, there is a
growing consensus for what will be an impactful storm across much
of the Carolina's with a strong signal for flooding and winds over
NC up into southeastern VA. For that, the SLGT expansion was
warranted with an emphasis on the coastal areas, as well as any
urbanized setting where runoff capabilities are more applicable.

..Previous Discussion..

A strong surface low is organizing off the Florida coast and
tracking in a general northward direction across the eastern
Carolinas through Wednesday night. As the low deepens near the
coast, rapidly increasing onshore flow and low level moisture
convergence along the developing coastal front will provide
sustained deep synoptic and mesoscale lift. An impressive easterly
low level jet at 850 mb increases to 65-75 knots (75-85 mph) just
north of the surface low center, intersecting the coastal front and
supporting the development of embedded convection with deep warm
layer cloud depths. Warm sea surface temperatures for this time of
year will also add anomalous moisture into the lower troposphere,
helping to increase MUCAPE to near 500 J/kg close to the southern
outer banks.

This will likely lead to a 2-4 hour period of rainfall rates of
1-1.5 inches per hour as the low steadily moves northward. This
would result in areal rainfall totals of 3 to 6 inches, with the
highest totals expected to be near Newport and Morehead City. The
time period of greatest concern is from 00-06Z for this region when
the majority of the rainfall is expected, and then 6-12Z across
northeast NC and into the Hampton Roads area.

The Slight Risk area has been extended northward up to include most
of southeast Virginia, including Hampton Roads cities, given a
faster trend with the RRFS and HRRR guidance. The central North
Carolina coast between Cedar Island and Cape Lookout is close to
needing a Moderate Risk given the general increase in QPF trends,
and this is something that will be re-evaluated on the day shift.

...Pacific Northwest...

16Z Update: No changes to the previous forecast as run to run
continuity in both the synoptic evolution and expected rainfall
remain consistent. Area of concern will likely reside within the
confines of the Skokomish River basin where river flooding is more
applicable given the slightly flashier nature of the river itself.
Expecting 2-4" with locally as high as 6" in the coastal areas of
WA state into the Olympic's.

Kleebauer

..Previous Discussion..

An active pattern of Pacific disturbances directing above normal
PWs on Wednesday will keep the Pacific Northwest coast active with
periods of moderate to occasionally heavy rainfall, mainly during
the 00-12Z time period Thursday. Enhanced 850 mb flow will act as a
strong source of moisture transport and upslope enhancement along
the coastal ranges and especially into the Olympics. Although
instability is generally under 200 J/kg along the coast, soils will
be growing more sensitive after repeated rounds of rain in the
days leading up to this. Burn scars are the most vulnerable for any
flash flooding. No major changes were needed for this outlook area.

Hamrick

...Florida Peninsula...

16Z Update: Heavy rainfall continues across South FL this morning
with a prolonged moderate to heavy rain prevalence extending all
the way back towards western Cuba, funneling northeast through the
FL Keys and southeastern FL. Deep moisture flux over the region
coincides with the heavier rainfall with the 12z sounding out of
KEY coming in with a 2.33" PWAT, solidly above the 99th percentile
for the date, even setting a daily record when assessing the
Climatological archive. Sub tropical jet extension on satellite is
beyond impressive and notes the type of environment we are
contending with this time of year. Expect several more hours of
moderate to heavy rains to occur over much of South FL into the
Keys leading to widespread 2-5" of rain with as much as up to 7"
possible for the period. King tide over the FL Keys are already
causing flooding issues for the island chain, so this will only
exacerbate the potential. As a result, there was no need to deviate
from the previous forecast as everything remains on track with a
solid consensus on rainfall totals and expected impacts among this
mornings guidance.

Kleebauer

..Previous Discussion..

On Wednesday morning, the trailing frontal boundary associated with
the strengthening storm off the Southeast coast is likely to
produce scattered to numerous thunderstorms across the
southeastern metro areas of South Florida Wednesday morning. The
front should clear the region by Wednesday night, but PWs in
excess of 2 inches and MUCAPE between 500-1000 J/kg ahead of the
front will provide a favorable environment for thunderstorms
producing rainfall rates potentially in excess of 2 inches per
hour. Given the potential for some localized totals approaching 6
inches across portions of Broward and Miami-Dade counties during
the 12-18Z time period Wednesday, a Slight Risk has been introduced
with this issuance.

Hamrick

...Central Gulf Coast...

16Z Update: Strong thunderstorm close to the AL/FL coast currently
allowed for a continuation of the MRGL risk in the area for at
least this update. Instability is generally focused along the
immediate coast and offshore into the adjacent Gulf which will keep
the threat likely tied to this area. Low-end MRGL risk remains, but
expecting the next 3-6 hours as the peak of the threat before
dwindling into the evening.

Kleebauer

..Previous Discussion..

A small Marginal Risk area remains across portions of far southeastern
Mississippi, southern Alabama, and the far western Florida
Panhandle. Although the threat of flooding is still considered low,
there may be a few convective cells that may be able to produce
higher rainfall rates closer to the coast. Although instability is
unimpressive, robust mid-upper level dynamics with diffluent
southerly flow ahead of an upper low will provide deep layer ascent
with widespread moderate to heavy rainfall across the region.
