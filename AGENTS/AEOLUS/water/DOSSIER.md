### ⚠️ El Niño does NOT refill the Colorado — the guard against the read everyone will reach for

The intuitive chain *"record El Niño → wet West → Colorado recovers"* **fails on geography.** The robust El Niño winter-wet signal is the **Southwest / Lower Basin** (AZ, NM, southern UT/CO). **Powell's inflow originates in the UPPER Basin** (WY, northern UT, western CO), which sits near the ENSO precipitation **dipole pivot** where the signal is weak and sign-ambiguous, and is further modulated at multidecadal (PDO) timescales.

⇒ **The wet anomaly lands downstream of the reservoir that needs it.** Stated as a **do-not-assume, not a directional call** (B2 — the correlation is weak in *both* directions, which does not license a forecast either way).

⇒ **Consequence, and it is the strategically important one: the policy clock and the hydrology clock are two quarters out of phase.** The **ROD (~10/1)** and the **12/31 guideline expiry** are decided at the hydrological **bottom**; any El Niño relief arrives via **spring-2027 runoff** at the earliest. Decisions get made on the worst data the system will show.

### The hydropower leg (WATT owns generation — reconcile to one figure)

- **Hoover @ Mead 1,035 ft** is an **economic** threshold: capacity **1,274 MW → 382 MW**, and below it *operating cost exceeds the value of the power produced* (Final EIS **TA-15**).
- **Glen Canyon @ Powell 3,490 ft** is **a ~52% derate, not a cliff** — ~630 MW remains vs 1,320 MW at 3,700 ft. Generation ceases *below* 3,490, where vortex formation begins.
- **Most of the loss is already realized:** combined Glen Canyon + Hoover generation is **−27.6% vs the 2016 peak** (−6,285,948 MWh/yr, EIA facility-fuel data). **Two-thirds of the decline happened before any threshold was touched** — a threshold-watching frame systematically misses this.
