## 1. The answer

**The water cut happens on 1 January 2027 whether or not the deal is signed.** The 2027–2028 Operating Guidelines apportion 6.25 million acre-feet (maf) to the three Lower Basin states for 2027, 1.25 maf below normal, and Reclamation's November study (dated 11/16) already says that shortage "will govern the operation of Lake Mead for calendar year (CY) 2027". **What the missing signature changes is who decides each state's share, and one hard rule:** without the agreement the Secretary of the Interior sets the split "in accordance with applicable law" (§5.3.A.3), and **no new Intentionally Created Surplus (ICS, water a user leaves in Lake Mead as a credit to draw later) may be created after 31 December 2026** (§5.4).

**The biggest named exposure is the Central Arizona Project (CAP) and the Phoenix–Tucson cities and tribes behind it.** Reclamation's own November model already cuts CAP's 2027 diversion to about 652,000 acre-feet (af), down 32% from 961,000 af in 2026, *assuming* the agreed split (Arizona −760,000 af). **Interior has already modelled the no-deal split, and it is lopsided:** its Final EIS priority model (Appendix C, Table C-3) puts **1,161,018 af on Arizona, 88,982 af on Nevada's SNWA and nothing on California**, because the 1968 law makes CAP junior to California's 4.4 maf. Against the agreement that is about 401,000 af more for Arizona, which would take CAP's city and tribal pools from cuts of 10–14% to somewhere around 47–77%. The ROD says those EIS assumptions do not decide the split, and Arizona disputes them, so this is the direction of risk, not an outcome; no default split has been published and none has a date. Arizona's own buffer (the Water Banking Authority's 1:1 make-whole) is written as contingent on the agreement, so it lapses with it.

**The exposure with a firm date before mid-2027 is power, not water.** The same November study projects Lake Mead below 1,035 ft (the Hoover Dam economic threshold) from **March 2027**, down to **1,014.00 ft in July 2027**, and prints Hoover's generating capacity at about **309 MW for May–September 2027**, against about **1,300 MW in summer 2026**. That happens on the hydrology, signed deal or not; the unsigned deal only removes the 1,010 ft consultation line that would otherwise have been closest to firing (the governing line reverts to 1,000 ft, ROD §10.7).

**Dated before mid-2027:** CAP board 12/03 · MWD board vote on the agreement **12/08** · federal answer in *Nevada v. Burgum* **12/10** · ICS creation ends **12/31** if unsigned · shortage year starts **1/1** · Arizona Legislature convenes about **1/11** (derived) · Mead crosses 1,035 ft about **March** on Reclamation's path · Hoover summer capacity low **May–September**. Everything else below is a risk without a date.

## 4. Who is exposed, by name

**The split is the whole question, and Interior has already modelled the no-deal answer.** The Final EIS's Appendix C, Table C-3 (July 2026; verified by AEOLUS at the PDF, page 18, 11/19) runs Interior's "Priority Shortage Allocation Model" — its reading of "applicable law (i.e., the priority system)" — at a 1.5 maf total shortage, which is the 1.25 maf Lower Basin case plus Mexico's 250,000 af:

| State | Agreement (§5.3.A.2) | Interior's priority model (Table C-3) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arizona | −760,000 af | **−1,161,018 af**, all on Arizona's 4th priority (CAP and on-river 4th-priority users) | **−401,018 more** |
| Nevada | −50,000 af | **−88,982 af**, all on the 8th priority (SNWA & Big Bend) | −38,982 more |
| California | −440,000 af | **0 af at every priority** (PVID, Yuma Project, IID/CVWD/PVID, MWD) | +440,000 better off |
| Mexico | −250,000 af (Minute 334) | −250,000 af | — |

The mechanism is the 1968 Colorado River Basin Project Act §301(b), which Interior applies so as to "reduce CAP and other Arizona fourth priority Colorado River water uses completely before water available to California is reduced below 4,400,000" af (App. C p. C-18). On that model California shares nothing until the Lower Basin cut passes roughly 1.51 maf (helper B arithmetic).

⚠️ **This is a model, not a decision, and the decision-maker has said so.** ROD §10.6: if no agreement is reached the Secretary "will apportion shortages according to the Law of the River, and the assumptions contained in the Final EIS shall not preclude or predetermine such apportionments." The Supreme Court left the Secretary "free to choose among the recognized methods of apportionment" (*Arizona v. California*, 1963, quoted in App. C). Arizona disputes the priority reading (ADWR), and Nevada's lawsuit attacks the model for not treating state shortages as co-equal. The ROD's §10.7 twin sentence omits "not" (KB-087). **Read the table as the direction of risk, not the outcome.**

## 8. Trade-assessment handoff (WQ-696 form, to TERRY)

**To TERRY, in my own words (sent by inbox packet the same session).**

- **What the evidence implies.** Two separate things. (1) **Power, dated, signature-independent:** Reclamation's own November study prints Hoover's summer-2027 capacity at about 309 MW against about 1,300 MW in summer 2026, with Lake Mead below 1,035 ft from March 2027. That removes roughly 1,000 MW of Desert Southwest summer peaking capacity that its holders (LADWP, Arizona Power Authority, Colorado River Commission of Nevada, SCE, MWD, small Southern California cities) must replace at market while still paying Hoover's fixed $76.2M base charge. (2) **Water, conditional, undated:** if the Lower Basin deal is still unsigned when the Secretary sets the split, Interior's own model moves about 401,000 af more of the cut onto central Arizona (CAP cities, tribes) and takes California to zero, and Arizona's banked-water buffer lapses on 1/1.
- **Horizon.** Water: the MWD board vote on 12/08 is the first catalyst; the split matters from 1/1/2027 for CY2027 deliveries. Power: March–September 2027.
- **What would invalidate it.** Water: MWD signs on 12/08 and Arizona ratifies (legislature from ~1/11), or Interior publishes a pro rata split close to 760/440/50, or a court acts. Power: a December–April study path that keeps Mead above 1,035 ft (a wet Upper Basin winter, or a larger Powell release under the §5.1.C.2 April review); and the summer-2027 capacity number is partly maintenance scheduling I cannot separate from elevation.
- **What is unknown.** The Secretary's actual split and its date; how much of Hoover's capacity loss is elevation versus outages; replacement-power cost and who passes it through; any rating agency's no-deal scenario; outstanding debt of the Arizona water issuers.
- **My read on tradeability.** I see **no investment implication with a liquid vehicle that I can name from this evidence.** The water exposure sits with public agencies, tribes and AA/AAA municipal water credits that rating agencies are treating as wait-and-see; the power exposure sits mostly with public power entities, and for the one listed holder (SCE) Hoover is a small slice of supply, as it is for NV Energy (~2%). The plausible market channel is a marginal summer-2027 bid for Desert Southwest replacement power and gas: that is WATT's domain to size, not mine. If TERRY sees a vehicle (Arizona-concentrated names whose water supply is CAP-dependent, or Southwest summer power), the 12/08 vote is the dated test of the water leg.
