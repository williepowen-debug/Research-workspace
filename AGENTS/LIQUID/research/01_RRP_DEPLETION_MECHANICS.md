# Research Prompt: RRP Depletion Mechanics & Historical Precedents

## Objective

Research the Federal Reserve's Reverse Repo (RRP) facility — its mechanics, historical behavior, and what happens when it depletes. This research supports the LIQUID agent's thesis that RRP depletion removes a critical funding buffer from the financial system.

---

## Context

**Current Situation (as of Jan 2026):**
- RRP balance is approximately $2.5 billion — effectively depleted
- Peak was ~$2.5 trillion in late 2022
- This represents a complete exhaustion of what was a massive liquidity buffer

**Why This Matters:**
The RRP facility acts as a "parking lot" for excess cash, primarily used by Money Market Funds (MMFs). When depleted:
- MMFs must deploy cash elsewhere (T-bills, repo)
- The system loses its shock absorber
- Any flow event (Japan selling, auction settlement) hits markets with no buffer

---

## Research Questions

### 1. RRP Mechanics
- How exactly does the RRP facility work?
- Who are the primary counterparties (MMFs, GSEs, etc.)?
- What is the relationship between RRP rate and other money market rates?
- How does the Fed set RRP capacity and rate?

### 2. Historical RRP Levels
- What was the RRP balance trajectory from 2021-2026?
- What drove the buildup to $2.5T?
- What drove the drawdown?
- Are there historical precedents for RRP near zero?

### 3. September 2019 Repo Spike (Key Precedent)
- What happened in Sept 2019 when repo rates spiked to 10%?
- What was the RRP balance at that time?
- What was the reserve balance situation?
- How did the Fed respond?
- What were the warning signs before the spike?

### 4. Fed Reaction Function
- What tools does the Fed have to address funding stress?
- Standing Repo Facility (SRF) — what is it, how does it work?
- What would trigger Fed intervention?
- Is there a stated or implicit "line in the sand" for SOFR stress?

### 5. Current Risk Assessment
- With RRP at ~$2.5B, what is the vulnerability?
- What types of events could trigger funding stress now?
- How does this compare to Sept 2019 conditions?
