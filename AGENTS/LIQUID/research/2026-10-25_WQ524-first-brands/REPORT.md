# WQ524 — First Brands receivables reach a bank loss

**Result, October 25, 2026:** the chain reaches an executed bank response: Western Alliance Bank's receivables-backed loan stopped paying, and Western Alliance Bancorporation recorded a $126.4M charge-off in Q1 2026. This supplies contractual and accounting information beyond a falling asset price. The checked publications do **not** establish an earlier public warning, a current bank funding squeeze, or a completed onward cash call on Jefferies. Final economic loss after collateral/litigation recovery remains unknown.

**Scope:** Will's WQ524 ruling; one case selected in `SELECTION.md` before substantive primary review. BROCK's existing three-followups and two-candidate handoff were consumed. GBDC/Pluralsight was considered only at selection depth: drawable principal and an executed next response were unavailable. No extra case, owner launch or standing program. This is research synthesis, not a change to any forecast, experiment, gate, trade or probability.

## Absorption and final loss

The claimed first protection was receivables/proceeds. Servicing/perfection failures challenge that protection, but the checked evidence does not quantify valid collateral, senior claims or ultimate recoveries. Litigation is a recovery effort, not recovered cash. A booked full charge-off does not extinguish collection rights or establish permanent zero recovery.

WAL's March release distinguished about $45M securities gains already realized from proposed $50M gains and $50M expense savings for the year. Those are earnings mitigants, not loan recoveries. The [March 31 Q1 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1212545/000162828026033054/wal-20260331.htm), consolidated income table/financial overview/regulatory-capital table, confirms $50.5M quarter securities gains, $178.9M common-stockholder net income, and $7.050B CET1 at 11.0% (11.0% at year-end). These are historical consolidated outcomes, not October liquidity. Planned expense savings were not independently established as executed; the gain total cannot all be attributed causally to the March 2 loss decision. Profitability/capital support containment of the recognized loss. They do not prove every liability or funding channel was unaffected, and securities gains cannot establish usable cash without sale proceeds and encumbrance details.

**Judgment:** useful mechanism evidence, with bank loss and response verified beyond a price observation; incremental pre-loss trading lead **not established**. This was already held in BROCK/WAL's historical work. No earlier price tape was reconstructed and this is not a census of all pre-March disclosures, so no lead/lag superiority over prices is claimed. September guarantee refusal could have been informative to a participant, but a March account cannot backdate public knowledge. October rereading is neither a new warning nor independent confirmation of present contagion.

**Contrary explanation:** a specific servicing, collateral-perfection and alleged-fraud failure in a limited-recourse structure can produce this loss without a broad credit/funding spiral. Partial repayment, earnings absorption and disputed parent recourse are compatible with containment. Borrower distress alone does not identify this contractual failure.

**Falsifier / stopping point:** a different charged-off instrument would break the entity link; executed agreements or an adjudication establishing outside guarantees would change the recourse boundary; verified recoveries would reduce eventual economic loss while leaving the historical charge-off intact. A genuinely dated pre-loss public source naming the same obligation and executed lender action would change the warning-value assessment. None was obtained in this bounded pass. Contracts, valid collateral/waterfall, current recovery ledger and final legal allocation remain missing. Existing owner court checks remain with WAL/OTTO; no new assignment or tracking duty created.
