## What You Need to Know

The second-order funding channel is more consequential than direct bank credit losses:

```
Warehouse lines freeze → CLO new issuance halts → marginal buyer of leveraged loans removed
→ loan prices decline → more CLO OC test failures → voluntary selling → prices decline further
→ marks forced lower → NAV facility borrowing bases shrink → margin calls → forced asset sales
```

### Key Numbers
- Bank committed lending to PC: $95B (Fed Y-14Q), $56B utilized
- Total bank lending to NDFIs: $1.14-1.57T (10.4% of total, up from 3.6% a decade ago)
- 60% concentrated in 5 G-SIBs (JPM largest)
- $2.8T in undrawn NDFI commitments — liquidity demand if funds draw to meet redemptions
- Fed estimates simultaneous full drawdown = ~2bps CET1. Manageable, but drawdowns are never simultaneous — they're sequential and panic-driven.

### Connection to Your Domain
- Warehouse line freeze = same mechanism as 2007 ABCP market collapse ($350B contraction in 5 months)
- Fed T-Bill issuance ($352B) + PC fund drawdowns on bank commitments = dual liquidity pressure on G-SIBs
- Reserve concentration at G-SIBs means this liquidity demand hits the same institutions holding the thinnest buffers
