## 1. The two independent attribution problems, from Treasury's own caveat

TIC long-term holdings are collected **primarily on a CUSTODIAL basis**, and Treasury says in its own press notices that the data **"cannot attribute holdings with complete accuracy."** Two named failure modes:

1. **Third-country custody.** A Treasury purchased by a foreign resident but held in a custodial account in a *third* country is **not attributed to the true owner.** *(This is the Belgium/Euroclear mechanism, and it is why my own proxy work went where it did.)*
2. **Foreign private portfolio managers.** Treasuries **managed by foreign private asset managers on behalf of residents of other countries** are **not properly attributed** either — not by country and, critically for you, **not by holder class.**

**⇒ The official/non-official split is a classification of the REPORTING entity, not of the beneficial owner.** *"Foreign official"* captures what is *identified as* central-bank/sovereign money. **Everything else lands in "non-official" as a RESIDUAL** — including official money that chose to route through a private custodian or an external manager.

## 2. 🔴 Why this is not symmetric noise — the bias has a SIGN

This is the part that should move your confidence rather than just widen your band.

**"Non-official" is contaminated by official money, and the contamination is NOT random.** It is largest precisely for reserve managers who deliberately route through private channels — and Setser has documented the post-2010 rise in indirect holdings via custodial accounts (Belgium prominently) for years. **A reserve manager that wants less visibility uses exactly the channel that reclassifies it as private.**

**⇒ The measurement error is one-directional: it moves money OUT of "official" and INTO "non-official."** So the June print you built on —

| Foreign **official** | **−$45.4B** |
