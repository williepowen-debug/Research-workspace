## The off-book finding, in one line

**Consumer BNPL balance sheets capture only ~half the consumer obligation** — the rest is sold, securitized or forward-flowed off-BS while **still owed by the household**. Any off-book credit sizing built from issuer balance sheets alone **understates by roughly 2×**.

**The measured case:** Affirm's on-balance-sheet gross loans HFI are **$8,573M** (2026-03-31) against an **$18.4B total platform portfolio** — the balance sheet holds **47%**; ~$9.8B sits off-BS. *(This corrected my own spine anchor mid-run: I had pulled Affirm's net on-BS figure at $8.06B, which is 44% of the true consumer obligation.)*

**It is systemic, not Affirm-specific:**
- **Klarna** offloaded **$1.6B** of Fair Financing receivables in **Q4'25 alone** (gain on sale $73M, "primarily US")
- **PayPal** sold **~$7B** of US pay-in-4 receivables to **Blue Owl** (two-year agreement, executed 2025-09-24)

## ⚠️ Two traps in that last figure — both live in the press right now

1. **The PayPal–Blue Owl "$7B" is a two-year purchase FLOW, not a $7B stock** (~14% of PayPal's annual US pay-in-4 issuance). I caught this being quoted as a balance.
2. **The same stock-vs-flow error one level up is the single largest error in this whole space.** Headline BNPL "~$70B (2025)" is annual *transaction volume*; treating it as outstanding **overstates ~23×**. CFPB's widely-quoted **$45.2B** is **2023 originations** — not current, and not a balance.

**Who is buying this paper is the part that bears on your lane:** private-credit and alt-manager balance sheets (Blue Owl here) are absorbing consumer receivables that leave the originator's balance sheet but not the household's. That is an off-book consumer-credit channel into the private-credit complex, and it is growing — worth pairing with the fund-finance work if it fits your current thread.

## Scale context so this is not over-weighted

Total BNPL outstanding is **$25–33B broad** / **$3.6–4.8B pay-in-4 only** — against **$1.25T** of card debt. Richmond Fed (Feb 2026) calls card debt *"roughly 400 times larger"* than pay-in-4 BNPL and finds *"no clear evidence"* of elevated BNPL stress; BNPL charge-offs **1.83% (2023)** vs card **4.19% (2023Q4)**. **This is a structural/plumbing finding, not a stress signal** — I am flagging the mechanism, not claiming it is firing.
