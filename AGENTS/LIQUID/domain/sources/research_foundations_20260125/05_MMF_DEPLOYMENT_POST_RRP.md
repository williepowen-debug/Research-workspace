# Research Prompt: Money Market Fund Behavior Post-RRP Depletion

## Objective

Research where Money Market Funds (MMFs) deploy their cash when the RRP facility is depleted. This is critical for understanding the funding market dynamics now that the ~$2.5 trillion RRP buffer has drained to near zero.

---

## Context

**Current Situation (as of Jan 2026):**
- RRP balance is ~$2.5 billion (effectively zero)
- At peak (late 2022), RRP held ~$2.5 TRILLION
- This cash has moved elsewhere — understanding where is crucial

**Why This Matters:**
MMFs manage ~$6+ trillion in assets. When RRP was available:
- MMFs parked excess cash at the Fed via RRP
- This was "dead money" — safe but out of the market
- It provided a buffer (cash that could move into markets if needed)

Now that RRP is depleted:
- MMFs must deploy cash elsewhere
- Options: T-bills, repo lending, bank deposits
- Each choice has different implications for funding markets
- There is NO BUFFER for the next flow shock

**Key Questions:**
1. Where did the $2.5T go?
2. What does this mean for T-bill yields?
3. What does this mean for repo market dynamics?
4. How do MMFs behave when there's a funding shock with no RRP buffer?

### 4. T-Bill Market Impact

- What happened to T-bill yields relative to RRP rate?

### 5. Repo Market Impact

- Who are the repo counterparties (dealers)?

### 6. Stress Scenario Behavior
- How did MMFs behave during March 2020?
- What happens to MMF flows during stress (redemptions)?
- With no RRP buffer, what happens if there's a funding shock?
- Could MMF behavior amplify a funding crisis?
