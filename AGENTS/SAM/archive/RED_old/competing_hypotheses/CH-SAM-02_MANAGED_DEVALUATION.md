## Hypothesis Statement

Japan implicitly accepts yen weakness as the preferred release valve, maintaining low rates to support the bond market while allowing the currency to depreciate to a new equilibrium (165-175 USD/JPY).

## Current Probability

**10-15%** (Subset of Scenario B in SAM framework)

## Scenario Path (If Managed Devaluation Occurs)

1. BOJ pauses rate hikes to support JGB market
2. Yen weakens gradually to 165, then 170-175
3. No dramatic intervention; verbal warnings only
4. Japanese exporters benefit; trade surplus improves
5. Imported inflation elevated but manageable (2.5-3%)
6. Life insurers continue orderly repatriation (no panic)
7. US grumbles but accepts (competing priorities)
8. New equilibrium established at weaker yen

**Outcome:** "Soft crash" for yen without market panic. Japan essentially exports deflation. Global impact moderate - commodity exporters benefit, US manufacturing complains, no systemic crisis.

---

## Key Difference from Primary Thesis

The primary thesis assumes Japan CANNOT accept this outcome because:
1. Imported inflation is politically untenable
2. GPIF rebalancing is mechanical (not a policy choice)
3. Life insurer hedging makes this unsustainable
4. Takaichi's political base requires yen strength

This hypothesis argues these constraints are softer than assumed and Japan may "muddle through" with a weaker yen as the least-bad option.
