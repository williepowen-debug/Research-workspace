## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Japanese fiscal year-end repatriation is primarily an FX-hedging event, not a Treasury-dumping event.** The popular narrative of synchronized March 31 liquidation is historically unreliable — March 2025 saw the LARGEST foreign bond purchases on record despite conditions favoring repatriation. The real risk is structural: a multi-year demand withdrawal of $50-120B annually from USTs as hedged returns go negative and JGB yields become competitive for the first time in decades. March 2026 is uniquely stressed (yen 159, oil shock, J-ICS regulatory cliff, BOJ at 0.75%), and February already produced ¥3.42T in net foreign bond sales — the largest monthly disposal since October 2024. But the acute FY-end flow is largely behind us. What matters for positioning is the structural shift, not the calendar date.

**Source quality:** Claude provided the most rigorous and corrected several errors in Gemini's analysis. Gemini had the deepest institutional detail but overstated the "avalanche" framing and mischaracterized Norinchukin as a current risk. Perplexity was the most cautious and correctly identified the FX-first transmission channel but lacked depth.

- **Norinchukin is NOT a current risk** (contra Gemini). Sold >¥10T ($63B+) Apr 2024–Mar 2025, took ¥1.9T record loss, CEO resigned, raised ¥1.2T capital. Unrealized losses fell from ¥2.5T peak to ¥886B. Actually increased CLO holdings to ¥8.2T. No longer in forced selling mode.

## 2. TRANSMISSION MECHANICS — FX FIRST, BONDS SECOND

### The Hedged Return Math (Definitive)
```
Hedged UST return = UST yield − hedge cost
                  = 4.01% − 4.35%
                  = −0.34%

JGB 10Y = 2.27% (zero currency risk)
JGB 30Y = ~3.5%

Differential favoring domestic: 200-280bps (widest since ~2007)
```

### Four Channels of Repatriation (Ordered by Frequency)

| Channel | What Happens | UST Yield Impact | FX Impact | How to Detect |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|---------------|
| **1. FX hedge adjustment** | Add JPY overlay or let forwards expire | **None directly** | Strengthens JPY | Cross-currency basis widening |
| **2. Bond maturity roll-off** | Let maturing USTs run off, don't replace | Minimal — gradual | None | TIC over months |
| **3. Composition shift** | USTs → corporates, or US → Europe | Relative, not absolute | Minimal | Auction data |
| **4. Outright sale** | Sell USTs on secondary market | **Direct — yields rise** | Strengthens JPY | MOF weekly, TIC |

**Critical insight (confirmed by BIS, CFR, Wellington):** During the April/May 2025 dollar decline, the dollar weakened during Asian hours while US Treasury prices ROSE during those same hours. This proves Asian investors were selling USD forward (adding hedges), not selling bonds. Wellington confirmed the same during the August 2024 carry trade unwind: "US Treasury yields sank and did not rise, which implies Japanese investors were not selling their US bonds but rather putting on currency hedges."

**"Japanese repatriation usually hits FX hedges and funding markets FIRST, and only sometimes Treasuries second."** — Perplexity synthesis

### Why Outright Selling is Constrained
- **Held-to-maturity accounting:** Selling crystallizes unrealized losses immediately, damaging balance sheets and capital ratios
- **Institutional inertia:** Japanese institutions are "extremely slow-moving, buy-and-hold investors with decade-long horizons" (JPM, Goldman)
- **Jan-Oct 2025:** ¥11.7T in net overseas debt purchases (HSBC) — the structural bid was still active until very recently

### Limitations
- **Custodial bias:** Holdings via Euroclear (Brussels) attributed to Belgium regardless of beneficial owner. Japan's true selling > headline "Japan" figure.
- **No institutional breakdown:** GPIF, life insurers, Norinchukin, MOF reserves all appear in single Japan aggregate (official vs private only).
- **Jan TIC showed Japan as net BUYER** — contradicting the repatriation narrative for that month.

### Real-Time Monitoring Stack (Priority Order)
1. **MOF weekly ITS data** (4-5 day lag) — best early signal. No country breakdown but shows aggregate foreign bond flows.
2. **Cross-currency basis swap pricing** — market-based stress indicator. Blowout = forced repatriation.
3. **Fed H.4.1 custody data** (weekly Thursday) — official sector only, no country.
4. **Treasury auction indirect bidder data** (same day) — proxy for foreign demand, no country attribution.
5. **USD/JPY implied vol term structure** — inversion = stress signal.
6. TIC monthly — definitive but arrives too late.

## 5. BOJ QT — GRAVITATIONAL PULL TOWARD DOMESTIC

### The Numbers
- BOJ tapering from ¥5.7T/month (Jul 2024) → ¥3T by Q1 2026 → ¥2T by Q1 2027
- Still holds ~52% of all outstanding JGBs. Total assets down ¥61.2T (8.1%) to ¥695T
- ~¥4T/month in additional net JGB supply must be absorbed by private investors

### JGB Yields Now Competitive
| Tenor | Yield | Context |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 10Y | 2.27% | Highest since 1999 |
| 20Y | ~3.18% | Life insurer hurdle territory |
| 30Y | ~3.5% | Competitive with hedged USTs |
| 40Y | Briefly >4.0% (Jan 2026) | Extraordinary — was near-zero 6 years ago |

### Self-Reinforcing Dynamic (Not a Clean Circular Flow)
```
BOJ QT → JGB supply pressure → domestic yields rise
→ JGBs competitive with hedged foreign bonds
→ Life insurers sell foreign bonds, buy JGBs
→ Repatriation strengthens yen
→ Yen strength further incentivizes repatriation
```

**BUT absorption is deeply uneven:**
- Banks want short duration (<2Y)
- Insurers want super-long but now buy only in episodic "solvency-buffer windows" under J-ICS
- Fukoku "largely stopped buying 30-40Y JGBs" by Jan 2026, pivoting to shorter maturities
- Foreign investors (65% of monthly JGB transactions) are volatile and opportunistic
- MUFG estimates banks can absorb ≤30% of BOJ holdings before hitting internal risk limits

**Super-long JGB curve has lost its anchor bid** (insurers stepping back) precisely when BOJ is withdrawing its own. MOF considering issuance cuts at super-long end + buy-back auctions for ultra-long bonds (first 20Y buy-backs since Sep 2008).

### The Real Thesis
**Not a March 31 event. A structural regime change.**

The risk is a sustained, multi-year marginal demand withdrawal of **$50-120B annually** from USTs. Japanese institutions going from +$30-80B annual buyer to neutral/modest seller. Concentrated in longer-duration securities. Occurring as US fiscal deficits expand supply. This is the term premium repricing story.
