# Perplexity — Japan FY-End Repatriation Mechanics
**Source:** Perplexity (standard, not Deep Research), received Mar 25 2026

## KEY FINDINGS

### Nuance vs Gemini — Important Corrections

1. **"Japan sells Treasuries" narrative is too crude.** Hedged private institutions (life insurers, banks) often adjust FX hedges FIRST and may or may not sell underlying USTs. Structurally unhedged investors (GPIF) are slower-moving and benchmark-driven, NOT fiscal-year-end liquidity traders.

2. **Synthetic repatriation is real.** A life insurer can leave the UST in custody, sell dollars forward, and convert exposure to yen WITHOUT dumping duration into the Treasury market. Impact shows up in USD/JPY, cross-currency basis, and front-end dollar funding — not necessarily in Treasury yields directly.

3. **GPIF foreign-bond benchmark is explicitly UNHEDGED on a yen basis.** Makes GPIF LESS likely to be a classic year-end hedged-UST liquidator. More likely gradual rebalancing around allocation bands.

### Sequence (consistent with Gemini but more nuanced)
1. Fast-money and treasury desks → adjust FX hedges and funding books first
2. Life insurers and bank portfolios → trim or rotate next (drawn-out, not one-day event — Fukoku Mutual selling low-yield foreign bonds since April)
3. Large public pensions → move last unless deviations from target weights force rebalancing

### Two Channels — Different Market Impact
| Channel | What Happens | Market Impact |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Outright UST sale | Adds supply to secondary market | Direct yield pressure |
| FX hedge adjustment (synthetic) | Keep bond, change currency overlay via FX forwards/swaps | Impact in USD/JPY, cross-currency basis, front-end dollar funding — NOT one-for-one yield rise |

### Key Hedging Math
Economic return = US yield − hedge cost − domestic alternative yield
When hedge costs are punitive (~450-500bps), they reduce exposure either by selling OR by changing hedge book. Both count as "repatriation" but have very different market footprints.

### Brad Setser Framework Referenced
- "Hedged investors" bucket: megabanks, life insurers, investment trusts
- "Unhedged investors" bucket: GPIF, pension-style investors
- Different buckets = different repatriation mechanics

## TIMING PATTERN (High-Frequency MOF Data)
- **Feb 2026:** ¥3.42T sold overseas bonds, bulk in week ended Feb 20 — well before FY-end
- **Mar 14 week:** ¥992B net sale — stress-related liquidation front-loaded into Feb and mid-March
- **2025 precedent:** Net sellers of ¥902.7B in long-term foreign bonds in March, after large Feb purchases
- **Pattern:** Rolling process beginning Feb, accelerating when JGB yields or hedge costs move sharply, continuing into early April. NOT a synchronized Mar 31 liquidation.

## TIC LAG
- ~6 week lag. Jan 2026 TIC released Mar 17. Feb release → Apr 15. Mar data → mid-May.
- TIC is monthly, residency-based, distorted by custody location and intermediary flows
- Useful for confirming broad trends, NOT pinpointing exact weeks of selling

## BOJ QT — More Complex Than "Circular Flow"
- BOJ purchases ~¥2.9T/month in Jan-Mar 2026, planned to keep declining
- **Key correction:** Life insurers — supposedly the JGB buyers replacing BOJ — may themselves be FORCED SELLERS of super-long JGBs ahead of Mar 31
- BOJ absorbing LESS net duration → adjustment shared by banks, insurers, pensions, broader market
- Gemini's clean "circular flow" is too neat. Reality is messier — both markets losing their anchor buyer simultaneously.

## STRESSED YEAR-END = THREE-WAY BALANCE SHEET PROBLEM
1. Hedge cost (punitive 450-500bps)
2. Domestic mark-to-market pain (JGB volatility — 20Y at 3.18%, 30Y surging)
3. Yen/liquidity risk (159, energy import drain)

Institutions acting earlier and more aggressively than normal year-end tidy-up. Feb-mid March flows confirm this.

## PRACTICAL FRAMEWORK — WHO DOES WHAT
| Actor | Primary Channel | Speed | UST Impact |
|-------|----------------|-------|------------|
| Life insurers | Outright foreign-bond rotation + hedge-ratio changes | Medium — drawn out but peaking mid-Mar | **DIRECT** — actual duration removed |
| Megabanks/trust banks | Funding and hedge books (swaps/forwards) | **Fast** — before cash-bond sales obvious | Indirect — FX/funding first, bonds second |
| GPIF/public pensions | Strategic unhedged benchmark rebalancing | **Slow** — usually NOT first FY-end sellers | Moderate — mechanical, not panic |
| Corporates | FX conversion and liquidity mgmt | Late Mar | Minimal — unless unusually large securities books |

**"Clean takeaway: Japanese repatriation usually hits FX hedges and funding markets FIRST, and only sometimes Treasuries second."**

### Stressed Year-End Behavioral Shift
- Weak yen + high import costs → MORE willing to raise hedge ratios or realize foreign-bond sales
- Rising JGB yields → improved relative appeal of bringing money home
- Result: **more front-loaded hedge adjustment, more outright rotation by private institutions, larger gap between hedged private behavior and slower GPIF**

## VALUE vs GEMINI
Perplexity is MORE CAUTIOUS on the "avalanche" framing. Key correction: much of the repatriation may be SYNTHETIC (FX overlay changes) rather than physical UST selling. This means:
- USD/JPY impact could be larger than Treasury yield impact
- The "missing Japan bid" at UST auctions is the bigger structural concern than active dumping
- TLT puts still work but the transmission mechanism may be SLOWER than Gemini implies
