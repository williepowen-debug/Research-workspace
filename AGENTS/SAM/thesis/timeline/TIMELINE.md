## RESOLVED — May 29 (APRIL ACTIVITY DATA — HAWKISH COUNTERWEIGHT TO DOVISH CPI)

### Fri May 29 — JAPAN APRIL ACTIVITY DATA ✅ RESOLVED — BEAT (hawkish counterweight; June split rebalanced)

- **Event:** METI/MIC April activity bundle released Friday Tokyo (industrial production, retail sales, unemployment).
- **Outcome:**
  - **Industrial production +0.8% MoM** vs −0.4% consensus — rebound from March's −0.4% fall. Driven by semiconductor equipment + machinery (AI-capex demand). Manufacturers' forecast: +5.1% May, −0.4% June (near-term volatility).
  - **Retail sales +2.1% YoY** vs +1.4% consensus — matched March pace; consumer spending resilient despite cost-of-living pressure.
  - Unemployment steady.
- **Why it matters:** This is the *quantity* half of the June BOJ picture, and it firmed the same week the *price* half (Tokyo May CPI core-core 1.6%) softened. The BOJ's normalization bias is activity/wage-spiral-driven, not spot-CPI driven — Ueda has explicitly said temporary downward price pressure won't prevent hikes. A firm real economy supports the "look through soft CPI" stance.
- **Threshold-vs-mechanism read (per [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]):** Tuesday's dovish CPI is a *threshold* read (price gauge below target); the activity beat speaks to the *mechanism* the BOJ actually reacts to (output gap / demand strength feeding the wage-price spiral). The two pointed opposite directions this week — so the net is a balanced coin-flip, not a dovish drift.
- **Market reaction:** Risk-on — **Nikkei +2.5–2.7% to ~66,300**, but that rally was primarily driven by the Iran/Hormuz ceasefire-extension report (energy lower), not the activity data. **USDJPY stayed flat at 159.28** through both the strong data and the risk-on tape — confirms rate-differential dominance and that risk-on is mildly carry-supportive (yen-negative), offsetting any hawkish-data yen bid. Market is not pricing the June hike off this print.
- **Net:** **SAM-21 (June hike) HELD at ~50%** — not raised. One activity print (vs the wage/price core the BOJ weights most) doesn't justify a probability shift on its own, but it rebalances the framing: the June call is a genuine coin-flip with offsetting inputs (soft price / firm activity), not a probability drifting lower off the Tokyo CPI miss alone. Counterweight logged to STATUS BOJ assessment.

- **Net:** Context, not a thesis mover. Reinforces both the subsidy-bias caveat on CPI reads and the structural long-end supply pressure. No mark changes.

- **Event:** Tokyo May 2026 CPI release (MIC) — leading indicator (~2-3wk lead) for national June print.

Critically, **core-core strips energy AND food** — so unlike the April national miss (which was dismissable as fuel-subsidy noise), this is the underlying-demand read softening, harder for the BOJ to look through.
- **Mechanism-vs-threshold decomposition (per [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]):**
  - **Threshold read (dovish):** Tokyo core-core 1.6% well below 2% target and falling. The pre-registered threshold breached materially.
  - **Mechanism read (what the BOJ reacts to):** BOJ normalization is wage-price-spiral driven (Shunto 5.26%, 3rd straight year >5%), not spot-CPI driven. Ueda has explicitly removed the growth precondition and said temporary downward pressure won't prevent hikes. So a soft CPI threshold is dovish-leaning but not decisive.
  - **Tokyo-vs-national caveat:** Tokyo CPI carries known structural downward bias vs national from Tokyo-metropolitan subsidies (free high-school education, childcare). National May core-core (Jun 19, post-BOJ) likely prints **above** Tokyo's 1.6%. Don't over-extrapolate Tokyo → national.
- **Market reaction:** Muted — USDJPY 159.27 (flat, +0.04%), FXY $57.65 (+0.19%), JGB 30Y +4bp to 3.896%. No discrete repricing on the Tokyo print at the tape level (national is the BOJ-relevant series).

Carry-unwind probs trimmed 30d 62→58%, 60d 80→77%.
