# SAM — Japan catch-up assessment, September 8, 2026

**Decision:** Keep the carry-convexity thesis retired at LOW. The yen rally is substantial and has a Japan-specific component, but the available evidence does not establish a systemic carry unwind. Wage growth supports BOJ tightening; much of that next step is already priced. Oil remains a net import-cost headwind. No successor thesis or trade is approved by this research.

**Coverage:** All eight items in the approved [agenda](2026-09-08_catchup-priorities.md) investigated. Research cutoff approximately September 8, 22:15 ET / September 9, 11:15 JST. Observations have different clocks, shown below. Future releases and unavailable direct funding/transaction evidence remain explicit follow-ups. This is a research assessment, not a broker reconciliation.

## 1. Correct the labor premise first

The September 4 BLS release reports **August payrolls +162,000, unemployment 4.1%, July revised from −23,000 to +21,000, June from +20,000 to +31,000**. The June–July revision is +55,000. The old −103,000 revision total belongs to an earlier release window; it is not another description of the current revision. The original print was revised, not fabricated or retracted. [BLS release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm).

**Implication:** The candidate's assertion that a negative July payroll print currently weakens the Fed's labor constraint is superseded. This reduces support for the Fed-easing mechanism; it does not independently forecast a hike. The candidate now has a dated correction and explicitly separates its August 7 argument from the current assessment. Historical prediction terms and grades remain unchanged.

Private consumption is flat, business investment −0.9%; domestic-demand contribution −0.1 percentage point and net exports +0.5. The upgrade is not evidence of strong domestic demand. July wages show total cash earnings +4.7% year-on-year, scheduled earnings +4.1%, special payments +6.3%, and real total wages +2.4% under the ministry's ex-imputed-rent deflator. These are preliminary, with sample-change and revision caveats. The ministry's wage release uses its stated CPI/index basis; do not splice it into SAM's rebased national-CPI comparisons. ## 7. Secondary investigations: China and Norway

**China:** MOFCOM announcement 37 imposes provisional anti-dumping deposits on Japan-origin dichlorosilane from September 8: **99.2% for Shin-Etsu/other Japanese firms; 80.8% for Denal Silane**. It covers the specified product, purity above 99%, not every chemical under tariff code 28539090. It is a provisional deposit measure, not a general Japanese semiconductor export ban. The first-order exposures are supplier margins/volumes and Chinese customer procurement costs. Product-level revenue exposure, inventories and qualified substitutes were not established, so no defensible earnings loss or global chip-output estimate follows. Escalate the macro assessment only on demonstrated production disruption or widening product scope. [MOFCOM announcement](https://cacs.mofcom.gov.cn/cacscms/article/jkdc?articleId=189166&type=11).

**Norway:** NBIM proposes JGB benchmark weight **4.6% → 7.4%**, JPY currency weight **4.6% → 7.8%**, U.S. government bonds **34.1% → 21.9%**, while total USD weight barely changes, **52.9% → 52.5%**. This is a proposed sector/geographic mix change, not a broad dollar exit. No approved implementation date or specific 30Y/40Y purchase is established. The January 25, 2027 expert-report deadline is not proof that implementation before then is legally impossible; SAM's “nothing lands in 2026” wording is corrected. [NBIM submission and Appendix G](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/submissions-to-ministry/2026/the-government-pension-fund-global--analyses-and-assessments-of-the-investment-strategy-for-bonds/).

For scale only, applying the 2.8-point JGB weight increase to the half-year **NOK5,860B fixed-income allocation** gives **NOK164.1B**. That is a counterfactual index-equivalent, not an order forecast; actual holdings, benchmark size, offsets and gradual implementation matter. BOND's ~$18B remains a separate estimate, not an official flow. [NBIM half-year report, Table 1](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/reports/2026/half-year-report-2026/web-report-half-year-report-2026/).
