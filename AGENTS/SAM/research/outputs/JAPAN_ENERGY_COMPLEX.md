## BOTTOM LINE

Japan's 2026 energy shock is an **oil shock, not a gas/grid shock**, and its BOJ transmission is **severed/inverted**. Crude is ~96% Middle-East-sourced (the acute Hormuz vulnerability), but LNG is only ~11% ME and the grid is buffered by restarting nuclear + non-ME coal. The shock is **not** feeding the BOJ-hawkish CPI case: volume collapse produced trade *surpluses*, and ¥3T of fuel subsidies deliberately sever the PPI→CPI link. The energy impact reroutes from **monetary (CPI) to fiscal (JGB supply)** — which feeds the long-end / J-ICS lifer-abandonment mechanism (THESIS Pillar 2), not the inflation print.

## 1. DIFFERENTIAL HORMUZ EXPOSURE (the organizing frame)

| Fuel | ME / Hormuz exposure | Share of grid / usage | Storage | Blockade impact |
|------|---------------------|----------------------|---------|-----------------|
| **Crude oil** | **~96% ME (FY2024 95.9%), ~93% via Hormuz** | Transport 41%, industry+petrochem feedstock 30%, power ~2% | **~205 days** (reserves) | **Acute** — ME imports record-low since 1979; gasoline ¥190.8/L record (Mar) |
| **LNG** | **~11% ME** (Qatar/UAE/Oman) | ~30% of electricity + city gas | **~3 weeks** | Price not volume — ME-LNG −76% YoY but only 11% to replace |
| **Coal** | **~0% ME** (Australia/Indonesia) | ~27% of grid | months | None — genuine non-Hormuz buffer |

**Takeaway:** the blockade hits oil hard, gas at the margin, coal not at all. Oil's saving grace is deep storage (~205 days); LNG's liability is shallow storage (~3 weeks).

## 2. CRUDE OIL

- **Demand:** ~2.36 mb/d crude imports (2025, IEA OMR); ~99% imported (near-zero domestic). Structural multi-decade decline — oil fell from ~80% of energy mix (1970s) to ~40% (2019); 2024 demand −4.4%, ~11.8% below pre-pandemic (OECD multi-decade low).
- **Source mix:** FY2024 ME share **95.9%** (The Diplomat/JCER); Hormuz handles ~93% of imports. UAE ~40% + Saudi/Kuwait/Qatar. **2026 alternatives:** US, Azerbaijan, South Sudan, Russia (Sakhalin), +20mb extra UAE via non-Hormuz ports (Nikkei).
- **Sectoral use (2018 baseline):** transport 41.2%, industry 29.7% (>half = petrochemical/chemical *non-energy feedstock*), power-gen minor.
- **Refining:** ~3.1–3.3 mb/d capacity, 20→18 refineries (shrinking on demand decline — ENEOS Wakayama, Idemitsu Yamaguchi closures). No verifiable 2026 run-cut figure.
- **2026 prices:** gasoline record ¥190.8/L (Mar 16) → ~¥169.5/L (Jun 8) via ¥30.2/L subsidy; diesel ¥178.4, kerosene ¥154.1. **PM refused rationing** (Apr 27). 80mb record reserve release.

*Reserves detail: see KB-SAM-187 / THESIS § OIL-IN-YEN. ~205 days cover (May 9) vs 254 baseline; Takaichi "no further release needed" May 12.*

## 3. LNG / NATURAL GAS

- **Volume / rank:** 65.89 Mt in 2024 (−0.4% YoY); world #1–2 importer (China 76.65 Mt; some 2025/26 data shows Japan retaking #1 as China demand slumped). Volumes structurally declining as nuclear restarts.
- **Supplier mix (FY2025):** Australia **39%** (largest), Malaysia/Russia next, US ~7% (down from 8.7%). **Middle East (Qatar/UAE/Oman) ≈ 11%** — the key Hormuz-exposure number (Foreign Policy, Jun 2 2026). *Far less Hormuz-exposed than oil (11% vs 96%).*
- **Use:** LNG ≈ 29–30% of electricity; nearly all imported gas → power + city gas; no domestic production.
- **2026 blockade impact:** ME-LNG to Japan −76% YoY (Pravda Japan, May 22); JKM spot ~doubled to high-$24s/MBtu; Tokyo FY2026 baseload power futures +34% in two days to record ¥16.38/kWh. Replaced via Australia/US/spot.
- **Contract vs spot:** predominantly long-term contract (volume-insulated); JERA–QatarEnergy 27-yr/3 MTPA deal starts 2028; ~40% of Japanese-handled LNG resold abroad (flex buffer).
- **Storage = the vulnerability:** only **~3 weeks** domestic inventory (vs months for oil). Power-utility stocks ~1.9–2.16 Mt, **below 5-yr avg of 2.03 Mt** (S&P Global) — flagged stress, not yet crisis.

## 4. ENERGY MIX, NUCLEAR & COAL BUFFERS

- **Mix (2025):** fossil >60% (LNG ~30%, coal ~27%, oil ~2%); low-carbon ~35% (solar ~11%, nuclear ~10%, hydro ~8%). ~88–90% of primary energy imported.
- **Nuclear (critical swing buffer):** 15 reactors operating, **33 GW, ~10% of grid**. Kashiwazaki-Kariwa Unit 6 restarted Feb 9 2026 (largest plant, 14-yr shutdown). Fleet: 32 operable / 3 approved / 6 under review / 8 no application. METI explicitly "accelerating nuclear restarts" as the medium-term energy-security lever; 20% nuclear by FY2040 target.
- **Coal (non-Hormuz buffer):** ~27% of grid, Australia (74 Mt 2024) + Indonesia (25.8 Mt) + South Africa/Colombia. Zero ME exposure — a genuine swing-to buffer during the crisis.

## 5. MACRO TRANSMISSION — energy → trade → CPI → BOJ (thesis-critical)

1. **Trade balance:** 3rd straight monthly surplus on collapsing energy imports — Hormuz vessel traffic ~100 (Feb) → <10 (Mar), no mid-May recovery. Supply-destruction *shrank* the import bill (April ¥+302B surplus pattern persists into May). *This is the v1.4 OIL-IN-YEN Phase-1 inversion, confirmed and extended.*
2. **CPI:** April core +1.4% YoY — below all economist estimates. Subsidies actively suppressing energy passthrough.
3. **Fiscal:** Takaichi >¥3T ($19B) supplementary + ¥500B reserve for Jul–Sep utility bills (~¥5,000/household), explicitly to cap energy-price passthrough.
4. **The severed link:** May PPI +6.3% YoY shows passthrough alive *upstream*, but subsidies sever the PPI→CPI transmission. **→ the energy shock does NOT deliver a hawkish-CPI surprise; it is dovish-on-headline and surfaces as JGB supply (¥3T = long-end fiscal pressure → feeds Pillar 2 / J-ICS lifer long-end abandonment).**
5. **Cost-not-volume:** METI Strategic Buffer LNG + Feb 2026 QatarEnergy/JERA emergency-LNG MOU secure *volume*; residual exposure is *price* (costlier non-ME barrels/cargoes), subsidy-softened at the consumer level.

**Thesis implication:** This is the structural reason the BOJ can hike June 16 on a 1.4% headline — energy is actively dovish on the print, and the hike rests on the **wage/activity mechanism** (Shunto 5.26%, activity beats), not energy-CPI. The energy shock's real thesis footprint is **fiscal/long-end (JGB supply)**, reinforcing Pillar 2, not the inflation case.

## 6. FORWARD WATCH

- **May trade balance (Jun 17):** cost-vs-volume decomposition — does the petroleum-input-cost rise show up even as ME volumes stay depressed (branch c)?
- **LNG utility inventories:** the ~3-week buffer is the one fast-bite tier; watch for summer-demand + any reopening delay.
- **Nuclear restarts:** each GW back online structurally cuts fossil/LNG dependence — a slow dovish-on-energy drift.
- **Post-deal normalization:** if Hormuz reopens (Fri Geneva ceremony), watch whether ME crude *volumes* recover → trade balance could swing back toward deficit on a 1–3 month lag (subsidy + price still capped).
- **Subsidy taper:** the ¥3T relief is the CPI-suppressor; any taper un-severs the PPI→CPI link and could deliver a delayed energy-CPI bump.
