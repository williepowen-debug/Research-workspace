# The US strong-yen endorsement and the October 30 BOJ decision

**Written:** 2026-09-29 10:12 ET · SAM, Will-directed deep dive · **Status: analysis, NOT a registered prediction** — no SAM-NN row, nothing arms, book FLAT, THESIS v1.7 unchanged.

## Bottom line
The US endorsement cuts **both ways** on October. It gives the BOJ political cover to hike, but by working through words it lowers the currency urgency, and it raises the cost of looking like the BOJ takes orders from Washington. **December stays the modal path; October is a real minority risk, and the market's ~36% (Totan, 9/29) is not obviously wrong in either direction.** The sharper point is the one below: a hike in October would land far less priced than September's did, so it would matter much more for the yen.

**Read:** Bessent's stated preference since January has been **faster BOJ hikes**, with intervention as the exception. September delivered the hike and the yen fell anyway (fully priced, dovish split, and the Fed also hiked — the 10Y gap widened 8bp on hike day). On 9/25 Washington switched to **words**. The phrase "reflects Japan's strong economic fundamentals" is, by inference, a nudge toward continued normalisation. It is **not** a request for an October move, and the inference is SAM's.

## For an October hike
1. **Political cover.** PM Takaichi herself said "an undervalued yen is problematic" (disclosed 9/25), which makes it harder for the government to object to a hike that serves that goal (the ~80% survey, REPORTED).
2. **Pricing drifted up through the campaign.** Totan meeting-OIS, Oct-30 incremental 25bp equivalent: **22% [9/18] → 27% [9/24] → 36% [9/29]**. The drift overlaps the July minutes (9/28) and services PPI (9/25), so it cannot be attributed to Washington alone.
3. **The July minutes are hawkish in method.** The goal is shifting from *raising* underlying CPI to *anchoring* it, and one member warned of a "double shock" if the bank responds late (2+ outlets; primary not read).
4. **Inputs are hot.** Services PPI Aug **+3.7% y/y**, fastest in 2+ years. Tokyo CPI Sep consensus **2.4% vs 1.8%** (REPORTED, FXStreet).
5. **October 30 is an Outlook Report meeting**, the natural venue for a move.

## Against an October hike
1. **Pace.** 2026 hikes came Jun-16 and Sep-18, three months apart; October would be six weeks. Momma (ex-BOJ ED, 9/27): "the basic pace will probably be once every three months", with back-to-back at **20–30%**. Economist survey: **58% January, ~35% December** (Bloomberg/Japan Times 9/28). ⚠️ **Economists are far less hawkish than the OIS** (Dec 72%).
2. **The board is already split.** Two of nine voted HOLD on 9/18 (Asada, Sato). A back-to-back move asks the doves to lose twice in six weeks.
3. **Words are working.** USD/JPY fell 159.04 → 156.50 with no money reported. If the yen holds, the FX case for rushing weakens: **the verbal campaign may substitute for an October hike rather than precede it.**
4. **A hike is not a reliable yen tool right now.** September proved it: a priced hike, the Fed hiking alongside, a widening gap and a weaker yen. The Fed decides Oct-28, the day before the BOJ.
5. **The independence optics.** A hike six weeks after the US Treasury publicly lobbied for a strong yen invites "Tokyo takes orders". The ex-BOJ credibility warnings (REPORTED) cut against speed.
6. **Domestic politics.** The Diet opens Oct-5 with a food consumption-tax cut on the agenda. Roughly 75% of Japanese mortgages float, and the Takaichi mortgage-ceiling zone (0.75%) is already 50bp behind. Katayama 9/29: "Interest rates are determined by markets" — hands-off in both directions.

## The contradiction worth watching
**The US is pushing the rate gap WIDER (the Fed hiked 9/16, SEP medians UP) while the US Treasury asks Japan to close it.** US 10Y 5.24% [^TNX 9/28] vs JGB 10Y 3.082% [MOF 9/28]: a 2.158pp gap, wider than on 9/24. As long as that holds, words have to keep doing work that rates are undoing, and the pressure lands on the BOJ or on MOF money.

## Why an October hike would matter more than September's
September was ~99% priced, and the yen fell. **October is ~36% priced**, so a hike would be about **two-thirds unpriced**, which is a genuine surprise and likely yen-positive. SAM's standing rule (CH-004): a *fully priced* hike does not unwind carry, and surprise is what moves it. SAM's registered 🔴 cross-agent trigger is a BOJ **surprise** hike (>25bp or unscheduled). A scheduled October 25bp at ~36% priced is a partial surprise, not the registered trigger. ⚠️ The historical base rate says the BOJ **guides pricing up before it moves**; it rarely surprises.
