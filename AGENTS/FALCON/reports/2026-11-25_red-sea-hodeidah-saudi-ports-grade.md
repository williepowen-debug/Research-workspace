## 1. For Will, in plain words

Early Wednesday local time, the Saudi-led coalition said it bombed "operational capabilities" at Hodeidah, the Houthis' main Red Sea port, plus targets in two northern Yemeni provinces. That is the coalition's own claim. It has not said what it hit, and nobody has reported damage or whether the port still works. Hours earlier the Houthis had said that if Hodeidah were hit, Jeddah and every other Saudi Red Sea port would become targets, and they told port workers to leave and shipping lines to pull their ships out. On their own terms, the condition is now met.

**The threat is live, but it has not been carried out.** I found no Houthi launch, claim or new statement against Jeddah, Yanbu, Jizan, Rabigh or King Abdullah Port after the strike. My search ran one to two hours after the strike, so it shows only what had been published by then, not that nothing happened. This is also the second time the same sequence has played out. On 11/20 the Houthis made the same conditional threat and emailed ships naming the Port of Jeddah; the coalition struck Hodeidah that day; the Houthis then fired missiles at Jizan and at Riyadh, and the coalition says it shot them all down. No Saudi Red Sea port was confirmed hit between 11/20 and 11/24. Ships did not leave either: tanker traffic through Bab el-Mandeb, the strait at the south end of the Red Sea, has held steady (85 tankers in the week to 11/24, against 78 the week before).

**Riyadh:** on Tuesday a drone hit a tower in the King Abdullah Financial District and wounded 11 people. Saudi authorities confirmed it and blame the Houthis; I found no Houthi claim. AFP witnesses say it was the sovereign wealth fund's tower, hit for the second time in six days. It is the first of these Riyadh tower hits with people hurt.

**Hormuz:** the UK navy's shipping office (UKMTO) says three ships heading out of the strait were hit by unidentified projectiles on Sunday 11/22 at about 20:25 UTC. All kept going and their crews are safe; the damage is unknown. Nobody neutral has said who fired. I could read this only second-hand because UKMTO's site refuses this machine.

**The UAE notice:** Abu Dhabi's state oil company has banned tankers from entering, waiting in or passing through the waters it controls unless they are calling at its own terminals. It runs for 90 days from 12 November, so to about 10 February. I read the notice itself on ADNOC's website. It is not an export cut: ADNOC's own loadings continue. Its likely purpose is to keep blockade-running and ship-to-ship transfer tankers out of UAE waters; that reading is mine, not ADNOC's.

**Is this a turning point?** Not yet. Saudi Arabia has been fighting the Houthis since the Houthis declared their blockade in July, and it already bombed Hodeidah on 11/20, so tonight is a repeat. What is new is that the Houthis have named the Saudi Red Sea ports as targets, in writing to ships, twice in five days. A Houthi strike that damages Jeddah or Yanbu would be the real turning point.

**Why the Red Sea ports matter:** Yanbu is the main outlet for Saudi oil that does not pass through Hormuz. It is fed by the pipeline across the country from the Gulf coast. If Hormuz and Yanbu were both shut at once, Saudi Arabia would lose most of its ways of getting oil to sea, which is a much bigger shock than either one alone. Tonight neither is shut. Hormuz is badly impaired (about 2 transits a day in the newest official count, 11/15, and ships hit most days) but still open. Yanbu has been threatened and not hit. BRENT owns the barrel figures.

**What I expect:** over the next 72 hours, more likely than not a Houthi launch or claim aimed at a Saudi Red Sea port (about 55%), but only about a 1-in-10 chance that a port is confirmed damaged. Over two weeks, about 25% that Saudi Arabia or Aramco confirms damage at a Red Sea port or coastal oil site (I have registered this as FAL-13), and about 8% that damage cuts export loadings. The more likely path is that the Houthis keep hitting Riyadh, where their drones have been getting through, and use the port threat to try to stop further strikes on Hodeidah. I would raise these odds if the Houthis fire at Jeddah or Yanbu (even if the missiles are shot down), shipping lines stop calling at Jeddah, or Aramco or the Saudi ports authority says anything about the ports. I would lower them on three quiet days or Omani mediation.

**Nothing on my registered letters fires.** My ladder holds at B 1 / C 14 / D 85. ⚠️ Even a confirmed hit on Yanbu's terminal, Jeddah port or a Red Sea refinery would not fire my production rung, which counts oil fields and processing plants only. That kind of hit would be a route and price event for BRENT, not a scenario move here.
