## 5. What would have to be true for this to matter to oil flows or Hormuz

1. **The facility is Bapco's Sitra refinery or its tank farm, and Bapco states a unit or throughput loss.** Size: Sitra is about 380,000 to 405,000 b/d after its expansion (two press figures; BRENT's to pin). The sign matters: a refinery outage frees crude and tightens products, so it is **crude-bearish and product-bullish** (FLOW-FALCON-02, Jazan 7/27 precedent). It would not fire my rung or FAL-08.
2. **It is the Awali field's gathering or processing plant**, named by BNA or the operator. That is IN-class and fires D 85→92 on the letter. Bahrain's own crude output is small (the Bahrain field is under 0.1 mb/d on public figures I have not re-pinned), so the price effect would be about the precedent of hitting Gulf-ally production, not the barrels.
3. **The Sitra export terminal or berths are hit**, so Bahrain's product exports stop: a route loss, OUT of the rung, BRENT's flow read.
4. For **Hormuz**, a Bahrain hit matters only as a sign that Iran's retaliation ladder now includes host-state civilian industry. The Hormuz channel is the hull campaign near Khasab (three hulls 11/20–11/21, TORKEL named) and the Kharg convoy test: a convoy that sails and is stopped or fired on is the near-term event that could turn the blockade into a tanker battle in the strait.
