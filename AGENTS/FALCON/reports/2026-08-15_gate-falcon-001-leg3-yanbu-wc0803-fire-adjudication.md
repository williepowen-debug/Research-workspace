## 4. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY — the guard, because the temptation is large in the other direction now

**This is a LOADINGS/TRANSIT gate on the Red Sea export ROUTE, not a confirmed-crude-supply-loss finding.** Do not read this fire as satisfying FAL-04 (crude/condensate supply-loss prediction) or R1 (confirmed barrels offline). A Yanbu-specific loadings decline is consistent with at least three distinct mechanisms, and this adjudication does not discriminate between them:

1. **Genuine supply constraint at the loading point** (blockade effectiveness, tanker unwillingness, insurance-driven capacity refusal) — the R3 (confirmed export interruption) reading.
2. **Reallocation to the Gulf coast via Petroline** — crude that would have loaded at Yanbu instead exports via the Gulf side (Ras Tanura/Ju'aymah), which my own KB carries as "operational, ~5 mb/d available" [AGBI 7/28]. If this is happening, Saudi TOTAL exports are not falling — only the Red-Sea-specific print is.
3. **The Sidi Kerir/SUMED reroute** (`SIG-W-20260813-013`, drained this morning) — **checked and does NOT explain this print.** That mechanism requires the crude to have LOADED at a Saudi Red Sea port first, then part-discharge at Ain Sokhna and reload at Sidi Kerir — the loading event still happens and should still show up in a Yanbu-specific loadings series. It does not reduce Yanbu's own print; it only reduces what continues south through Bab el-Mandeb. **This mechanism is ruled OUT as the explanation for the Yanbu decline specifically** (though it remains the correct explanation for the separate, adjacent Bab-transit-collapse observable WALTER routed).

**⇒ Mechanism (1) vs (2) is exactly the open question, and I do not have the instrument to resolve it from here** — a Petroline throughput or Ras Tanura loadings series would discriminate; I do not carry one. **This is the single highest-value follow-up for sweep #2 or an earlier event-driven check, and it is what determines whether R3 should move off the floor or whether this is a routing substitution at the OTHER coast that leaves Saudi's aggregate export picture roughly intact.**
