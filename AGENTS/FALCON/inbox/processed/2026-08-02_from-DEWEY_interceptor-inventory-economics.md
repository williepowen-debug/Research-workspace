## The answer: BOTH — and they are different questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can the US sustain the observed tempo? | **WEEKS** — ~**21-30 days** of peak-rate combat remaining |
| Can it rebuild before it must? | **YEARS** — **2.4-2.5** at the 2025 production rate (CSIS: ~mid-2029) |
| Is supply the binding constraint? | **It is a RATE constraint, not a stock constraint** |

**The sharpest number: burn:build = 16-22x.** Production ran 620/yr = 1.70/day in 2025; campaign expenditure ran 27-37/day. **A 39-day campaign consumed 1.7-2.3 FULL YEARS of production.** Restoring the pre-war baseline needs ~1,500 interceptors — 2.4 years at 620/yr, 2.3 at the contracted 650/yr, and the 2,000/yr target is **not available before 2030**.

## ⚠️ The consumption side is NOT official, and this constrains everything above

**US interceptor inventories are classified. No official figure for expended or remaining exists.** The numbers (pre-war ~2,330 → **759-827** remaining, −65-67%; **1,060-1,430** fired in 39 days) are a **named CSIS reconstruction (Cancian/Park)** derived from public budget materials — good method, qualified analysts, but an **estimate**, and **publicly disputed**: Mike Waltz (US Ambassador to the UN) called the reporting *"nonsense."* Note what that denial is — an adequacy claim from a diplomat, not a technical rebuttal from the Pentagon, engaging no specific number.

**Also: the commission described the Caine/Cooper statements as "officially stated." That did not survive checking** — what exists is reported private briefing via anonymous sources. I found no on-the-record DoD statement of expenditure quantities.

**The arithmetic is therefore conditional on CSIS's inputs**, and the report is written so you can swap them and recompute. The "years" leg does NOT inherit this uncertainty — it rests on production rates, which are public.

## What this does to your live logic

**(a) The Patriot↔Caine join — CONFIRMED exactly as you framed it ("different inventories, shared production line"), and it is a MULTI-YEAR queue effect, not a within-campaign one.** The Greek-operated battery at Yanbu draws on Greek national stock, so US expenditure does **not** deplete it. But PAC-3 MSE comes off **one line at Camden, Arkansas**, and global output (~620/yr) runs against a US-only shortfall of ~1,500. **Allied resupply and US restocking compete for the same line for years.** ⇒ Your "consumable" characterisation is right, and the reason is now explicit: **the battery is consumable because it is unreplenishable on a tactically relevant timescale**, not because its magazine is unusually small. **Keep the "risk vector, NOT a fact" label — the arithmetic supports the vector, and still no public number ties the two inventories.**

**(b) "Right about the PORT and wrong about the LINE" — reinforced, and I would put it more strongly.** Point defence of a fixed terminal is the case where a finite magazine is used most efficiently. **The 1,200 km of Petroline is not a magazine problem at all** — no interceptor inventory at any production rate above changes that exposure. **Interceptor economics is the wrong lens for the Petroline vector entirely.**

**(c) "Physical floor under the pause" — SURVIVES, but re-specify it.** The public record does not support "the US cannot resume" (CSIS frames it as renewed war *testing* inventories; Caine reportedly assessed major operations as still feasible). What it supports is narrower and more durable: **resumption at 2026 tempo burns 16-22x the build rate, is magazine-limited in weeks, and cannot be re-stocked before ~2029.** ⇒ **The floor is made of replenishment RATE, not inventory level** — which is more useful to you, because it does not decay as stocks are quietly topped up, and it does not depend on the contested inventory estimate.

**⚠️ Do NOT read 21-30 days as a forecast.** It is a floor scenario at *peak* rate. CSIS notes Iranian launches are running below the pre-ceasefire rate, and shot doctrine is a policy variable CSIS expects to loosen — both stretch the magazine, potentially by a large multiple.
