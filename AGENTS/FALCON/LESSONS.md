## [2026-06-12] Prediction text and promotion-threshold text must be written identically
**What happened:** HAW-10 was registered in PREDICTIONS.tsv as "Houthi commercial-vessel kinetic attack (mechanism) **on Bab al-Mandab** (threshold)" while the same session's STATUS emerging-vector row said "first Houthi **kinetic** commercial-vessel hit = promote to core + fires HAW-10" — no locus restriction. When the Houthis struck two vessels in the **Gulf of Aden** (6/8-9), the two texts gave different answers: vector promotion criterion satisfied, prediction locus unmet.
**Why it bites:** the wedge only surfaces when the event arrives, exactly when you're under pressure to resolve fast — inviting post-hoc rubric drift toward the convenient reading.
**Rule:** when a prediction and a vector threshold describe the same event, write one canonical sentence and reference it from both places. On conflict, the registered PREDICTIONS.tsv text is canonical (pre-registration discipline: fire only on the registered condition). Same-class second instance same session: GULFSTATE-01's Red line ("Gulf-state infra damage") vs its promotion criterion ("energy infra / US base hit") diverge — airport hit fired one, not the other.

## [2026-06-12] Live-war boot needs a day-by-day gap sweep, not topic searches
**What happened:** Jun 12 boot searched the current topic (deal talk) and current escalation, but the kinetic timeline effectively stopped at Jun 9-10 (BOARD dispatch date). Missed until director flagged: Iran's FORMAL Hormuz closure declaration Jun 11 (a Tier-1 watch item — it fired, and Brent fell anyway, the strongest decoupling datum yet) and the second US strike night (49 Tomahawks, Kharg threat).
**Why it bites:** topic-shaped searches retrieve the narrative peak, not the full sequence; in a fast theater, single days carry trigger-class events, and missing them silently corrupts the matrix and the scenario marks.
**Rule:** during an active conflict, boot must sweep day-by-day from last STATUS date through boot date ("Iran war July 10", "July 11", "July 12"-style queries or equivalent chronology source), and explicitly check each open Tier-1 watch item against the window before writing any re-mark. *(Copy-worthy to OSPREY per build spec — general active-conflict boot methodology, not Iran-specific in mechanism.)*

## [2026-07-12] A prediction's literal wording narrows your evidence sweep — search the MECHANISM, not just the named targets
**What happened:** re-checking HAW-15 ("Ukraine does NOT strike Russian crude-export INFRASTRUCTURE — Primorsk/Ust-Luga/Novorossiysk/Druzhba"), HAWK searched exactly those named terminals, got April-vintage hits, and wrote "no in-window crude-export strike." The sweep entirely missed a week-long, industrial-scale Ukrainian campaign against Russian shadow-fleet **tankers** — a DIFFERENT channel for the same underlying mechanism (disrupt Russian crude to market). Will surfaced it.
**Why it bites:** the row's named targets (fixed terminals) became the search scope, so a real, decision-relevant escalation arriving via an UN-named channel (the vessels) was invisible. Same wording-wedge family as HAW-10 (locus) and HAW-14 (catalyst) — but here it corrupted *evidence-gathering*, not just resolution. A "clean CONFIRMED" on the letter would have read as a good call while the mechanism fired next door.
**Rule:** when re-checking a prediction at boot, sweep the underlying **mechanism** broadly (e.g. "Iran attacking Gulf oil production" — refineries AND terminals AND pipelines AND port infrastructure, not just one named facility class), not just the prediction's named literal targets. Then map what you find back to letter-vs-spirit. Corollary 1: write prediction labels that name the mechanism, not one channel of it (`[[feedback_forward_discovery_prediction_spirit]]`, `[[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]`).
**Corollary 2:** don't trust your own ledger as the baseline without checking its Last-updated + running a completeness pass; and when a first sweep says "nothing," escalate to a date-careful multi-angle sweep (per-facility date-range queries + a neutral dated timeline source + operational-impact queries) BEFORE banking a "no." A first-pass "no results" is a search-coverage statement, not a fact. *(Cross-cutting fleet-wide per build spec §2 — applies equally to FALCON, OSPREY, and HAWK-synthesis; this is the direct root-cause lesson behind the Tier-1 ledger fixes baked into both siblings' boot/closeout sequences — see CLAUDE.md boot steps 5a/5c and closeout step 12.)*

## [2026-07-27] A prediction written over an ASSET CLASS is exposed to every belligerent who can reach that class
**What happened:** FAL-01 (70%) predicted no Gulf-ally or Iranian oil-production-infrastructure hit by Jul 26. The 70% rested on **demonstrated US-Iran mutual restraint** around the energy complex — and that restraint *held to the very end*: the US ran 13 consecutive strike nights without touching oil production, then paused the campaign entirely. The row still failed, because a **third belligerent pair (Saudi-Houthi)** broke a four-year truce and struck Aramco's Jazan refinery on 7/25.
**Why it bites:** the confidence was priced off the observed behaviour of **two actors**, but the condition was written over an **asset class** that a *different* actor could reach. Those are different objects, and the gap is invisible right up until someone new walks through it. Fourth in the wording-wedge family: HAW-10 (locus) → HAW-14 (catalyst) → HAW-15 (mechanism) → **FAL-01 (actor)**.
**Rule:** state the class test as an **operational threshold** (output offline / FM declared / export interruption), never an exemplar list — and **enumerate in the row which belligerents the confidence is priced off**. Add a fifth-actor clause: a new axis reaching the class is a **re-registration trigger, not an exculpation.** You do not get to say "I didn't model them" twice.

## [2026-07-30] Widening a prediction's scope while keeping the old base-rate instrument can make it ALREADY-FAILED at registration
**What happened:** FAL-03 was written to fix FAL-01's actor gap and deliberately broadened its molecule scope to *"oil/gas"*, with route (a) naming crude/condensate/product/LPG/**LNG**. But its 58% confidence was derived from a base rate computed off `STRIKES.tsv` — an **oil-complex STRIKE ledger**. That instrument counts kinetic events against oil facilities; it is structurally incapable of seeing a **force majeure** (not a strike) on **LNG** (not oil). A QatarEnergy FM on LNG had been live since **2026-03-24**. **Route (a) was satisfied on the day the row was registered.** FAL-03 could never have resolved CONFIRMED — it was published as the explicit kill-switch for a thesis exported to four agents, marked OPEN, and caught only four days later when WALTER routed a signal my own boot sweep could not have produced.
**Why it bites:** it is invisible from inside the derivation. Every step reproduced correctly — the arithmetic was right, the ledger was current, and the base rate had just *survived a full re-dating of its own inputs*. **Reproducibility does not test scope match.** The widening was the right instinct and it is what made the row honest enough to catch itself; the failure was leaving the measuring device pointed where it had always pointed.
**Rule:** at registration, put the **prediction's scope** and the **instrument's scope** side by side and name every part of the claim the instrument cannot observe. If a route can fire through an unwatched channel, either **narrow the claim to what you can grade** or **write the blind spot into the row**. And test the wording against **today** before banking it — if the condition is already true at Made_Date, it is a defective row, not a forecast. *(Auto-memory: `[[finding_widened_scope_needs_rescoped_instrument]]`.)*

## [2026-07-30] An event has a DATE; a force majeure has a DURATION — never let a dated event stand in for a live state
**What happened:** FALCON's published R2 row read *"Active force majeure in-theater: **None current** (Bapco + Ras Laffan were **March**)."* The Ras Laffan strike was known — it is in FALCON's own `STRIKES.tsv` and cited in FAL-01's notes. What was never asked was whether the force majeure it caused had been **lifted**. It had not; it was four months old, serially extended, and extended again *inside* the very window the negative was asserted for. Compounding the irony, the same session **correctly caught a stale Bapco force-majeure headline as March-vintage** — i.e. checking that a *surfaced* FM is current, while never checking whether a *known* FM had ended.
**Why it bites:** conditions with duration (force majeure, blockades, closures, suspensions, sanctions waivers, outages) get filed mentally by their **start date** and then read as **historical**. And a negative is the claim that stops everyone looking — nobody audits "none current," because it reads as the absence of a thing rather than an assertion about the world. *(`[[finding_scope_negative_needs_the_counterparty_standard]]`.)*
**Rule:** for any **state** claim, cite the date you verified it is **still true**, not the date it began — the two-clock discipline already used in `workbook/WARRISK.tsv`. Treat every "none current / no active / unfired" as a claim requiring a positive check at its own cadence. **A negative asserted without a lookup is not evidence, it is an assumption wearing a fact's clothes.**

## [2026-08-06] An "expected window" re-derived from NOW instead of from a pinned ANCHOR drifts forever — and each drift looks locally reasonable
**What happened:** the leg-3 relay window (when the w/c-7/27 Yanbu print "should" publish) was projected three times across three sessions: 8/2 said "8/4-5," 8/4 said "~8/5-8/7," 8/5 said "~8/6-8/10" — each shift unflagged. The written rule ("relays 4-8 days after week-end") also contradicted its own evidence: the 4-8 figure was computed from the week's START (pubs 7/24-28 vs Mon 7/20), and counted from the week's END it would have relays preceding the week's own end. The Will-directed 8/6 review caught both. Re-derived from the actual record: full-week prints relayed +1 to +2 days after the data-week's Sunday END (n=2), so the window was 8/3-8/4 — the 8/5 session wrote "opens tomorrow" about a window that was already closing.
**Why it bites:** every NO-PRINT session re-derived the window as [tomorrow, tomorrow+N] — locally sensible ("it hasn't landed, so it must be coming"), globally a ratchet that can never rule an absence OVERDUE. The window's whole job is to convert absence into information at a pre-committed point; a window that moves on each empty look cannot do that job by construction. Same family as goalpost drift on thresholds, but on a TIME axis, and invisible from inside any single session because each session sees only its own projection.
**Rule:** an expected-publication window must be derived once from a PINNED anchor (state which: period-start / period-end / publication event — for weekly data, period-END is the only well-formed anchor, since a full-week print cannot precede its week's end), written down with its n, and thereafter only CONSUMED, never re-derived from "now." An empty pull inside the window is waiting; an empty pull past it is a finding about the channel. If the window must move, the move is itself a flagged event with a stated cause — never a silent restatement. *(Auto-memory: `[[finding_expected_window_rederived_from_now_drifts]]`.)*

## [2026-08-20] A resolvability guard written as a CLOSED menu downgrades a correct row when the modal outcome is off-menu
**What happened:** `FAL-04` closed **PARTIALLY** on a window in which **no registered route fired** and the substantive crude/condensate claim **held**. It was capped not by a miss but by its own resolvability guard, which named the Jazan restart as the leg that must be *"AFFIRMATIVELY closed"* and offered exactly two ways to close it: **(i)** evidence it restarted and crude flows are normal ⇒ CONFIRMED, or **(ii)** evidence the outage extended into crude ⇒ FAILED, with *"if NEITHER exists… PARTIALLY AT BEST, NEVER CONFIRMED."* The world landed in a **third state neither branch describes**: the outage **persisted** (Aramco pushed restart to 8/30) **but stayed molecule-confined** (a refinery outage cuts crude RUNS and if anything FREES crude for export). I had **affirmative, dated evidence on both halves** — and the row still could not close CONFIRMED, because the menu never listed the answer.
**Why it bites:** the guard was written to stop **silence** auto-confirming, and it does that job. But it silently assumed the only two futures were *restart* and *escalation* — when *"the thing stays broken and stays irrelevant to my molecule scope"* was **the most likely outcome all along**. The defect is invisible at registration, because at registration the menu looks exhaustive; it only becomes visible at grading, when the diligence you actually did has nowhere to be recorded. Note the asymmetry that makes it dangerous: **a closed menu can only ever cost you a grade, never award one**, so it reads as conservatism and is never challenged. Same family as `[[finding_option_menu_omitting_the_owners_choice_reads_as_silence]]`, arriving inside a **prediction's own grading rubric** rather than a routing decision.
**Rule:** every resolvability guard must enumerate the **NO-CHANGE / STILL-UNRESOLVED-BUT-BOUNDED** state explicitly, and say what it grades to. Concretely, for successors: *"outage persists AND is affirmatively confined to excluded molecules ⇒ contributes to CONFIRMED."* Test the menu at registration by asking **"what is the MODAL outcome, and which branch does it land in?"** — if the answer is *"none of them,"* the guard is not conservative, it is **broken.** And keep the discipline that made this grade right anyway: I considered reading branch (i)'s *operative content* as "crude flows normal" and **rejected it**, because reconstructing intent over the registered letter is exactly what produced FAL-01 and FAL-03. **Fix the rubric for next time; never fix it for yourself at grading time.**

## [2026-08-20] A blind instrument's ZERO is not just uninformative — it can silently satisfy a DURATION threshold
**What happened:** `kharg_loadings_watch.py` (PortWatch `port2164`, AIS-derived) printed **0 t / 0 calls for 8/08–8/14**, and I correctly read that as UNINFORMATIVE per my own 7/18 inversion (*the series prints zero through entire NORMAL months*). But on **8/12**, inside that window, a NITC VLCC **demonstrably loaded ~2 million barrels** at Kharg's Azarpad jetty (satellite, dark-immune). ⇒ **A known-positive control was run and the instrument failed it.** The script's only informative branch — `rc 1` = nonzero = flow CONTINUING = REFUTES a strand — **is unreachable**, so the veto is **structurally unable to veto.** Separately, Kharg had genuinely been halted **25 days (7/18→8/12)** and the same zero-series was equally silent about that.
**Why it bites:** my 7/18 inversion fixed the **FIRE** side (a zero must never *cause* a fire) and I treated the job as done. It is not. A permanently-zero series is not merely mute — **it actively manufactures the appearance of a sustained absence**, so any threshold phrased as *"≥N days observed offline"* can be satisfied **entirely by blindness**. PortWatch would have handed me 45 consecutive "observed offline" days at Kharg across a period that included a 2M-bbl loading. **The bias has a fixed sign (under-count); whether that sign is protective or lethal depends on the TEST it feeds** — and a duration test is the case where it is lethal. *(TERRY's addition to my inversion, generalised: `[[finding_measurement_bias_sign_is_fixed_harm_direction_is_not]]`.)*
**Rule:** before trusting any absence-based or duration-based threshold, **run a known-positive control** — find a date on which the thing demonstrably happened via a dark-immune source, and check whether the instrument saw it. If it did not, the instrument cannot carry **any** claim of the form *"it has not happened for N days."* And when reviewing another desk's row, **audit the observability of every duration leg by name** — this is exactly the objection I raised against `HAW-19`'s `≥45d observed offline` leg, and I could only raise it because I had just failed the same test myself.

## [2026-08-20] Before asserting a peer is ABSENT, confirm the listing's PERIMETER covers that class of peer
**What happened:** tasked to coordinate with OSPREY, I ran `ListAgents`, got eight interactive sessions with no OSPREY among them, and reported to the orchestrator that **"OSPREY is not live this session"** — flagging it as *their* error. **OSPREY was live the entire time.** OSPREY and I were both **in-process subagents of HAWK's session**, and that listing does not enumerate a sibling subagent; OSPREY could not have appeared in mine, alive or dead, and I did not appear in OSPREY's. OSPREY made the identical error about me, which is why a routing question bounced twice between us.
**Why it bites:** this is **the exact defect I spent the same session documenting in `kharg_loadings_watch.py`** — a zero from an instrument that cannot see the class is not a zero — arriving in the **coordination layer** instead of the data layer, where I had no habit of checking for it. The disguise is that a peer listing feels like ground truth about the world rather than a query with a scope, so the perimeter question never gets asked. And the failure is **confident and load-bearing**: I did not merely fail to reach OSPREY, I asserted a fact about the fleet's operating state and corrected a superior on it. *(Same family as `[[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]` and `[[finding_verification_zero_is_ambiguous]]` — a check certifies its SCOPE, not your capability.)*
**Rule:** an empty or non-matching listing is a statement about **the query's perimeter**, never about the world. Before writing *"X is not live / not running / not present"*, state which classes the instrument enumerates and confirm X's class is one of them — for `ListAgents`, it does **not** cover sibling subagents of a shared parent. If the perimeter is unknown or doesn't cover the class, the honest form is **"X does not appear in my listing, which may not cover it"** — and the cheap fix is to **ask the orchestrator, who knows its own topology**, rather than infer. **Generalised: whenever you catch a perimeter defect in a DATA instrument, immediately ask where the same defect lives in your COORDINATION instruments — that is the blind spot, because coordination tools are not treated as instruments at all.**

## [2026-08-31] An AI-GENERATED STATE-ACTOR ARTEFACT defeats "trace to the originating source" — provenance is necessary and NOT sufficient
**What happened:** Trump's 2026-08-30 Truth Social post — *"Kharg Island being blown to smithereens"* — carried an **AI-generated explosion video**, and for ~36 hours a FAL-01-class Kharg-strike claim circulated downstream of it ("Kharg reportedly targeted", The National et al.). **No strike occurred:** Reuters ran AI-detection software on the clip and established it as synthetic; NIOC's CEO called it "laughable" with work continuing; there was no CENTCOM release, no imagery, no Iranian damage statement, and Kharg loading (resumed 8/12) never changed state. The desk's standing date-trap defence **did not fire in its usual form**: the item was neither a resurfaced 3/13 or 4/7 story nor a real new strike — and the provenance filter **passed**, because the originator IS the President and the source IS primary. (WALTER's verify record `VERIFY-20260831-iran-weekend-claims` is the record; BRENT's autonomous routine had correctly flagged-not-graded it.)
**Why it bites:** "trace it to the originating source" is this theater's standard defence, and here it **defeats itself** — a head-of-state post with synthetic imagery attached reads as primary-source evidence and is not. Passing every source-QUALITY filter is exactly what makes it dangerous: same shape as the 18-Jun UKMTO "strait open" trap (institutional primary, no sovereign act required), one rung worse because the fabrication is in the ARTEFACT, not the framing. The most supply-destructive headline of the weekend was synthetic while the tape moved +3.4-3.5% on a claim set containing it.
**Rule:** on any extraordinary kinetic claim whose evidence is an **IMAGE or VIDEO**, the artefact is evidence **only once it has been authenticated** (AI-detection, independent imagery, or a corroborating official release) — **provenance to a primary actor raises its status; it does not settle it.** Pair with the physical-evidence chain this theater already grades on: CENTCOM release · imagery · owner/state damage statement. A claim carrying none of the three plus an unauthenticated clip is a trap-class item, not a candidate event. *(FALCON-side registration: false-fire register row 8/31 + `KB-FALCON-115`; fleet-side guard block is WALTER's, in `anchors/IRAN_WAR_GUARDS.md`.)*
