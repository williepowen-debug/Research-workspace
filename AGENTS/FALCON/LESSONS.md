## [2026-06-12] Prediction text and promotion-threshold text must be written identically
**What happened:** HAW-10 was registered in PREDICTIONS.tsv as "Houthi commercial-vessel kinetic attack (mechanism) **on Bab al-Mandab** (threshold)" while the same session's STATUS emerging-vector row said "first Houthi **kinetic** commercial-vessel hit = promote to core + fires HAW-10" — no locus restriction. When the Houthis struck two vessels in the **Gulf of Aden** (6/8-9), the two texts gave different answers: vector promotion criterion satisfied, prediction locus unmet.
**Why it bites:** the wedge only surfaces when the event arrives, exactly when you're under pressure to resolve fast — inviting post-hoc rubric drift toward the convenient reading.
**Rule:** when a prediction and a vector threshold describe the same event, write one canonical sentence and reference it from both places. On conflict, the registered PREDICTIONS.tsv text is canonical (pre-registration discipline: fire only on the registered condition). Same-class second instance same session: GULFSTATE-01's Red line ("Gulf-state infra damage") vs its promotion criterion ("energy infra / US base hit") diverge — airport hit fired one, not the other.

## [2026-06-12] Live-war boot needs a day-by-day gap sweep, not topic searches
**What happened:** Jun 12 boot searched the current topic (deal talk) and current escalation, but the kinetic timeline effectively stopped at Jun 9-10 (BOARD dispatch date). Missed until director flagged: Iran's FORMAL Hormuz closure declaration Jun 11 (a Tier-1 watch item — it fired, and Brent fell anyway, the strongest decoupling datum yet) and the second US strike night (49 Tomahawks, Kharg threat).
**Why it bites:** topic-shaped searches retrieve the narrative peak, not the full sequence; in a fast theater, single days carry trigger-class events, and missing them silently corrupts the matrix and the scenario marks.
**Rule:** during an active conflict, boot must sweep day-by-day from last STATUS date through boot date ("Iran war July 10", "July 11", "July 12"-style queries or equivalent chronology source), and explicitly check each open Tier-1 watch item against the window before writing any re-mark. *(Copy-worthy to OSPREY per build spec — general active-conflict boot methodology, not Iran-specific in mechanism.)*

## [2026-07-12] A prediction's literal wording narrows your evidence sweep — search the MECHANISM, not just the named targets
**What happened:** re-checking HAW-15 ("Ukraine does NOT strike Russian crude-export INFRASTRUCTURE — Primorsk/Ust-Luga/Novorossiysk/Druzhba"), HAWK searched exactly those named terminals, got April-vintage hits, and wrote "no in-window crude-export strike." The sweep entirely missed a week-long, industrial-scale Ukrainian campaign against Russian shadow-fleet **tankers** — a DIFFERENT channel for the same underlying mechanism (disrupt Russian crude to market). Will surfaced it.
**Why it bites:** the row's named targets (fixed terminals) became the search scope, so a real, decision-relevant escalation arriving via an UN-named channel (the vessels) was invisible. Same wording-wedge family as HAW-10 (locus) and HAW-14 (catalyst) — but here it corrupted *evidence-gathering*, not just resolution. A "clean CONFIRMED" on the letter would have read as a good call while the mechanism fired next door.
**Rule:** when re-checking a prediction at boot, sweep the underlying **mechanism** broadly (e.g. "Iran attacking Gulf oil production" — refineries AND terminals AND pipelines AND port infrastructure, not just one named facility class), not just the prediction's named literal targets. Then map what you find back to letter-vs-spirit. Corollary 1: write prediction labels that name the mechanism, not one channel of it (`[[feedback_forward_discovery_prediction_spirit]]`, `[[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]`).
**Corollary 2:** don't trust your own ledger as the baseline without checking its Last-updated + running a completeness pass; and when a first sweep says "nothing," escalate to a date-careful multi-angle sweep (per-facility date-range queries + a neutral dated timeline source + operational-impact queries) BEFORE banking a "no." A first-pass "no results" is a search-coverage statement, not a fact. *(Cross-cutting fleet-wide per build spec §2 — applies equally to FALCON, OSPREY, and HAWK-synthesis; this is the direct root-cause lesson behind the Tier-1 ledger fixes baked into both siblings' boot/closeout sequences — see CLAUDE.md boot steps 5a/5c and closeout step 12.)*

## [2026-07-27] A prediction written over an ASSET CLASS is exposed to every belligerent who can reach that class
**What happened:** FAL-01 (70%) predicted no Gulf-ally or Iranian oil-production-infrastructure hit by Jul 26. The 70% rested on **demonstrated US-Iran mutual restraint** around the energy complex — and that restraint *held to the very end*: the US ran 13 consecutive strike nights without touching oil production, then paused the campaign entirely. The row still failed, because a **third belligerent pair (Saudi-Houthi)** broke a four-year truce and struck Aramco's Jazan refinery on 7/25.
**Why it bites:** the confidence was priced off the observed behaviour of **two actors**, but the condition was written over an **asset class** that a *different* actor could reach. Those are different objects, and the gap is invisible right up until someone new walks through it. Fourth in the wording-wedge family: HAW-10 (locus) → HAW-14 (catalyst) → HAW-15 (mechanism) → **FAL-01 (actor)**.
**Rule:** state the class test as an **operational threshold** (output offline / FM declared / export interruption), never an exemplar list — and **enumerate in the row which belligerents the confidence is priced off**. Add a fifth-actor clause: a new axis reaching the class is a **re-registration trigger, not an exculpation.** You do not get to say "I didn't model them" twice.

## [2026-07-30] Widening a prediction's scope while keeping the old base-rate instrument can make it ALREADY-FAILED at registration
**What happened:** FAL-03 was written to fix FAL-01's actor gap and deliberately broadened its molecule scope to *"oil/gas"*, with route (a) naming crude/condensate/product/LPG/**LNG**. But its 58% confidence was derived from a base rate computed off `STRIKES.tsv` — an **oil-complex STRIKE ledger**. That instrument counts kinetic events against oil facilities; it is structurally incapable of seeing a **force majeure** (not a strike) on **LNG** (not oil). A QatarEnergy FM on LNG had been live since **2026-03-24**. **Route (a) was satisfied on the day the row was registered.** FAL-03 could never have resolved CONFIRMED — it was published as the explicit kill-switch for a thesis exported to four agents, marked OPEN, and caught only four days later when WALTER routed a signal my own boot sweep could not have produced.
**Why it bites:** it is invisible from inside the derivation. Every step reproduced correctly — the arithmetic was right, the ledger was current, and the base rate had just *survived a full re-dating of its own inputs*. **Reproducibility does not test scope match.** The widening was the right instinct and it is what made the row honest enough to catch itself; the failure was leaving the measuring device pointed where it had always pointed.
**Rule:** at registration, put the **prediction's scope** and the **instrument's scope** side by side and name every part of the claim the instrument cannot observe. If a route can fire through an unwatched channel, either **narrow the claim to what you can grade** or **write the blind spot into the row**. And test the wording against **today** before banking it — if the condition is already true at Made_Date, it is a defective row, not a forecast. *(Auto-memory: `[[finding_widened_scope_needs_rescoped_instrument]]`.)*

## [2026-07-30] An event has a DATE; a force majeure has a DURATION — never let a dated event stand in for a live state
**What happened:** FALCON's published R2 row read *"Active force majeure in-theater: **None current** (Bapco + Ras Laffan were **March**)."* The Ras Laffan strike was known — it is in FALCON's own `STRIKES.tsv` and cited in FAL-01's notes. What was never asked was whether the force majeure it caused had been **lifted**. It had not; it was four months old, serially extended, and extended again *inside* the very window the negative was asserted for. Compounding the irony, the same session **correctly caught a stale Bapco force-majeure headline as March-vintage** — i.e. checking that a *surfaced* FM is current, while never checking whether a *known* FM had ended.
**Why it bites:** conditions with duration (force majeure, blockades, closures, suspensions, sanctions waivers, outages) get filed mentally by their **start date** and then read as **historical**. And a negative is the claim that stops everyone looking — nobody audits "none current," because it reads as the absence of a thing rather than an assertion about the world. *(`[[finding_scope_negative_needs_the_counterparty_standard]]`.)*
**Rule:** for any **state** claim, cite the date you verified it is **still true**, not the date it began — the two-clock discipline already used in `workbook/WARRISK.tsv`. Treat every "none current / no active / unfired" as a claim requiring a positive check at its own cadence. **A negative asserted without a lookup is not evidence, it is an assumption wearing a fact's clothes.**
