## 📕 LESSONS 1–11 (2026-06-12 → 2026-08-31) — CLAIM LINES ONLY

⚑ **Full text, verbatim → `archive/LESSONS_archive_2026-09-11.md`** (rotated 2026-09-11 to stay under the boot read cap; nothing deleted or reworded). **The claim is what has to fire at boot; open the archive for the worked example.** Items 1, 2 and 4 are inherited from HAWK at spinout; the rest are FALCON-authored.

1. **[06-12] Prediction text and promotion-threshold text must be written IDENTICALLY** — two wordings of one condition drift apart and grade differently. *(inherited)*
2. **[06-12] A live-war boot needs a DAY-BY-DAY gap sweep, not topic-shaped searches** — topic searches return the loudest day, not the missing one. *(inherited)*
3. **[07-12] A prediction's literal wording NARROWS your evidence sweep — search the MECHANISM, not the named targets.** The named targets become the search scope, so a real escalation arriving by an un-named channel is invisible, and a "clean CONFIRMED on the letter" reads as a good call while the mechanism ran the other way. **Corollary: a first-pass "no results" is a search-coverage statement, not a fact.** *(inherited)*
4. **[07-27] A prediction written over an ASSET CLASS is exposed to EVERY belligerent who can reach that class** — scope the actor, or inherit all of them.
5. **[07-30] Widening a prediction's scope while keeping the OLD base-rate instrument can make it ALREADY-FAILED at registration.** Invisible from inside the derivation: every step reproduces correctly. **REPRODUCIBILITY DOES NOT TEST SCOPE MATCH.** At registration, put the claim's scope and the instrument's scope side by side and name what the instrument cannot observe.
6. **[07-30] An event has a DATE; a force majeure has a DURATION — never let a dated event stand in for a live state.** Duration-conditions get filed by start date and then read as historical. **And a negative is the claim that stops everyone looking** — nobody audits "none current." Cite the date you verified it is STILL true, not the date it began.
7. **[08-06] An "expected window" re-derived from NOW instead of from a PINNED anchor drifts forever**, and each drift looks locally reasonable — a ratchet that can never rule an absence OVERDUE, which is the window's only job. Derive once from a stated anchor, then only CONSUME it.
8. **[08-20] A resolvability guard written as a CLOSED menu downgrades a CORRECT row when the modal outcome is off-menu.** At registration the menu always looks exhaustive. **Test it by asking: what is the MODAL outcome, and which branch does it land in?**
9. **[08-20] A blind instrument's ZERO is not just uninformative — it can silently SATISFY a duration threshold** out of pure blindness. Never let a blind series carry an ABSENCE or DURATION claim.
10. **[08-20] Before asserting a peer is ABSENT, confirm the LISTING'S PERIMETER covers that class of peer** — an absence read off an instrument that never watched is not an absence.
11. **[08-31] An AI-GENERATED STATE-ACTOR ARTEFACT defeats "trace to the originating source" — provenance is NECESSARY AND NOT SUFFICIENT.** Passing every source-QUALITY filter is exactly what makes it dangerous. An image or video is evidence **only once authenticated**.

## FAL-08 (2026-09-07) — **AN APPROVED-BUT-UNAPPLIED REPAIR IS A LIABILITY THAT COMES DUE ON THE EXACT EVENT IT WAS WRITTEN FOR**

**What happened.** On 2026-08-10 I caught a defect in my own instrument and routed it rather than applying it: `VX-FALCON-SUNK-01` **scores on hull class alone across four live belligerent axes**, so *"a Houthi USV sinking a dhow"* and *"an IRGC missile hitting an ADNOC VLCC"* score identically — **it needs an attacker-identity axis.** Will **APPROVED the repair on 2026-08-17**. I **deferred it on 8/20 with cause** (base-rate-before-registration canon, and the cause was good). **It was still unapplied on 2026-09-05, when a US strike sank an Iranian tanker — the single event class the missing axis exists to distinguish.**

**The cost, stated precisely.** The gate fired **correctly on its letter** and the fire is **analytically ambiguous**, and I could not resolve the ambiguity because the resolving instrument had been approved and not built. I had to ship an adjudication that says, in effect, *"FIRED, and here is why you should distrust it."* **A desk that had done the repair would have shipped a clean verdict.**

**Why the 8/20 deferral was still right — and what was wrong.** The deferral reasoning holds: shipping an un-base-rated threshold to hit a window reproduces the defect the canon exists to prevent. **The error was not deferring. The error was deferring into a system with no clock on the deferral.** The approval sat in a `board_log` row and a SCRATCH bullet — **neither of which any boot step reads as an obligation with a date.** Compare `WARRISK.tsv`, which fires a 7-day alarm because someone gave it a content clock and an affordance.

**⚠️ AND THIS IS THE SECOND TIME IN THREE WEEKS.** The same session had to tell WALTER that its Sirik question exposed a **real** gap: D-indicator #5 was **retired as written** on 8/10, its replacement casualty-ratchet instrument was **approved 8/17**, and it was **never built** — so a civilian mass-casualty event attributed to a US munition engaged **no registered trigger at all**. **Two approved repairs, one ruling date, twenty-one days, both surfaced by OUTSIDE desks (BRENT and WALTER, plus DAEDALUS on `EXIT_PROTOCOL`'s twice-fired rewrite trigger) rather than by any instrument of mine.**

**The generalisation, and it is the transferable half.** **A retirement leaves a HOLE with no staleness affordance.** A stale *value* announces itself — a reader sees an old date. **A retired tell announces nothing: the row is struck through, the successor does not exist, and no check anywhere asks "what is standing in for this?"** The whole class of "approved, not yet built" lives in exactly that blind spot.

**FORWARD RULE, adopted.** **Every Will-approved build I defer gets a DATED row on a surface a boot step actually reads**, with the trigger it is standing in for named. **A `board_log` disposition and a SCRATCH bullet are a RECORD, not a CLOCK.** ⇒ Corollary earned the same day, from `EXIT_PROTOCOL`: **a dated trigger inside a file that nothing greps is not a trigger** — that file's own rewrite trigger fired **twice** (8/20 and 9/1) and was surfaced by DAEDALUS, not by me. **When you write a dated condition, name the reader that will see it fire, or you have written a wish.**

This entry is the FALCON-side instance and the first where the cost was paid on the record.)*

## FAL-09 (2026-09-07) — **FIXING A LINE CAP CAN BREACH A BYTE CAP, AND THE FIRST PASS LOOKED LIKE PROGRESS**

DAEDALUS PR#5 flagged `STATUS.md` at **253 lines** against a 250-line cap (YEY-002 open 12 days). I rotated four blocks out; **the file hit 228 lines and the flag cleared.** ✅ Line cap fixed. ⛔ **And the file had gone from 64,581 B (119% of the fleet read cap) to 76,223 B — 141% — because the rotation removed short lines and added a dense session block.** **I made a live breach worse while clearing the flag that was pointed at me**, and I would not have seen it if `read_cap_check.py` had not been run separately from the line count.

**The generalisation:** **lines and bytes are two different caps on the same file, and satisfying one is silent about the other.** A per-line-dense edit trades them against each other. **Never report a cap as cleared on the instrument that was flagged — run BOTH.**

**What actually fixed it:** a hot/cold split, not more rotation — the reasoning to `reports/`, the false-fire register to its own `domain/` file, the resolved prediction rows to `PREDICTIONS_ARCHIVE.md`. **`thesis/PREDICTIONS.tsv` had ALSO silently gone over the cap (101%) the moment `FAL-05` landed, and nothing on this desk would have told me** — final state 156 lines / 42,083 B (78% of cap, still over the 60% budget, declared) and PREDICTIONS 31,929 B (59%).

## FAL-10 (2026-09-08) — **A SCRIPTED EDIT NEEDS A STRUCTURAL ASSERTION BEFORE IT WRITES — two near-misses in one session, same class**

**What happened, twice.** *(1)* The STATUS rewrite was chained as `python … && cat > part1 …; cat part2 >> assembled …; cp assembled STATUS.md`. The Python step died on a variable-name error, the `&&` chain skipped the heredoc, the `>>` redirection created an EMPTY file, and the unguarded `cp` overwrote `STATUS.md` with zero bytes. Recovered from git HEAD only because this session had not yet committed a STATUS change. *(2)* The staleness-sweep script carried a line I had labelled `# placeholder no-op` — `s = s[:j] + "\n"` — which **truncated `EXIT_PROTOCOL.md` in memory after kill-table row 4**. Every later anchor in that file "could not be found", and I spent two re-runs chasing a phantom character mismatch. The only reason the file survived is that an `assert count == 1` on a later anchor failed **before** the write.

**Why it bites.** Both failures look, from the outside, like the *edit did not happen* — an empty artifact and an assertion error — which is the benign-looking face of a destructive edit. The first was caught by `wc -c` printing `0`; the second was caught by an assertion whose purpose was anchor uniqueness, not truncation — **luck, not design.** A no-op that touches the working string is not a no-op; a chain that can fail mid-way can leave an artifact that is *present and empty*. Same family as `[[finding_anchor_splice_deletes_everything_between_nested_anchors]]` and `[[finding_record_of_an_action_is_not_the_action]]`.

**Rule.** Every scripted write to a live surface asserts a **structural invariant of the whole file** immediately before writing — the count of top-level section headings, or a byte floor as a fraction of the file's prior size — and the shell side never `cp`s an assembled file without a non-empty check (`[ "$B" -gt <floor> ] && cp …`). A step described as a no-op in a comment is either deleted or proven inert. And when several anchors "cannot be found" in a row, **suspect the STRING, not the anchors** — print `len(s)` before chasing characters.

## FAL-11 (2026-09-11) — **A SOURCE CAN BE DIRECTIONALLY RIGHT AND SPECIFICALLY WRONG ON EVERY CHECKABLE PARTICULAR — AND THE PARTICULARS ARE WHAT MADE IT CREDIBLE**

**What happened.** On 9/10 a claimant-aligned outlet reported a Petroline strike with vivid specifics: *six* FIRMS hotspots, FRP >70 MW *"across a stretch of only a few kilometres"*, *"a few km SE of Medina"*, at *17:56 UTC*, by the Houthis. This desk refused to grade it (false-fire row ⑤) and wrote five pre-committed resolvers. On 9/11 the **Saudi MoE confirmed the attacks and shut the line** — the headline was **true**.

**And every particular was wrong.** Own first-party FIRMS pull: **four** zones not six; **66–156 km apart**, not "a few km"; nearest **66 km** from Medina, not a few; first DETECTION **09:30Z — 8.5 h before** the quoted time, which is a **report** time; and attribution points at **drones from Iraq**, not the Houthis.

**The lesson.** Had I propagated on the strength of those specifics, I would have carried a false count, geometry, location, time and attacker into the most consequential week on the board — **and the headline being right would have concealed all five.** The specificity is not corroboration; **specificity is the mechanism by which a weak source acquires authority**. `[[finding_exact_level_authenticates_a_wrong_direction]]`

**Corollary 1 — what a false-fire row actually asserts.** Row ⑤ said *"unconfirmed at every primary; do not propagate."* Confirmation of the event does **not** retroactively make that row wrong. **A false-fire row is a claim about EVIDENCE QUALITY, never about truth.** Resist the pull to read a later confirmation as "the register was too cautious" — the register bought a correct answer for the price of one day, and the alternative was being right by luck.

**Corollary 2 — run the population control before believing your own geometry.** I found four fire zones up to **840 km apart** agreeing to **4.3 km** in cross-track offset from the Abqaiq→Yanbu chord, and read it as proof they lay on one bowed line. **The control refuted it**: the median cross-track of all 427 detections was **−57.7 km**, 39% in that same bin — I had found the population median and mistaken it for a signal. **When a geometric coincidence points exactly where your prior points, the next step is the null distribution, not the write-up.** `[[finding_confounds_align_with_the_prior_you_brought]]`

**Corollary 3 — a report time is not an event time.** `17:56Z` propagated across every wire and into my own `KB-FALCON-166` as though it were when the strike happened. **Thermal data is the cheapest available check on a claimed event time, and it inverted this one by 8.5 hours.** Ask of every timestamp: *is this when it happened, or when someone said so?*

## FAL-12 (2026-09-11) — **A CONTROL THAT KILLS AN ASSOCIATION MUST TRAVEL TO EVERY CLAIM THAT DEPENDS ON IT — I KILLED ONE MYSELF, IN THE SAME DOCUMENT, AND STILL REASONED ACROSS IT**

**What happened.** I ran a cross-track test, found four fire zones apparently aligned on the Abqaiq→Yanbu pipeline, then ran a population control that **refuted my own inference** — and wrote the refutation up at full strength (§3.4, `KB-FALCON-177`). Two sections earlier, in the same report, I used those same hotspots' persistence to argue **"the outage is not a same-day reset"** — a claim *about the pipeline*. **I disclaimed the link and then reasoned across it.** That inference shipped to BRENT as forward-looking guidance, and an external reviewer caught it, not me.

**Why it bites, and why the honest version is worse than carelessness.** Shipping the negative result felt like the rigorous act — and it *was* — so it **bought credibility that the rest of the document then spent**. A self-refutation is filed mentally as a completed obligation ("I was rigorous, I wrote it down") when it is actually an **opened** one: every downstream claim resting on the killed association must now be re-checked. A refutation confined to the section where it was found is decoration.

**Rule.** When a control kills an association, **immediately grep your own draft for every claim that depends on it, before publishing** — the refutation is not done until the dependents are re-graded. Ask literally: *"What else in this document only works if the thing I just disproved were true?"* Related but distinct from `[[finding_an_amendment_read_for_one_item_leaves_the_others_derived_from_the_original_live]]` — there the amendment came from outside; **here I was the author of the refutation and still failed to propagate it**, which is the harder case, because there is no inbound packet to prompt the sweep.

**Corollary 1 — an interval is a measurement; do not narrate one.** I wrote *"still intensifying at 36 hours."* The true span was **25h21m**, and I never computed it — the two real FRP figures beside it (29.6 → 158.5 MW) made the invented number read as measured. `[[finding_exact_level_authenticates_a_wrong_direction]]`. **If a number has a unit, it must come from an operation, not from prose momentum.**

**Corollary 2 — non-detection is not proof, and a remote-sensing product assigns no cause.** *"Dark on the baseline ⇒ not flares"* and *"no terminal fire ⇒ terminals undamaged, damage is mid-line"* both convert a blank into a positive finding. Cloud, smoke, overpass gaps and detection limits hide fires. **The strongest honest form is "no anomaly was detected in this product in this window"** — which establishes neither absence, nor cause, nor damage location, nor operating status.

**Corollary 3 — an untriggered prediction is not a measured zero.** *"Zero confirmed crude barrels offline, 197 war-days"* stated a **prediction state** as a **measurement**. FAL-05 not firing means its registered evidentiary thresholds were not met; a real disruption can exist below them. Say **"net supply loss is unquantified; the row has not met its failure conditions."** The same slip let me call route (b)'s volume limb *"cleared many times over"* in one paragraph while flagging its only figure as untrusted in another. **Nameplate ≠ routed flow ≠ qualifying offline capacity ≠ net supply loss** — four quantities, and I collapsed them.

**Corollary 4 — check your own desk before announcing a discovery.** I presented the JWC/JWLA-034/Yanbu finding to Will as new. `domain/FRESH_LEG_BASELINE.md:21` had carried it since **2026-08-31**, including the successor-circular check I "re-did" tonight with a weaker method. **The failure was consumption, not acquisition** — the fact was acquired 11 days earlier and never reached `WARRISK.tsv`. **Before reporting a find, grep your own domain files for its key terms.**
