## 1. The defect being fixed

**E3 as written:** *"any manager cuts its offer below the 5% design cap or suspends, while its own demand ≥ its Q2 level."*

CCLFX has **never** offered below 5%. Its accommodation lever is the **discretionary +2% top-up**, and the interval-fund rule floors the offer at 5%. So the line keys on a number that structurally cannot move down, and it scored the fund's single most adverse accommodation event — **full top-up in March, zero top-up in May while demand rose** — as a non-event.

**Class:** a **cap-breach test wearing an accommodation-withdrawal label.** This is the same defect class as my own headline finding against BRK-32 (a level test wearing a compounding label), shipped by me in the same session. Logged as such, not softened. → [[finding_prereg_branch_label_can_contradict_its_condition]]

## 3. ⚠️ BROCK's suggested shape is ALSO defective — and in the mirror direction

Suggested: *"a cut below that fund's own **prior-quarter effective offer**."*

**The published quantity is % REPURCHASED, which conflates demand with accommodation.** At 3.42% and 2.90% the fund was **undersubscribed** — nobody was constrained and no accommodation decision was made. Those numbers measure **demand**, not policy.

⇒ **False-positive path:** prior quarter 7.00% (constrained, full top-up) → next quarter 2.90% **because demand collapsed**. "Effective offer" falls 4.1pp and the line fires **"accommodation reversal under load"** on an event that is the *opposite* — demand fell away and no accommodation was needed. **That is a de-escalation firing a stress trigger**, which is worse than the false negative it replaces.

**Fix: the load condition must be a REQUIREMENT, not context.** The accommodation lever is only engaged when demand is at-or-above the cap. Both quarters must be oversubscribed, or the comparison is meaningless.

## 4. E3 — RE-SPEC

**Label:** accommodation reversal under load.
**Condition (checked against the label's sign):** load present in *both* quarters **AND** accommodation *falling*. ✅ label and condition agree.

The line **splits**, because its two halves publish on different instruments at different cadences and a single line would be gated by the slower one.

### E3a — offer withdrawal *(fast leg — N-23C3A, ~quarterly, ~0-day lag)*

> **FIRES** if any register fund's **announced offer percentage** in its N-23C3A falls **below its own design cap** (5% for the 5%-class funds), **or** the fund fails to make / suspends a scheduled quarterly offer.

Knowable within days of commencement. **Base rate: 0 of 6 announced offers** (4 executed + 2 pending) have ever been below 5%. A fire here is a genuinely new event and needs no band.

### E3b — top-up withdrawal under load *(slow leg — N-CSR/N-CSRS, semi-annual, ~2-3 month lag)*

Define, per fund per quarter:
- `oversubscribed` := `pct_repurchased ≥ cap` (i.e. demand met or exceeded the cap)
- `utilization` := `(pct_repurchased − cap) / max_topup` — the share of discretionary top-up capacity actually used *(CCLFX: cap 5.00, max_topup 2.00)*

> **FIRES** if, for the same fund, **quarter n−1 and quarter n are BOTH `oversubscribed`** *(the load requirement — non-negotiable)* **AND** `utilization` falls by **≥ 25 percentage points**.
> **DOES NOT FIRE** if utilization falls by **< 10pp**.
> **NO VERDICT** if utilization falls **10–25pp** *(proration mechanics and rounding live in here)*.
> **NO VERDICT** — explicitly **not** a fire — if quarter n is **not** oversubscribed. Demand fell below the cap; the accommodation lever was never engaged. **This is the guard against firing on de-escalation.**

### Day-one test *(would it fire on the standing state? → [[finding_escalation_line_needs_delta_not_level]])*

- **E3a:** current announced offer = 5.00% = the design cap. **Does not fire.** ✅
- **E3b:** last two *published* oversubscribed quarters ran utilization **16% → 100%** — a **rise** of +84pp. **Does not fire.** ✅

### Does it catch the event it was built for?

The alleged 5/29/2026 event — 5% with **no** top-up while demand rose — computes as utilization **100% → 0% = −100pp**, with both quarters oversubscribed. **FIRES decisively.** The original E3 scored it as a non-event.

## 5. ⚠️ Resolvability, stated rather than papered over

**E3b runs at a ~6-9 month lag on a register that operates quarterly.** The 5/29/2026 and 8/31/2026 pricings do not publish until ~Dec 2026. **This is the same instrument-cannot-measure-it-in-its-window defect I challenged BRK-32 for**, and I am not going to hide it inside my own fix.

Three things follow, and I'd rather state them than pretend the split solves everything:

1. **E3a is the escalation line that can actually run quarterly.** E3b is a **confirming/retrospective** leg — it should be labelled that way in the register, not presented as a live quarterly trigger.
2. **The DEMAND series has a different source than the executed series, and I have NOT verified it.** BROCK's 14%→17% demand figures do **not** appear in any EDGAR filing I pulled. They come from BROCK's register (manager disclosure / secondary). **E3b's `oversubscribed` test is only as fast and as sound as that source** — BROCK should name the instrument and its cadence in the register, or E3b inherits an unaudited input. Flagged, not assumed. → [[finding_rederived_signal_loses_the_senders_caveats]]
3. **If a faster primary for executed repurchases exists** (a fund-level 8-K-equivalent, a website disclosure, an N-PORT field), E3b should be re-pointed at it. I looked and did not find one for CCLFX; that is a negative result from one fund, not a general one.

## 6. Base rate — and why I am not manufacturing one

n is **4 published quarters**, of which **2 were oversubscribed**, giving exactly **one** measurable utilization transition (16% → 100%, a rise). **A single transition cannot support a base rate**, and inventing a separation statistic off it would be the defect I flagged in BRK-32's back-fitted 86% floor.

So, honestly: **the 25pp boundary is a judgment, not a derived figure, and it is labelled as one.** What justifies it is not a base rate but a structural argument — utilization is bounded [0,100] and the mechanic is coarse (one discretionary board decision, not a continuous variable), so a quarter of the full range is the smallest move that cannot be proration noise. **If BROCK has a longer cross-fund utilization series, that should override this number**, and I would rather adopt theirs than defend mine. → [[finding_base_rate_the_threshold_before_building_it]]

## 7. Falsifiers for the re-spec itself

| # | Condition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | A register fund exercises a partial top-up in consecutive oversubscribed quarters with utilization swinging **>25pp in both directions** within a year | 25pp is inside the noise band — widen it or move to a 2-quarter smoothed basis |
| **G2** | The `oversubscribed` flag proves unobtainable at quarterly cadence for **≥2 of the 5 register funds** | E3b is not gradeable as specified → mark **STUCK** and run on E3a alone rather than grading it loosely |
| **G3** | A fund cuts its **announced** offer below cap (E3a fires) **while undersubscribed** | E3a needs its own load condition; as written it would flag a fund shrinking an offer nobody wanted |
