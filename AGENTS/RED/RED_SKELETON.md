## STANDING COUNTER-EVIDENCE REGISTRY

These are known disconfirming data points that the network tends to underweight. RED maintains these as standing counter-arguments, updated each sweep.

### vs CARL (Consumer Stress)

**CARL says:** Consumer credit is BREACHED; 10 vectors RED; auto DQ 6.65%; front-loading paradigm active.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **Employment remains strong.** Unemployment 4.1%; initial claims stable ~220K; job openings 7.5M+. Consumer stress without job losses has limited transmission history.
2. **Wage growth > inflation.** Real wages positive for 12+ months; low-income wage growth strongest. This directly undermines the cost-of-living squeeze thesis.
3. **Consumer spending resilient.** Retail sales growing; holiday 2025 solid; services spending robust. Behavior doesn't match "stressed consumer."
4. **DQ levels in context.** Auto DQ 6.65% is elevated but below 2009 peak (5.3% — wait, current exceeds 2009). Credit card DQ rising but charge-off rates still manageable.
5. **Stock market wealth effect.** S&P 500 +15% 2025; household equity allocation 47% ATH = top 60% feels wealthier, not poorer.

**Strength:** MODERATE to STRONG. Employment + wages + spending form a coherent counter-narrative. CARL's thesis requires these to deteriorate — they haven't yet.

**Key question for CARL:** If the consumer is truly breaking, why is spending still growing? What's the leading indicator that spending rolls over?

### vs SAM (Japan Fiscal Crisis)

**SAM says:** JGB 40Y RED (4.24%); fiscal sustainability crisis; procyclical Japan via Norinchukin; Feb 5/Feb 8 critical.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **Japan has survived worse.** Debt/GDP 260% for decades; BOJ owns 50%+ of JGBs; self-financing dynamic persists.
2. **BOJ retains tools.** Yield curve control adjustments, rate hikes, FX intervention — tools exist and are being used.
3. **Yen weakness is manageable.** 154 is not 2022's 150 crisis. Intervention signals reduce carry trade risks.
4. **Norinchukin is one institution.** $67B CLO pivot is material but Japan's financial system is larger than one entity.
5. **Election outcome uncertain.** Takaichi fiscal expansion isn't guaranteed; LDP may moderate.

**Strength:** MODERATE. Japan's ability to muddle through for decades is the strongest counter-evidence. But long-end yield breakout is genuinely novel.

**Key question for SAM:** What's the specific mechanism that breaks 30 years of "Japan muddles through"? Why is THIS time different?

### vs REGINALD (Regional Banks/CLO)

**SAM says:** VLY 9.5 RED; bank stress from CRE, CLOs, deposits.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **VLY is priced in.** Market knows; stock -40% already; earnings won't surprise.
2. **Larger banks are fine.** JPM, BAC reporting strong; stress concentrated in tail of small regionals.
3. **Deposit outflows stabilized.** Post-SVB normalization complete; money market flows slowed.
4. **CLO performance holding.** AAA tranche defaults essentially zero; spread widening modest.
5. **FHLB system functioning.** Capacity remaining; no systemic funding breakdown.

**Strength:** MODERATE. Individual bank stress ≠ system stress. BUFFER confirms system-level capital adequate (CET1 12.7%).

**Key question for REGINALD:** How does VLY/CMA stress transmit to the broader system given FHLB capacity and strong larger bank earnings?

### vs HENRY (Market Valuation/Concentration)

**HENRY says:** CAPE >40; concentration >1929; household equity 47% ATH; crash risk elevated.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **Zweig Breadth Thrust ACTIVE.** 100% historical success rate for +23.3% 12-month forward returns. This is the single strongest bullish signal in HENRY's own data.
2. **Structural Bid is HENRY's own finding.** $1.5T+ annual mechanical demand that didn't exist in 1929/2000. HENRY built the counter-thesis.
3. **Earnings growth supports valuations partially.** +8.2% blended growth; forward P/E more reasonable than trailing CAPE.
4. **Innovation cycle (AI) is real.** Unlike 2000 dot-com, current tech leaders have massive revenues and profits.
5. **Correction ≠ Crash.** CAPE >40 predicts lower 10-year returns, not imminent crash. Timing is notoriously poor.

**Strength:** STRONG. ZBT + Structural Bid + earnings growth = coherent bull case that uses HENRY's own data. The contradiction between ZBT (100% bullish) and insider selling (bearish) is unresolved in HENRY's framework.

**Key question for HENRY:** How do you reconcile ZBT's 100% success rate with crash-level valuation metrics? Which signal dominates?

### vs CREED (CRE Extend-and-Pretend)

**CREED says:** Office CMBS DQ 11.31% RED; maturity wall Q2-Q3 2026; extend-and-pretend exhausting.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **Forbearance has worked before.** 2009-2012: regulators extended CRE timelines 3+ years. They can do it again.
2. **Office is isolated.** Multifamily, industrial, data center performing well. CRE stress is sector-specific, not market-wide.
3. **Conversion pipeline growing.** Office-to-residential conversions accelerating; supply adjustment beginning.
4. **Cap rate adjustment occurring.** Transactions at new prices = market clearing, not crisis.
5. **Bank CRE DQ 1.56% (GREEN).** The "masked" interpretation requires evidence that banks are hiding losses, which is assumption not proof.

**Strength:** MODERATE. Forbearance precedent + sector isolation = the real containment story. But CMBS 11.31% vs bank 1.56% gap IS suspicious.

**Key question for CREED:** If forbearance extends another 2 years (precedent says it can), does the maturity wall thesis expire?

### vs OTTO (Auto Fraud)

**OTTO says:** 3 fraud cases; $370M+ bank losses; pattern at 90% confidence.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **Three cases may be the full pattern.** Not every fraud case reveals a systemic problem. Could be isolated bad actors.
2. **Bank losses are manageable.** $370M across JPM/Fifth Third is noise for banks that size.
3. **Warehouse lenders tightening = self-correcting.** The fraud is being discovered and addressed; market correcting itself.
4. **Subprime auto is small.** Total subprime auto ~$250B; even significant fraud is a fraction of consumer credit.

**Strength:** WEAK to MODERATE. OTTO's thesis is well-evidenced for the specific cases. The question is scale, not validity.

**Key question for OTTO:** What's the total addressable fraud (how many similar lenders exist)? Without a 4th case, does confidence decay?

### vs LIQUID (Treasury/Funding)

**LIQUID says:** RRP depleted ($1.96B); ARMED status; funding stress approaching.

**Counter-evidence:**
1. **RRP depletion was expected and managed.** Reserves remain ample (~$3.4T); Fed monitoring QT pace.
2. **SRF exists precisely for this.** $500B facility designed to prevent funding dislocations.
3. **Money market funds have alternatives.** Bills, repo, ON RRP — multiple parking spots.
4. **Jan 29 7Y auction = noise.** Treasury auctions rarely cause lasting dislocations; 3bps tail is immaterial (PROME 002 already noted).

**Strength:** MODERATE. SRF as backstop is the strongest counter-evidence. RRP depletion is a real structural shift, but managed by design.
