## 5. HISTORICAL CHOKEPOINTS THAT RESOLVED FASTER THAN EXPECTED

### Suez Crisis 1956 (Resolved in ~3 months, expected 6-12)
**Why it resolved fast:** US pressure on UK/France (massive geopolitical lever). Eisenhower threatened to withdraw dollar support from pound sterling. Economic coercion from the superpower ended it — not mine clearing or military resolution.
**Relevance:** If China applies equivalent economic pressure on Iran, it could resolve faster than "mine clearance timelines" suggest.

### Iran-Iraq Tanker War 1984-1988 (Extended, but flows never fully stopped)
The "closure" was never total. Ships took the risk with war-risk premiums. Rate spikes attracted more shipping, not less. At some point, insurance rates normalize because actuarial data proves risk is lower than feared.
**Relevance:** The current "closure" may not remain at zero. As premiums adjust and ships test the waters, partial flows resume faster than full political resolution.

### Libya 2011 (Oil back online within ~14 months, expected longer)
Libyan oil was expected to take years to come back. It took ~14 months to restore significant production despite ongoing instability. The economic incentive for flows is powerful.

### Gulf War I 1991 — Kuwaiti Oil Fires
Market expected 18-24 months to restore Kuwaiti production. Actual restoration: ~12 months. Wild-well firefighters (Red Adair et al.) worked faster than expected.
**Relevance to us:** The institutional consensus on "how long" chokepoint crises take is systematically too pessimistic. Markets overcorrect on duration.

### The Pattern: Why Resolutions Are Faster
1. **Economic pain creates political will** — the faster the economic damage, the faster all parties want out
2. **"Face-saving" exits are always available** — both sides always construct a narrative where they "won"
3. **Institutional forecasters are systematically too pessimistic on duration** — their estimates are politically conservative

### Bias #4: STNG Conflation
