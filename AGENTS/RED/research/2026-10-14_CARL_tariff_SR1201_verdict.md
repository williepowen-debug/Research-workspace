# RED verdict on CARL's tariff counter (COUNTER_LOG 2026-10-10, "RED to attack or adopt")

**Written:** 2026-10-14 ~23:15 EDT (S52, on Will's word: "go ahead with #11 the CARL tariff question"). **Primary read by RED:** NY Fed Liberty Street Economics, *"How Fast Do Tariffs Pass Through into Consumer Prices?"* (Amiti / Heise / Weinstein, 2026-10-06), fetched 10/14 ~23:10 ET. Neither CARL nor RED had read it before tonight: CARL's entry came via WALTER SIG-W-20261008-025, and WALTER's correction COR-20261012-19 followed.

**CARL's gloss (10/10):** tariffs added ~2.9pp to goods inflation by Feb 2026; the contribution falls to ~0 by Aug 2026; *"that leg is spent as a FORWARD driver and survives only as a past level shock already in prices."*

## Verdict: ADOPT for 12-month inflation (and stronger than CARL said); ATTACK "spent" for monthly flow and for the consumer squeeze

The paper answers three different questions, and they get three different answers. Which one matters depends on the measure a thesis actually uses (`[[finding_level_and_rate_look_like_agreement_until_you_name_which]]`).

| Measure | What the primary forecasts (verbatim, at end-Sep 2026 tariffs + the announced Jan 2027 Canadian auto increase) | CARL's gloss | RED |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12-month goods inflation** (y/y) | *"peaked in February 2026, at 2.9 percentage points, and is forecast to fall to around zero by August 2026. **It then turns negative** as the large tariff increases of 2025 drop out of the twelve-month comparison, before turning slightly positive by mid-2027."* | "falls to ~0: spent" | **ADOPT and STRENGTHEN.** It is not just spent: from ~Sep 2026 into 2027 tariffs are a **negative** base-effect contribution to y/y goods inflation. Neither CARL nor WALTER's correction carried the negative interval. |
| **Monthly flow** (m/m; what 3-month annualized core, and so RED's FT-08, measures) | *"After August 2026, the tariff effect on the price level **edges up again**. About half … the August 2026 tariffs on Canadian goods, which are still passing through. The rest reflects the tariff increase on Canadian autos announced for January 2027."* | not addressed | **ATTACK "spent" for m/m.** A rising level means a small *positive* monthly push from August on. A y/y base effect does not relieve a monthly-pace measure. |
| **Price level** (the consumer cost squeeze) | peak *"near 3 percent in February 2026"*; *"easing to about 2 percent by August 2026"* (the Supreme Court ruling ended the emergency-powers tariffs, replaced by a 10% surcharge); *"tariffs have left consumer goods price levels higher"* | "survives only as a past level shock already in prices" | **Partly ATTACK.** For a *cost-squeeze* thesis, the level **is** the burden, and it is persistent (~2% higher) and edging up, not spent. **What is true:** it is no longer an *accelerating* burden. |

**Scope caveats that travel with any use of this paper:**
- **Coverage:** 67 non-oil consumer goods categories. Services are excluded.
- **Fixed factors:** the estimates hold *"exchange rates, wages, consumer demand, and monetary policy"* fixed.
- **A forecast, not observed policy:** it assumes end-September tariff levels plus the announced January 2027 Canadian auto increase.
- **Pass-through is ~25%,** and about a third of the effect comes through US-made goods (indirect, slow).

## Cross-check against the data (correlation, not attribution)

Core goods CPI (FRED `CUSR0000SACL1E`), MoM: **Jun −0.09 · Jul +0.20 · Aug +0.11 · Sep +0.25**. September's firmer core goods print is *consistent* with a level that is edging up again after August. It is not attributed to tariffs: BLS also names used cars, apparel and recreation among September's risers.

## What it does to RED (no weight moves)

- **Stag bucket:** a **small bull counter on y/y readings** (the tariff base effect turns negative into 2027), weighted small because it is a research counterfactual. Logged for the next discretionary re-derivation beside the anti-spiral counter.
- **FT-08 (m/m-based):** **not relieved.** If anything, a small positive m/m push continues, which slightly lowers the already-small odds that FT-08 exits on 11/10.
- **CARL's thesis:** CARL's own call. RED's reading for CARL: the tariff leg is spent as an *inflation-rate* driver on y/y, *not* spent as a *level* burden on households, and *not* spent on m/m.

## A note on the relay chain (for WALTER)

WALTER's COR-20261012-19 correctly restored the post-August level rise that CARL's gloss dropped. But it summarized the 12-month path as *"turns slightly positive by mid-2027"* without the **negative interval** in between, which is the larger near-term effect for any y/y reader. That is a correction to the correction, routed to WALTER as a packet (`[[finding_rederived_signal_loses_the_senders_caveats]]`).
