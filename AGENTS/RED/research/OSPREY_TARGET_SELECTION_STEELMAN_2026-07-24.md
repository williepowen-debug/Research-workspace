## 1. The three hypotheses, tested

### H3 — reporting/selection bias ("crude infra is being hit, we just don't see it") — **~10%, largely KILLED at the outcome layer**
The channel model's load-bearing claim is about OUTCOMES, and the outcome series is bias-immune: **crude exports cannot print a post-2022 record if crude-export infrastructure is being covertly degraded.** Kpler flow data doesn't care what either belligerent reports. Refinery fires are FIRMS-visible and photogenic while port damage is suppressible — the bias is real at the *strike-report* layer — but it cannot survive the flows. Residual 10% covers: shadow-fleet opacity at the margin and strike-attempt (vs strike-success) undercounting, which matters for INTENT inference (see §3).

### H2 — capability limit — **split it in two; the split is the whole answer (~35% for the half that survives)**
- **H2a: RANGE limit — REFUTED.** Omsk (>2,500km, 7/6) demonstrates an envelope that covers every western crude-export terminal (Primorsk ~850km, Ust-Luga ~850km, Novorossiysk ~400km). And Channel 2 carries *prior firings* — Primorsk (~6/25), NOVATEK Ust-Luga (7/10), Vysotsk. Ukraine can reach these targets and has hit them.
- **H2b: DAMAGE-CLASS limit — SUPPORTED, and it's the interesting half.** Terminals have been struck, yet exports print records — strikes on hardened port infrastructure (moorings, SPMs, tank farms with berms) do not produce *lasting* outages at achievable warhead masses, while a distillation column burns and stays down for months. Even the CPC halt — the campaign's only real crude interruption — works through **insurer/owner risk tolerance** (Exxon/Chevron refusing calls), not destroyed capacity, and the SPMs are officially intact. The revealed production function: Ukraine's drones can *soft-kill* refining cheaply and can only *harass* crude export. On that economics, products-heavy selection is what a rational targeteer does **regardless of strategic instruction** — which is precisely why intent is hard to identify from target counts alone.

### H1 — strategic choice — **~55%, but in a WEAKER form than the sweep's framing**
Not "crude is spared" (Primorsk/Ust-Luga/CPC-tankers falsify the strong form) but: **products-first prioritization, with crude-directed action kept below the lasting-damage threshold.** Supporting evidence: (a) the post-exhaustion pivot went DOWN-CHAIN on products (NS-Oil, Armavir depot, Tuymazy pump, 7/22-23 in a 223-drone wave) rather than up-weight on terminals — with all refinery novelty exhausted, the next marginal strike still chose products; (b) the military logic is self-reinforcing (diesel starves the front; crude earns Russia less per barrel at a ~25% Urals discount than the political cost a global price spike would impose on Kyiv's backers); (c) allied price-sensitivity is a real, documented constraint channel. **The identification caveat RED must flag: H1 and H2b predict nearly identical target counts.** The clean H1 evidence would be reachable, soft, crude-critical targets NOT taken (e.g., repeated non-follow-up on Primorsk after a demonstrated hit); that pattern exists but is thin. 55/35/10 is a judgment on revealed preference + context, not a measurement.
