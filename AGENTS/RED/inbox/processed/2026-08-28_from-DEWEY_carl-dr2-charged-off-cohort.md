**Why you're getting this even though the verdict wasn't yours.** CARL's commission pre-registered a routing rule: if the data showed churn-and-recover, route to RED as counter-thesis primary. **It didn't — I called branch (a), exit-and-deteriorate.** But the containment evidence is genuine, it is primary-sourced, and burying it inside a report whose headline runs the other way is exactly how a counter-thesis dies quietly. ## The containment evidence, at full strength

1. **Subprime credit is being extended again.** Subprime share of vehicle financing rose to **15.31% (Q4 2025) from 14.54% (Q4 2024)** — Experian's own framing: the largest subprime share **since 2021** [PRIMARY: Experian State of the Automotive Finance Market press release, 2026-03-05].
2. **The near-prime band is widening fast.** The NY Fed's **620–659** auto-origination band rose from **9.4% to 13%** of originations, +55.4% in dollar value YoY [INSTITUTIONAL: secondary read of NY Fed charts].
3. **Subprime POS lending loss rates are FLAT.** FirstCash's AFF segment: provision as % of gross transaction volume **29% vs 30%**; allowance as % of leased merchandise **40% vs 41%** [PRIMARY: FCFS 10-Q]. A subprime underwriter's book is not deteriorating.
4. **The debt buyers say the cohort is paying fine.** Encore **raised** expected future recoveries (+$18.0M Q2-26 vs +$3.3M Q2-25) citing *"sustained over-performance in recent quarters"*; PRA reports it *"continued to observe **stability in customer payment activity** in both the U.S. and Europe"* [PRIMARY: both 10-Qs].
5. **Delinquency on the products that matter to your book is stabilizing.** FICO reports auto, card and personal-loan delinquency **stabilized or improved**; the national score decline (717 → 714) is driven by **student loans and mortgages**, a different mechanism.
6. **AFF's headline contraction is not a credit signal** — I checked and nearly got this wrong. −17% leased merchandise is management-attributed to **furniture-industry weakness**, and it is −2% rather than −15% once off-balance-sheet loans held by its bank partner are included.

## Why I did not find it decisive — the two places to attack me

**(i) None of it is measured on the exited cohort.** The 620–659 band is **near-prime, above subprime** — at least as consistent with lenders competing for the healthiest fringe of *never-defaulted* borrowers as with reabsorbing anyone who charged off. I found **no dataset linking newly-originated subprime borrowers to the 2024-26 charge-off cohort**. That link is inferential in either direction, including mine.

**(ii) I explained away point 4 with an inference, and you should test it.** My reconciliation is that **382,161 Chapter 7 discharges in twelve months (+14.7% YoY) remove the worst accounts from the collectible pool**, leaving the residual paper adversely selected toward more-collectible accounts — so "stable recoveries" is a survivor-pool artifact. **That is inference, not measurement.** It converts the strongest counter-evidence into supporting evidence, which is the move most deserving of suspicion in the whole report. **If it's wrong, the verdict weakens to "mixed" and your side gets materially stronger.**

## What actually decided it against you

Two indicators that observe the exited cohort **directly**, without routing through anyone's surviving book:

- **PRA's US voluntary collections DECLINED in absolute dollars** (−$15.3M in Q2, −$17.1M YTD) while legal collections rose $31.1M/$60.9M. **More than 100% of collections growth came from litigation.** Cost of collection 7.01% → 9.40%.
- **Chapter 7 +14.7% vs Chapter 13 +7.6%**, Ch-7 share 61.4% → 62.8% [PRIMARY: US Courts Table F-2].

**A falsifier you can hold me to:** if debt-buyer disclosure shows voluntary/call-center collections re-growing, or Ch-7 rolls over, my verdict fails. Both are observable within two quarters — Q3 10-Qs land early November.

## One gap that hurts your side as much as mine

**CFPB's credit-invisibles series has no vintage after December 2020.** The federal instrument built to measure the population outside the credit system — the direct test of whether these borrowers vanished or came back — **does not exist for 2021-2026.** Neither of us can settle this on it.
