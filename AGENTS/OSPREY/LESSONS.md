## [2026-07-12] A prediction's literal wording narrows your evidence sweep — search the MECHANISM, not just the named targets
**PRIMARY INHERITOR — OSPREY's founding calibration lesson.** Source: HAW-15 (Russia/Ukraine crude-export channel), HAWK's own incident.
**What happened:** re-checking HAW-15 ("Ukraine does NOT strike Russian crude-export INFRASTRUCTURE — Primorsk/Ust-Luga/Novorossiysk/Druzhba"), HAWK searched exactly those named terminals, got April-vintage hits, and wrote "no in-window crude-export strike." HAWK entirely missed a week-long, industrial-scale Ukrainian campaign against Russian shadow-fleet **tankers** (Sea of Azov + Black Sea, 7/6-12, 21-42 vessels claimed) — a DIFFERENT channel for the same underlying mechanism (disrupt Russian crude to market). Will surfaced it.
**Why it bites:** the row's named targets (fixed terminals) became the search scope, so a real, decision-relevant escalation arriving via an UN-named channel (the vessels) was invisible. A "clean CONFIRMED" on the letter would have read as a good call while the mechanism fired next door.
**Rule:** when re-checking a prediction at boot, sweep the underlying **mechanism** broadly (here: "Ukraine attacking Russian oil" — refineries AND terminals AND tankers AND pipeline), not just the prediction's named literal targets. Then map what you find back to letter-vs-spirit. Write prediction labels that name the mechanism, not one channel of it (`[[feedback_forward_discovery_prediction_spirit]]`, `[[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]`).
**Corollary — own-ledger-staleness (same incident, same day, Will pushed a SECOND time):** HAWK's "tighter confirmation" *also* concluded "terminals unstruck in-window" — wrong again. Two compounding causes: (a) a **single-search-per-terminal-name sweep returned only the 'famous' Mar/Apr headlines**, masking the June/July re-strikes (Primorsk ~6/25, NOVATEK-Ust-Luga 7/10, Vysotsk 7/6, Kavkaz 6/20); (b) HAWK's **own "canonical" ledger was stale (last updated 6/20) AND had silently missed the 5/23 + 6/8 Novorossiysk crude-terminal re-strikes** — so the internal baseline under-stated the very channel the prediction tracked. **Rule: don't trust your own ledger as the baseline without checking its swept-through mark + running a completeness pass first; when a first sweep says "nothing," escalate to a date-careful multi-angle sweep before banking a "no." A first-pass "no results" is a search-coverage statement, not a fact.** This is the direct root cause of OSPREY's Tier-1 ledger fixes (swept-complete mark, boot staleness check, closeout sweep — see CLAUDE.md).

## [2026-06-12] Live-war boot needs a day-by-day gap sweep, not topic searches
**Copy-worthy, HAWK-sourced (Iran incident, general methodology).** Source: HAWK, Jun 12 boot miss.
**What happened:** a boot searched the current topic (deal talk) and current escalation, but the kinetic timeline effectively stopped several days earlier. Missed until flagged: a formal chokepoint-closure declaration and a second strike night — both trigger-class events.
**Why it bites:** topic-shaped searches retrieve the narrative peak, not the full sequence; in a fast theater, single days carry trigger-class events, and missing them silently corrupts the matrix and the scenario/channel marks.
**Rule:** during an active conflict, boot must sweep day-by-day from last STATUS date through boot date (date-specific queries or an equivalent chronology source), and explicitly check each open watch item against the window before writing any re-mark. Applies directly to OSPREY's Russia-theater boot sweep — same failure mode, different war.

## [2026-07-12] A swept-complete DATE is not proof of swept-complete CONTENT
**OSPREY's own first-session incident (not inherited).** Source: OSPREY's first live domain sweep, 2026-07-12 PM.
**What happened:** `STRIKES.tsv`'s header carried `swept-complete through: 2026-07-12` — a mark inherited verbatim from HAWK at spinout, dated the same day as OSPREY's own boot. A fresh day-by-day web sweep (per LESSONS.md item 2 discipline) found 4 material strikes the ledger had missed despite the current-dated mark: Omsk (Russia's LARGEST refinery, ~10% of national capacity, first-ever strike, new drone-range record — the highest-materiality single gap found this session), plus re-strikes on Ufa, Saratov, and Syzran.
**Why it bites:** a swept-complete mark answers "when was this last touched," not "was the sweep behind it actually exhaustive." Trusting a current-looking date as a proxy for completeness is the same failure class as HAW-15's stale-ledger corollary (LESSONS.md item 1), but a step subtler: there the mark was visibly OLD (6/20); here the mark was dated TODAY and still wrong. A same-day mark can inherit a shallow or rushed prior sweep.
**Rule:** don't let a boot-time staleness check (comparing today's date to the mark) substitute for actually re-running the sweep when the theater is ACTIVE — the Tier-1 fix #2 staleness check (CLAUDE.md SPAWN PROTOCOL step 5b) catches OLD marks; it does NOT catch a fresh-dated-but-incomplete mark. When inheriting or reading someone else's "complete" ledger at the start of a live-war domain, always run one independent day-by-day gap sweep before trusting the mark, regardless of how recent it looks.

## [2026-07-31] ★ A REGISTERED GATE CAPTURES ATTENTION — the loud tracked event hides the quiet countable one
**OSPREY's own incident, and the direct cause of OSP-01 FAILED.** Third failure in the crude-export-terminal channel (HAW-15 ×2, now OSP-01), but the **first with this root cause**.
**What happened:** from 7/20 the desk ran a daily, dated, named adjudication on the CPC halt — `GATE-OSPREY-001`, Will-approved, PROME-registered, day-count clock, leg-by-leg checks, packets to BRENT. CPC is **Kazakh crude and explicitly non-countable** to OSPREY's own predictions; the desk had correctly written that down. Meanwhile, on **7/22**, **Sheskharis** (Novorossiysk, Transneft) — a **Russian** crude-export terminal moving **~650 kb/d, about one-fifth of Russia's seaborne crude exports** — halted loadings for five days. Bloomberg reported it on **7/24**, the same day this desk ran the day-5 CPC gate check and wrote *"Channel 2 — no new terminal damage found, non-countable attribution HOLDS."* It stayed wrong for nine days and was routed to BRENT and HAWK in that state.
**Why it bites, and why it is NOT the earlier lessons:** this was not a stale ledger (item 1's corollary — the ledger had been swept 7/23), and not a named-target-only search (item 1 — the desk searched broadly and *found* things). It is **attention capture**: a registered gate creates a dated, named, recurring obligation, and **nothing creates an equivalent obligation for the un-gated part of the same channel.** The gate manufactured the *feeling* of covering Channel 2 — daily checks, real evidence, real adjudication — while covering only the one instrument that could not move the desk's own predictions. **The most-instrumented object silently redefined the channel's scope.** Worse, the gate's subject was *pre-registered as non-countable*, so effort and decision-relevance were pointed in opposite directions by construction.
**Rule:** when a gate/tripwire is live on ONE instrument inside a channel, **every session must run a separate, explicit sweep of the channel's OTHER instruments and say so in writing** — the gate check does not discharge the channel check, and a channel score must never be re-affirmed from gate evidence alone. Concretely for OSPREY: a CPC adjudication may **never** be the basis for a Channel-2 mark. Generalized: **ask "what in this channel is NOT being watched by the thing I'm watching?"** and if a gate's subject is registered as non-countable to your own predictions, treat that as a standing flag that your attention and your decision-relevance have been decoupled. *(Promoted to auto-memory — the pattern is fleet-general: any agent running a registered gate is exposed to it.)*

## [2026-08-20] ★ AN ENUMERATED-MECHANISM TEST IS A HIDDEN CLAIM THAT THE MECHANISM LIST IS COMPLETE
**OSPREY's own incident — the OSP-05 post-mortem, and the first failure in this desk's record that is a CONSTRUCT defect rather than a confidence or a search defect.**
**What happened:** OSP-05 (adopted from RED's rotation steelman) asked whether Ukraine had rotated to a crude-axis campaign, and operationalised it as 2-of-3 legs: **R1** a crude-terminal fixed-infra strike with a published damage assessment, **R2** sustained crude-tanker interdiction >2 weeks at one export point, **R3** shipments below ~3.8 M bpd and strike-attributable. It graded **0-of-2** on 8/20 (R3 having been ruled resolvability-defective) — **in the same week Russian seaborne crude exports printed 3.58 M bpd, the lowest since April, a fifth consecutive weekly fall, the largest such slide of the war, and Bloomberg attributed it to drone strikes.** The supply event the row existed to detect was happening while the row read zero.
**Why it bites, and why it is none of the earlier lessons:** the ledger was current (swept 8/15), the sweep was mechanism-level not named-target (item 1), and no gate captured attention (item 5). The defect is that **R1 and R2 enumerate two ways barrels can be lost — destroyed capacity and blockade — and the barrels left by a third: deterrence of offtake.** Charterers and owners decline to berth under kinetic threat, loadings stop, shore tanks top out, the pipeline backs up, upstream cuts. Zero capacity destroyed, no interdiction longer than seven days, and ~-640 kbpd gone anyway. **A legs-based test looks rigorous precisely because each leg is falsifiable; the unfalsifiable part is the list.** Bloomberg's own wording carried the tell and I had not built for it: *"actual **and threatened**"* strikes.
**Rule:** before registering any N-leg test, **base-rate the LIST, not just the legs** — ask *"name a way the claimed outcome could occur that fires none of these legs,"* and if you can name one in under a minute, the test is mis-specified. Prefer at least one **outcome-measuring** leg alongside the mechanism-measuring ones; an outcome leg is agnostic about route and is the only thing that catches an unenumerated mechanism. When a test grades 0-of-N while the outcome visibly occurs, **the finding is the test, not the world** — write the post-mortem against the construct, and do not let a correctly-calibrated-looking confidence launder a mis-specified row.
**Same shape elsewhere, same week:** HAWK's `HAW-18` leg (b) died on a construct mismatch, and `HAW-19` LEG A (≥200 kbpd crude **+ irreversibility**) is a pre-registered NON-fire on this exact event — objection routed to HAWK 8/20. Companion wording lesson from the same session: my published 8/8 falsifier ladder read *"any strike on CPC or a non-Russian tanker"* while the agreement it tested exempts non-Russian hulls **carrying Russian cargo** — an over-broad instrument that would have false-fired a "BROKEN" verdict to two consumers. **Both halves are the same discipline: the letter you publish is the letter you will be graded on, so it must match the thing it tests — no broader, no narrower.**

## [2026-08-20] ★ A NAMED-FACILITY LIST IS A CLOSED SET STANDING IN FOR AN OPEN ONE — and adding the missing name is the same error again
**OSPREY's own incident, found on a Will-tasked sweep. FOURTH failure in the crude-export-terminal channel** (HAW-15 ×2, OSP-01, now this) — and the same root cause as item 1's corollary, recurring 21 days after I wrote that I had fixed it.
**What happened:** on **2026-07-30** Ukrainian Unmanned Systems Forces + SSU struck **Tamanneftegaz** at the Port of Taman — the **largest privately owned oil transshipment facility in southern Russia**, ~19.9 Mt/yr (~400 kbpd), tank farm >1M m³, handling crude, products, LPG and ammonia — and **at least 10 storage tanks were destroyed**. It was **re-struck 8/19-20**. **This desk had zero Taman rows.** The 7/30 event sat *inside* the window I had recorded in the ledger header as a *"full mechanism-level sweep 7/23→7/31."* It surfaced only on 8/20, **21 days late**, and only because a sweep was externally tasked.
**Why it bites, and why "I swept the mechanism" was not true:** I believed I had fixed item 1 by searching mechanisms rather than named targets. **In practice my sweep was still anchored on an enumeration** — Novorossiysk, Sheskharis, CPC, Primorsk, Ust-Luga — because those are the facilities this desk already knew about. **Taman was never on the list, so it could not be missed *from* the list; it was invisible to it.** A named-facility list **certifies a coverage it cannot deliver**: every query returns results, nothing errors, and the sweep reports complete.
**⚠️ The rule is NOT "add Taman."** That is the same error with one more name, and it will fail again at the next facility I have not heard of. **Anchor the sweep on MECHANISM + GEOGRAPHY — every Russian Black Sea / Azov / Baltic oil-handling port, named or not — and treat any facility-name list as a *starting* set that must be exceeded, never as the scope.** Concretely: at least one query per sweep must contain **no facility name at all**.
**The connection that makes this one finding rather than two:** this is **LESSONS item 6** — *an enumerated-mechanism test is a hidden claim that the mechanism list is complete* — recurring in **evidence gathering** instead of **test design**. Item 6 was about legs in a prediction; this is about names in a search. **Same defect, two activities, one day.** ⇒ Wherever this desk writes a list and then treats it as scope, the list is an assumption of completeness that nothing checks. **Consequence:** OSP-05's R1 leg was graded *"not fired, all campaign"* on 8/20 — a statement made in ignorance of an event my own ledger should have held. The verdict survived; the reasoning did not.

**⇒ COROLLARY A (added 2026-08-20, verified against HAWK's own record before adoption — HAWK pulled `KB-HAWK-280` and confirmed the gap is named in the same clause as the decline): DECLINING TO PATCH IS A DETECTOR.** Instance 4 of the day's enumeration defect — that the redundancy-exhaustion clause I proposed *was itself an enumeration*, covering **moorings** because I derived it from CPC SPM-2, while a transshipment terminal's binding constraint is **tankage** — was not caught by any check either desk ran. It surfaced at the moment HAWK **articulated why it was refusing to extend the fix**, because justifying the refusal required naming what the existing clause did not reach. **A patch that gets ADOPTED is never asked what it fails to cover; a patch you REFUSE TO EXTEND must be.** *Practice, one sentence: whenever you decline to patch, write down what the fix you already have does not reach.* ⚠️ This does not replace the two-desks-one-object argument — two lanes is what put the object in front of both of us — but it names the **mechanism that converted an observation into a finding**, which is more actionable than "keep two desks."

**⇒ COROLLARY B (same session): an EVENT-RELEASE ABSENCE is weaker evidence than a SERIES NON-INCREASE.** The non-appearance of something that only publishes *on events* says far less than the non-movement of something that publishes *on a schedule*. I reached this grading my own sixth-week export check — *"no sixth decline published" is a **calendar** fact, not an evidentiary one; the week to 8/23 had not closed* — and WALTER reached it the same day, independently, downgrading its own leg (c) because *"no CENTCOM release"* is not a flat published count. **Different theaters, different data, neither desk having seen the other's.** ⚠️ **DOWNGRADED SAME SESSION, BY ME, BEFORE ANYONE RELIED ON IT — I first wrote this as "a genuine convergence, not a shared antecedent" and that OVER-CREDITED IT.** Neither desk saw the other's reasoning, so it is not a shared antecedent in the technical sense — **but the two desks share a fleet, a week, and a house discipline about sourced negatives that was being drilled across every desk that same day, and a COMMON TRAINING ENVIRONMENT IS A COMMON CAUSE EVEN WHEN THE INPUTS ARE DISJOINT** (`finding_shared_antecedent_independence_test`). ⇒ **NOT LOAD-BEARING.** Held at B3 (mirrors `KB-HAWK-288`). **PROMOTION CONDITION, pre-registered: one more instance from OUTSIDE this fleet's habits** — an external analyst, a published methodology, or a desk that had not spent the day being drilled on exactly this. Until then, cite the distinction because it is useful, never the convergence as evidence for it. ⚠️ **And note where this correction nearly died:** I made it in a MESSAGE to HAWK while this FILE still read "a genuine convergence" — the correction lived in the conversation and not in the artifact, which is the propagation defect this same session catalogued four times. Fixed here at closeout. **A caveat that exists only in a message has not been recorded.** ⇒ **Before logging any absence, ask which kind it is** — and never let an event-release absence reach a consumer wearing the confidence of a scheduled non-print.

**⇒ COROLLARY C (2026-08-20, from HAWK's `43d360abd` correction — its rule, with one sharpening this desk owes back): A CAUSAL CLAIM ABOUT ANOTHER AGENT'S REASONING CANNOT BE VERIFIED FROM YOUR OWN ARTIFACTS.** Twice in one session a desk asserted *why* something happened on another desk. **Mine about HAWK held** — HAWK grepped `KB-HAWK-280` and confirmed it. **HAWK's about my motive did not**, and HAWK had not checked it: it credited me with an *unprompted* self-discount when in fact I only re-examined the claim **because HAWK had over-credited it**. HAWK's rule: *"verified against the record" is not sufficient when the record does not contain the cause* — put it to the other desk instead.
**⚠️ THE SHARPENING, AND IT CUTS TOWARD MORE WORK FOR ME, NOT LESS:** my claim was *also* about another desk's reasoning — it only became checkable **because HAWK had written the reasoning down**, in the same clause as its decision. ⇒ **A causal claim about reasoning is verifiable exactly to the extent the reasoning was recorded.** The asymmetry was not that one claim was about text and the other about a mind; **it is that one desk had written its WHY and the other had not.** ⇒ **Practice: record WHY, not just WHAT — a decision whose rationale is unwritten cannot be checked by anyone, including a future you, and forces every downstream reader to guess at motive and occasionally guess wrong in your favour.** *(See `finding_deliberate_and_unnoticed_asymmetry_look_identical` — read the RATIONALE before flagging; this is that finding's precondition: there has to BE a rationale to read.)*
**⚠️ And the flattering-error direction is the one to watch:** HAWK's unchecked causal claim credited me with the *harder* version of what I did. **An unverified guess about another agent's motive lands charitable far more often than it lands harsh**, because there is no cost to generosity and no prompt to check it — so the errors of this class accumulate silently as *inflated* credit across a fleet's records. **Refuse the credit and book the accurate version; nobody else is positioned to.**

**⇒ COROLLARY D (2026-08-20, HAWK's `HAW-19` consolidation — the enumeration finding one level down, and this instance is MINE): A FIX INHERITS THE PARAMETERS OF THE LIMB IT REPLACES, not just the enumeration of the case it came from.** My redundancy-exhaustion clause carried **two** defects, both of which I wrote and neither of which I checked:
- **`≥N of M` with N and M left as UNBOUND PLACEHOLDERS.** I never parameterized them, so the sub-limb HAWK adopted is **ungradeable as registered**. **A threshold with a free variable is not a threshold — it is a sentence shaped like one**, and it passes every structural review because it *looks* like a bound.
- **`≥45d`, carried verbatim from the deleted limb my clause replaced**, never re-checked against the row's own **8/20 → 9/30** window. **Verified: the latest start for a ≥45-consecutive-day run finishing by 9/30 is 2026-08-17 — three days before the window opens ⇒ no in-window event can satisfy it, and LEG A cannot fire at all.**
**Why this is the sharpest instance of the day's defect and not a fifth footnote:** I had *just established* that a fix inherits the **enumeration** of the case it came from — and in **the same packet** committed the parameter version of the identical error. **And the direction is the tell: I was arguing LEG A was TOO HARD to fire, which is exactly the position that should have made me check the duration bound.** An argument's own direction tells you which number you have not checked — *the one whose value would embarrass your thesis if you looked.*
**⇒ Practice: when you propose a clause built from an existing one, list every constant you carried over and re-derive each against the NEW row's window, units and resolver. Carried constants are the least-reviewed text in any spec, because they arrive pre-legitimised.** *(And: an unbound placeholder must never leave a draft — bind it or delete the limb.)*

## [2026-09-02] ★ ITEM 8 — A NAME-SHAPED QUERY CAN ONLY RETURN EVENTS YOU ALREADY SUSPECT. For a clock that counts EVENTS, sweep a source indexed by TIME, not by name.

**What happened.** The Channel-3 kill clock counts days since the last **vessel-strike incident**. Off the 8/16 *Skiros* row it stood at **17 of 21 days** on 9/2 and would have expired **9/6** — days away from this theater's **first ever channel kill**. Two searches said the window was quiet: a **vessel-name** query, and a **"shadow fleet tanker"** keyword query. Both returned nothing dated in-window. What actually happened: **two Russian shadow-fleet vessels — a bulk carrier and a tanker — were struck 8/24**, plus a chemical tanker 8/25 and a sinking 8/27. The clock resets to **8/24 = 9 days.** They were found in a **dated-window maritime security bulletin** — a weekly report indexed by **time and sea area**.

**Why the searches failed, and it is not that they were badly worded.** A name query can only surface events whose **names you already hold**. The incidents that matter most to a completeness check are precisely the ones you cannot name — a *"Russian shadow fleet vessel"* with no published name is invisible to every name-shaped query that will ever be run. A keyword query is only marginally better: it depends on an outlet using **your** vocabulary. **The kill clock's unit is `an event in a window`. So the source must be indexed by `window`.** Match the index to the clock.

**⚠️ THIS IS THE THIRD INSTANCE OF ONE SHAPE ON THIS DESK, and the escalation is the point:**
1. **HAW-15 (7/12, the founding lesson)** — searched the prediction's **named terminals** instead of the mechanism. Wrong twice in one day.
2. **8/16 (*Skiros*)** — a **hull-class** check ("is it shadow-fleet?") would have declared a channel kill on a false quiet; §1's letter says *"vessel-strike incident"*, unqualified.
3. **8/24 (this session)** — a **vessel-name / keyword** check returned a false quiet on a 21-day clock four days from expiry.
4. **8/1 YANINA, found 9/8 (38 days late)** — the 8/1-8/10 sweep was anchored on an OBJECT CLASS ("ALL refinery-class") and missed a confirmed **sinking** of a Russian container ship 130 nm off Novorossiysk; Windward's dated vessel list surfaced it, not any query of mine. Same shape as 1-3, one level up: a class anchor is a name anchor with a wider net and the same hole.

Each time the fix has been *narrow the gap in the name list*; each time the list was not the problem. **Adding another vessel name is the same error with one more name.**

**⇒ PRACTICE, REGISTERED AS AN INSTRUMENT, NOT AS A RESOLUTION:**
- **Before any name-shaped query, sweep a dated-window source covering the whole gap** — for Channel 3, a weekly maritime security bulletin covering the Black Sea / Sea of Azov / Baltic. Name queries come **after**, to enrich rows the window sweep already found.
- **Never grade a channel kill on the absence of NAMED events.** Absence of names is a fact about your index, not about the sea.
- **When a clock is within one sweep-interval of expiring, that is the session to distrust your own quiet.** The near-expiry case is exactly when a false negative is most expensive and least visible — the quiet reads as *confirmation the campaign stopped*, which is the finding you were hoping for.
- Generalisation for any desk: **ask what UNIT your clock counts, then ask what your source is INDEXED BY.** If those two differ, the search cannot see what the clock measures, and every check will pass clean.
