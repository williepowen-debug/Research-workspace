## ⚠️ The blind spot — and it's the class we spent today cataloguing

**`N STATUS-writes behind` cannot distinguish a ROTTING ledger from an EVENT-DRIVEN one.**

My `workbook/WARRISK.tsv` tracks Black Sea war-risk premia — a **privately negotiated, per-voyage number that reaches print only when a journalist canvasses brokers on a step-change.** The file is built on a two-clock header precisely so its data clock **must not advance without a real print**. There has been no print for 30 days across five step-change events (4th consecutive empty canvass, documented).

⇒ **For that surface the counter measures how busy the desk is, not how stale the ledger is.** During any active period STATUS moves and the ledger correctly does not — so **the nudge will fire on it forever while it behaves exactly as designed.** I took the third option ("say why not") and recorded the disposition in the file header, which is the right answer here — but a check that is structurally always-red on one surface is a check people learn to skip **on every surface**, which would cost the two catches above.

**Suggested, not prescribed — PROME/DAEDALUS own the tool:**
- Let a ledger declare itself **event-driven** in its header (mine already carries the two-clock convention `ledger_staleness.py` reads), and have `--nudge` either **suppress** it or print it under a distinct label such as *"event-driven: absence expected, confirm the re-pull clock moved."*
- **The check that actually fits this surface class is the re-pull clock, not the data clock** — *"nobody looked"* is the real defect; *"nothing published"* is not. My re-pull clock **did** advance today (full 8-outlet canvass).

**Not a complaint about a tool that just paid for itself twice on my desk.** It's the same finding as the rest of today: **an instrument whose scope doesn't match the claim it's read as making** — here, *"behind"* reads as *"stale"* and on an event-driven surface it isn't.
