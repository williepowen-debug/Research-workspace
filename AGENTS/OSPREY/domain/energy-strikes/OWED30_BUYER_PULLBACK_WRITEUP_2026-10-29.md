## 1. Definition: who is a "buyer"
**A buyer is the party that takes title to Russian crude or products for refining or resale:** Indian, Chinese and Turkish refiners, and trading houses lifting FOB at Russian ports.
⛔ **PROME's standing constraint, carried verbatim:** *carrier/insurer withdrawals do NOT become buyers.* MSC (8/27, liner suspension at Novorossiysk), FESCO (8/21) and the standard war-risk market refusing Black Sea risk (Gibson via Noah, 8/21, KB-073) are **carriers and insurers**. They act on the *cost and risk of the call*, which is `FLOW-OSPREY-03`'s first link. They are not buyer decisions.

## 2. Source and observation: what a reachable reading would look like
| Observable | Source class | Reachable? |
|---|---|---|
| Named refiner cuts or suspends Russian crude purchases, citing strike or war-risk (not sanctions or price) | Reuters/Bloomberg refinery-source reporting; company statements | **Yes, but rare**, and the cause is usually stated as sanctions or price, not strikes |
| Russia-origin arrivals at a named importer fall on Kpler/Vortexa tracking | Vortexa/Kpler via Bloomberg or Reuters | **Partly**: paywalled; reaches this desk as B3 relays |
| Urals FOB discount widens at Russian ports beyond the freight/insurance delta | Argus/Platts | **No**: paywalled. BRENT owns price; OSPREY carries no Urals figure (OWED-33 closed 9/21) |
| Loaded-but-unsold crude on water rises | Kpler floating storage via Bloomberg | **Partly**: BRENT-owned. KB-014 (July) is the one in-theater instance: ~135M bbl loaded-not-delivered, "buyer hesitancy" |

**Finding:** a *strike-caused* buyer pullback is **observable only as a residual**. Buyers rarely state strike risk as their reason; they state sanctions, price or freight. A reachable buyer limb would have to be graded on stated causes the market does not publish. That is the same unmeasurability that made the CAUSE option fail for WQ-470 (KB-231).

## 3. Counterexample (LESSONS 6: name a way the outcome occurs that fires no leg)
- **Liftings fall with no buyer pulling back:** carriers and insurers withdraw, charterers can't fix ships, and loadings stop while buyers still want the barrels (August 2026, `FLOW-OSPREY-03`). A buyer limb reads "no pullback"; the outcome happened anyway.
- **Buyers pull back with no strike cause:** the US Graham Act (H.R. 5334, signed 9/18) authorises 100% tariffs on the top-5 importers of Russian crude **from 10/18**. EO 14436 (10/16) set them up and **waived them in the same order** (Sec. 4; KB-234). Had the waiver not issued, Indian/Chinese/Turkish purchase cuts would have printed as a "buyer pullback" with a **policy** cause, not a strike cause. An unqualified buyer limb would have counted that as campaign evidence. This is the 9/19 attacker defect again: someone else's action reading as Ukraine's campaign.

## 4. Channel 2 overlap
- **Channel 2's limb 2 already measures the OUTCOME:** Bloomberg 4-wk seaborne crude ≥3.5 M bpd with no shut-in. A buyer pullback that matters shows up there as lower liftings. **Counting it again as a separate limb would double-count the same barrels.** HAWK made this point 9/10 and now keeps carrier/insurer and crude-buyer withdrawal as separate fields.
- **Where the class still earns a place:** as an **attribution note** on a Channel-2 print, never as a gate. When the 4-wk series falls, the note asks: was it damage, carrier/insurer deterrence (`FLOW-OSPREY-03`), buyer pullback, or policy (tariffs, sanctions, GL 135-type licences)? The answer changes BRENT's read of persistence, not OSPREY's kill letter.
