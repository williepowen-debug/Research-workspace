**1. The trilateral (catalyst, late next week).** White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters that Trump, Putin and Xi will meet on the sidelines of the APEC summit in **Shenzhen**: "President Trump will conclude his visit to China with a trilateral meeting" (BBC, updated 15:53Z Nov 14; read at source). Schedule as BBC gives it: ASEAN summit in the Philippines **Tuesday (Nov 17)**; Trump arrives in Shenzhen late **Wednesday (Nov 18)**; bilateral with Xi **Thursday (Nov 19)**; the trilateral closes the visit. Topic named by the White House: "the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world". BBC adds, without naming a source: "It is understood Iran may also be discussed... but not Ukraine." **Russia has confirmed; China has not reacted.** Chinese state media reported only the Xi "bilateral". A senior US official (via AP and Reuters) said no agreement is expected.

Context from the same article: the US-Russia nuclear treaty expired in February; and last month's US deal to import Russian diesel, with sanctions suspended on the biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas including China.

**2. Donbas for NATO.** Zelensky, in remarks made Friday and released by his office Saturday: "The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins Nato? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?' This was the first time this had been raised." He said a proposal with security guarantees, no limits on Ukraine's military and reconstruction funds would go to a referendum, but warned "this is an extremely difficult conversation". US envoys Kushner and Witkoff met him in Dublin Thursday after talks with Putin in Moscow; Ukraine-Russia-US talks were held in Abu Dhabi this week. BBC: Russia has revived its 2021 demands for NATO to roll back from central and eastern Europe. No US response is quoted.

**Why it matters.** It is the first scheduled three-way meeting of the largest nuclear powers. Iran's largest oil buyer (China) will be in the room if Iran comes up, and the Ukraine track has a new US offer on record. ⚠️ Iran: "may be discussed" is BBC's line, not the White House's; a trilateral is not a US-Iran channel.
