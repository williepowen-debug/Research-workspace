- **Authors:** Jesse Thompson (senior business economist, Houston Branch) and Garrett Golding (assistant vice president, energy programs).
- **Key findings (PRIMARY, read):**
  - Global refinery capacity has fallen **"by as much as 10 percent."** The causes listed are the Hormuz closure, Houthi Red Sea attacks, damaged Middle East refineries, Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries, and Chinese and Russian export restrictions.
  - The global fuels market "has sustained as much as 6 mb/d to 8 mb/d of reduced capacity since late spring."
  - Crack spreads (the gap between crude and wholesale **diesel, gasoline and jet**) "have exceeded levels last seen in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine." **The text gives no $/bbl crack figures**; they are in Chart 1 only.
  - The authors attribute the pricing to "acute, multifaceted supply constraints in the global market for refined fuels" rather than retailer or distributor markup.
  - Outlook: "U.S. fuel prices will likely remain unusually elevated relative to crude prices" even after Hormuz flows normalize, partly because damaged Middle East refineries may take months to recover.
  - Diesel: inflation-adjusted "retail diesel prices have never exceeded gasoline by as much." No cents/gal gap is stated in the text. Europe is worse, aggravated by low Rhine water levels.
  - Russia: Ukrainian strikes cut Russian refining capacity by as much as 60% over summer 2026, and processing plus product exports fell 1.5 mb/d (IEA).
  - Middle East: the March 2026 Hormuz closure blocked ~5 mb/d of Gulf product exports, and Middle East throughput fell 2.0 mb/d from February (preliminary IEA August data).
  - China: crude imports fell >5 mb/d, including ~2 mb/d less refinery processing.
  - US: crude exports rose from 4 mb/d (Feb) to 5.2 mb/d average (May) with production flat. US crude and product stocks are at multiyear lows.
  - SPR: "In September, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve held 284 mb of crude oil, its lowest level since 1982." It was draining at 1.2 mb/d. That gives less than six months to the official 80 mb minimum, and less if private estimates of a ~200 mb minimum are used.
  - Global inventories: about 8 billion bbl in late September, roughly 450M bbl below prewar levels.
  - Structural context: from 2019 to 2025, consumption rose 4 mb/d while capacity rose 2.3 mb/d. China and the Middle East added 4.4 mb/d; Europe and North America shed 2.1 mb/d.
- **Region:** global analysis with US implications. The only US-regional mention is "new Gulf Coast hydrocrackers processing light shale oil". There are no PADD or West Coast specifics.
- **Time window:** data through **late September 2026**. Preliminary IEA data cover August. Structural data cover 2019–2025.
