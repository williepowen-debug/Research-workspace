## Working framework

Separate three layers: (1) reversal of an election-positioning trade; (2) durable reassessment of Brazil fiscal/inflation/interest-rate risk; (3) losses transmitting through shared leveraged investors into other markets. Evidence establishes a large local repricing and exchange interruption. Fiscal interpretation is reported by Reuters and economists, not independent causal identification. Forced liquidation and global funding contagion remain unproved. The three layers can coexist.

Reuters says stocks had risen almost7% and the real almost5% since before the first round on expectations of a change of government. A surprise outcome reverses a price embedded before election day. Longer-lived stress depends on actual spending/revenue commitments, fiscal credibility and borrowing conditions, not solely election identity. Numerical analyst forecasts are not measured outcomes.

A carry trade borrows cheaply and owns a higher-yielding currency/asset. Currency depreciation and local bond/equity losses can overwhelm annual income. Leverage can amplify the investor's losses and generate collateral demands; that investor might sell liquid US assets or buy back its funding currency. Yen financing, participant identity, leverage and collateral calls in this event have not been measured. BIS September2024 documents that general transmission mechanism; analogy is not evidence Brazil is now causing it.

USD investors can lose both on local stocks and on currency: illustrative local equities-15% and real dollar value-10% imply0.85*0.90-1=-23.5%, excluding fees/tracking. This is hypothetical arithmetic, not decomposition of today's EWZ move. USD/BRL gains and the real's dollar-value loss are reciprocals, not identical percentages.

## Ordered questions for existing owners; proposed work, not launched

1. LIQUID/SAM: are other carry currencies, yen crosses and US risk assets moving in synchronized fashion after Brazil's opening? Compare Brazil with MXN/ZAR, dollar and global news, avoid causal attribution from correlation alone. Identify named shared investors, margin/haircut changes, fund redemptions or financing withdrawal if public evidence exists. A second/third market affected plus a documented cash/financing link is more diagnostic than Brazil prices alone.
2. LIQUID/BOND: price shock or impaired access? Obtain official auction/cancellation/rebuy notices; check the retained LFT sale, subsequent resumption/clearing, cash buffer, maturities, domestic-vs-FX debt and external funding. Track matched sovereign dollar-bond spreads/CDS and local real/nominal curves. No unsourced debt ratios or CDS-implied default probabilities.
3. Existing macro coverage: policy versus positioning. Verify fiscal statements, finance-team continuity/change, Congress and enforceable spending/revenue plans; measure how much first-round repricing reversed versus genuinely new deterioration. Options/skew/flows may reveal crowding but do not identify every carry investor.
4. HENRY/VIOLET/REGINALD: test US transmission against existing work using post-event credit, banks, breadth and volatility; retain observation/publication lags. Brazil alone cannot fire a US bank-credit thesis. Names and funding mechanisms matter.
5. TERRY only after evidence defines the scenario: screen whether prices/options already charge for it, identify a falsifier and loss budget. A large percentage fall alone is neither bargain nor short signal. No trade proposed here.

Near-term discriminators are Monday's close and the retained October27 auction/outcomes, not an invented permanent watch. Containment: currency/markets trade orderly, retained funding works, other EM and US markets remain resilient. Escalation: continuing disorder, impaired actual funding, documented collateral demands and selling across unrelated markets. Pre-event calm cannot satisfy either verdict.
