## What narrowing meant in previous episodes

Contemporaneous warnings and exact source/anchor qualifications are in [HISTORICAL-SOURCES.md](HISTORICAL-SOURCES.md). Follow-through below uses vendor SPY adjusted daily closes, rather than the price indexes used in the warning documents. Three/six months mean 63/126 trading sessions; drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline in closing prices within the first 126 sessions, including the formation close.

The 2024 observation is a monthly analogue to today's daily divergence. June 28 is the report's measurement date; its precise publication date is unverified, so this row is retrospective episode analysis, not an executable signal. The other cases use the October 9, 2007 Murphy article, August 5, 2015 after-close Pring article (next-session anchor), and June 5, 2023 LPL commentary (following-session anchor). Cases use different breadth definitions and were selected with hindsight. Four stories cannot supply a hit rate or crash probability.

Narrowing can expose dependence on a few leaders: if they stop advancing before other stocks recover, the index loses support. It can also resolve through broader participation while the index continues rising. The 2007 episode occurred amid already-disrupted credit markets; breadth alone does not distinguish that setting from 2023. A positive endpoint can conceal substantial interim losses: the 2015 three-month gain included an 11.07% closing drawdown. The 2023 six-month drawdown was 9.974%, close to but below a literal 10% threshold.

Full 21/63/126/252-session outcomes, dates and relative equal-weight performance are in analysis.json. The 2024 one-year drawdown of 18.76% occurred between February 19 and April 8, 2025; it must not be attributed to the August 2024 carry episode. Its first-six-month trough was August 5, 2024. The earlier 42-formation ETF proxy study remains separate: these stories do not enlarge its sample or resolve its regime and overlapping-outcome limitations.

## Does financing vulnerability accompany relative weakness?

The earlier nine-issuer pilot could not establish a comparable pre-window exposure ordering. Royal Caribbean and Norwegian now provide a dated same-industry contrast, based on the same June 30, 2026 balance date and disclosures published before the September 18 formation close.

| June 30 measure, USD billions unless a ratio | Royal Caribbean / RCL | Norwegian / NCLH |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross scheduled principal including finance leases | 23.407 | 15.467745 |
| Cash plus available general revolver | 6.875 | approximately 1.5181 |
| That general liquidity / gross principal | 29.37% | approximately 9.81% |
| Six-month GAAP operating income / gross principal | 10.55% | 3.85% |
| September 18–October 16 adjusted stock return, 20 sessions | +12.97% | +6.59% |

Sources: [RCL June 30 10-Q, filed July 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000088488726000038/rcl-20260630.htm), [NCLH June 30 10-Q, filed August 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513761/000110465926089657/nclh-20260630x10q.htm). Inputs and annual maturity buckets are in cruise-exposures.json. Earmarked ship export-credit commitments are excluded from general liquidity; revolver availability remains conditional. Operating income/principal is not interest coverage, EBITDA leverage or cash flow. Both ratios share a denominator and are not independent confirmations.

Norwegian had the thinner disclosed cushion and lagged Royal Caribbean by **6.38 percentage points** in the subsequent window. **Both stocks rose.** This is evidence of relative performance, not an absolute financing-led selloff. The five/60-session gaps were −1.72/−18.12 points. CCL rose 18.59% over 20 sessions, but its May 31 accounting date prevents treating it as a same-date balance-sheet comparison.

Exploratory pre-window models retain the direction of Norwegian's relative lag: approximately −1.78 points under market-only residual proxies and −2.52 points with size/sector proxies. These are differences between compounded regression-residual proxies, not actual stock returns or identified credit effects. Coefficients were fit to 126 daily returns ending September 18; outcome coefficients were not fitted using the following 20 sessions. Large positive residual levels reflect models expecting negative returns, including pre-window intercepts; they are not evidence of financing relief. All ten models were independently recomputed with QR.

This is not a clean matched causal test. Using pre-window disclosed share counts and September 18 prices gives roughly a tenfold equity-market-cap difference ($65.74B versus $6.48B). Business mix, demand and execution differ. Royal Caribbean had July/August financing changes before formation and announced the approximately $3B Sandals/Beaches stake on September 23, with completion expected in 2027. Norwegian priced $950M of 8.75% 2031 notes on September 30 and completed the financing on October 15, replacing some cheaper 2028 debt, paying down its revolver and extending maturities. It also reported stronger Q3 expectations. These are consequential intervening events; June 30 measurements are not September 18 actual balances.

Norwegian's refinancing illustrates a mixed transmission channel: more funding room and later maturities, at a higher coupon. Revolver repayment of $376.3M plus $315M of added commitments implies $691.3M of additional nominal room before other changes, not additional cash. Sources: [September 30 pricing](https://www.nclhltd.com/news-media/press-releases/detail/828/ncl-corporation-ltd-announces-upsizing-and-pricing-of), [September 30 guidance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513761/000110465926112116/tm2626633d1_8k.htm), [October 15 closing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513761/000110465926116254/tm2627514d1_8k.htm), [RCL September 23 transaction](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/884887/000110465926109755/tm2625963d1_8k.htm).
