The Q3 2026 Quarterly Data Review: Momentum Meets Resistance in CRE






Share This Article














October 26th, 2026 | 3 min. read

  








Subscribe    







  








Trepp Research











 Print/Save as PDF



(Below is a sneak peek of the first story that comes from Trepp and Commercial Real Estate Direct's Q3 2026 edition of The Quarterly Data Review Magazine).

Commercial real estate (CRE) entered the fourth quarter of 2026 with property prices stabilizing but credit conditions under renewed pressure. The Q3 2026 Quarterly Data Review Magazine from Trepp and Commercial Real Estate Direct tracks where momentum is holding and where it is meeting resistance. Here are a few highlights.





The biggest source of resistance is rising rates. With the 10-year Treasury reaching 5.23% on October 9, Trepp found that 75% of maturing class-A commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) office loans would need fresh equity to refinance at current rates, more than the 61% for class-B/C.

That pressure lands heavily on office, which accounts for $22.2 billion of the $61.1 billion of private-label CMBS loans reaching hard maturity in 2027. The strain is already showing in the credit data. CMBS delinquency rose 11.64% in 2026 through September, pushing the delinquency rate to 8.02%, with office the most distressed at 12.33%. Special servicing climbed to $69.53 billion, its largest volume since the post-Global Financial Crisis era.

CRE property prices, by contrast, are holding steadier. The most recent Trepp Property Price Index data shows prices down just 0.12% in the second quarter and up 4.96% from June 2022, with retail the only property type to gain on both measures. Lenders, for their part, are holding the line, with delinquency among life insurers still extremely low.
