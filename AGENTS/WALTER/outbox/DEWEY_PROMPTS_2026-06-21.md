## PROMPT A — South Florida → FL-bank loss-transmission timing  *(lean first-run pick)*

**Consumer:** CORAL (action) + REGINALD (bank cohort) · **Cluster:** BANK_COLLATERAL / CONSUMER_STAGFLATION

**Context to load (so you don't re-derive):** Our prior deep-research (SIG-W-20260619-008) concluded the S-FL "distress tidal-wave" is *overstated*, the consumer side is *genuinely deteriorating*, bank loss-transmission was *not yet in Q1'26 prints*, and the base case is ~70/30 normalization-vs-crash with acute bank stress pushed to ~winter 2026-27. A load-bearing correction it surfaced: FL insurance is *not* uniformly easing — personal lines softened but Citizens *Commercial* condo-master premiums rose (+10.4% capped / +18.8% uncapped). This prompt deepens the ONE thing SIG-008 left open: the timing + leading-indicator set for bank-P&L transmission.

**Decision this informs:** When does documented S-FL household / condo / insurance stress actually show up as credit losses at FL-concentrated banks (rising 30-89 day past-dues → NPAs → reserve builds → NCOs), and is there a leading-indicator set with enough lead time to position *ahead* of the print rather than after it?

**Core question:** What is the realistic timeline and transmission mechanism from S-FL consumer/condo/insurance stress to credit losses at FL-exposed banks, and which observable leading indicators — with their current readings — would confirm, pull-forward, or push-back the ~winter-2026-27 working call?

**Key sub-questions:**
1. **Exposure universe:** Which banks carry the most concentrated S-FL residential / CRE / condo-association exposure? Quantify FL (ideally S-FL) loan concentration from filings/call reports. Candidates to verify, not assume: Amerant (AMTB), BankUnited (BKU), Seacoast (SBCF), plus any others the data surfaces.
2. **Lag structure:** Historically, what is the lag from (a) negative-equity/underwater mortgages, (b) insurance non-renewal / premium shock, (c) condo special-assessment defaults → 30-89 day delinquency → charge-offs in bank financials? Use FL analogs (2007-09; any condo-specific precedent).
3. **Leading indicators + current readings:** Which indicators have a *real* lead time — 30-89 day past-dues by FL bank, FL foreclosure starts, condo-association delinquency, Citizens policy counts/premium trajectory, FL existing-home inventory/days-on-market — and what do they read *right now*?
4. **Backstops:** What structurally delays transmission (extend-and-pretend, reserve cushions, deposit stickiness, the current rate regime) and how much can it absorb before losses surface?

**Counter-evidence mandate:** Steelman BOTH "transmission is imminent / faster than winter 2026-27" AND the ~70% "normalizes without a bank-loss event" base case. Name the single datapoint that would most move you each way.

**Output:** Lead with a 1-paragraph answer — timeline + confidence + the 2-3 highest-signal leading indicators to watch with their current values. Then: transmission-mechanism map; FL-bank exposure table with quantified concentration; leading-indicator dashboard (current readings); the two-sided case; a falsification list. Cite every figure to a primary (FFIEC call reports, FL OIR / Citizens, FL clerk-of-courts foreclosure data, company 10-Qs). ~1,200-1,800 words.

## PROMPT B — FL Nov-3-2026 property-tax amendment  *(clean / forward / low-duplication)*

**Consumer:** CORAL (action) + MARCO (migration/fiscal) · **Cluster:** CONSUMER_STAGFLATION / BANK_COLLATERAL

**Decision this informs:** CORAL flags the Nov-3-2026 FL property-tax constitutional amendment as the single biggest forward variable for the FL thesis, and explicitly two-sided. We need the actual mechanism, passage odds, and the transmission paths so CORAL/MARCO can price it instead of carrying it as a vague "big unknown."

**Core question:** What exactly is on the FL Nov-3-2026 ballot regarding property taxes, what is the realistic probability it passes, and what are the concrete two-sided transmission paths to (a) FL housing demand/prices, (b) FL municipal/county/school-district fiscal health + muni credit, and (c) FL-exposed banks?

**Key sub-questions:**
1. **Exact text/structure** of the amendment(s) — homestead-exemption expansion vs assessment-cap change vs full-repeal proposals; effective date; who benefits/loses.
2. **Passage probability** — FL requires a 60% supermajority for constitutional amendments; pull polling, sponsor, official fiscal note, organized opposition.
3. **Fiscal hit if it passes** — quantified revenue loss to counties/municipalities/school districts (heavily property-tax-dependent) + second-order effects (service cuts, offsetting taxes/fees, special-assessment shifts, muni-credit pressure).
4. **Housing transmission** — does lower carrying cost support demand/prices (bullish), or does the fiscal hole + insurance-cost dominance swamp it (neutral/bearish)?
5. **Bank angle** — muni exposure, property-tax escrow dynamics, second-order CRE/residential effects.

**Counter-evidence mandate:** Steelman both "meaningful demand tailwind for FL housing" and "fiscal time-bomb for FL municipalities that nets negative/neutral for the thesis."

**Output:** Lead with the answer — what's on the ballot, P(pass), net directional read on the FL thesis with confidence. Then mechanism, fiscal-impact scenarios, two-sided transmission, key dates/catalysts. Cite to actual ballot language, FL Legislature / Dept of Revenue fiscal notes, polling, muni-analyst sources. ~1,000-1,500 words.

## PROMPT C — Private-credit → bank / insurer contagion path  *(ambitious cross-cluster; watch overlap)*

**Consumer:** BROCK (PC/BDC) + SHADE (insurer-wrapper) + REGINALD (bank NDFI) · **Cluster:** PC_STRESS / BANK_COLLATERAL

**Decision this informs:** Is the documented private-credit/BDC stress (rising non-accruals, NAV markdowns, div cuts, KBRA DLD record-match) actually transmitting into the regulated system — bank balance sheets via NDFI/subscription/NAV lines, and the PE-owned-insurer / structured-wrapper layer — or is it being absorbed / hidden / financed? This is the cross-cluster contagion question SHADE is testing and BROCK is confirming fund-side; DEWEY's job is the synthesis across the hops, not to re-confirm fund-side stress.

**Core question:** Trace the transmission path from current PC stress to the regulated financial system. Where would it surface first, what are the observable markers, and what's the current evidence on each hop?

**Key sub-questions:**
1. **Bank exposure** — size/structure of bank lending to non-bank financial institutions (NDFI / subscription-credit / NAV lines); most-exposed banks; any drawdowns, covenant stress, pullback. (FFIEC RC-C NDFI line — the ~$1.4T industry figure SHADE/BROCK worked; verify current.)
2. **Insurer/wrapper layer** — how PE-owned insurers + offshore/captive reinsurance + funding-agreement / rated-note wrappers absorb or transfer PC fund stress; regulatory (NAIC) or rating-agency flags.
3. **Hidden-vs-realized** — which mechanisms (continuation funds, NAV financing, PIK, redemption gates) are deferring loss realization, and how durable are they?
4. **Systemic gauges** — what would systemic indicators (spreads, funding) show if this were transmitting, and do they confirm yet?

**Counter-evidence mandate:** Steelman "contained / absorbed in private vehicles, no systemic transmission" vs "SVB-style hidden-leverage build that surfaces abruptly."

**Output:** Lead with the answer — is it transmitting, where, confidence. Then a transmission-path map (PC fund → bank lines / insurer wrappers → systemic), current evidence per hop, two-sided case, falsification markers. Cite to FFIEC / FR Y-9C, KBRA/Fitch/Moody's, NAIC, rating-agency reports, company filings. ~1,500-2,000 words.
