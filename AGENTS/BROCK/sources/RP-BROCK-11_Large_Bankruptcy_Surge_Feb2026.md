## Executive Summary

The U.S. is experiencing the most intense wave of large corporate bankruptcies since the COVID-19 pandemic. Per The Kobeissi Letter, **9 large bankruptcies (>$50M liabilities)** were filed in the week ending approximately February 11, 2026, bringing the 3-week total to **18 filings** and the rolling 3-week average to **6 per week** — the highest since 2020. The peak this century was a 3-week average of 9 filings in 2009 during the Global Financial Crisis.

This report identifies at least 10 confirmed large filings, totaling an estimated **$6-8+ billion in combined liabilities**, with retail and restaurant sectors bearing the heaviest concentration.

## 6. Pattern Analysis

### Common Factors Driving Filings
1. **Overleveraged capital structures**: Fat Brands ($1B+), Saks ($2.7B), Pretium ($1.84B), Eddie Bauer ($1.7B)
2. **Persistently high interest rates**: Floating-rate debt compressed liquidity
3. **Post-pandemic demand normalization**: End of stimulus/demand patterns
4. **Tariff pressures**: Eddie Bauer, Francesca's cited Trump tariff costs
5. **Consumer spending shifts**: Prioritizing essentials over discretionary
6. **Structural disruption**: E-commerce (Francesca's), cord-cutting (QVC), freight weakness (Paladin)

### Fraud Allegations
At least **3-4 filings** involve fraud allegations:
- **Fat Brands**: CEO tax fraud/money laundering investigation
- **Tricolor Holdings**: Double-pledging, fabricating records — CEO/COO indicted
- **First Brands Group**: $7M inquiry into misappropriation
- **Pat McGrath Labs**: Lender alleges loans directed to personal accounts

## 7. Leading Indicator Assessment

### Arguments for Systemic Concern
- 3-week rolling average of 6 large filings = highest since pandemic, approaching GFC
- **January 2026 Chapter 11 commercial filings: +76% YoY** (956 vs. 544)
- Total U.S. bankruptcy filings +11% in 2025; consumer filings +12%
- Subprime auto DQ at record 6.65% (higher than GFC)
- Consumer debt >$18 trillion; CC delinquencies at 12-year highs
- Fraud collapses made warehouse lenders risk-averse

### Arguments Against Imminent Crisis
- Epiq AACER: "returning to pre-pandemic norms"
- Prime borrowers stable at 0.37% DQ
- TransUnion projects overall auto DQ leveling to 1.54% by end 2026
- Many filings are prepackaged restructurings
- No major bank failures yet

### Net Assessment
> **Late-cycle credit deterioration accelerating faster than consensus expects**, particularly in consumer-facing sectors and PE-backed leveraged companies. K-shaped divergence (prime stable, subprime deteriorating) is the critical dynamic.
