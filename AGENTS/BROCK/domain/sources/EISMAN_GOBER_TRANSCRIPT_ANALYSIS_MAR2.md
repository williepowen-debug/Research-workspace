## Eisman's 4 Problems

### 1. PE Selling Own Paper to Captive Insurers
- Apollo→Athene: affiliated paper went **$10B → $40B in 5 years**
- Conflict of interest: PE controls investment decisions for policyholders
- "Not arms-length transactions"
- Shifting from traditional rated bonds to "exotic, highly illiquid investments — joint ventures, limited partnerships with affiliates in the Cayman Islands"

### 2. Hidden Leverage via Offshore Captive Reinsurance
- One large insurer: **$200B total liabilities, $195B seated offshore to affiliates**
- US captives started in Vermont ~2001: grew from **$12B → $440B in 10 years**
- When Gober raised alarm, industry shifted to Bermuda/Barbados/Caymans
- Financial statements are SECRET — even policyholders can't see them
- Gober saw one set of captive financials (accidentally published): **$7B liabilities, $200M real assets**. Rest was "XOL assets" — worthless contingent instruments

### 3. Sellside Analyst Blind Spot
- Analysts covering APO/KKR/BX are asset manager analysts, NOT insurance analysts
- They discuss fee revenue, realizations, spread — never look at statutory filings
- Statutory filings are 7,700 pages — the secrets are in Schedule S Part 3 Section 1
- "There is not one sellside analyst who covers Apollo/KKR who has ever looked at a statutory filing"

### 4. Regulatory Capture
- 5 complicit states: Vermont, South Carolina, Delaware, Arizona, Iowa
- "Permitted practices" = allowing what statutory accounting guidelines prohibit
- State regulators: underfunded, understaffed, unsophisticated
- Regulators vs most sophisticated financial people on Earth = mismatch

## Critical Data Points

### Athene Deposit-Type Contracts: $37.9B (RUN RISK)
- Previously $12-15B, now $37.9B
- Institutional money (Vanguard, pension plans) parked short-term
- Invested long-term in illiquid private credit
- **Duration mismatch = the kill shot**
- If Vanguard calls for $3B back → Rowan can't say no without triggering domino chain
- Not enough short-term investments to match $37.9B in short-term debt

### PHL/Phoenix Life Failure (Precedent)
- Part of Nassau group, ~$2.5B
- Failed "terribly" just a year ago
- Rehabilitator court filing: "The hole was much bigger than anticipated because the commissioner had allowed an XOL asset as an asset and upon further review turned out to have no value"
- **Same pattern as large companies, just smaller**

### Egan Jones Ratings (Criminal Investigation)
- Small rating agency giving investment-grade ratings to non-investment-grade assets
- Mischaracterized investments (called US bonds when actually SPVs in Ireland)
- 18-20 analysts gave thousands of ratings
- Criminal investigation ongoing

### GAP vs Statutory Accounting Arbitrage
- Offshore affiliates use GAAP (defer acquisition costs over 25 years)
- US entities use statutory (cash-based, solvency-focused)
- When $10B liabilities transferred offshore, full $10B subtracted from US books
- But offshore entity books it differently — "significantly underfunding future death claims"
