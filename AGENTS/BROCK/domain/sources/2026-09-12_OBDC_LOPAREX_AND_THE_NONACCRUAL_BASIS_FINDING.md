## 🔑 §2. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE NAME — NON-ACCRUAL BASIS

**OBDC's MD&A prose (line-level, its own words):**

> *"As of June 30, 2026, **seven of our portfolio companies are on non-accrual, which represents 0.8% of our portfolio at fair value.**"*

**The SAME filing's performing/non-accrual table:**

| OBDC | Amortized cost | % | Fair value | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-accrual, 2026-06-30** | **$428,716** | **2.8%** | $126,408 | **0.8%** |
| Non-accrual, 2025-12-31 | $375,873 | **2.3%** | $187,697 | **1.1%** |

### Three things follow, and they are the session's most important output

1. 🔴 **THE TWO BASES MOVED IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS.** Non-accruals **ROSE at amortized cost (2.3% → 2.8%)** and **FELL at fair value (1.1% → 0.8%)** over the same two quarters. A reader following the fair-value series concludes credit quality **improved**; a reader following the cost series sees it **deteriorate**. `[[finding_normalization_choice_picks_opposite_winners]]` — **the disagreement IS the finding**, and here it is on a filed schedule rather than in a methodology note.
2. 🔴 **THE MECHANISM IS THE MARKDOWN ITSELF.** Fair-value non-accrual % falls *because* distressed names get written down — they shrink their own numerator. **Loparex is the proof: $135.3M of amortized cost contributing only $8.3M of fair value.** A name can be ~94% written off and then contribute **almost nothing** to the ratio that gets quoted. **The worse the credit, the less it counts.**
3. 🔴 **THE HEADLINE SENTENCE QUOTES THE LOWER NUMBER.** OBDC's prose says **0.8%**; its own table says **2.8%**. Both are true and correctly labelled — nothing here is a misstatement. **But 0.8% is the figure that travels**, and it is 3.5× below the cost-basis measure.

**This is my core thesis — "reported default/non-accrual rates understate the shadow rate" — observed as a MEASUREMENT-BASIS artifact in a single 10-Q, with both numbers printed in the same document.** It is a stronger and more checkable form of the claim than the PIK-masking argument, because it requires no estimate: the issuer discloses both bases.
