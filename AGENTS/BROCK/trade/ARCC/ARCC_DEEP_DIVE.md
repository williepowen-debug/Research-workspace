## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 10-K confirms our earlier finding: ARCC's software concentration is 23.8% — exactly as we flagged. But digging deeper reveals additional concerns: PIK income is growing as a share of total income, Grade 1+2 investments are increasing, NII is declining year-over-year despite a larger portfolio, and the dividend appears vulnerable if rates decline. ARCC is the world's largest BDC ($29.5B portfolio) and the poster child for the sector — if ARCC has problems, every BDC has problems.

### THE MANAGEMENT SPIN
Management has historically described software as ~12% of the portfolio in public commentary. The 23.8% in the 10-K includes what they categorize differently (IT services, data analytics, etc.) but which the NAIC industry classification groups under "Software and Services."

If Zito is right that software loans recover "20-40 cents" in the wrong scenarios, a 20% haircut on $7.0B = **$1.4B impairment = ~10% of NAV.**

| PIK Category | 2025 ($M) | 2024 ($M) | 2023 ($M) | Trend |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| PIK interest (non-controlled) | $184 | $174 | $143 | ↑ +29% over 2yr |

| PIK dividends (non-controlled) | $266 | $253 | $195 | ↑ +36% |
| PIK dividends (controlled) | $2 | $4 | $2 | Flat |
| **TOTAL PIK** | **$487M** | **$463M** | **$363M** | **↑ +34% over 2yr** |

| PIK Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|-----------|------|------|------|
| Total investment income | $3,052M | $2,990M | $2,614M |
| **PIK as % of total income** | **16.0%** | **15.5%** | **13.9%** |
| PIK as % of NII | 34.4% | 32.2% | 28.9% |

### RED FLAGS:
- **PIK = $487M in 2025** — income that has been ACCRUED but NOT RECEIVED IN CASH. This is phantom income. The money exists only as an accounting entry — the loan principal grows instead of ARCC receiving cash.
- **PIK as % of total income: 13.9% → 15.5% → 16.0%.** Steadily rising. The portfolio is generating an increasing share of income that isn't real cash.
- **PIK as % of NII: 34.4%.** More than a third of net investment income is non-cash. This directly affects dividend sustainability — ARCC must distribute 90%+ of taxable income, but if 34% of that income isn't cash, the dividend requires either borrowing, asset sales, or equity issuance to fund.
- **The advisor earns fees on PIK income.** Ares Management collects income-based fees on accrued PIK even though ARCC hasn't received the cash. If the borrower defaults, the PIK is lost but the fee has already been paid. This is a structural conflict of interest that the 10-K explicitly acknowledges.

## 4. NII TREND — DECLINING DESPITE GROWTH

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| Total investment income | $3,052M | $2,990M | $2,614M |
| Total expenses | $1,594M | $1,514M | $1,328M |
| **Net investment income** | **$1,415M** | **$1,436M** | **$1,258M** |
| NII per share (basic) | **$2.03** est | $2.30 est | $2.27 est |
| Net income per share | $1.86 | $2.44 | $2.75 |
| Cash dividend per share | $1.92 | $1.92 | $1.92 |

### RED FLAGS:
- **NII DECLINED in 2025** — $1,415M vs $1,436M in 2024 — despite the portfolio growing 10.3%. More assets, less income.
- **Net income per share: $2.75 → $2.44 → $1.86.** Declining each year. The trajectory is clearly downward.
- **Net realized losses:** $20M in 2025, $88M in 2024, $171M in 2023. Realized losses are shrinking, which is good — BUT net unrealized went from +$435M (2023) → +$188M (2024) → **-$96M (2025)**. The portfolio is now showing unrealized depreciation.
- **$0.48/quarter dividend = $1.92/year.** NII/share appears to barely cover this. As rates decline (Fed cuts), NII will fall further because 72% of the portfolio is floating rate. The dividend is at risk.

## 6. LEVERAGE & LIQUIDITY

| Metric | Dec 2025 | Detail |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Outstanding borrowings | ~$16.0B | Across credit facilities, CLOs, unsecured notes |
| Asset coverage | 189% | Min 150% required. Buffer = ~$5.7B before breach. |
| Debt-to-equity | ~1.12x | Near the upper range for BDCs |
| Interest expense | $793M | Up from $715M (+11%) — cost of leverage rising |
| IHAM AUM | $14.6B | Sub-advised vehicles add hidden leverage to system |
| SDLP commitment | $6.2B aggregate ($1.4B from ARCC) | Off-balance sheet joint venture with Varagon |

### RED FLAGS:
- **Asset coverage at 189%** = $5.7B buffer. A 20% haircut on software alone ($1.4B) would bring coverage to ~179%. A broader 10% portfolio decline (~$3B) would bring it to ~170%. A 20% portfolio decline brings it below 150% → regulatory intervention, dividend suspension, forced deleveraging.
- **IHAM is a BDC within a BDC.** ARCC's wholly owned subsidiary manages $14.6B in assets across 23 vehicles, with its own investments of $3.2B at cost. IHAM's consolidated investments = $11.8B. This creates layered leverage — risk compounds through the structure.
- **SDLP off-balance sheet:** The Senior Direct Lending Program is a $6.2B joint venture that doesn't fully consolidate. 39 borrowers, first lien senior secured. This is additional exposure not obvious from the balance sheet.

## 7. RATE SENSITIVITY — THE FED CUT TRAP

From the 10-K:
> "As of December 31, 2025, **72% of the investments at fair value** in our portfolio bore interest and dividends at variable rates... 97% of the remaining variable rate investments at fair value contained interest rate floors."

| Rate Change | Estimated Annual Interest Income Impact |
|-------------|----------------------------------------|
| -300 bps | Significantly negative (floors help but don't fully protect) |
| -200 bps | Materially negative |
| -100 bps | Moderately negative |
| +100 bps | Positive |
| +200 bps | Positive |

### RED FLAGS:
- **72% floating rate = massive rate sensitivity.** If the Fed cuts (which Hamilton framework says they'll be forced to), ARCC's income drops immediately.
- **Interest rate floors provide partial protection** — but only up to the floor level. If rates drop 200+ bps below current SOFR, floors stop helping.
- **Borrower distress paradox:** The same rate cuts that reduce ARCC's income are usually triggered by economic weakness — which ALSO increases defaults. Double hit: less income AND more losses.

## 9. THE TRADE CASE

### Bear Case (Our Thesis)
1. **Software haircut** — 20% on $7B = $1.4B = ~10% NAV decline → stock to ~$18 (assuming market prices at NAV)
2. **Rate cuts** — Fed forced to cut → 72% floating rate income drops → dividend at risk
3. **PIK exposure** — 34% of NII is phantom. If defaults rise, PIK converts to realized losses
4. **Grade 1+2 growing** — Credit deterioration accelerating (+45% YoY)
5. **Sector contagion** — Zito broke omertà, redemption wave systemic, bank pullback from PC lending

### Bull Case (What Bears Must Overcome)
1. **First lien senior secured** — 90%+ of new investments are first lien. Recovery rates historically 60-70%+ for first lien.
2. **Diversification** — 603 companies across many industries. No single name dominates.
3. **IHAM fee income** — Provides non-interest revenue stream ($52M management fees)
4. **Floors** — 97% of variable rate investments have interest rate floors
5. **$1.0B buyback authorization** — Price support at discounts to NAV

### Our Assessment
The bull case depends on "first lien = safe" and "diversified = protected." But:
- First lien on a software company worth 20-40¢ is still a big loss
- Diversification doesn't help when the entire 23.8% software sector is correlated
- Floors help for 100-150bps of cuts, then stop
- Buyback authorization ≠ buyback execution (they've never bought back at scale)

**The key question for the trade: Is the options market pricing any of this?** We need to check the ARCC options chain. If puts are cheap (low IV), the risk/reward could be excellent.

## BOTTOM LINE

ARCC is the largest BDC in the world, and its 10-K reveals a portfolio that's deteriorating at the margins while generating an increasing share of phantom (PIK) income. The 23.8% software concentration is the known risk — but the NII decline, the shift to negative unrealized gains, and the Grade 1 growth (+73%) suggest broader deterioration beyond just software.

The dividend is the anchor for ARCC's stock price. If NII continues declining (rate cuts + defaults), the dividend gets cut, and the stock reprices from a premium-to-NAV to a discount-to-NAV. That's a 15-25% move.
