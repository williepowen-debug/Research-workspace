## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The statutory filings confirm and DRAMATICALLY extend the Gober thesis. The Bermuda Triangle is larger, the surplus is thinner relative to liabilities, and the structural complexity has increased. Key finding: **AARe (Bermuda) total statutory capital and surplus = $25.7B** — but after ACRA adjustments it's **$23.7B** allocated across $273.9B in Iowa liabilities alone. The ratio is better than Gober's 2023 number (1.44%) BUT the funded liabilities, deposit-type contracts, and affiliated structures have grown massively.

### RED FLAGS:
- **DEPOSIT-TYPE CONTRACTS: $64.3B, UP $27.9B (+76.6% YoY).** This is the statutory equivalent of the GAAP "funding agreements." On a statutory basis, it's even larger than the ~$85B GAAP number because of different classification. The growth is **staggering** — nearly doubled in one year.
- **Funds held under coinsurance: $65.5B** — This is the ModCo mechanism. Athene holds assets on behalf of affiliated reinsurers (AARe) but credits the reinsurer. The assets stay on Athene's books but the economics belong to Bermuda. Up $15.2B (+30%).
- **AVR grew $1.6B (+42%)** — The Asset Valuation Reserve is a statutory buffer for investment losses. Growing AVR means the portfolio's risk profile is deteriorating (more below-IG assets, more volatile investments, more potential losses to buffer against).

### RED FLAGS:
- **ALRe surplus DECLINED 22%** — from $17.6B to $13.7B. While Athene Iowa's surplus grew slightly, the Bermuda reinsurer's surplus fell by $3.9B. **Capital is moving FROM Bermuda BACK to the US entities** (or being consumed by losses).
- **ALRe total assets shrank 14%** — this is unusual. The reinsurer is getting SMALLER while the ceding entity (Iowa) is getting larger. This suggests restructuring of the offshore arrangements — possibly in response to regulatory pressure.
- **Investments in affiliates dropped $3.2B** — ALRe is liquidating affiliate positions. This could be positive (simplification) or negative (forced sales to meet capital requirements).

## 9. NEW FINDINGS NOT IN GOBER ANALYSIS

1. **Deposit-type contracts grew 76.6% YoY** ($36.4B → $64.3B). This is FASTER than the GAAP funding agreement growth we found in the 10-K (+56%). The statutory classification is broader.

2. **ALRe (Bermuda) is SHRINKING** — assets down 14%, surplus down 22%. Capital appears to be migrating back to the US or being consumed. This is the opposite of what you'd expect if the Bermuda structure were healthy.

3. **AVR grew 42%** ($3.8B → $5.4B). The Asset Valuation Reserve is supposed to buffer against investment losses. Growing it this fast means the risk profile of the portfolio is deteriorating.

4. **Unassigned surplus is negative** ($1.647B). Cumulative operating history has consumed $1.6B of contributed capital. The company has never built enough retained earnings to offset its capital base.

5. **Reinsurance consumed $1.384B of surplus in 2025.** The reinsurance transactions with affiliates are actively DRAINING statutory surplus.

6. **ACRA 1 + ACRA 2 adjustments = $6.574B** removed from AARe's stated surplus. This is third-party (ADIP) capital that inflates the stated numbers. Real Athene-owned surplus is lower than headlines suggest.
