## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The 10-K confirms and **amplifies** every structural concern from the Eisman/Gober thesis. Athene has grown faster than we expected — $386B total investments (up $71.5B YoY, +23%), funding agreement liabilities exploding (+49% YoY), and the affiliated/captive paper web expanding. The numbers are bigger, the growth is faster, and the liquidity mismatch is wider than Eisman described in his Mar 2 presentation.

**Key finding:** The Eisman thesis used $442B AUM. The 10-K shows $386B in *investments alone* (GAAP balance sheet, not AUM). Total AUM is much larger. The numbers we've been citing may have been CONSERVATIVE.

## 4. FUNDING AGREEMENTS — THE DURATION MISMATCH

| Type | Dec 2025 ($B) | Dec 2024 ($B) | Change |
|------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| FHLB funding agreements | **$23.3** | $15.6 | **+$7.7B (+49%)** |
| FABN (medium-term notes) | **$34.6** | $24.1 | **+$10.5B (+44%)** |
| Secured/other (FABR + direct) | **$27.1** | $14.8 | **+$12.3B (+83%)** |
| — of which FABR | $21.0 | $12.0 | +$9.0B (+75%) |
| — of which direct | $6.1 | $2.8 | +$3.3B (+118%) |
| **TOTAL FUNDING AGREEMENTS** | **~$85.0B** | **~$54.5B** | **+~$30.5B (+56%)** |

### RED FLAGS:
- **Funding agreements grew $30.5B in ONE YEAR (+56%).** This is Eisman's "deposit-type contracts" concern. These are institutional hot money — callable, confidence-sensitive, and they run.
- **FABR** (repurchase-backed) grew 75%. These are the most fragile — they're repo agreements wrapped in insurance clothing. If the collateral value drops, counterparties demand margin.
- **FHLB** grew 49% to $23.3B. Athene is using the Federal Home Loan Bank as a piggy bank. This was one of SVB's playbooks.
- **Remaining FABN capacity:** $10.4B authorized but unissued. They plan to keep going.
- **Pension group annuity decline:** The 10-K explicitly says lawsuits are hurting PGA inflows, so Athene is **replacing them with more funding agreements.** They're substituting stickier liabilities with runnier ones.

| Deployable capital available | **$8.6B** | — |
| — Excess equity capital | $3.2B | — |
| — Untapped leverage capacity | $2.6B | — |
| — ACRA undrawn | $2.8B | — |

### RED FLAGS:
- **$8.6B deployable capital against $386B portfolio = 2.2% cushion.** If marks move 2-3%, they're underwater on capital.
- **~11% of liabilities are freely surrenderable without penalty.** On $292B net reserves, that's ~$32B that can walk with no friction. In a confidence crisis, MVA subsidies (rates decline → MVA reduces surrender cost) could unlock MORE.
- **MVA paradox:** 69% of policies have MVAs. If interest rates DECLINE (which they would in a flight to safety), MVAs effectively subsidize surrenders by reducing the penalty. The protection mechanism becomes a run accelerator.

## 6. SPREAD COMPRESSION

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 | Trend |
|--------|------|------|------|-------|
| Net investment earned rate | 5.25% | 5.03% | 4.61% | ↑ |
| Cost of funds | 3.69% | 3.29% | 2.71% | ↑↑ FASTER |
| **Net investment spread** | **1.61%** | **1.78%** | **1.93%** | **↓↓ COMPRESSING** |

### RED FLAGS:
- **Spread compression:** 1.93% → 1.78% → 1.61%. Down 32bps in 2 years. Revenue is growing only because the denominator (assets) is growing faster than spreads are shrinking.
- **Cost of funds outpacing investment returns.** CoF grew +40bps YoY vs earned rate +22bps. At this trajectory, spread goes to zero in ~4 years. But that's the optimistic linear case — a credit event compresses it to zero overnight.
- **"Volume over margin" business model.** This is a classic late-cycle pattern. You can't earn enough on each dollar, so you gather more dollars. Until you can't.

## 7. THE ARI ACQUISITION — IN PLAIN SIGHT

From the 10-K (Note 18):
> "On January 27, 2026, Athene entered into a definitive agreement to acquire an approximately **$9 billion portfolio of commercial mortgage loans from ARI**. The purchase price will be based on **99.7% of the total commitment amounts** of the loans."

### RED FLAGS:
- ARI is Apollo Real Estate Finance — an Apollo-managed REIT. Apollo is selling ARI's CMLs to Athene at **99.7 cents on the dollar.** 
- This is a related-party transaction where the asset manager (Apollo) is telling the insurance company it also owns (Athene) to buy $9B in commercial mortgages from another vehicle it manages (ARI) at essentially par.
- If these CMLs were worth 99.7¢, ARI could sell them on the open market. The fact that Athene is the buyer tells you the market price is lower.
- Expected closing Q2 2026 — subject to ARI shareholder vote. **This is a catalytic event.** If shareholders reject or if the CML marks deteriorate before close, it surfaces valuation problems.

## 8. KEY RISK FACTORS (APO's Own Words)

Selected from Item 1A — these are the risks APO THEMSELVES identify:

1. **"Many of the funds we manage invest in illiquid assets... we may fail to realize profits from these assets for a considerable period of time, or lose some or all of the principal"**

2. **"If a material liquidity demand is triggered and we are unable to satisfy the demand... our retirement services business could be forced to sell certain of its assets and there can be no assurance that it would be able to sell them for the values at which such assets are recorded and it might be forced to sell them at significantly lower prices"**

3. **"This potential mismatch between the liquidity of assets and liabilities could have a material and adverse effect"** — They literally use the word "mismatch."

4. **"Rapid and unforeseen technological transformations, such as the emergence of large language models and generative AI... may introduce operating risks to certain portfolio companies and other investments, particularly those in the software industry"** — They know software marks are at risk from AI disruption.

5. **"We value the illiquid investments held by the funds we manage based on our estimate of their fair value... Because these valuations are inherently uncertain, they may fluctuate greatly from period to period"**

6. **Repo risk:** If Athene is required to return cash collateral or post margin on short notice, "it may be forced to sell securities... in a volatile or illiquid market for less than it otherwise would have been able to realize."
