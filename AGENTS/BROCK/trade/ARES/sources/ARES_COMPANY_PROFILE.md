## 4. Key Fund Vehicles

### 4a. Ares Capital Corporation (ARCC) — Flagship BDC

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Ticker** | NASDAQ: ARCC |
| **Type** | Publicly traded BDC (Business Development Company) |
| **Total Assets** | ~$27B+ |
| **Investment Portfolio** | ~$25B+ (middle market direct lending) |
| **Market Cap** | $12.9B |
| **Dividend** | $1.92/yr (10.7% yield) |
| **CEO** | Kort Schnabel (transitioned from Kipp DeVeer, who served as CEO 2014–2024) |
| **Managed by** | Ares Capital Management LLC (sub of ARES) |

**Fee Structure (ARCC → ARES):**
- **Base management fee:** 1.5% of total assets (including leverage) — this is on GROSS assets, not net, which amplifies fees
- **Incentive fee (income):** 20% of pre-incentive net investment income exceeding a 7% hurdle rate (with catch-up), subject to a total return lookback
- **Incentive fee (capital gains):** 20% of cumulative realized capital gains net of losses
- **Revenue to ARES from ARCC:** Estimated ~$450–500M annually in combined management + incentive fees

> ⚠️ **Vulnerability:** ARCC's fee-on-gross-assets model means ARES earns fees on the leveraged book. If credit losses force ARCC to delever, ARES management fees compress on both sides (lower assets + lower leverage). ARCC's 1.2x debt-to-equity leverage ratio amplifies this sensitivity.

In a stress scenario, redemption queues could form — similar to what hit Blackstone's BREIT in 2022–23. This would pressure NAV marks and force asset sales.

### 4c. CLO Platform

Ares is one of the largest CLO managers globally (~$50B+ in CLO AUM). CLOs are a significant source of management fees and represent embedded leverage in the credit system.

## 5. Aspida — Insurance Subsidiary

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Name** | Aspida (formerly F&G Annuities & Life via Fidelity & Guaranty) — *Note: Ares' insurance platform is distinct; "Aspida" was formed/acquired ~2022* |
| **Type** | Insurance holding company; life & annuity |
| **Domicile** | Iowa (primary operating entity) |
| **Est. General Account Assets** | ~$35–40B (est. 2025) |
| **Strategy** | Fixed annuities, fixed indexed annuities, funding agreements, pension risk transfer |
| **Asset Allocation** | General account invested primarily in Ares-managed credit strategies |

**The Apollo/Athene Playbook:**
Aspida is Ares' answer to Apollo's Athene. The model:
1. Sell insurance products (annuities) → collect premiums → build general account
2. Invest general account in higher-yielding Ares-managed private credit
3. Earn spread between insurance liability cost (~3–4%) and investment return (~6–8%)
4. ARES collects management fees on Aspida's invested assets AND earns the insurance spread

**Key Concern for SHORT Thesis:**
- **Asset-liability mismatch:** Insurance liabilities are long-duration, but private credit assets are illiquid and hard to value. In a stress scenario, mark-to-market losses on private credit holdings could impair Aspida's statutory surplus.
- **NAIC scrutiny:** State insurance regulators (NAIC) have been increasingly focused on PE-owned insurance companies. New capital requirements for structured credit/CLO holdings could force Aspida to restructure its portfolio or raise capital.
- **Surrender risk:** If policyholders surrender annuities at elevated rates (driven by higher competing rates or loss of confidence), Aspida may need to liquidate illiquid assets at losses.
- **Opacity:** Unlike ARCC (public, quarterly reporting), Aspida's financials are buried in state insurance filings (Iowa Insurance Division). Limited public transparency.

> ⛔ **Aspida details are partially estimated.** Ares does not break out Aspida-specific AUM clearly in public filings. The $35–40B estimate is based on Ares' reported insurance strategy AUM and industry comparisons. Recommend pulling Iowa NAIC statutory filings for precise figures.

*Comparison: Apollo's Athene has ~$300B in assets — roughly 8–10x Aspida's size. Aspida is earlier-stage but growing rapidly.*

## 7. Fee Model — How ARES Makes Money

### Revenue Streams

| Stream | Mechanism | Est. % of Revenue |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Management fees** | % of AUM across all vehicles (1.0–1.5% depending on vehicle) | ~55–60% |
| **Performance / Incentive fees** | 20% carry on PE/credit opp funds; 20% incentive on ARCC income | ~15–20% |
| **Fee-related earnings (FRE)** | Recurring, high-margin management fee income (key alt-manager metric) | Core metric |
| **Principal investment income** | Returns on ARES' own balance sheet co-investments | ~10% |
| **Insurance spread** | Aspida general account investment spread over liability cost | Growing, ~5–10% |

### Fee Sensitivity to Credit Stress

1. **ARCC fees compress** if NAV declines (fees on gross assets → lower assets = lower fees)
2. **Non-traded fund fees at risk** if redemptions trigger gates and AUM shrinks
3. **CLO fees stable** short-term (locked capital) but new issuance dries up in stress
4. **Incentive/performance fees disappear first** — these are the most cyclical
5. **Aspida fees persist** (insurance liabilities are sticky) BUT surplus impairment could limit growth
