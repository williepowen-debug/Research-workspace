# SIGNAL: OBDCII $600M Asset Sale — Managed Shrinkage
**Date:** 2026-02-17 (8-K filed) | **Source:** KBRA commentary on Blue Owl 8-K | **Priority:** 🔴🔴

---

## Facts

- OBDCII sold **$600M of loans at 99.7% of par** (including unfunded commitments)
- Proceeds ~$538M after fees → repay secured debt + **distribute ~30% of total net assets** pro-rata
- Part of **broader $1.4B Blue Owl asset sale** across OBDC, OBDCII, and Blue Owl Technology Income Corp
- Buyers: **public pension plans and insurance companies**
- KBRA ratings: OBDCII BBB+/Stable, OBDC BBB+/Stable, Blue Owl Tech Income BBB/Stable

## Analysis

1. **Adverse selection risk** — selling best loans at par, leaving weaker assets in the portfolio. Remaining NAV quality deteriorates even if headline NAV holds.
2. **30% NAV distribution = managed shrinkage** — this isn't a portfolio rotation, it's a partial wind-down. They're returning capital because they can't deploy it profitably or are facing redemption pressure.
3. **$1.4B platform-wide** — not one fund, three vehicles liquidating simultaneously. Blue Owl as a platform is de-risking.
4. **Pensions and insurers buying** — the bag is being passed to institutional capital. Same buyers KBRA conference described entering through "simple structures."
5. **99.7% of par** — suspiciously clean. Either they really did cherry-pick the best assets, or the marks on the remaining portfolio are generous.

OBDCII Q1 report tomorrow (Mar 27). Key questions:
- What's the NAV AFTER the $600M sale + 30% distribution? Smaller fund = more concentrated risk.
- What's the quality of the REMAINING portfolio? Adverse selection.
- Are there MORE sales planned? If $1.4B was tranche 1...
- Redemption requests beyond the pro-rata distribution?

**This changes the T-04 framework:** Don't just look at NAV level — look at portfolio composition shift pre/post sale. A steady NAV on a shrinking, adversely-selected book is bearish, not neutral.
