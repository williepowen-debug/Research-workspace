## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Global Atlantic is structurally similar to Athene but **smaller in scale and less aggressively leveraged on the funding agreement side**. The same playbook is present — PE parent routes originated paper into a captive insurance subsidiary, uses FHLB/FABN funding, cedes liabilities to Bermuda affiliates, and earns a spread. But the magnitudes are different. Funding agreements are $12.2B vs Athene's ~$85B. Total investments are $200B vs Athene's $270B+. The Bermuda reinsurance structure exists but appears less extreme. **Tier 2 trade target — same risk DNA as APO but less advanced in the vulnerability chain.**

## 2. FUNDING AGREEMENTS / DEPOSIT-TYPE CONTRACTS

| Metric | Global Atlantic | Athene |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| **FABN Outstanding** | **$8B** | ~$30B+ |
| **Total Funding Agreements (all types)** | **$12.245B** (FY2025) | **~$85B** |
| **YoY Growth** | **+71%** ($7.158B → $12.245B) | **+56%** |
| **FHLB Collateral Pledged** | **$7.1B** (up from $4.6B, +54%) | Much higher |
| **Issuances in FY2024** | $2.37B new | Much higher |

### 🔴 RED FLAG: Funding Agreement Growth Rate (+71% YoY)
While the absolute size ($12.2B) is far smaller than Athene's ($85B), the **growth rate is actually faster** — 71% vs 56%. This is the same trajectory Athene was on 3-4 years ago. Global Atlantic is earlier in the curve but accelerating.

The FHLB collateral pledged grew 54% ($4.6B → $7.1B), confirming increasing reliance on institutional hot money.

## 3. AFFILIATED / RELATED PARTY INVESTMENTS

| Metric | Global Atlantic | Athene |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| KKR-managed capital in GA | **$219B AUM** (all of it) | All Apollo-managed |
| Third-party co-invest vehicles | **$58B** of GA AUM | ~$50B+ |
| Due from Affiliates (consolidated) | $2.3B | Higher |
| Due to Affiliates (consolidated) | $442M | Higher |

**Key finding:** KKR manages 100% of Global Atlantic's investment portfolio through investment management agreements that were approved by state and Bermuda regulators. KKR's capital markets business, credit business, and real estate business all originate assets that flow into Global Atlantic.

### 🟡 YELLOW FLAG: Affiliated Investment Channel
The 10-K explicitly states: *"Capital invested is the aggregate amount of capital invested by (i) KKR's investment funds and Global Atlantic insurance companies."* Global Atlantic is a deployment vehicle for KKR-originated paper — same structure as Apollo/Athene.

However, the 10-K does NOT break out how much of GA's $200B portfolio is specifically "affiliated" paper the way Apollo discloses Athene's ~$35B. The related-party note shows relatively modest balances ($2.3B due from affiliates). **This number likely understates the true affiliated channel** — the entire portfolio is managed by KKR under investment management agreements, meaning KKR decides what GA buys.

$58B of GA's AUM comes from third-party co-investment vehicles (the "Ivy" vehicles) that participate alongside GA in reinsurance and other activities — this is the same sidecar capital structure Apollo uses.

## 4. OFFSHORE REINSURANCE / BERMUDA STRUCTURE

| Metric | Global Atlantic | Athene |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| **Bermuda Subsidiaries** | Global Atlantic Re Limited, Global Atlantic Assurance Limited | Athene Life Re |
| **Bermuda Statutory Surplus** | **$5.1B** (up from $4.2B) | ~$2.88B |
| **Reinsurance Recoverable (ceded)** | **$26.2B** (gross), $48B total on balance sheet | **$155B+** |
| **Captive Reinsurers** | Vermont & Iowa SPFCIs | Multiple offshore captives |
| **Financing Arrangements (captive support)** | **$2.6B capacity** ($31.6M fees paid) | Higher |

### Structure:
- **Bermuda subs** (Global Atlantic Re Limited, Global Atlantic Assurance Limited) are regulated by the BMA
- **Co-investment vehicles** sponsored by GA also operate from Bermuda
- **Vermont & Iowa captives** handle redundant reserve financing (Reg XXX/AG38)
- Reinsurance between U.S. and Bermuda subs requires state regulatory approval

### 🟡 YELLOW FLAG: Bermuda Triangle Present but Smaller
The same offshore reinsurance architecture exists — Bermuda subs, captive reinsurers, third-party financed reserve support. But the scale is significantly smaller:
- GA's reinsurance recoverable ($26.2B from policy liability table, $48B on balance sheet) vs Athene's $155B+ ceded
- GA's Bermuda surplus ($5.1B) is actually **larger** than Athene's ($2.88B), suggesting less strain
- Captive financing capacity is only $2.6B

The 10-K risk factors extensively discuss regulatory scrutiny of offshore reinsurance between affiliates and captive reserve financing — they know this is a pressure point.

## 5. SPREAD DYNAMICS

| Metric | Global Atlantic (FY2025) | Global Atlantic (FY2024) | Change |
|--------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------|
| Net Investment Income | **$7.224B** | $6.329B | +$895M (+14%) |
| Net Cost of Insurance | **($5.229B)** | ($4.449B) | +$780M (+18%) |
| Insurance Operating Earnings | **$1.109B** | $1.015B | +$95M (+9%) |
| Implied Spread (NII - NCoI / avg investments) | ~1.05% | ~1.10% | Compressing |

### 🔴 RED FLAG: Cost of Insurance Growing Faster Than Investment Income
Net cost of insurance grew 18% vs net investment income growing 14%. The 10-K explicitly states the cost increase was driven by: *(i) higher crediting rates and (ii) the routine run-off of older business originated in a lower interest rate environment.* This is the **exact same dynamic** as Athene's spread compression (1.93% → 1.61%).

Insurance operating earnings grew only 9% despite 14% investment income growth — the spread is compressing.

**Average crediting rates by product (FY2025):**
| Product | Avg Interest Rate |
|---------|-------------------|
| Fixed Rate Annuities | 4.43% |
| Fixed Indexed Annuities | 2.96% |
| Interest Sensitive Life | 3.29% |
| Funding Agreements | 4.18% |
| Other | 3.34% |
| **Blended** | **3.82%** |

With NII of $7.224B on ~$196B avg investments → earned rate ~3.69%. Blended cost of 3.82% on liabilities means the spread is razor-thin on a per-dollar basis when you include all costs.

## 7. RISK FACTORS (FROM 10-K)

The 10-K explicitly acknowledges these risks:

1. **Duration mismatch:** *"If Global Atlantic fails to match asset and liability cash flows... matching more difficult."*
2. **Affiliated investments scrutiny:** *"Regulators have undertaken a review... concerns stemming from increasing usage of offshore reinsurance transactions and expanding allocations to affiliated assets and alternative assets."*
3. **FHLB dependency:** *"Access to FHLB loans is an important source of liquidity for our insurance business. If those sources of borrowing are restricted..."*
4. **Captive reinsurance risk:** *"Further changes in statutory accounting rules will likely make it difficult for Global Atlantic to establish new captive financing arrangements."*
5. **Spread compression:** *"Higher pre-existing crediting rates for longer than expected... Global Atlantic's ability to lower crediting rates is subject to several constraints."*
6. **Capital regime uncertainty:** NAIC RBC reform ongoing, BMA BSCR updates, potential for increased capital requirements
7. **Bermuda regulatory risk:** *"We cannot provide any assurances that insurance supervisors in the United States or elsewhere will not review Global Atlantic's activities and assert that our Bermuda insurance subsidiaries are subject to a U.S. jurisdiction's requirements."*

### 🟢 POSITIVE: Better Capitalized Than Athene
Global Atlantic's surplus/liability ratio (~7.9%) is meaningfully better than Athene's razor-thin 1.44%. This is the **single biggest structural difference** between the two.

### 🟡 YELLOW FLAG: Commonwealth Dividend Restriction
Commonwealth Annuity (the main US sub) has **negative unassigned surplus** and cannot pay dividends without Massachusetts regulator approval. This means Global Atlantic's US operations cannot upstream cash to KKR without regulatory permission — a capital trap.

## BOTTOM LINE ASSESSMENT

**Is Global Atlantic "another Athene"?**

**Partially yes, partially no.**

**YES — Same DNA:**
- PE parent routes originated paper into captive insurance sub ✅
- FHLB/FABN funding agreements ✅
- Bermuda reinsurance to affiliates ✅
- Spread-based business model with compressing margins ✅
- Third-party co-investment sidecars ✅
- Investment management agreements creating affiliated channel ✅

**NO — Key Structural Differences:**
- Funding agreements are **7x smaller** ($12B vs $85B) — less hot money risk
- Surplus ratio is **5.5x better** (~7.9% vs 1.44%) — more capital cushion
- Bermuda cessions are **less extreme** ($26-48B vs $155B+)
- Captives are primarily **onshore** (Vermont/Iowa) vs offshore
- GA is **earlier in its growth curve** — acquired 2021, 100% owned only since Jan 2024

**CLASSIFICATION: Tier 2 Trade Target**

Global Atlantic is Athene circa 2019-2020 — the same structure is being built but hasn't reached the same extremes. The funding agreement growth rate (+71%) is the canary in the coal mine. If GA continues on this trajectory, it will reach Athene-like vulnerability in 2-3 years.

**Key monitoring triggers that would upgrade to Tier 1:**
1. Funding agreements exceed $25B (doubling from here)
2. Surplus ratio drops below 5%
3. Bermuda cessions accelerate materially
4. NAIC/state regulators take action on affiliated investments
5. Spread compression continues (operating earnings growth stalls)

**For now, KKR as a trade is primarily an asset management story, not a pure insurance risk story like APO. The insurance risk is present but less acute.**
