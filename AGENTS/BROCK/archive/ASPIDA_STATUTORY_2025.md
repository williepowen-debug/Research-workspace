## Red Flags

1. **Consecutive losses bleeding surplus.** -$105M cumulative on $635M base. At this burn rate, surplus erodes ~$50M/yr — 8% annual capital destruction.

2. **Group Code = Null.** ARES's $622.5B AUM operation owns this entity but it's not reported as part of a holding company group. Why? This limits consolidated regulatory oversight.

3. **"Capital-light" claim vs reality.** ARES management calls this model capital-light, but the entity is running 22:1 leverage, writing $4.4B/yr in annuities, and losing money. That's not capital-light — it's capital-thin.

4. **$14.7B assets — what's in them?** The critical unknown. If Aspida's assets include ARES-originated credit (ARCC paper, IHAM CLOs, affiliated funds), that's the circular risk / self-dealing flag from the RRC NAIC memo.

5. **Growth trajectory.** Insurance AUM went from $75.3B to $139.1B (+85% YoY) per ARES 10-K. But the statutory entity shows $14.7B in assets. **Where's the other $124B?** Likely in offshore reinsurance entities (Bermuda) or other affiliated vehicles not captured here.

### The $124B Question — ANSWERED
- ARES reports $139.1B insurance AUM
- Aspida Life (US) has $14.7B in assets
- **~$124B sits in Aspida Life Re Ltd. (Bermuda)** — offshore, BMA-regulated
- Same structure as Athene/ALRe: US entity writes annuities → cedes risk to Bermuda reinsurer → Bermuda entity holds the assets under lighter regulation
- The investment portfolio composition (CLOs, affiliated paper, complex assets) is in the BERMUDA entity, not the US filing

### $3B Capital Raise vs $635M Surplus
- Jan 2025: raised $3B+ (equity + credit facilities)
- But US statutory surplus = only $635M, and LOSING $50M/yr
- Where did the $3B go? → Aspida Holdings and/or Aspida Re (Bermuda)
- US entity is thinly capitalized while offshore entity is the capital center
- This is the "capital-light" claim in action — light in the US, heavy offshore
