## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Unlike Apollo (APO), which faces a high-profile securities class action (Feldman v. Apollo, 1:26-cv-01692), **Ares Management does NOT currently face any confirmed securities class action lawsuit.** However, Ares faces significant and escalating legal/regulatory risk across multiple vectors — most critically the **ASIF redemption gating** (Mar 24, 2026), **prior SEC enforcement history**, **aircraft auction fraud litigation**, and **systemic NAIC regulatory tightening** on PE-owned insurers (Aspida). The absence of a class action is actually a key finding: the conditions that produced the APO class action (fund gating + NAV stress + investor grievance) are now materializing at ARES, but no plaintiff firm has filed yet. This creates a **"litigation gap"** — a potential catalyst that hasn't been priced in.

## 1. ACTIVE LITIGATION

### 1A. Aircraft Auction "Rigged" Lawsuits (Ares + Wilmington Trust)
- **Status:** ACTIVE — progressing toward trial as of Nov 2023
- **Source:** Ishka Global (Nov 9, 2023)
- **Allegations:** Two landmark lawsuits filed by junior bondholders on separate aircraft leasing transactions allege Ares, Vmo Aircraft Leasing Holdings (Vmo), and Wilmington Trust conducted "sham" aircraft auctions during the pandemic
- **Claims:** Rigged auction process to benefit Ares-controlled entities at the expense of junior bondholders
- **Significance:** Direct fiduciary/fraud allegations against Ares entities. If these go to trial and produce unfavorable outcomes, establishes pattern of self-dealing
- **Court/Case No.:** Not confirmed from available sources — likely SDNY or Delaware
- **⚠️ RESEARCH GAP:** Need PACER search for current status (trial date, settlement talks, dispositive motions)

### 1B. SEC Compliance Enforcement — SETTLED (2020)
- **Case:** SEC Administrative Proceeding (May 26, 2020)
- **SEC Press Release:** 2020-123
- **Outcome:** $1M settlement
- **Allegations:** Ares Management LLC failed to implement and enforce policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent the misuse of material nonpublic information (MNPI). Specifically, Ares bought stock in a portfolio company where an Ares employee held a board position — a conflict of interest flagged as non-compliant with Ares' own PE compliance policies
- **Mitigating factors:** Ares cooperated fully; significantly ramped up compliance since
- **Significance for short thesis:** Establishes precedent that SEC has previously found compliance failures at Ares. Pattern evidence if new issues arise

### 1C. Violation Tracker Record

- **Total penalties since 2000:** $29,277,105
- **Number of records:** 89 (across Ares-owned portfolio companies)
- **Note:** This includes subsidiary/portfolio company violations, not all directly attributable to Ares management decisions

## 2. REGULATORY RISK

### 2A. ASIF Redemption Gating — CRITICAL (Mar 24, 2026)
- **Event:** Ares Strategic Income Fund (ASIF), a $22.7B non-traded BDC, received redemption requests of **11.6% of outstanding shares** in Q1 2026
- **Action:** Ares capped redemptions at **5% of shares ($524.5M)**, pro-rating requests
- **Source:** Reuters (Mar 24, 2026); SEC regulatory filing
- **Context:**
  - Follows identical gating by Apollo (APODS, 11.2%, capped at 5%, Mar 23)
  - BlackRock HPS also capped at 5%
  - Blackstone bought back >5% (different approach)
  - ASIF launched 2022, focuses on illiquid private credit
  - ASIF claims: zero loans on non-accrual, ~$5B undrawn capacity, $708M gross Q1 inflows
  - Fund letter says most redemptions from "a limited number of family offices and smaller institutions in select geographies" (<1% of 20,000+ shareholders)
- **ARES stock:** Down **36% YTD** as of Mar 24, 2026
- **Litigation risk:** **HIGH.** The APO gating (APODS, Mar 23) has not yet produced a class action but conditions are identical. If a plaintiff firm files against ARES over ASIF gating, the playbook mirrors what produced Feldman v. Apollo on the Epstein side — investor grievance + material disclosure questions
- **Key question:** Did ARES adequately disclose liquidity risk and gating potential to ASIF investors in offering documents? If not → securities fraud basis

### 2B. NAIC / State Insurance Regulation — Aspida
- **Entity:** Aspida Life Insurance Company (Ares' insurance subsidiary, analogous to Apollo's Athene)
- **Domicile:** Iowa (pending confirmation)
- **NAIC PE-Owned Insurer Count:** 139 PE-owned U.S. insurers as of June 2025 (Aspida is one)

**Active NAIC Regulatory Workstreams Affecting Aspida:**
| Workstream | Status | Impact |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| **FABN/FABS Disclosure Proposal** | Exposed for comment (Jan 26, 2026 deadline) | Would require Aspida to disclose funding agreement programs → transparency on interconnectedness with capital markets |
| **RBC Factors for Collateral Loans (2025-16-L)** | Conceptual proposal exposed (comments due Jan 13, 2026) | Would revise risk-based capital treatment for collateral loans based on collateral risk characteristics — could increase capital requirements for Aspida's private credit-backed assets |
| **CLO Financial Modeling** | Effective 2026 | SSG modeling of CLOs could reclassify risk on structured credit held by Aspida |
| **Combination Reinsurance Risk Transfer** | Adopted Fall 2025 | Interdependent reinsurance features must be analyzed in aggregate for risk transfer — tightens captive/affiliated reinsurance structures PE insurers use |
| **AG 49-A Revisions** | Adopted Dec 2025 | Tightens illustration requirements for index-based interest products — directly impacts Aspida's annuity sales practices |
| **PRT Reinvestment Guardrail** | Under discussion (Feb 2026) | Pension risk transfer reinvestment guardrails could constrain how Aspida invests PRT assets |

- **⚠️ No specific enforcement action against Aspida found** — but the regulatory environment is tightening meaningfully across all PE-owned insurers
- **AG 55 (Reinsurance):** NAIC continues to develop reinsurance-related actuarial guidelines. The combination reinsurance risk transfer guidance adopted in Fall 2025 is directly relevant to PE insurers' offshore reinsurance structures

### 2C. SEC — Current Enforcement Posture
- **No confirmed current SEC investigation of Ares** as of Mar 26, 2026
- **No Wells notice disclosed** in Ares' most recent 10-K (FY2024, filed Feb 2025)
- **Context:** SEC under Atkins has revised enforcement manual (Feb 2026) — extended Wells notice timelines, more pre-charge dialogue. This may slightly delay but not prevent enforcement actions if issues arise from ASIF gating practices

## THESIS IMPLICATIONS FOR SHORT POSITION (ARES $95P Jun 18)

**Bull case for the put (stock goes down):**
- ASIF gating is Mar 24 — fresh wound, not yet fully priced
- -36% YTD with no class action = room for further downside if litigation materializes
- NAIC regulatory tightening on Aspida is slow-burn but real
- Q2 redemption data could be worse (Evercore expects elevated)
- If plaintiff firm files on ASIF → immediate catalyst (see APO pattern)

**Bear case for the put (stock stabilizes):**
- No Epstein-type scandal = no emotional/headline catalyst for securities fraud
- ASIF claims zero non-accrual, $5B capacity
- Gating is industry-wide (APO, BX, BlackRock all did it) → harder to single out ARES
- SEC under Atkins is enforcement-light

**Net assessment:** The litigation gap (conditions present, no case filed) is the most interesting aspect. Monitor plaintiff firm announcements weekly. The ASIF gating is a necessary but not sufficient condition for a class action — the trigger would be either (a) NAV markdowns revealing overstated asset values, or (b) discovery that offering docs were misleading about liquidity. Without one of those, gating alone may not produce a viable securities claim.
