## What the Gate Reveals

### 1. Liquidity Mismatch Exposed

Private credit's fundamental problem: illiquid assets, liquid liabilities. OBDC II held middle-market loans with no secondary market while offering quarterly redemptions to retail investors. When redemption requests exceeded available liquidity, the gate was the only option.

**Comparison to 2008:** This mirrors the breaking of money market funds (Reserve Primary Fund) and mortgage REIT gates. Once one fund gates, contagion spreads as investors race to redeem from similar structures.

### 2. NAV Marks Were Fiction

Investors in OBDC II are trapped at a ~20% discount to stated NAV. The forced sale of 35% of loans will reveal actual market prices, likely below marks. This price discovery will cascade to:
- Other Blue Owl funds (OBDC, OTCH)
- Competitors holding similar assets
- Bank warehouse lines secured by these assets

## Structural vs Credit Analysis

### Cliffwater Perspective (Bull Case)

Cliffwater, a private credit consultant, argues OBDC II's problems stem from "obsolete fund structure (BDC 2.0 model)" rather than portfolio deterioration:
- BDC 2.0 model offered features (quarterly redemptions) that don't match illiquid assets
- This is a structural failure, not systemic credit stress
- Other funds with different structures are fine

### Bear Case (Our View)

Even if structural, the implications are severe:
1. **Gates = Gates:** Investors are trapped regardless of cause
2. **Price Discovery:** Forced sales reveal true marks
3. **Contagion:** Retail investors don't distinguish structural vs credit
4. **Big Bank Exposure:** $100B+ committed doesn't care about root cause
5. **Transmission:** Regional banks with fund finance don't distinguish either

**Key Insight:** The *cause* matters less than the *transmission*. Whether Blue Owl failed due to structure or credit, the gates are real, the forced sales are real, and the bank exposure is real.
