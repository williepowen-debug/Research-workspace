## 🔷 Lessons from September 3, 2026 (BCRED Q3 graded · the "0 of 9" denominator audit)

31. **My threshold habit is not "too easy to fire" — it is "authored against the panel I already have, without asking whether that panel can grade it." LESSONS #23 was the same defect with the sign flipped, and I did not see it until an outside reviewer forced the denominator.** #23 recorded n=2 redemption-channel triggers that fired too easily. The WQ-158 re-spec I wrote to *fix* that produced the opposite failure on the same channel: *"≥3 consecutive sub-100% satisfaction quarters"* and *"<20% single-quarter satisfaction"*, reported on my register as **"0 of 9 vehicles."** PROME's reviewer flagged the levels as in-sample design statistics. The denominator audit found worse: **the honest panel is 6 measured vehicle-quarters across 4 vehicles, not 9** — two vehicles cannot produce the metric at all (one is a public BDC with no redemption mechanism; one is restricted multi-year with no cap/request structure) and three more have a value only if you *assume* fill-to-cap. 🔑 **Only BCRED had ever been observed three consecutive quarters, and it reached three on the day of the audit.** A 3-consecutive rule was being graded against a panel that had never observed three consecutive quarters anywhere — **zero fires was guaranteed at registration by the panel's shape, not by the world's behaviour.** And the *"<20%"* level sat **3pp below the observed minimum** (OCIC 23%) of the same six cells it graded. **Why both directions have one root cause:** in each case I set the level by looking at the numbers already on my register and placing the bar just past them — too-easy when the register was full of fires, un-reachable when it was full of near-misses. **The register is a description of what I have collected, never a sample of the world.** ⚠️ **And the reporting harm is worse than the dead rule:** *"0 of 9"* reads to every downstream consumer as *the world is not doing this*, when it means *this panel cannot see it* — an impossibility and a calibrated negative are indistinguishable from the output alone. **Three checks, cheapest first: (a) before registering any N-period persistence rule, print the per-unit consecutive-observation histogram — if `max(depth) < N` the rule is inert, say so on the register instead of reporting zero fires; (b) separate MEASURED cells from DERIVABLE-BY-ASSUMPTION cells before counting either; (c) confirm the measure is the same measure across units — mine silently mixed dollars-accepted÷dollars-requested, a ratio of two rounded share-percentages on a prior-quarter-end base, and two press figures under one threshold.** ⛔ **The corollary I got right and should keep: when a panel cannot grade a level, do NOT re-place the level off that panel — find an out-of-sample referent (here BREIT/SREIT 2022-23, same sponsor and cap architecture) or declare the rule inert.** Re-placing off the panel that could not grade it just re-derives the design statistic. Promoted fleet-wide as `[[finding_persistence_threshold_needs_panel_depth_not_just_a_metric]]`.

32. **A prediction can resolve TRUE and be worth nothing, and the place to say so is ON the resolution — before anyone downstream banks it.** BRK-30 resolved TRUE on 9/3 (BCRED Q3 satisfaction ~50% < 100%). I had already documented in #23 that it was priced **65% against the thesis** while its **letter** fires on <100% at any *one* of five funds — **already true 5-for-5 at its 6/26 Made_Date.** So the TRUE carries **~zero calibration information**, and a reader six months out sees only `RESOLVED-TRUE, 65%` unless the row says otherwise. **I wrote the calibration-zero flag into the Result and Notes cells, into STATUS, into the OTTO packet and into the PROME delivery — and PROME recorded BRK-30 with the flag attached.** ⚠️ **The near-miss: my instinct at the moment of grading was satisfaction that the row had come good.** That instinct is the whole failure mode — **a fired letter plus a no-call companion (BRK-32) is the diagnosis, not a vindication.** **Rule: when a prediction resolves TRUE, ask what would have made it resolve FALSE. If the honest answer is "not much," the resolution is a letter-grade — stamp it as one at resolution time, because nobody downstream will ever re-derive its weakness.**

33. **Three times in one day I published a settled decision as an open one — and every instance rotted in the same direction, which makes the audit cheap and my failure to run it worse.** (a) My boot line carried *"BANK_BDC_MATRIX flagged — owner to confirm freeze-vs-refresh"* for **two months** after it was FROZEN 2026-07-04. (b) My STATUS calendar carried *"BRK-02 name set PROPOSED, awaiting PROME"* for **three weeks** after Will ruled it on 8/13 — and I then **re-propagated it into a Will-facing inventory and into a direct statement to Will that a dated item 27 days out was blocked on someone else.** (c) A third, caught only because PROME re-read the ruling record rather than my summary of it. 🔑 **The diagnostic that makes this cheap: these labels NEVER rot toward "done." They rot toward "awaiting," "proposed," "pending," "owner to confirm" — the vocabulary of unfinished work.** A finished thing mislabelled open generates a task; an open thing mislabelled finished generates silence, and I have not produced one of those. ⇒ **Audit only the open-vocabulary labels, and audit them against the RULING RECORD, not against another of my own surfaces.** ⚠️ **The mechanism in (b) is the sharpest and it is not staleness — it is PROPAGATION FROM THE WRONG COPY.** My own SCRATCH item D (written 9/2) said correctly *"the blind spec is ratified as resolver of record and the six-name set is ruled."* When I rewrote the forward-state on 9/3 I copied the **STATUS gloss**, not my own correct note and not the ruling file. **I had the right answer in the same directory and reached past it for the summary** (`[[finding_summary_section_merges_what_the_body_separates]]`). **Rule: when restating the state of a RULED item, cite the ruling file and quote its owner line — never restate from your own dashboard row, which is the copy most likely to predate the ruling.** ⛔ **And the cost here was not internal:** a wrong "whose move" on a live dated row is how an item sits untouched while both desks believe the other holds it. *(Caught by PROME 2026-09-03, verified by me at `PROME/proposals/2026-08-13_private-credit-batch-RULED.md` §①/§⑤ before accepting — both stamped "Owner: BROCK.")*
