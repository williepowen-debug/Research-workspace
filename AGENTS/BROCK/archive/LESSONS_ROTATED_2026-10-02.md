## 🔶 Lessons from July 27, 2026 Session (dates · BRK-30 grade · spec repair)

23. **My redemption-channel triggers skew too-easy-to-fire — n=2 in a single day, same direction.** Two independent triggers on the PC-redemption channel turned out to be mis-specified the same way within hours of each other. (a) The `PC_REDEMPTION_REGISTER` escalation line ("≥2 vehicles gated simultaneously ⇒ STRUCTURAL LIQUIDITY-MISMATCH CONFIRM") **fires 7-fold on day one**, because 7 vehicles had been simultaneously gated since June — and it double-counts the shared antecedent the 6/26 verdict ruled must be counted ONCE. (b) **BRK-30** was priced at 65% against the *thesis* ("does the compounding queue persist?") but its *letter* fires on **<100% satisfaction at any ONE of five funds** — already true 5-for-5 at registration, and it would fire even in a substantially clearing market. The companion built to test the actual thesis (**BRK-32**) priced at **45%**: a 20-point spread off the same evidence. **Both errors run in the same direction — too easy to fire — and both are on a channel where I already believed the thesis.** That is a habit at the authoring step, not two unlucky thresholds. **Fix: on any redemption/gate-channel trigger, run the inversion test BEFORE registering — describe a world where the thesis is FALSE and check whether the trigger still fires. If it does, either tighten it or state plainly that it is a weak proxy and price the confidence accordingly.** Also: when the gap is found in an *open* prediction, do **not** re-base it (the replacement is often already true at the original Made_Date — verified here: "demand rises QoQ" was already true 3-for-3 at BRK-30's 6/26 Made_Date). Let it resolve on its letter and register a separate companion. A fired letter + a no-call companion is **not a contradiction, it is the diagnosis.** Promoted fleet-wide as `finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter`.

24. **Check whether an entity is actually non-traded before routing to EDGAR filer-history — and check it against the submissions API, not memory.** I recorded that OTF and BCRED "are both non-traded ⇒ dates land via EDGAR filer-history, not IR pages," then spent the session's opening on filer-history archaeology. **OTF is NYSE-listed** (ticker OTF, `exchanges=['NYSE']`, **Large accelerated filer** — a 40-day 10-Q deadline, not 45) and had **pre-announced its Q2 date by 8-K on 7/1**, three weeks before I went looking. The premise held only for BCRED. Cost was small here because the filer-history projection (8/5-8/7) happened to contain the true date (8/5), but the same error on a name whose cadence had shifted would have produced a confidently wrong date. **Fix: one call to `data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json` returns `exchanges`, `tickers`, and `category` — read those three fields before choosing a date-resolution method. Listed ⇒ look for the scheduling 8-K/IR release first; non-traded ⇒ filer-history derivation, and label the output `[EST]` with a statutory-deadline backstop.** Related: the derived date must also carry its *filing-class* precedent — OTF's 10-Q lands on the **results-release** date, not the earnings-**call** date, which is only knowable by checking that the prior quarter's 10-Q and results 8-K were filed the same day.

## 🔴 Lessons from July 28, 2026 Session (a false A1 fact, ~4 months old, in my own KB)

25. **A pro-rata FILL RATE is not a RECOVERY PRICE — and the misread survives because both are "45%" and both sound bearish.** `KB-BRK-103` recorded Apollo ADS as *"RETURNING 45 CENTS ON THE DOLLAR… forced liquidation pricing… this is what 'marks are wrong' looks like in practice,"* rated **A1 / EMPIRICAL / CONFIRMED**, and it stood for ~4 months. The primary says: *"each redeeming investor will be returned approximately **45% of their requested capital**"* — i.e. **5% offer ÷ 11.2% demand = 44.6% of the AMOUNT REQUESTED, paid AT NAV.** No haircut exists. `KB-BRK-061`, same event and same date, had it right, so the KB contained the correct and the false reading side by side. **Why it evaded review:** the number was *plausible* and it *confirmed the thesis* — a 45¢ private-credit print is exactly what I was looking for, so it was never re-read. **Three defenses, in order of cheapness:** (a) when a percentage could be either a **share of a quantity** or a **price**, write the denominator into the fact itself — "45% of requested capital," never "45%"; (b) **read one paragraph further** — the same letter reported NAV/share −1.2% and total return +1.0%, which contradicts a 55-cent haircut outright, so the refutation was *inside the source I was already citing*; (c) any datum that would satisfy a **registered trigger** deserves a primary re-read before it is stored, not before it is used — the near-miss here is that **BRK-25 (my canonical Stage-3 arms-length sub-90¢ trigger) would have resolved TRUE on a number that does not exist.** *(Found 7/28 working NEXUS's queue; corrected KB-BRK-203/204, source file bannered + archived, contamination traced and contained to one unreferenced research brief.)*

26. **When two of your own sources conflict, de-rate — but do not tiebreak on recency.** Two WALTER signals disagreed on the Silver Point facility ("$300M" vs "UNSIZED/UNRANKED"); I de-rated to `[PRESS, UNVERIFIED, CONFLICTED]` rather than carry either. **That process was right and the primary vindicated the OLDER signal** — $300.0M senior secured, exactly. The newer signal was describing the *reporting*, not the facility. **A later account is not a correction unless it says it is correcting something.** The general form: conflict is a signal to go to primary, never a signal to prefer the freshest telling. *(Related: LESSONS #20's "don't derive from the summary.")*

27. **A status field maintained by a remembered ritual will rot — and it rots in the direction that looks like unfinished work.** My catalyst calendar advertised the 7/21 PROME OZK packet as *"UNREAD; carried to next session as task #1"* for **7 days after I had actually consumed and graded it** (its contents are in the 7/27 PASS-2 block). Cost: it presented my oldest *open* inbox debt when there was none, and NEXUS spent an item on it. **Check the TARGET (is the file in `processed/`? is the content in a session block?), not the tracking row** — `[[finding_record_of_an_action_is_not_the_action]]` applies to my own bookkeeping, not just to other agents' claims.

## 🔴 Lessons from August 28, 2026 (PROME-orchestrated catch-up, two touches + QC)

28. **A correctly-caveated body does not protect a headline that claims past it — and my "limits stated" discipline cannot catch this, because the limits ARE stated.** Twice in one session, RAV QC found me over-claiming in a bold clause while the paragraph beneath it was accurate. (a) BCRED's distribution cut: I wrote *"sized exactly to the coverage gap"* and *"cut to the coverage line and not a basis point further."* The arithmetic I had published two sentences earlier — $2.16 post-cut against a $2.14 NII run-rate — is **99.1% covered, ~$0.02/share short.** Cut *toward* coverage, not *to* it. (b) First Brands: I headed the re-size *"ESTIMATE → MEASUREMENT"* while the body correctly said the $237M par was unverified with an unstated perimeter and that two holders are not the sector. **Two measured marks on an unverified par is a REVISED ESTIMATE; it inherits the par's defects however good the marks are.** ⚠️ **This is NOT my registered bias.** My known failure is too-easy-to-fire-bearish (RED, n=4), and I spent the same session guarding it — I even *withdrew* an over-concession. This is a different mechanism: **the caveat and the claim live in the same artifact, the caveat is correct, and the reader who reads only the bold clause gets the stronger claim.** `[[finding_summary_section_merges_what_the_body_separates]]`, in my own files, twice, and neither instance tripped any check I run. **Test to add: after writing a bold headline clause, read it ALONE, with the paragraph covered, and ask what a reader would carry away. If that differs from the paragraph, the headline is the defect — not the paragraph.**

29. **A DIP mark is not a recovery estimate, and treating it as one manufactures a conclusion about the estate.** I wrote that First Brands' super-priority New Money DIP marked at 22.80¢ was *"a filer's own valuation saying the estate cannot cover its most senior debt."* **A DIP mark embeds claim disputes, collateral, and timing alongside expected recovery** — it is a price, not an estate solvency verdict. Corrected to *"market-implied severe impairment reaching even the DIP."* The observation was the sharpest datum in the sweep and survives intact; only the inference narrowed. **General form: when a security's price is unusually informative, that is exactly when it is most tempting to read a single clean proposition out of it — and prices are never single propositions.**

30. **A dated catalyst row can point at empty space, and it will sit there looking merely un-fired.** My calendar carried *"BCRED Q2 final repurchase satisfaction, ~8/13-8/17 window"* for weeks. **Verified at the filer history: BCRED filed NO SC TO document between 2026-06-04 and 2026-08-04.** The Q2 final was disclosed in the **6/4 SC TO-I/A**, roughly ten weeks before the window I was watching. **The defect was the DATE MODEL: I had modelled the disclosure off QUARTER-END, and BCRED's tender results land ~28-34 days after the OFFER** (2/2→3/2 · 5/1→6/4 · 8/4→~9/2-9/8). ⚠️ **An un-fired row and a mis-pointed row are indistinguishable from the row itself** — both read "waiting." **Before trusting any modelled window, verify the ISSUER'S OWN CADENCE from its filer history rather than from a plausible general rule**, and state which anchor the model uses (offer date vs period end) so a later reader can falsify it. The Q3 row is built on the offer-date cadence for exactly this reason.
