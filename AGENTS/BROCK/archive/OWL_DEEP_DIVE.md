## 6. THE $1.4B ASSET SALE — "99.7 CENTS"

On Feb 18, 2026, Blue Owl sold $1.4B in loans across three funds:
- **$600M from OBDC II**
- **$400M from OTIC (Blue Owl Technology Income)**
- **$400M from OBDC**

Buyers: Four institutional investors (CalPERS, OMERS, BCI, Kuvare — per earlier reports). All buying equal stakes.

OBDC's $400M slice details (from 10-K):
- 74 portfolio companies across 24 industries
- 91.9% first lien, 4.7% second lien, 3.4% unsecured
- 98.3% floating rate
- 100% rated 1 or 2 (best quality) on OBDC's internal 5-point scale
- Average investment size: $4.8M (tiny — 5% slices of positions)
- Weighted average spread: 5.5%
- **Price: 99.7% of par** (equivalent to 99.8% of fair value)

### WHAT THIS TELLS US:
1. **They sold their BEST loans.** 100% rated 1 or 2 = cherry-picked the cleanest credits. This leaves the remaining portfolio with a WORSE average quality.
2. **5% slices** — they sold a little bit of everything, not concentrated positions. This is designed to avoid showing a specific name at a distressed price.
3. **99.7¢ is the "good" portfolio's clearing price.** The question is: what would the OTHER portfolio — the Grade 3/4/5 stuff, the software, the non-accruals — clear at? 90¢? 80¢? 60¢?
4. **Kuvare reportedly rejected 7 of 117 companies.** Those 7 rejections are the signal — what names were too toxic even for a buyer getting cherry-picked collateral at 99.7¢?

## 7. OBDC II REDEMPTION HALT — THE TEMPLATE

**Timeline:**
- **Nov 2025:** Blue Owl abandons plan to merge OBDC II into OBDC after investor revolt
- **Feb 18, 2026:** Permanently halts redemptions. Announces $1.4B asset sale. Plans to return up to 30% of NAV ($2.35/share) via return of capital distribution by March 31.
- **Mar 5, 2026:** Saba Capital + Cox Capital launch hostile tender for 8M shares at $3.80/share (34.9% discount to NAV)
- **Mar 12, 2026:** OBDC II board rejects tender, calls price "inadequate." Kroll provides fairness opinion.
- **Mar 13, 2026:** Board formally recommends shareholders reject.

### WHAT THIS TELLS US:
1. **"Permanently halting redemptions" IS a gate.** Packer's spin — "we're not halting, we're changing the method" — is irrelevant. Investors who want their money back can't get it at NAV. That's a gate.
2. **Saba's $3.80 bid = the market's real price.** OBDC II's NAV is ~$5.84. Saba is offering $3.80 = 65¢ on the dollar of stated NAV. This IS the discount the market applies to private credit portfolios. The 33-35% discount is the real clearing price.
3. **El-Erian comparison to August 2007 is significant.** A mainstream economist publicly drawing the parallel means the narrative has crossed from "sector concern" to "systemic risk conversation." This changes how institutional allocators think.
4. **The template for other vehicles:** If OBDC II permanently halts, other non-traded vehicles under pressure (Cliffwater, MS North Haven, BlackRock HLEND) now have permission to follow. Each halt accelerates the confidence crisis.

OWL/OBDC is 6-12 months ahead of ARCC on the stress curve. The pattern:

```

Step 2: Redemption pressure → gates/halts (✅ OWL done, ARCC N/A - public)
Step 3: Asset sales to fund liquidity (✅ OWL done, ARCC not yet needed)

```

ARCC is at Step 1-2. OBDC is at Step 5-7. The gap is our edge — we can see ARCC's future by watching OBDC's present.
