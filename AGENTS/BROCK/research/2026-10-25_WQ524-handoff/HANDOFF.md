## 1. Recommended: First Brands / LAM / Western Alliance

**Entities and instrument.** Jefferies Financial Group Inc.'s March 9 SEC-filed letter identifies the disputed loan as Western Alliance Bank's loan to **LAM TFG I SPV LLC**. **LAM Trade Finance Group LLC** bought First Brands receivables and sold them to that SPV. Both borrowers are described as owned by the Point Bonita master fund; that letter does not establish the master's full legal name. First Brands Group is the seller group named in the releases; individual legal sellers, receivable obligors and the collateral agent's identity are not established here. Western Alliance Bancorporation is the reporting parent of the lending bank. Leucadia Asset Management LLC is the named manager/defendant. Do not turn manager affiliation into a Jefferies parent guarantee.

**Dated exposure stock.** WAL's March 6 8-K identifies **$126.4M outstanding principal** and announces an equal noncash Q1 impairment. Its March 31 10-Q confirms that the remaining loan balance was charged off. This was funded exposure, not an unfunded commitment or a $126.4M fresh cash payment on March 6. Charge-off removes the recognized loan asset; it does not extinguish legal collection rights. Undrawn commitments and current litigation recovery are UNKNOWN in this handoff.

**Connection, constraint and executed response.** WAL's March 6 release says it learned of servicing failures/UCC filing lapses in September 2025; entered forbearance in October with full prepayment due March 31; received payments through January 15, 2026; and faced a failed February 27 payment followed by notification that further principal payments would stop. WAL concluded on March 2 that the remaining balance should be charged off. The bank and collateral agent filed suit March 6 against Jefferies, LAM and affiliates. The Q1 10-Q confirms the $126.4M charge-off. These are concrete lender responses beyond a borrower mark. The missed contractual cash receipt is the immediate bank constraint; the charge-off itself is noncash recognition of the asset loss, not an additional cash drain.

**Which institution absorbs what?** The recorded loan loss sits at WAL's consolidated bank group. Jefferies' March 9 letter asserts that the loan is non-recourse beyond the SPV receivables, that guarantees requested from Jefferies and the master fund before First Brands' September bankruptcy were refused, and that WAL nevertheless entered forbearance. Those are counterparty primary statements, not an adjudication of WAL's disputed contract/fraud claims. No automatic Jefferies parent liability or quantified Point Bonita investor cash call is established. WAL's claim against other entities is an attempted transfer/recovery of loss through litigation, not a completed recovery.

**Protection and containment.** Receivables/proceeds were the asserted collateral, while servicing/perfection failures undermine that protection. Prior repayments are already reflected in the charged-off remainder: never subtract the January payment or Jefferies' cumulative-repayment claim again. WAL's release proposed $50M securities gains and $50M expense savings, with about $45M gains already realized quarter-to-date as of March 6; the Q1 income table later reports $50.5M securities gains for the whole quarter. Those earnings sources are not recovery on the SPV loan. Sales may have preceded the March 2 impairment decision, so their entire execution cannot be causally attributed to that decision. Planned expense savings are not established as completed. This demonstrates loss absorption, not proven bank funding failure.

**Publication clock and contrary explanation.** March 6 8-K/release disclose the bank response; March 9 letter publicly supplies the precise legal-entity/non-recourse bridge. The May-filed Q1 10-Q confirms the booked loss. The guarantee refusal reportedly happened in September 2025 but is documented here by a March 2026 publication: no pre-loss tradable lead is established. BROCK already held the H1 loss in KB225 on August 28. Operational/fraud/perfection failure with limited recourse is a credible case-specific explanation; this case does not establish widespread private-credit under-marking or continuing contagion. The historical loss is outside BRK-31's Q3/Q4 window, and a charge-off is not its reserve-build operator.

**Decisive missing evidence / falsifier.** The executed loan and forbearance agreements, collateral/perfection duties, actual receipt waterfall, current recovery ledger and adjudicated recourse determine final loss allocation. Evidence that the charged-off facility was a different instrument from the First Brands SPV loan would defeat this specific link. Verified cash recovery or enforceable third-party payment could reduce the remaining economic loss, without erasing the historical Q1 charge-off. A claimed parent-guarantee spillover fails absent an enforceable obligation; affiliation and litigation allegations alone cannot establish one. No further search or new actor assignment is prescribed by this support packet.
