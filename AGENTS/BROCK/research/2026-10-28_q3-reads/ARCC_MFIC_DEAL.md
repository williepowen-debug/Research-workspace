ARCC's $263M equity haircut equals **~9.5% of MFIC's 6/30 portfolio FV ($2,770.3M)**. If the whole haircut were asset value, ARCC would be valuing MFIC's book at about 90¢ per dollar of current marks. So 90¢ is a ceiling-type reading, not a mark.

## 4. What it means for the thesis
1. **First arm's-length control price on a stressed public BDC this cycle.** The strongest top-tier manager paid ~75% of NAV from its own balance sheet for the long tail's weakest book. That fits "bifurcated: disciplined top tier vs fragile long tail", and the top tier is now absorbing the tail rather than the tail failing.
2. **Counterweight, in ARCC's own words:** "the vast majority of the portfolio, we saw relatively eye to eye on the marks"; the discount is "centered" on "a smaller percentage of the portfolio" with uncertain outcomes. That is named-borrower stress, not systematic mis-marking, matching what the HTGC work found (catch-up 10/25). It is the buyer's description, made while selling the deal.
3. **Consolidation is a floor under the discount.** The market had MFIC at 65% of NAV; a strategic buyer paid 75% (85% with the subsidy). Other deeply discounted public BDCs now carry takeout optionality, which narrows discounts without any credit improvement. This is a measurement caveat for the NAV-discount vector, not a credit signal.
4. **Bank funding is open to the top tier:** Wells Fargo upsized $750M for the acquisition. That is counterevidence to a bank-funding squeeze on BDCs, at least for ARCC.
5. **Apollo (APO):** Apollo exits management of its public middle-market BDC, while its affiliates (5.2%) take the premium and the MidCap Financial platform continues (Zito). MFIC's ~$2.8B of assets is small against Apollo's fee base, so this is immaterial to APO earnings [judgement, not computed from APO filings]. The vehicle-mismatch caveat on the APO put stands; any disposition is Will's, and TERRY constructs.
6. **Not a loan price.** BRK-25 (arm's-length sub-90¢ LOAN print) is untouched (trap 2).
