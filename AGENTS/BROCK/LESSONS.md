# BROCK LESSONS — Mistake Patterns & Rules

*Review at spawn. If you catch yourself breaking one, stop.*

## 🔴 Data Verification

1. **PSEC PIK was 8.6%, not 35%.** Agent hallucinated a 4x inflated number. Always verify BDC financials against SEC 10-K/10-Q filings, not summaries.
2. **BDC earnings dates shift.** Check company IR pages, not cached data. OZK was wrong twice in other agents.
3. **NAV is self-reported.** BDCs mark their own books. Market price (discount to NAV) is the market's opinion of real value. Always track both.

## 🟡 Process Rules

1. **Don't conflate "gating" with "restricting."** Blue Owl restricted quarterly redemptions ≠ formal gating. Blackstone honoring 100% ≠ stress-free. Precision in language matters for cross-agent signals.
2. **Track the delta, not just the level.** BCRED going from 5% to 7.9% redemptions matters more than the absolute 7.9%. Rate of change is the signal.
3. **Mark-to-model ≠ mark-to-market.** Private credit NAVs are model-derived. When BDCs trade at 73% of NAV, the market is saying the model is wrong. Don't trust the model over the market.
4. **Insurance/reinsurance is a parallel domain.** Don't let Athene/ILS analysis crowd out core BDC monitoring. Track both but keep STATUS.md focused on credit first, insurance second.

## 🔵 Lessons from Mar 9-12, 2026 Events

5. **Transparency cascades follow disclosure anchors.** DB disclosing €26B PC exposure forces other banks to quantify their own NBFI/PC books. The first mover in a disclosure cascade is always the most important signal — not because of their individual exposure, but because of what it makes others reveal. Watch for JPM, BofA, Citi quantifying their PC books in 2-4 weeks.

6. **When the world's largest bond manager calls something structural, it IS structural.** PIMCO Stracke calling this a "crisis of bad underwriting" with multi-year defaults matters not because PIMCO is always right, but because institutional allocators respond to PIMCO publicly. This is the mechanism: PIMCO speaks → allocators cut PC strategic allocation → redemption pressure is sustained over years, not months. Different from retail gate cascade.

7. **Verify the ownership chain on named companies.** Thoma Bravo owns Medallia (78¢ bellwether). This wasn't in our initial framing — the bellwether WAS ALREADY a Vista/TB signal all along. When working with named PE sponsors, immediately trace portfolio ownership to see if you already have live pricing.

8. **Structural vs cyclical distinction changes trade duration.** A cyclical credit stress resolves in 12-18 months — position with Dec expiry. A structural repricing (returns 10%→6%, institutional reallocation) plays out 2-4 years. Dec 2026 puts may need to be rolled again. Don't let "cyclical frame" collapse a structural position prematurely.

9. **Distressed dry powder is a two-sided signal.** $100B+ vulture capital entering = validates stress is real AND provides a floor. The presence of buyers moderates the cliff-down scenario and favors a grinding, stepwise decline. Adjust expected path: staircase down, not elevator.

10. **Sector broadening is the confirmation, not the trigger.** When consumer products hit >12% default rate (doubled YoY), that confirms software wasn't idiosyncratic. The LABOR→consumer→private credit channel is active. Consumer credit stress is the second epicenter and deserves its own watch vector.

## 🟢 Lessons from May 1, 2026 Catch-up Session

11. **Fee economics vs lending economics — read alt-manager Q1 prints with separation.** OWL Q1 2026 (Apr 30) posted +14% FRE / +11% DE on record AUM $314.9B but direct lending strategy returned **-1.1% for the quarter** with negative net deployment of $0.5B. Stock surged on the FRE beat. Bulls and bears are pricing different things. When reviewing PE/alt earnings, separate management-fee P&L (scales with AUM, agnostic to credit) from balance-sheet/portfolio P&L (the actual credit signal). The fee economics can mask the lending decay for several quarters.

12. **Narrative confirmation ≠ thesis terminus.** Howard Marks memo (Apr 9) + JPM/S&P short product (Apr 10) felt like Stage 3 was unstoppable — peak-bearish narrative. Three weeks later, alt-managers had rallied 16-22% off lows on OWL Q1 fee-economics beat. Stage 2→3 transitions can stall on sponsor backstops (BCRED $400M from BX/execs), fee-economics earnings beats, and HY OAS compression from non-PC drivers. Don't treat "consensus catching up" as the trigger to fully cash thesis — it can be local top, with bear-thesis re-arming on the next 10-Q cycle.

13. **National Dentex was Cerberus, not Thoma Bravo.** BRK-21 prediction notes (written Mar 17) attributed National Dentex to Thoma Bravo. Cerberus actually owns it (acquired Oct 2020). Caught at resolution Apr 30 when researching the maturity outcome. **Concrete validation of Rule #7 (verify ownership chain on named companies)** — wrong sponsor attribution wouldn't have changed the prediction outcome but matters for cross-portfolio concentration analysis (Cerberus has separate concentration profile from Thoma Bravo). Always verify sponsor before writing notes that reference cross-fund overlap.

14. **TSV schema: trailing empty fields require explicit tab terminator.** When appending PREDICTIONS rows where the Notes (last) column was empty, awk read those rows as 8 cols instead of 9 — schema validation flagged them. Fix: ensure every row ends with the correct number of `\t` separators even for empty trailing fields, or insert a placeholder character. **Verify NF count via `awk -F'\t' '{if (NF!=N) print NR": BAD "NF}' file.tsv` after every TSV write or append.** Catching this at write time is much cheaper than discovering it during a downstream analysis.

## 🟣 Lessons from May 21, 2026 Revival Session

15. **Match the vehicle to the open transmission channel — equity puts bleed when tape is regime-suppressed even if the thesis validates substance-side.** The May 1 → May 21 window proved this hard: BROCK-domain equity put book (APO Jun $100P, APO Dec $95P, ARES Jun $95P, OWL Jun $9.5P, HYG Jun $75P) ran **-84% on $3,200 cost basis** while substance fully validated (FSK Q1 Max Bear data, NDFI scope 11× bigger than tracked, Ch11 +42% April, regulator escalation, sponsor-bifurcation diagnostic). Same broad credit-stress thesis expressed via TLT puts (LIQUID-side duration channel) was **+$600 cumulative**. The substance was right; the equity-vehicle channel was closed (HY OAS never broke 260, VIX never spiked, gamma suppression held). The duration channel was open (10Y +42bps over window, TLT down -3.2%). **Before sizing into single-name equity puts on a credit-cycle thesis, check which transmission channels are currently open and express via those — not via the channel your narrative wants to use.** Long-dated strike-near-spot puts (e.g., APO Dec $95P at 210d) are the equity-channel exception: enough time for the regime to break. Near-dated deep OTM puts (Jun $100P at 25% OTM, 28d) are the regime-suppression bleed-trap.

16. **Execution rails matter as much as decisions.** TRADE.md's May 1 plan to roll HYG Jun→Dec was correct framework. It never executed during the 20-day BROCK dark window because there was no mechanism to act on it: Will wasn't watching, BROCK wasn't booted, no agent-authorized execution path existed. By 5/21 the HYG mark had collapsed to $0.05 ($40 residual on $245 cost) and the roll math no longer worked. **A decision in a STATUS or TRADE file with no execution mechanism is half-finished work.** Either: (a) explicitly schedule Will-action with a clear trigger ("on next green day, roll"), (b) flag to Prome for monitoring, or (c) accept that the decision is conditional-on-being-booted-when-the-trigger-hits and is therefore weaker than it reads. Don't write rollover plans into STATUS files as if they'll self-execute.

## 🟤 Lessons from June 4, 2026 Sweep Session

17. **Verify filing status against EDGAR before marking "TBD pending" in STATUS calendars.** The 5/21 revival session listed OTF, GCRED, BCRED, CTAC as "TBD pending" in the Q1 10-Q calendar — **but OTF (filed May 6, NAV $16.49 / -4.85% / $494.3M unrealized loss), BCRED (May 14, non-accruals 4×), OCIC (May 11, Q1 redemptions 21.9% of shares) had all already filed by 5/21.** Tier-1 OTF (highest-priority forced-mark candidate per BROCK NDFI framework, 70% software concentration) sat unread for 29 days in this session. Cost: missing the OTF "reduce software exposure" mgmt guide — the first Tier-1 non-traded BDC mgmt acknowledgment of structural-not-cyclical software impairment, which is a Stage 3 narrative-phase tell. **Fix: every BROCK STATUS update touching the 10-Q calendar must verify filing status via EDGAR FTS (efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?forms=10-Q&dateRange=custom&startdt=X&enddt=Y) or company IR page before writing "TBD pending."** A "pending" label that is actually "filed-and-unread" is worse than no label — it generates a false sense of awaited resolution while the resolution sits in the public record. Validates the root-cause pattern of LESSONS #2 (BDC earnings dates shift — check IR, not cached data) at the broader filing-status level: **don't carry forward a "pending" status across sessions without verification.**

18. **Verify data points carried forward across sessions against primary source — concentration figures rot.** BROCK had been carrying **OTF software concentration at 74.2%**. Q1 mgmt transcript states **~70%**. 4.2 percentage points lower than tracked, suggesting either prior-quarter mark-rolloff or BROCK had the figure wrong from initial intake. **Fix: when a BDC concentration figure is recited in STATUS / KB / cross-agent signals, source-tag the most recent 10-Q quarter — e.g., `OTF software ~70% [CONF OTF Q1 2026 transcript 5/7/26]`. If the underlying 10-Q is more than 1 quarter old, the figure is stale.** Concentration drifts on every mark-cycle; carrying a figure forward without quarterly verification creates a slow-drift hallucination that compounds. Parallel to LESSONS #1 (PSEC PIK 8.6% not 35%) at a less-egregious magnitude.

19. **Cross-agent signal date-stamping must match the actual filing date, not the sub-agent's intermediate framing.** A 5/21→6/3 news sweep returned a "BCRED 8-K May 20 IOU mechanic" — but the actual filing introducing the promissory-note mechanic is **BCRED's June 4 SC TO-I/A**, not a May 20 8-K. The May 20 reference appears to have been a hallucinated date (the URL the agent cited points to the 10-Q period-end date, not an 8-K filing date). **Fix: before integrating any cross-agent or sub-agent signal that names a specific filing date, verify the filing date against EDGAR's filing-index (not just the period-end date in the URL or the period covered by the document). Wrong dates propagate into outbox signals, STATUS dashboards, and prediction resolutions — and corrections are expensive once propagated.** Combines with LESSONS #14 (verify NF on TSV writes): both are "verify at write time, not discovery time" patterns.

## ⚪ Lessons from June 8, 2026 Pre-Open Sweep

20. **Dividend-cut framing is its own signal layer — extract from 10-Q sweep explicitly, don't derive from NA/NAV summary.** The 5/21 revival sweep tagged OBDC's Q1 10-Q as "MIXED — no forced-mark cascade" and MFIC's as "Partial Bear — NA $167M vs $48.5M YoY." Both BDCs **announced public-BDC dividend cuts in the same window** (OBDC $0.37→$0.31 -16.2% on 5/7; MFIC $0.38→$0.31 -18.4% on 5/5), concurrent with OCSL's already-tagged $0.40→$0.34 cut. **3 public-BDC div cuts clustered in 3 days (May 5-7) sat unframed for 18 days** while STATUS narrative continued to call OCSL "first public-BDC div cut." Cost: missed a Stage-3 narrative-phase tell (publicly-traded BDC dividend cluster is a different signal class than non-traded gate cascade) and missed the cross-agent signal opportunity to OTTO/REGINALD when the data was fresh. **Fix: when sweeping BDC 10-Qs, run an explicit "div action" pass alongside NA/NAV — declared dividend vs prior-quarter declared dividend, expressed as % delta. Build into the 10-Q calendar review template.** Parallel to LESSONS #17 (verify "TBD pending" against EDGAR) at the within-filing-content level: even when the filing IS read, key signal classes can be missed if not explicitly extracted. **Validates the pattern: 5/21 revival sessions are particularly vulnerable to surface-skim of multi-signal filings under closeout pressure.**

21. **Distinguish metric classes when transcribing aggregator-search results — issuance vs flows is the LESSONS #19 echo at the metric-definition level.** The 6/8 sweep pulled a search result containing both "issuance fell to $44.76B in 3mo to May, down ~40% vs Q1" AND "private credit flows in Q2-to-date down 70% from Q1 average." These are different metrics: gross issuance vs net flows (issuance minus repayments / fund deployments). BROCK transcribed both as if they described the same series and shipped "PC issuance Q2-to-date -70% vs Q1 avg" to LIQUID in the outbox. Cloud-side Prome verification 6/8 caught it against PitchBook primary (the -40% issuance figure is the correct verified series; the -70% flows figure was not independently verified to a primary source by either side). **Fix: when an aggregator-style search result contains two adjacent figures with different verbs ("issuance fell" vs "flows dropped"), treat them as separate metrics until you either (a) source both to primary or (b) drop the unsourced one. The convenient combined-narrative ("plumbing slowing fast") is what made me carry both; the LESSONS #19 fix ("verify at write time") applies to metric-class precision, not just dates.** Echoes LESSONS #18 — once a precision-loss enters the propagation chain, downstream rewrites compound it. Catch at write-time.

22. **External adversarial-verification layer earned its keep on first BROCK pass.** The 6/8 commit `9c4881b0` was independently verified by orchestration-layer (cloud-side Prome) against primary sources within the same session, before any reader acted on it. Prome caught: (a) the -70% flows/issuance conflation [LESSONS #21], (b) Reuters/cryptobriefing source tag on Fitch's primary print [verification discipline], (c) Partners Group -17% as the high end of a 13-18% range, (d) "+3 evergreens $9.7B / Delaware US PE ~6%" specifics not in primary docs, (e) a convergence-sum arithmetic error (57 vs 59). 4 of 5 catches were genuine errors; 1 (alleged garbled REGINALD outbox line) was not present in committed file and may have been a cached-copy issue on Prome's side. **Operationalizes `finding_domain_agent_steelman_backstop` and `finding_cross_surface_validation_pattern` — independent adversarial review of fresh BROCK STATUS work is high-yield, especially on aggregator-sourced figures and convergence arithmetic. Default to inviting this verification before any signal propagates further than the originating commit, NOT after.**

## 🔶 Lessons from July 27, 2026 Session (dates · BRK-30 grade · spec repair)

23. **My redemption-channel triggers skew too-easy-to-fire — n=2 in a single day, same direction.** Two independent triggers on the PC-redemption channel turned out to be mis-specified the same way within hours of each other. (a) The `PC_REDEMPTION_REGISTER` escalation line ("≥2 vehicles gated simultaneously ⇒ STRUCTURAL LIQUIDITY-MISMATCH CONFIRM") **fires 7-fold on day one**, because 7 vehicles had been simultaneously gated since June — and it double-counts the shared antecedent the 6/26 verdict ruled must be counted ONCE. (b) **BRK-30** was priced at 65% against the *thesis* ("does the compounding queue persist?") but its *letter* fires on **<100% satisfaction at any ONE of five funds** — already true 5-for-5 at registration, and it would fire even in a substantially clearing market. The companion built to test the actual thesis (**BRK-32**) priced at **45%**: a 20-point spread off the same evidence. **Both errors run in the same direction — too easy to fire — and both are on a channel where I already believed the thesis.** That is a habit at the authoring step, not two unlucky thresholds. **Fix: on any redemption/gate-channel trigger, run the inversion test BEFORE registering — describe a world where the thesis is FALSE and check whether the trigger still fires. If it does, either tighten it or state plainly that it is a weak proxy and price the confidence accordingly.** Also: when the gap is found in an *open* prediction, do **not** re-base it (the replacement is often already true at the original Made_Date — verified here: "demand rises QoQ" was already true 3-for-3 at BRK-30's 6/26 Made_Date). Let it resolve on its letter and register a separate companion. A fired letter + a no-call companion is **not a contradiction, it is the diagnosis.** Promoted fleet-wide as `finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter`.

24. **Check whether an entity is actually non-traded before routing to EDGAR filer-history — and check it against the submissions API, not memory.** I recorded that OTF and BCRED "are both non-traded ⇒ dates land via EDGAR filer-history, not IR pages," then spent the session's opening on filer-history archaeology. **OTF is NYSE-listed** (ticker OTF, `exchanges=['NYSE']`, **Large accelerated filer** — a 40-day 10-Q deadline, not 45) and had **pre-announced its Q2 date by 8-K on 7/1**, three weeks before I went looking. The premise held only for BCRED. Cost was small here because the filer-history projection (8/5-8/7) happened to contain the true date (8/5), but the same error on a name whose cadence had shifted would have produced a confidently wrong date. **Fix: one call to `data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json` returns `exchanges`, `tickers`, and `category` — read those three fields before choosing a date-resolution method. Listed ⇒ look for the scheduling 8-K/IR release first; non-traded ⇒ filer-history derivation, and label the output `[EST]` with a statutory-deadline backstop.** Related: the derived date must also carry its *filing-class* precedent — OTF's 10-Q lands on the **results-release** date, not the earnings-**call** date, which is only knowable by checking that the prior quarter's 10-Q and results 8-K were filed the same day.
