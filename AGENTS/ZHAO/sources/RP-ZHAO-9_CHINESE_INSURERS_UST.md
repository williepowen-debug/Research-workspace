## Executive Summary

**Chinese insurers' UST holdings are TINY — likely $3-10B (not hundreds of billions).**

This is NOT a Japan-like situation. Chinese insurers have ~$76B total overseas investments (end-2024), with only 23% in fixed income (~$17.5B). UST is a subset of that subset.

**Key implication for thesis:** Confirms that official-sector and state bank channels (covered in RP-ZHAO-2, RP-ZHAO-3) are the dominant China → UST transmission vectors, not insurers.

## The Math: Bounding UST Exposure

From IAMAC survey (end-2024):
- Total overseas investment: **$76.1B**
- Fixed income share: **23%** = ~$17.5B
- Within FI, 30-70% sovereign/agency (assumed)
- Within sovereign, 50-80% U.S. (assumed)

**Result:** UST exposure bounded at **~$3B to ~$10B** (mid: ~$6B)

This is consistent with:
- Small net FX exposures in disclosed financial statements
- Heavy HK concentration (51% of overseas in HK)
- C-ROSS Phase II capital charges discouraging unhedged FX

## Why China ≠ Japan Trajectory

### 1. Binding Regulatory Constraints
- **15% overseas cap** — hard ceiling that doesn't exist for Japan
- **120% solvency ratio** required for overseas investment eligibility
- **C-ROSS Phase II** — risk-based capital charges on FX/alternatives

### 2. Starting Point Gap
- Chinese insurers: $76B overseas → 1.6% of assets
- Japanese lifers: 25% of assets in foreign securities
- Would need 15x growth to match Japan's allocation

### 3. Operational/Currency Management
- New China Life: Only RMB 10.5B (~$1.5B) in USD financial assets
- CPIC: FX sensitivity implies ~RMB 21B net exposure (managed)
- Hedging derivatives capped at 102% of hedged assets

## Liquidation Triggers

### Quantifiable Triggers:

1. **Solvency Constraint (<120%)**
   - NFRA requires 120% ratio for overseas investment
   - Solvency drop → forced de-risking → UST sales

2. **15% Cap Binding**
   - Currently not tight (overseas ~1.6% of assets)
   - BUT: RMB depreciation → overseas assets revalue UP in RMB → ratio rises mechanically
   - Scenario: USD/CNY >7.50 could push some insurers toward cap

3. **Hedging Cost Pressure**
   - Derivatives capped at 102% of hedged assets
   - Rising USD hedging costs → reduce hedged USD FI rather than expand

### Hard-to-Quantify:

4. **Surrender Wave / Liquidity Demand**
   - Policy surrenders + margin calls = need cash
   - UST is most liquid → first to sell
   - Public surrender thresholds not disclosed

## Regulatory Framework

### Key Constraints:
- **Overseas cap:** 15% of prior year-end total assets
- **Emerging markets cap:** 10% of total assets
- **Credit quality requirements:** Major currencies, minimum ratings
- **Solvency linkage:** <120% ratio → must reduce higher-risk overseas

### Hedging Rules:
- Derivatives notional ≤ 102% of hedged base assets
- Hedging costs ≤ 10% of hedged base assets
- OTC counterparty exposure ≤ 1% of total assets
- **No speculative use**

**Implication:** When hedging capacity exhausted → cash sales (including UST) to reduce FX exposure
