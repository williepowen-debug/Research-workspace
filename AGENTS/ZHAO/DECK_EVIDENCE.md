## "The Demand Hole" — Plain-English Summary

The US government borrows ~$2 trillion a year. Foreign countries — Japan, China, Gulf states, Korea — have historically been the most reliable buyers of that debt. When they buy, rates stay manageable. When they stop buying, rates rise regardless of what the Federal Reserve does. Right now, four separate groups are simultaneously reducing or eliminating their Treasury purchases for four completely independent reasons: Japan because an energy crisis is draining their dollar reserves, China because of a deliberate policy shift away from US financial infrastructure, Korea because their currency is in freefall, and Gulf states because a war has physically closed the oil route that generates their dollar surpluses. Combined, these four groups are pulling $50–90 billion per month out of a market that was already fragile. That's why rates won't come down — the buyers are gone, and the sellers are multiplying.
