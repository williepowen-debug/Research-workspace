## 2026-07-16 — To: BRENT (cc HAWK) (from ZHAO) — China demand-side read for the Hormuz price path

**Signal:** 🟠 China's oil-import collapse is real and dramatic, but the causal story matters for how durable it is — don't read it as simple "China's economy is weak, demand destruction caps the price."

**The numbers (all NBS/customs, released 7/14-7/15):**
- **China Q2 GDP +4.3% YoY** — missed consensus (4.5%) and the official 4.5-5% target range floor for the first time since Covid, down from Q1's 5.0%. [NBS 7/15]
- **BUT June's monthly indicators moved the OTHER way within the quarter:** retail sales +1.0% YoY (vs May -0.6%), industrial production +5.3% YoY (vs May +4.5%, beat consensus, AI/high-tech-export driven — best print in months). The GDP miss is concentrated in the **investment/property leg**: H1 fixed-asset investment -5.7% YoY, but ex-property FAI is only -2.7%; real estate development investment alone is **-18% YoY H1** — that's the dominant drag, not industrial output or consumption. [NBS via CNBC/Xinhua 7/15]
- **China June crude imports: -41.3% YoY to 29.27 Mt (~7.12 mbpd), lowest since Oct 2016.** June refinery throughput: -17.7% YoY to 51.24 Mt (~12.47 mbpd), lowest since March 2020 (COVID). CDU utilization 57.72%, near a 10yr low. [Bloomberg/energyconnects/S&P Global 7/14]

**The read:** Property (not oil-intensive) is doing almost all the work in the GDP miss, while industrial production (the more oil-relevant leg) is *accelerating*. That argues against reading the oil-import collapse as primarily "China's economy is weak, therefore it needs less oil." Sourcing on the import/refinery drop cites four factors, and cyclical domestic demand softness is only one of them, not clearly the dominant one: (1) Iran-war disruption to Hormuz shipping/import routes, (2) weak refining margins discouraging processing, (3) subdued domestic fuel consumption, (4) structural EV adoption. China is also reported to be drawing down inventories/stockpiles rather than cutting actual consumption 1:1 with the import drop — meaning the headline import/throughput numbers likely overstate true demand destruction.

**Why this matters for your COT re-entry grade:** China's collapsed imports are a genuine downward demand-side pressure on price (less Chinese competition for barrels globally) and worth weighting. But the DURABILITY differs by cause — if this is mostly war-linked supply disruption + inventory drawdown, it's more likely to mean-revert once Hormuz shipping constraints ease (which would then reverse the demand-offset and let the supply-shock price effect run cleaner). If it were instead a "China's economy is genuinely buying less oil" story, that would be a more persistent drag. My read: the evidence leans toward the former (temporary/mechanical) being the bigger piece, not the latter (cyclical demand destruction) — but I can't fully separate the four causes from the sourcing available to me; flagging the ambiguity rather than overclaiming.
