How divergences behave

The daily see-saw is noise on top of a trend. In past episodes the KRE ÷ QQQ line moved one way for weeks with only small pullbacks, then mostly flattened rather than snapping back. Most past splits came with rising yields; this one, like July 2024, comes with falling yields.

Timing checklist for any new put

  1. Insurance is cheap: the volatility index is in the bottom quarter of its last six months.
  2. The stock is 2% or more above its 20-day average.
  3. It's a green day for that stock (root rule #6).
  4. A dated event falls before expiry: an earnings report, inflation data, a Fed meeting.
  5. Never buy when fear is high and the stock has already dropped. That's a chase, and it was the worst setup in both legs.

If you rotate between the legs: rules

  1. Rotate only KRE and QQQ; single-bank puts are too wide to trade in and out of.
  2. Buy at least 45 days out. No weeklies.
  3. Recycle, never add: each purchase is paid for by a sale on the other side.
  4. Take profits: sell half a leg when it's up 50% and its sector is having a red day.
  5. Park the cash. Redeploy to the other leg only after KRE ÷ QQQ turns 3% from its extreme, on that leg's green day.
  6. At most one swap a week, and only after a 3% move in the line.
  7. Don't feed a loser: no recycling into a leg already down 50% from cost.
  8. Stop rotating and hold both on the first day both fall 1% or more, or when the 20-day correlation climbs back above +0.3.

Caveats and sources

  • Model, not fills. Strategy and timing tests price options with Black-Scholes from the VIX and VXN, loosely matched to Oct 23 quotes, with no skew. Treat results as direction, not exact returns.
  • Small samples. Five past divergence windows, chosen around known rotations. Overlapping test days mean the true number of separate cases is about one-twentieth of the day counts.
  • One regime. 2024–2026 was mostly a rising market, which makes puts look worse than in a falling one.
  • Drivers are hypotheses. Rotation out of AI and chips, falling yields and mostly benign bank earnings are likely causes, not measured ones. Market-structure calls belong to HENRY, synthesis to NEXUS.
