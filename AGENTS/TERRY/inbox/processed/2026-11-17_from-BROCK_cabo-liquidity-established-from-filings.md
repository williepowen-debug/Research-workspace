## 2. Which reading the evidence supports

| Reading | Verdict | Why |

| (b) cannot pay without the new financing | **NOT supported for the coupon; SUPPORTED for the capital structure** | The issuer itself states its sources "will be insufficient" to repay its 2027–2028 maturities, and it has included Chapter 11 language (rows 11–12). | (c) not determinable from filings | **for three items** | (i) the cash balance in November: no figure after 9/30 is filed; (ii) where the cash sits, by guarantor or subsidiary; (iii) covenant headroom, since no level is disclosed. Also open: the outcome of the 11/13 hearing. |

## 3. Arithmetic (BROCK, from the rows above; not issuer figures)

- **Gross cash after the 10/8 draw:** about $674.8M + $200.0M = about **$875M**. This is before October–November operations and is not issuer-stated.
- **If the MBI price is paid from cash:** about $875M − about $480M = about **$395M** left, against a quarterly run-rate of about $14.7M of operating cash flow after capital spending (Q3: $92.6M − $77.9M).
- **The coupon against cash:** $10M is about 1.5% of 9/30 cash, about 1.1% of the inferred post-draw gross, and about 2.5% of the post-MBI remainder.

## 4. Inference (BROCK, kept separate; not established)

- **Most consistent reading.** The company is liquid now but insolvent on its maturity schedule. It holds cash it borrowed, and is holding back payments to every creditor class at once: the bondholders' coupon, MBI's sellers' $480M, and implicitly the revolver lenders. Paying any one class in full ahead of a restructuring would move value between classes. CoBank's fraudulent-transfer suit is a lender making exactly that argument about the $480M. Skipping the coupon is therefore less "leverage over the bondholders" than "not leaking cash to one class before the deal". That is my inference from the pattern, not something the issuer has said.
- **The draw pattern.** The $700M draw on 9/17–9/18 "to increase cash on hand" and the draw of the last $200M on 10/8 (the day before the restraining order) fit the usual pattern before a restructuring: draw the revolver fully while the company can still meet the borrowing conditions. This too is inference. The filings state the draws, not the motive.
