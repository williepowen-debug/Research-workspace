**Written:** 2026-11-19 · **Priority:** 🟡 · **ACTION:** a tradeability read at your discretion; "no card" is a complete answer. My own read is below: no investment implication with a liquid vehicle that I can name.
**Full document:** `AGENTS/AEOLUS/research/2026-11-19_lake-mead-jan-1-2027-unsigned-agreement.md` (section 8 is this text). Evidence rows: KB-AEO-280, 281, 283, 291, 292.

**What the evidence implies.** Two separate things. (1) **Power, dated, does not depend on the deal:** Reclamation's November 24-Month Study (dated 11/16) prints Hoover generating capacity at ~309 MW for May–Sep 2027 vs ~1,275–1,312 MW in Jun–Aug 2026, with Lake Mead below 1,035 ft from March 2027 (low 1,014.00 ft in July). About 1,000 MW of Desert Southwest summer peaking capacity has to be replaced at market by its holders (LADWP 23.9%, Arizona Power Authority 18.4%, Colorado River Commission of Nevada 18.4%, SCE 13.5%, MWD 12.05%, small Southern California cities), who still pay Hoover's fixed $76.2M base charge. (2) **Water, conditional, no date:** if the Lower Basin deal is unsigned when Interior sets the 2027 split, Interior's own FEIS priority model (App. C Table C-3, verified at the PDF) puts 1,161,018 af on Arizona, 88,982 af on Nevada's SNWA and 0 on California, about 401,000 af more on central Arizona (CAP cities and tribes) than the agreement; Arizona's banked-water make-whole lapses with the agreement on 1/1.
**Horizon.** Water: MWD board vote on the agreement **12/08** is the first catalyst; the split governs CY2027 from 1/1. Power: March–September 2027.
**What would invalidate it.** Water: MWD signs 12/08 and Arizona ratifies (legislature from ~1/11), or Interior publishes a split near 760/440/50, or a court acts. Power: a December–April study path that keeps Mead above 1,035 (wet Upper Basin winter, or a larger Powell release at the April review). Part of the printed summer capacity loss may be maintenance scheduling; I cannot separate it from elevation.
**What is unknown.** The Secretary's split and its date; replacement-power cost and pass-through; any rating agency's no-deal scenario (Arizona water credits are AA/AAA and treated as wait-and-see); Arizona issuers' outstanding debt.
**My read.** No liquid vehicle that I can name. The water exposure sits with public agencies, tribes and highly rated municipal water credits; the power exposure sits mostly with public power, and for the listed holders Hoover is a small slice (NV Energy ~2% of energy). The plausible market channel is a marginal summer-2027 bid for Southwest replacement power and gas, which is WATT's to size. If you see an Arizona-concentrated vehicle, 12/08 is the dated test of the water leg.
