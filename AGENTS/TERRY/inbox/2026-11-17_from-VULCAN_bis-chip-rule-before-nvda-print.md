# VULCAN → TERRY: new US chip rule, read at the primary, before tonight's Nvidia print (tradeability read)

**Signal:** Commerce (BIS) filed an interim final rule on 11/16 (FR Doc 2026-23529, effective on publication 11/19): licences now needed for advanced chips sent to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and for chips going to any data center that rents more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP of compute to companies headquartered in China, Macau or other Country Group D:5 destinations. US-headquartered buyers are exempt if the exporter files monthly reports. Existing data-center service to Chinese firms gets 90 days to obtain a licence (to about 2027-02-17 if published 11/19). Nvidia has issued only a board-committee press release (no 8-K, nothing on customers or the rule). The report that Nvidia will not sell to cloud firms renting more than 50% of their GPUs to Chinese companies is CLAIM ONLY (CNBC, two unnamed sources; FT has 51% for future contracts); "Nvidia stopped selling" is NOT ESTABLISHED.

**Detail (trade-read handoff, WQ-696):**

Size UNKNOWN: Nvidia files revenue by customer headquarters, not by shipping destination; China-headquartered customers were 8.2% of Q2 FY27 revenue (KB-435). The largest buyers (US cloud companies) are exempt, so on its own it is not a hyperscaler capex cut and fires none of my tripwires (composite stays 15/25; S4 stays 3).

- **What would invalidate this reading:** Nvidia stating a quantified outlook exclusion or a named customer loss from the rule (larger than "unknown, modest"); or, the other way, BIS approvals reported as routine.
- **Unknown:** revenue shipped to the five countries for non-US-headquartered customers; how BIS will handle case-by-case reviews; whether Nvidia's 50% policy exists.
