## DRAFT TEXT (inserted verbatim after SL-5, before "## Registration form")

### SL-6 — An instrument premise names its DISPOSITION SOURCE, and every re-verification reads the disposition, not the text (added 2026-10-01; Will 2026-09-26 15:04 ET, WQ-295 R4, with rider)
*Instance:* ZHA-16. KB-ZHAO-131 re-verified its premise on 9/2 by reading EO 14358's TEXT. EO 14389 had terminated that framework on 2/24. The text was accurate and the instrument was dead, and nothing in the letter pointed at the place that records the death.
*The rule (proposal § R4, verbatim):* **A letter whose premise rests on a legal or administrative INSTRUMENT (an executive order, a rule, a notice, a court order, a statute's effective date) names the instrument AND its DISPOSITION SOURCE — the feed that records amendment, stay, termination or vacatur (Federal Register disposition notes, a court docket, a ministry gazette) — and every re-verification reads the disposition, not the text. A premise re-verified from the text alone is UNVERIFIED for grading. Registered letters older than 90 days with an instrument premise are re-read at the disposition source at the owner's next boot.**
⛔ **Will's rider, encoded in the letter (verbatim: *"preserve conflicting evidence for adjudication—a later publication date alone must not decide authority"*):** when two disposition sources disagree about the instrument's status (for example a docket shows a stay while the gazette shows the measure in force), **both are named and both are kept in the letter.** The premise's state is `CONTESTED` (the `STATE_VOCABULARY.md` Class 2 contract: a named adjudicator and a dated review, never down and never fired; here it is applied to a disputed PREMISE), and the letter is **NOT GATE-CITABLE** until the adjudicator rules (the WQ-250 form, as in SL-4(b)/SL-5(e)). ⛔ **The later publication date ALONE never settles it.** A later date is evidence to weigh, never the verdict. A later source can be stale on the point, can report the other source, or can be a lower authority.
⚠️ **Scope note — the one clause that reaches EXISTING letters:** this standard is otherwise forward-only (header § Scope). The 90-day re-read clause is part of the ruled letter and applies to letters already registered. It obliges a re-read **at the owner's next boot**. It does not re-grade anything by itself. A re-read that finds the instrument amended, stayed, terminated or vacated goes to the letter's owner under the correction form (`CORRECTION_FORM.md`), never silently into the grade.
*Acceptance tests (the proposal's five; test 2 rewritten by the rider):* **(1) ordinary:** a letter naming the EO plus its Federal Register disposition source passes. **(2) overlap:** a letter naming the instrument with two disposition sources that DISAGREE ⇒ both are named and preserved, the premise is `CONTESTED` with an adjudicator and a review date, and the letter is NOT GATE-CITABLE meanwhile. A letter whose later-dated source "wins" on its date alone FAILS this test. **(3) wrong owner:** a press characterisation offered as the disposition source is refused. A report ABOUT the disposition is not the disposition record. **(4) missing information:** instrument named, no disposition source ⇒ REGISTRABLE, NOT GATE-CITABLE (the WQ-250 form). **(5) concurrent activity:** N/A. It is a property of one letter's text, with no shared state.
Kin: `finding_record_of_an_action_is_not_the_action` (the text is a record of what was enacted, not of what is in force) · SL-4 (the grading source must be able to produce the level; here, the re-verification source must be able to show the instrument's CURRENT status).
