# Why gold is weak despite bank-buying headlines

**MIDAS · October 25, 2026 · Will follow-up.** This is a sourced explanation of the evidence already gathered, not a new forecast, trade or causal decomposition. No threshold or score changes.

**Assessment:** official buying is real and remains a longer-term support. The recent price weakness is consistent with investor selling/risk reduction against a broader rates-and-dollar headwind. That interpretation is better supported than a claim that central banks have abandoned gold; the relative causal contribution of each factor is unmeasured. Forced selling or margin calls are not established.

## Buying evidence and reporting clock

- WGC's **October 6** release reports global central-bank **net +39t for August**, using IMF/central-bank reports. This is a lagged reported series, not a complete real-time measure of all official trading. [PRIMARY WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/10/central-bank-gold-statistics-central-banks-continue-summer-spree-august), re-opened October 25.
- WGC's **October 15** China update reports PBoC **+23t September, +63t Q3**; prior MIDAS primary SAFE quantity verification stands. The same note describes weak jewellery-related wholesale demand and higher yields/stronger dollar during September. Official buying and weak demand elsewhere coexist. [PRIMARY WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/10/china-gold-market-update-q3-ends-strong-official-gold-buying), re-opened October 25.
- Headlines dated this week may therefore describe August/September buying. Goldman’s 79t August estimate and WGC's 39t reported figure measure different coverage; the underlying Goldman note remains unheld. Do not turn the estimate into confirmed October purchases.
- Distinguish central-bank reserve purchases from commercial-bank customer custody and from relocation of bars already owned. Higher reported reserve dollar value can reflect price changes without added ounces. No new commercial-bank accumulation claim was verified in this follow-up.

## Selling and competing-return evidence

- **CFTC as-of October 20**, already consumed October 23: gold legacy noncommercial net position/OI fell a fifth consecutive week to **49.6832%**. The latest weekly long count fell **8,158 contracts**, with shorts down 144, supporting reduced long exposure rather than a new-short-only story. The observation precedes October 22; it cannot identify that day's seller. Canonical ledger: `sources/cot_vintages_consumed.tsv`, KB187.
- **PRIMARY GLD October 22** bullion fell **11.98t** to **1,045.64t** as its price declined. This is one fund/day, not a claim that all global gold ETFs are losing assets. September global ETF additions and the smaller January-to-October GLD holdings decline remain part of the supportive evidence. Friday issuer quantities remain unavailable in the captured vintage.
- Higher inflation-adjusted Treasury yields offer competing income and a stronger dollar can pressure dollar gold. DXY January 29→October 23 rose **5.74%**, calculated from the saved Yahoo MIRROR series; FRED latest-revised real 10Y was **1.89% January 29**, versus **2.64% October 22** in the boot FRED/primary Treasury read. The **recent** October 9→22 real-yield fall of27bp means this longer-period headwind cannot by itself explain last week's gold weakness. Gold fell4.05% over that relief window while Treasury funds rose.
- Price can fall even while an important buyer accumulates: price depends on the terms on which all participants are willing to transact. Reserve managers are not committing unlimited buying at a fixed market price. Comparing China's monthly additions directly with one day's fund withdrawals does not measure aggregate net market demand.

**Operator read:** take official buying seriously as longer-term demand, but do not read the headlines as proof that this week's selling has ended or a floor exists. Continued official accumulation plus gold beginning to respond positively to rate relief would strengthen the rebound case. The latter response has been missing. No measured counterfactual price without central-bank buying, exact seller identities or causal percentages are claimed.
