## The balancing evidence matters

Gold has not lost all sensitivity to financial conditions. In the latest **60 sessions**, GLD rose on **15 of 22** observed days when real yields fell, averaging **+0.37%** on that subset. When both yields and DXY fell, gold rose on **10 of 14**, averaging **+0.68%**. The latest **20 sessions** are weaker: **5 of 9** yield-decline days were positive, mean **−0.08%**, and **3 of 6** joint-yield/dollar-decline days were positive, mean **−0.02%**. These nested samples overlap, are small, and are not a significance test, predictive rule or a causal estimate. They show intermittent support alongside a weak aggregate price path.

Global ETF demand was also positive before the latest GLD withdrawal. The **October 19 WGC report, printed page 13**, reports **+13.9 tonnes / US$1.916 billion** net global weekly ETF demand/flows for the **week ended October 16**, with holdings **4,279.1 tonnes**. The table marks **82 funds (41%) complete**, representing **74.8% of AUM**; it is a partially reported snapshot, not a fully final global census. The current WGC data landing page says weekly data normally update the following Monday. This vintage cannot resolve global flows for the week ended October 23. Sources: [WGC PDF](https://www.gold.org/download/file/21288/WMM_19_Oct_2026_Bonds_back_in_the_room.pdf), [data and update schedule](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/data/gold-etfs-holdings-and-flows). Existing primary capture `sources/2026-10-20_news/wgc_weekly.pdf` and the inspected page image are retained, with hashes in this study's verification record.

GLD itself still holds **43.76 tonnes more than July 16**, despite its price being back near that low. That is evidence against treating this as an already-established wholesale investor exit. It has not prevented recent losses. Reported bullion quantities also include ordinary fund fee/operating effects and are not gross creations/redemptions.

Across the latest 60 sessions, GLD rose on **12 of 33** SPY down days, averaging **−0.52%** on that subset. The same three SPY declines of at least 1% sit inside both the 20- and 60-session windows; they are not six independent events. These descriptive samples differ from the frozen WQ-451 stress-day definition and do not replace its evidence or forecasts.

## What would change this assessment?

A rebound would initially be an early sign. A subsequent pullback holding above the new low, followed by sustained strength above the preceding rebound high, would make a base more credible. Stabilising fund quantities and an improved response to rate/dollar relief would corroborate that price change. Improved protection on equity down days would separately strengthen the haven case. All are evidence directions, not newly adopted conjunctions, time limits, numerical bands or a requirement that every condition occur before price turns.

For the present check, the latest price and investor evidence do not warrant saying the decline is ending. The limited positives are an intact July closing low, earlier global fund additions, higher GLD tonnes than in July and continued positive responses on some easing days. None establishes the timing of a bottom.
