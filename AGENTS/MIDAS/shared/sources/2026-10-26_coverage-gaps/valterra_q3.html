Valterra Platinum Limited Production Report for the third quarter ending 30 September 2026

23 October, 2026
Share

Resilient performance amid safety and operational headwinds

  • Safety performance – One work-related fatality at Amandelbult in July. The total recordable injury frequency rate (TRIFR) at 1.45 per million hours at own operations improved by 5% compared to the prior period.
  • Total PGM production (expressed as 5E+Au metal-in-concentrate (M&C)) decreased by 6% to 804,300 ounces, driven by lower own-mined volumes and by weaker purchase of concentrate (POC) volumes.
  • Own-mined PGM production decreased by 5% to 511,900 ounces, primarily driven by the impact of safety stoppages, at Amandelbult. A strong performance at Mototolo partially offset the lower production at Mogalakwena and Unki.
  • Purchase of PGM concentrate (POC) decreased by 7% to 292,400 ounces, reflecting reduced volumes from various third-party producers.
  • Refined PGM production (excluding tolling) decreased by 41% to 576,900 ounces, primarily due to the re-phasing of scheduled processing maintenance and annual stock counts from the first to the third quarter. Year-to-date refined production for the nine months ended September 2026 remained broadly in line with the prior year.
  • PGM sales volumes decreased by 27% to 683,300 ounces, driven by lower refined production, partially offset by a drawdown of refined inventory. Year-to-date sales volumes for the nine months ended September 2026 remained in line with the prior year, this period normalizes for the change in timing of the processing maintenance.
  • Guidance for 2026 remains unchanged, with M&C and refined production expected to be between 3.0-3.4 million ounces. Cash operating unit costs are expected to be at or around the upper end of the guided range of R19,000- R20,000 per PGM ounce, driven by inflationary pressures on key consumables, including fuel, drill steel, chemicals and certain explosives, associated with the ongoing Middle East conflict, coupled with the impact of the safety-related stoppages on production volumes. Mitigation initiatives remain in place through operational efficiencies, disciplined cost management and procurement interventions. The targeted all-in sustaining cost (AISC) remains at approximately US$1,050 per 3E ounce.

Craig Miller, CEO of Valterra Platinum, said:

“Ensuring that our employees return home safely every day is our highest priority. The tragic loss of our colleague, Ms. Olwethu Kutta, at the Amandelbult Tumela Mine in July is unacceptable, and our thoughts are with her family, friends, and colleagues. We remain unwavering in our commitment to preventing a recurrence and have launched an immediate call to action to strengthen safety leadership and controls across the business, supported by an independent review from industry-leading safety specialists. The findings and recommendations from these processes are being implemented across our operations as we continue to strengthen our safety culture and pursue our goal of zero harm.

“Own-mined M&C production was lower during the quarter, primarily reflecting company-wide safety stoppages undertaken following the fatalities in the first half of the year, to reinforce safety leadership and critical controls across the business. A strong performance at Mototolo partially offset the lower production. Refined production reflected the planned re-phasing of processing maintenance and annual stock counts into the third quarter, supporting a more balanced distribution of refined production across the year.

“We achieved another important strategic milestone during the quarter through the conclusion of two new purchase of concentrate agreements with Tharisa plc. These agreements further enhance the margins generated from our third-party processing portfolio while supporting utilisation of our processing infrastructure, reinforcing the value and competitive advantage of our integrated processing platform.

“Looking ahead, production remains on track to be delivered within our 2026 guidance range, and we remain focused on operating safely, executing with discipline and delivering sustainable value for all our stakeholders.”

Overview – Q3 2026 performance vs prior period Q3 2025

Safety performance
Regrettably, we experienced a fatal incident at our Amandelbult Tumela Mine in July 2026. Ms. Olwethu Kutta, lost her life in a fall-of-ground incident while conducting a planned workover inspection. In response, we implemented a company-wide call to action to strengthen critical safety controls, reinforce frontline risk management and enhance leadership engagement and verification across our operations. We remain resolute in our commitment to learning from incidents to prevent recurrence and have engaged leading external safety specialists to review our safety practices. While the loss of life remains our overriding concern, broader safety indicators improved during the quarter, with the total recordable injury frequency rate (TRIFR) at own operations decreasing by 5% to 1.41 per million hours worked.

Total M&C PGM production
Total PGM production in Q3 2026 decreased by 6% to 804,300 ounces compared to the prior period, primarily driven by a 5% decrease in own-mined production and by a 7% decrease in POC volumes.

PGM production from own mines
Own-mined production decreased by 5% to 511,900 ounces compared to the prior period, mainly due to lower output from Amandelbult, Mogalakwena and Unki, partially offset by higher production at Mototolo.

  • Mogalakwena’s PGM production decreased by 4% to 217,600 ounces, primarily reflecting lower milling throughput following the expiry of the Baobab concentrator lease at the end of 2025, consistent with the planned milling profile for 2026. Ore tonnes increased in line with the pit sequence, which also supported an improved head grade, while recovery was impacted by the processing of a different ore blend compared to the prior period. Consistent with our value-over-volume approach, we continued to optimise feed grades through the strategic blending of low-grade ore stockpiles.
  • Amandelbult PGM production decreased by 10% to 137,900 ounces, reflecting the impact of Section 54 regulatory and self-imposed safety stoppages across the mine, following the fatal incident at Tumela mine. Operational performance recovered progressively during the latter part of the quarter as compliance measures were implemented and operations resumed. By quarter-end, the operation had returned to steady-state production, supporting an expected step-up in output in the fourth quarter.
  • Mototolo’s PGM production increased by 7% to 70,800 ounces, reflecting strong performance from the Borwa shaft and the continued ramp-up of the Der Brochen shaft. The increase was further supported by higher milling throughput and an improved head grade.
  • Unki’s PGM production declined by 12% to 50,600 ounces, primarily due to lower head grades as mining progressed into planned lower-grade areas of the orebody with higher internal waste dilution, together with lower milling throughput associated with planned plant maintenance.
  • Modikwa PGM production (50% own-mined) decreased by 5% to 35,000 ounces, driven by lower concentrator recoveries, reduced head grades and marginally lower milling volumes. Recoveries were impacted by the treatment of weathered open-pit ore, while operational disruptions affected grades and throughput during the quarter.

Purchases of PGM concentrate
Purchase of concentrate volumes decreased by 7% to 292,400 ounces, reflecting reduced volumes from various third-party producers, as well as lower production at Modikwa.

Refined PGM production
Refined PGM production (excluding tolling) decreased by 41% to 576,900 ounces, reflecting the re-phasing of the processing maintenance and stock counts from the first to the third quarter of the year. As a result, year-to-date refined production for the nine months ended September 2026 remained broadly in line with the prior year, with both periods reflecting the impact of planned maintenance and stock count activities. Processing operations were fully ramped up by the end of August, with refined production returning to unconstrained run rates during September.

Base metal and chrome production
Nickel production decreased by 39% to 3,825 tonnes and copper production decreased by 22% to 3,294 tonnes, driven by the re-phasing of scheduled processing maintenance and stock counts to the third quarter of the year.

Total dry 40.5% UG2 chrome concentrate production for the quarter increased by 7% to 277,000 tonnes, mainly due to the restoration of Amandelbult to stable production levels and improved chrome yields, partially offset by lower chrome production at Mototolo.

PGM sales volumes and realised basket price
PGM sales volumes decreased by 27% to 683,300 ounces, driven by lower refined production during the quarter, partially offset by a drawdown of refined inventory. Year-to-date sales volumes for the nine months ended September 2026 remained in line with the prior year, this period normalizes for the change in timing of the processing maintenance.

The average realised basket price was R38,815/PGM ounce, or US$2,383/PGM ounce, representing year-on-year increases of 15% in rand terms and 24% in dollar terms. Although major PGM prices moderated quarter-on-quarter amid weaker automotive sales and broader macroeconomic concerns, they remained well above prior-year levels. Minor PGMs, particularly iridium and ruthenium, continued to outperform, supported by robust demand from emerging technology applications.

Guidance for 2026
Guidance for 2026 remains unchanged, with M&C and refined production expected to be between 3.0-3.4 million ounces. Cash operating unit costs are expected to be at or around the upper end of the guided range of R19,000- R20,000 per PGM ounce, assuming current macroeconomic and commodity market conditions persist for the remainder of the year. Cost pressures are primarily related to key consumables, including fuel, drill steel, chemicals and certain explosives, associated with the ongoing Middle East conflict, together with the impact of safety-related stoppages on production volumes. Mitigation initiatives remain in place through operational efficiencies, disciplined cost management and procurement interventions. The targeted all-in sustaining cost (AISC) remains at approximately US$1,050 per 3E ounce.

ABOUT VALTERRA PLATINUM

Valterra Platinum is one of the world’s leading integrated producers of platinum group metals (PGMs) with a primary listing on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange and a secondary listing on the London Stock Exchange. We operate world class, long-life mines and the industry’s most efficient processing assets, responsibly mining, smelting, and refining PGMs and associated co-products from operations located in South Africa and Zimbabwe. With integrated marketing hubs in London, Singapore and Shanghai, we deliver tailored solutions for our customers.

We continue to integrate sustainability into everything we do, invest in our mining and processing capabilities and advance market development initiatives to grow and commercialise new demand segments. We make a meaningful impact in the communities where we operate and remain committed to delivering consistent and superior returns to shareholders. Guided by our purpose of unearthing value to better our world, we are committed to zero harm, disciplined capital allocation and delivery on our value-accretive strategic priorities.
