U.S. stocks were pressured today (and lately) on concerns:
AI
debt, the Middle East/oil and European sovereigns. 1) The AI trade is under renewed pressure after mixed megacap signals as Meta announced they are spending more (capex to $135B-$145B vs. prior $130B-$145B), while Tesla is spending less (capex down to $20B from $25B). Nasdaq futures fell over 2.5%. 2) Middle East risk is feeding directly into the macro picture. Brent is around $112/bbl, European NatGas is firmer and fresh attacks on Saudi infrastructure keep the inflation/stagflation risk alive. 3) In Europe, France remains the fixed-income stress point as a stronger
INSEE
manufacturing survey could not help French bonds, with 10yr yields now around 5.10%. Overall, the risk-off mood boosted havens, with Treasuries, the Yen rising.
Asian markets have been weak as China’s Benchmark stock index, the China Securities Index (
CSI
) 300, closed in a bear market on Thursday, 20.2% below its 22nd June peak, per Reuters. It ended at 4,036.30, the lowest close since July 2025. The index tracks 300 large companies listed in Shanghai and Shenzhen. It had already fallen for a record 10 straight weeks through last Friday, per Bloomberg.
Max bearishness for markets as Hedge funds have loaded up on bearish positions in global equities, with the short-to-buy ratio reaching about five times and short leverage climbing to the 99th percentile of readings over the past five years, according to Goldman Sachs. The charts showed net selling in 2026 bottoming near about 4% of total global gross market value three times in April, late July, and October, although the latest reading has partially rebounded to about 3.7%.

Economic Data
Weekly Jobless Claims fell to 194,000 from 200,000 prior (and consensus 200,000), as the 4-week moving average fell to 197,750 from 198,750 prior week (previous 198,500) and continued claims climbed to 1.728M from 1.703M prior week.
Commodities
U.S. WTI crude oil futures settle at $99.47 per barrel, rising $2.32, or 2.39% to a five-month high on Thursday after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia stoked concerns of further disruption to energy supplies from the key exporting region. (more below). Brent crude surged 4% or $4.35 to $113.17.
1) A New York Times report outlined how the U.S. and Israeli militaries have developed plans to renew strikes on Iran after the midterm elections. The Times, citing unnamed U.S. officials, said this would be a “short but intensive” operation.
2) Overnight, the State Department announced it was ordering non-emergency government employees to leave Saudi Arabia amid the Houthi attacks on the kingdom.
3) Iran’s IRGC threatened US bases in the region if Washington keeps escorting ships through what it calls an “illegal” Hormuz route, per Bloomberg.
Another day and another brutal selloff in precious metals as December gold fall -$89.60, or -2.16%to settle at $4,060.40 an ounce and silver falls -$2.70, or -4.68%, to settle at $55.01 an ounce. Gold fell to its lowest level in more than two months on Thursday as higher oil prices fueled inflation concerns, reinforcing expectations that interest rates could remain elevated for longer, while investors looked ahead to next week’s Federal Reserve meeting.
Currencies & Treasuries
The yield on 10-year Treasury note fells below 5% for first time since September 23. US Treasury yields tumbled as equities declined as concerns about the sustainability of the AI outlook resurfaced, boosting the safe-haven appeal of government debt. The Nasdaq fell around 3% today led by shares of META after earnings results and announcement of new non-voting shares to help fund the social media company’s AI expansion. The yield on the benchmark US 10-year Treasury note fell 19.3 bps to 4.953% for its biggest daily drop since August 1, 2025 (down from highs of 5.3645% on 10/7, highest since April ‘02). Markets have largely priced out any rate increase by the Fed at its policy meeting next week next week as probability of a hike of at least 25 bps has fallen to 10.5%. The yield on the 30-year bond tumbled 15 bps to 5.29% and the 5-year yield fell -20 bps to 4.768%, on pace for its biggest decline since August 2025.
