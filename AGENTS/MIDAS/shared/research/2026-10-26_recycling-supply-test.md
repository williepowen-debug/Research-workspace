# Recycling supply: an explicit counterweight to mine constraints

**October 26, 2026; Will-authorized bounded pass.** The previous coverage recommendation is now executed. Recycling weakens a mine-shortage-only argument, especially for palladium, but its effect differs by metal. This integrates existing model assumptions and newer processor evidence; it does not add recycling twice to a balance already containing it.

## Published balances and sensitivity

Positive balances mean surplus; negative mean deficit. `koz` is thousand troy ounces; `Moz` is million troy ounces. Each row uses its own source vintage and definitions.

| Model / publication | Recycling: 2025 → 2026 forecast | Increase | Published 2026 balance | Balance with recycling flat at that model's 2025 level |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| [JM May 14](https://matthey.com/media/2026/johnson-matthey-publishes-2026-pgm-market-report1), palladium | 3,135 → 3,427 koz | +292 koz, +9.3% | +214 koz | **−78 koz** |
| Same JM model, platinum | 1,547 → 1,576 koz | +29 koz, +1.9% | −371 koz | −400 koz |
| [WPIC September 9 Q2 report](https://platinuminvestment.com/files/354715/WPIC_Platinum_Quarterly_Q2_2026.pdf), platinum | 1,669 → 1,802 koz | +133 koz, +8.0% | +265 koz | **+132 koz** |
| [World Silver Survey, April](https://silverinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/World-Silver-Survey-2026.pdf), silver | 197.6 → 211.3 Moz | +13.7 Moz, +6.9% | −46.3 Moz | **−60.0 Moz** |

The last column is our arithmetic: reported balance minus year-on-year recycling growth, with **every other source-model input held fixed**. It is a sensitivity, not a replacement forecast, price target or estimated probability. Prices, scrappage, investment demand and mine response would interact in reality. WPIC's rounded supply less demand is 264 koz versus its reported 265 koz; the sensitivity preserves the reported headline. Silver's rounded subtraction similarly differs by 0.1 Moz.

**Palladium:** JM's projected primary supply falls 557 koz; recycling offsets 292 koz. However, demand also falls 895 koz, including investment moving from +382 to −115 koz. Recycling is material, rather than the whole surplus explanation. JM explicitly cautions that collector growth can reflect dishoarding or market-share consolidation rather than organic scrap growth. This makes comparable recovered ounces a useful falsifier of the surplus thesis.

**Platinum:** WPIC's forecast surplus survives removal of recycling growth; its 1,582 koz demand decline is much larger. Its Q2 estimate already shows recycling 466 koz, up 9% year on year: autocatalysts 374 koz (+17%), jewellery 68 koz (−24%), industrial 23 koz (+16%). Components round to 465 koz. Thus recovery is partly observed, but uneven. JM's older platinum deficit uses a different demand/supply model: do not average or splice the two balances. WPIC's existing November 18 update remains the ordinary checkpoint.

**Silver:** added scrap does not erase the April forecast deficit. Projected mine output falls only 2.5 Moz, but net hedging supply falls 34.7 Moz, helping explain why the deficit widens despite recycling growth. An annual deficit is not proof of current London free-float scarcity or a squeeze; our existing allocation/lease gaps remain. This uses April's 211.3 Moz forecast, rather than substituting February's earlier outlook or treating the report's historical October squeeze as October 2026.

## Actual processor evidence: useful, with boundaries

[Sibanye's H1 2026 issuer report, filed September 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1786909/000162828026059701/form6-kh137.htm) reports ounces recycled and sold, excluding mixed scrap:

| Metal | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Reported change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gold | 63,992 oz | 95,188 oz | +49% |
| Five PGMs combined | 155,375 oz | 194,926 oz | +25% |
| Silver | 932,712 oz | 2,498,226 oz | +168% |

The 142% total-ounces headline is dominated by silver and includes North Carolina after its September 2025 acquisition. These are company sales/recovery measures, with no matched constant-perimeter Pt/Pd split or global open-loop classification. They corroborate activity, not a 142% global PGM recycling increase. Gold-equivalent ounces are a value conversion, not physical gold tonnage.

[Umicore's July 31 H1 release](https://www.umicore.de/en/media/half-year-results-2026/) reports recycling EBITDA +36%, but Precious Metals Refining volumes lower after scheduled maintenance. Prices, mix and other recycling businesses supported profit. Profit growth alone therefore fails the physical-supply test.

**Gold separately:** [WGC's July 30 Q2 supply table](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q2-2026/supply) estimates recycling 326.1 tonnes versus 346.7 tonnes, down 6% year on year. Europe and US growth coexisted with emerging-market declines. That dated aggregate does not support a universal scrap surge or prove October distress selling.

## Integrated judgment

Palladium's tightening-only thesis remains **WEAKENED**. Platinum's 2026 surplus/longer-term constraint reading is **UNCHANGED**, with material model disagreement. Silver's April deficit survives its assumed recycling recovery; current physical tightness remains **UNRESOLVED**. Gold has no demonstrated aggregate recycling flood in the held Q2 data.

JM counts primary producer sales and open-loop recovery; closed-loop returned metal is netted from demand. Do not add a processor's gross recovered metal to that balance, count mine/refinery throughput and sales twice, or combine company acquisitions with organic industry growth. Comparable recovery below forecast would strengthen scarcity risk; sustained recovery with weak demand would weaken it. These are qualitative falsifiers within existing reviews, with no new numerical rail or scheduled patrol.
