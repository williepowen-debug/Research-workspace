# Precious-metals research additions worth considering

**October 26, 2026, Will asked what is not already integrated.** This is a bounded coverage check and recommendation, not adoption of a new channel, monitor, forecast, threshold or research schedule. Existing prices, scoring and frozen letters remain unchanged.

The core topics are already covered or explicitly recorded as gaps: rates/dollar, central-bank buying, investor quantities/positioning, silver physical availability and solar thrifting, China auto demand/substitution, Russian supply, fund/inventory/lease data and certified gold settlement. Recommending those again as new work would duplicate the register. The following two additions have better incremental value.

## First priority: recycled metal as the supply counterweight

The live THESIS, OPEN_ITEMS and TRADE files contain no explicit recycling/scrap supply test. Our held World Silver Survey and JM/WPIC reports include recycling in their balances; the omission is analytical integration, not that the desk has never obtained the source reports. The few KB hits for “recycl” concern recycled stories or vehicle classification, rather than a measured scrap-supply contribution.

**Question:** is jewellery scrap, spent automotive catalyst and other recoverable metal returning quickly enough to offset mine constraints, or are collection, finance and refining bottlenecks holding it back?

Why it matters: mine-supply tightness can coexist with an improving total supply balance if recycling recovers. Conversely, weak recycled flows can preserve tightness despite high prices. The [WGC Q2 supply report, published July 30](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q2-2026/supply), reports gold recycling down 6% year on year and describes regional, capacity and financing differences. [JM's May 14 PGM report announcement](https://matthey.com/media/2026/johnson-matthey-publishes-2026-pgm-market-report1) expects higher PGM prices to support automotive recycling recovery. These are different metals, periods and evidence types: **the gold observation is Q2, and the PGM statement is a forecast, not proof of October realized supply**.

Useful bounded follow-up: split mined and recycled supply for silver, platinum and palladium in the already-held balance reports; identify which current balance depends on a recycling rebound; then check a small number of public refiner/processor operating disclosures for realized volumes. Gold provides a comparison, not a pooled elasticity. Keep open-loop new supply separate from closed-loop return of a manufacturer's own metal. A supplier's annual forecast cannot establish that October physical availability improved. No new numeric trigger is proposed.

## Second priority: South African mined output versus refinery catch-up

Our Russian/Nornickel read and Tharisa coverage are current, but I did not find an integrated read of **Valterra's October 23 Q3 release** in the current thesis/KB. Its [issuer release](https://www.valterraplatinum.com/media_centre/valterra-platinum-limited-production-report-for-the-third-quarter-ending-30-september-2026/) reports refined PGM production down 41% to 576,900 ounces, largely because scheduled processing maintenance/stock counts shifted into Q3. It also says processing returned to unconstrained rates in September and full-year guidance remains unchanged. Own-mined production was down 5%; company-wide safety stoppages were a separate factor.

**Question:** how much of the apparent supply decline is permanent lost metal, and how much is delayed conversion of already-mined concentrate into refined inventory that can return later?

A focused South Africa/Russia comparison would separate mine output, purchased concentrate, refining, work-in-progress and sales/inventory release. Keep Pt and Pd components distinct: the issuer's headline is a PGM basket, not a platinum-only decline. The release is preliminary operational evidence, not an audited market balance. **The 41% refined decline alone does not establish a current outage or automatic I2 score change**. The issuer describes resumed processing and unchanged guidance; a full causal/supply-loss calculation has not been done in this coverage check.

This complements Nornickel's catch-up production and keeps a refinery-maintenance headline from masquerading as a permanent mine shortage. It is an issuer-evidence comparison within I2, not mining-equity coverage or a new fifth channel.
