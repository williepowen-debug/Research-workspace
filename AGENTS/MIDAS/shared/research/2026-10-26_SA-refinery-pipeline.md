# Valterra: mine output, refining delays and saleable supply

**October 26, 2026; Will-authorized bounded comparison with Nornickel.** Valterra's Q3 refined-output collapse overstates the decline in its underlying mine production and nine-month market deliveries. Processing resumed in September; mine stoppages were real, but neither release establishes a new acute, broad PGM supply shock. This is a Valterra case study, not a census of South African producers.

Q3 mine/concentrate −6%, refining −41% and sales −27% answer different questions. Nine-month total concentrate and sales are approximately flat; platinum refining is **+3.3%**, palladium **−4.3%**. The basket decline cannot be presented as a 41% year-to-date platinum shortage.

## What moved through the pipeline

The issuer attributes the refining decline primarily to maintenance and stock counts shifted from Q1 to Q3; September returned to unconstrained processing. Amandelbult safety stoppages also cut mined output; the company says it returned to steady state by quarter-end. Other mine-level constraints persist, so recovery does not establish zero lasting lost output. Unchanged 3.0–3.4 million-ounce guidance is management's expectation, not proof of delivery.

Sales exceeded owned refined production by **106.4 koz in Q3** and **101.5 koz over nine months**. The issuer independently describes a refined-inventory draw. Our differences corroborate the direction, but are not an audited inventory reconciliation: opening/closing stocks, adjustments and matching scope are required for equality. Nor is concentrate minus refined output a direct work-in-progress change; timing, recoveries and stock adjustments intervene. No quantified permanent-loss share can be isolated from these figures.

Toll refining is separately reported at 209.2 koz in Q3. It belongs to customers and is outside owned-output guidance; adding it to owned production and then counting customer supply again would duplicate metal. Purchased concentrate likewise is not a second mine supplying the market when refined.

## Fourth-quarter arithmetic, not a new forecast

| Guidance measure | Nine months achieved | Q4 needed for 3.0 Moz low end | Q4 needed for 3.2 Moz midpoint | Q4 needed for 3.4 Moz high end |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Owned refined | 2,318.8 koz | **681.2 koz** | 881.2 koz | 1,081.2 koz |
| Metal in concentrate | 2,323.2 koz | **676.8 koz** | 876.8 koz | 1,076.8 koz |

The refining low-end requirement exceeds Q3's 576.9 koz but is below Q2's 963.5 koz. Recovery is plausible on that comparison, not assured: the remaining quarter still has to deliver. Guidance cuts, renewed interruptions or missed conversion into sales would strengthen scarcity risk; recovery plus sustained sales would weaken it. These are existing-channel qualitative falsifiers, not new numeric score thresholds or registered predictions.

Full rounding reconciliation and frozen setup remain at [the completed L701 read](2026-10-26_nornickel-9M-L701.md).

Valterra's Q3 conversion delay and Nornickel's catch-up point in opposite quarterly directions; neither should be annualised. Do not sum a six-metal Valterra basket with individual Russian Pt/Pd ounces, or infer a global surplus from two companies.
