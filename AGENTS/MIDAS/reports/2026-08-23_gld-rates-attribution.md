### 2.3 ⚠️ Reproduction note — the registered betas do NOT reproduce exactly, and I found the fork
Re-estimating this session on the same nominal window (2024-01-01 → 2026-08, FRED DFII10 × yfinance closes), I could not reproduce −0.0514/−0.0634 to the digit. I located the method fork: **how you handle FRED's missing values before differencing.**

| Method | `GC=F` | `GLD` | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Holiday-bridged** (drop NaN rows, *then* diff — so a yield change can span 2+ calendar days and is paired with a 1-day price return) | **−0.0481** (R² 0.0207) | **−0.0603** (R² 0.0325) | 657 / 656 |
| same, truncated at 8/18 | **−0.0468** (R² 0.0196) | −0.0585 (R² 0.0308) | **655** / 654 |
| **Interval-matched** (diff in place, drop the mismatched pairs — yield change and price return always span the same interval) | **−0.0563** (R² 0.0274) | **−0.0691** (R² 0.0413) | 628 / 627 |
| **Registered (KB-046)** | −0.0514 | −0.0634 | 655 |

**Findings:** (a) the registered **n=655 reproduces exactly** under the holiday-bridged method truncated at 8/18, so the published pair is from that method family; (b) the registered values sit **~9% steeper than my holiday-bridged reproduction** and ~9% flatter than my interval-matched one — an unresolved gap I am recording rather than papering over; (c) **the interval-matched method is the more defensible one** (34 pairs in the sample mismatch a multi-day yield change against a single-day return, which mechanically attenuates the slope — the same errors-in-variables direction as the roll); (d) **decisively for the conclusion: every variant is steeper than or equal to the registered rolled beta, and steeper betas shrink the residual.** The registered figure is the *most* flattering one in the set. **No variant changes the verdict.**

**Rule adopted:** a beta quoted as load-bearing must carry its **missing-value convention** alongside its ticker and window. Ticker + window was not a sufficient specification. → **L-22**.

## 3. 🔴 THE CORRECTION THAT MATTERS — "89% UNEXPLAINED" IS A SINGLE-FACTOR CLAIM

§2 measures the move against **one** regressor. The dollar fell **0.82%** that session, and gold's dollar beta is large. Adding DXY as a second factor over the same window:

| Model (2024-01 → 2026-08) | b_rates (%/bp) | b_usd (%/1%) | R² | 8/19 explained | **unexplained** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GLD, interval-matched | **−0.0275** | **−1.289** | 0.1648 | +1.223pp (rates +0.165 · usd +1.057) | **68.1%** |
| GLD, holiday-bridged | −0.0196 | −1.307 | 0.1626 | +1.190pp | **69.0%** |
| GCQ26/GC=F, interval-matched | −0.0174 | −1.207 | 0.1354 | +1.094pp | **61.3%** |
| GCQ26/GC=F, holiday-bridged | −0.0102 | −1.217 | 0.1335 | +1.059pp | **62.5%** |

**Read it straight:**
1. **The unexplained share falls from ~89% to a 61–69% band.** My published headline was too strong by roughly 20–28 percentage points of the move.
2. **In the joint model the rates coefficient collapses** (−0.0634 → −0.0275, or −0.0514 → −0.0174). Much of what my univariate gold/real-yield beta was measuring **was the dollar**. The rates leg on 8/19 explains **3–4%** of the move, not 8–15%.
3. **R² roughly quadruples** (0.035 → 0.165). The single-factor model was a weak instrument and I should have said so when I published the 89%.

### 3.1 Why the conclusion nonetheless SURVIVES — and the caveat that keeps it honest
- **Mechanistically, USD weakness is not an independent explanation of a debasement bid — it is the same tell in another currency's mirror.** "Gold rose because the dollar fell" and "gold rose because the dollar is being debased" are not competing hypotheses; the second predicts the first. Attributing part of the move to DXY **does not restore a rates explanation** — it re-labels part of the residual as *dollar-denominated*, and the rates leg gets *smaller*, not bigger.
- **Statistically, a joint fit with correlated regressors splits shared variance by an arbitrary convention.** DXY and real yields co-move; the 2-factor split is one defensible allocation, not the truth. That is exactly why **both** the single-factor (85–93%) and two-factor (61–69%) bands are published here, and why the honest headline quotes the **two-factor** number as the conservative one.
- **The residual is still large and still rare:** +2.52pp on the day = **+2.00σ** of the two-factor residual distribution, the **33rd-largest absolute residual of 627 sessions (top 5.3%)**. A model that knows the day's real yields *and* the day's dollar still misses this session by two standard deviations.

⇒ **Revised canonical sentence, to be cited in place of the 89%:**
> **On 2026-08-19, gold rose +3.84% (GLD) / +2.83% (GCQ26) while the 10y real yield fell 6bp and the dollar fell 0.82%. Real rates alone explain 8–15% of it; real rates plus the dollar explain 31–39%. Roughly two-thirds of the session is unexplained by both, at +2.0σ. Rates-assisted, dollar-assisted, and neither-explained.**
