FOREWORD

This Platinum Quarterly presents platinum supply and demand movements for the third quarter of 2026, an updated forecast for 
2026f and a first outlook for 2027f. It also provides WPIC’s views on relevant issues and trends for investors considering exposure 
to platinum as an investment asset, and an update on how we continue to meet investors’ needs through our product partnerships. 
The Platinum Quarterly data and commentary (starting on page 5) are prepared independently for WPIC by Metals Focus.

After three consecutive years of deficit, the platinum market is expected to move into a temporary surplus in 2026f before returning 
to deficit in 2027f. This year’s surplus and the move back to a deficit next year largely reflect platinum investment demand trends. 
Rising inflation expectations have led to a revised interest rate outlook, negatively impacting precious metals investor sentiment. 
Investment activity is expected to pick up in 2027f, based on an assumption that tensions in the Middle East will ease, resulting in a 
transitioning through a period of energy price linked inflation.

2026 platinum market surplus deepens on investment outflows

•  Total platinum supply is forecast to increase by 2% year-on-year to 7,366 koz in 2026f, with broadly stable mine supply (+1%) 

complemented by 6% growth in recycling.

•  Total platinum demand is expected to decline by 21% year-on-year to 6,898 koz in 2026f. Net disinvestment of 398 koz, driven 
by ETF liquidations and exchange stock outflows, accounts for most of the decline, together with weaker jewellery demand.

•  The  2026f  surplus  of  468  koz  is  203  koz  larger  than  the  265  koz  surplus  projected  in  our  previous  Platinum  Quarterly, 
primarily  reflecting  a  315  koz  downgrade  to  investment  demand,  partly  offset  by  the  inclusion  of  newly  identified  platinum 
electrical demand – specifically the demand from the crucibles that are used in the production of optical interconnect crystals  
for datacentres.

Platinum markets to return to a deficit in 2027

•  Total  platinum  supply  is  forecast  to  increase  by  1%  year-on-year  to  7,418  koz  in  2027f,  as  5%  recycling  growth  offsets  a  

1% decline in mine supply.

•  Total  platinum  demand  is  forecast  to  increase  by  12%  year-on-year  to  7,696  koz  in  2027f,  led  by  a  911  koz  recovery  in  

investment demand.

•  The platinum market is forecast to return to a deficit of 279 koz in 2027f, reducing above ground stocks to 1,813 koz, equivalent 

to less than three months of demand.

The platinum investment case – fundamentals are robust but are taking a backseat to the macroeconomic overlay 

Macroeconomic and macropolitical developments have been the biggest driver of value establishment in the precious metals complex 
during 2026. Prior to the US midterms and following the Fed’s September rate hike, the market had priced in a December 2026 rate 
hike and two further increases in 2027. These rate-increase expectations, together with US dollar appreciation, pushed the platinum 
price below the US$1,500/oz support level, and gold and silver below their respective US$4,000/oz and US$50/oz support levels.

The US midterm elections have now concluded with a strong shift towards the Democrats giving them control of both the House 
and the Senate. The initial market response to the election result has been lower oil prices, declining bond yields and a weaker US 
dollar. Market hopes that Democratic control of Congress would force an end to the Iran-US war are signalling some inflation relief 
and potentially fewer Fed hikes in 2027. All three precious metals have rallied back above the aforementioned supports. 

In the longer term, the precious metals complex’s response to the US midterms may not prove to be enduring should the conflict 
continue to drag out and inflation and interest rate expectations remain elevated. Nevertheless, several themes remain compelling 
components of a robust investment case for platinum. First, the political environment is likely to remain volatile. With the Democrats 
controlling Congress, they have the power to block Republican policy decisions, although there is a risk that President Trump could 
try to ignore  Congress and govern through executive orders. The ability to exercise blocks and force policy compromises  could 
reduce volatility, which would be welcomed by the markets and likely be mildly positive for precious metals, whereas rule through 
executive orders could reignite the credibility-linked debasement trade, which would likely be extremely positive for precious metals. 

Second, differing opinions on how to address the fiscal backdrop could continue (i.e. the size of the federal deficit), fears around 
which  have  on  occasion  been  supportive  of  precious  metal  investment  flows.  With  Republicans  unlikely  to  want  tax  hikes  and 
Democrats unlikely to want spending cuts, we do not expect a near-term solution to large US deficits, which could bring debasement 
back into play. Finally, the trend of physical stockpiling of critical metals is growing. The US and EU are actively trying to de-risk 
supply chains for critical industries amidst elevated trade tensions, and this process is likely happening in other countries too. In 
parallel to the stockpiling and physical asset security narrative, several countries have undertaken to re-domicile gold to domestic 
or conceptually lower-risk jurisdictions, while the literal safe-haven reputation of precious metals has also been enhanced by the 
US$6bn of bitcoin stolen by hackers in October (equivalent to around 120 tonnes or 3.9 Moz of platinum). 

In addition to the overarching support for holding platinum as a precious metal, its investment case remains structurally underpinned 
by supply and demand. That said, the 2026f platinum market surplus has been revised from 265 koz to 468 koz, primarily reflecting 
weaker investment demand due to higher interest rates weighing on macroeconomic sentiment. Elsewhere, a downgrade to jewellery 
demand has been more than offset by an upgrade to automotive demand and the inclusion of previously unreported sources of 
demand linked to platinum crucibles used for optical crystals in AI technology. 

Entering 2027f, platinum demand is expected to increase by 12% year-on-year to 7.7 Moz. Demand growth will be led by a 911 koz 
year-on-year recovery in investment demand. First, ETF selling that occurred in 2026f due to declining platinum prices is expected 
to slow. Second, the outflow of exchange stocks that occurred during 2026f due to easing US tariff concerns will not repeat itself 
as NYMEX warehouse stocks of 252 koz as of 2 November 2026 are broadly in line with historic levels. Greater participation on 
the GFEX is expected with a commensurate rise in stocks. Finally, total platinum bar and coin demand (incl. China large bars) is 
expected to increase by 41% y/y in 2027f as mints increase output following the return of lease rates to low single-digit percentage 
terms and supply of platinum investment products from buyback softens on stronger price levels.

Beyond investment demand, 2027f will see industrial platinum demand growth partially offsetting declining automotive and jewellery 
demand. Industrial platinum demand of 2,660 koz (+4% y/y) in 2027f is an all-time high within the WPIC data series and reflects 
platinum’s versatile properties and uses across multiple end markets. In contrast to record industrial platinum demand, jewellery 
demand of 1,710 koz is forecast to be the weakest in our time series dating back to 2013. Notably, China is now expected to be a 
smaller market than each of India, the US, Europe and Japan, making any further downside in China less of a negative impact on 
global jewellery volumes than it has been in the recent past. 

Total platinum supply is forecast to increase by 1% year-on-year in 2027f as 5% growth in recycling offsets a 1% erosion of mine 
supply. We expect 2027f to prove an instructive year for both mine supply and recycling. During the year, we will see whether new 
supply  from  Ivanplats, Tharisa  and  Russian  Platinum  is  able  to  offset  the  longer-term  trend  of  supply  erosion  from  aging  South 
African mines. Meanwhile, the economics of automotive recycling may again get tested (as in 2023 and 2024) with palladium prices 
having dipped below US$1,000/oz.

Platinum supply and demand update 

A Q3 2026 deficit does not alter the 2026 surplus outlook 

During Q3’26, the platinum market recorded a deficit of 110 koz, compared with a 193 koz surplus in Q2’26 and a 367 koz deficit 
in Q3’25. Total platinum supply fell by 6% year-on-year to 1,693 koz, as a 20% decline in South African output, due to rescheduled 
processing maintenance and safety stoppages, drove refined mine supply 12% lower. Recycling edged up by 1%, albeit the clear 
slowdown in the pace of growth is showing up sequentially each quarter. Total demand fell by 17% year-on-year to 1,803 koz in 
Q3’26. Investment demand was broadly neutral (-2 koz) compared with 305 koz in Q3’25. Jewellery (-17%) and automotive (-2%) 
demand declined, although industrial demand rose by 6% year-on-year on stronger chemical, electrical and glass demand.

Market trends during Q3’26 do not alter the expectation that the platinum market will record a surplus in 2026f, where the year-to-
date surplus is 343 koz and the full-year surplus is projected to be 468 koz, equivalent to 7% of annual demand. In 2026f, total supply 
is expected to increase by 2% year-on-year as broadly stable mine supply (+1%) is complemented by higher recycling supply (+6%). 
Platinum demand is forecast to decline by 21% year-on-year in 2026f, principally due to net disinvestment of 398 koz from ETF 
liquidations (-490 koz) and exchange stock outflows (-300 koz). Jewellery demand is forecast to fall by 20%, on collapsing Chinese 
fabrication, while industrial demand rises by 9% on strong glass (+30%) and electrical (+35%) demand.

PLATINUM QUARTERLY Q3 2026A first 2027 outlook sees the platinum market return to deficit

Mine supply is expected to record a minor 1% year-on-year decline in 2027f to 5,571 koz as South African production faces some 
erosion across ageing mines. Notably, Zimbabwean output rises by 6% to a new record on the drawdown of semi-finished inventory at 
Zimplats. Recycling supply is forecast to increase by 5% year-on-year in 2027f, helping total platinum supply to rise by 1% year-on-year 
to 7,418 koz, as a 1% decline in mine supply is offset by an increase in spent autocatalyst availability.

Total platinum demand will increase by 12% year-on-year in 2027f, largely due to an effective 911 koz recovery in investment demand 
to 513 koz. Bar and coin demand is forecast to rise by 55% to 448 koz and exchange stocks are expected to build by 110 koz, while 
ETF outflows slow to 150 koz. Industrial demand is expected to grow by 4% to 2,660 koz, as a 29% increase in chemical demand and 
a 30% rebound in petroleum demand more than offset lower glass (-11%) and electrical (-35%) demand. Automotive demand will erode 
by 5% in 2027f due to ongoing drivetrain electrification and some substitution from platinum to palladium. In aggregate, investment-led 
demand growth outpaces a modest uptick in supply, moving platinum markets back into a 279 koz deficit in 2027f, with above ground 
stocks declining by 13% to 1,813 koz.

WPIC initiatives highlights

We continue to work with WPIC’s wide and growing global network of product partners to identify appropriate strategies to increase 
investment in platinum. Product partner sales in Q3’26 improved on Q2’26 but remained well down on the strong first quarter of the 
year with the macroeconomic overlay, the dominant global influence. 

In Q3’26 our partners in Europe and North America saw an increase of over 10% in net product sales over the weak Q2’26 yet 
were still heavily down on Q3’25. Gross platinum bar and coin demand remained strong but was largely satisfied from the buyback 
of secondary product. We increased the data, research and insights provided to our partners as more investors are interested in 
owning platinum, particularly as the macroeconomic-driven price pullback occurred.

In China our product partners saw a pleasing increase in quarter-on-quarter platinum product sales of over 15%, despite remaining 
over 30% down year-on-year on subdued consumer confidence and the VAT change introduced last year. Despite volumes remaining 
lower than in 2025, underlying interest in precious metals remains resilient, with many buyers adopting a wait-and-see approach 
pending more favourable market conditions.

WPIC continues to work with partners in China to strengthen distribution channels and expand the range of platinum investment 
products. The 2026 Beijing International Coin Expo highlighted further progress, including the China Gold Coin Group’s launch of 
its Year of the Goat platinum bar, an increase in the 100g Platinum Panda Coin mintage to 10,000 pieces for 2027 against 2,000 for 
2026, and the introduction of a fixed Yuan margin that ensures an attractive retail price despite market price volatility – appealing to 
retail bullion investors.

In Japan, our partners experienced net selling in Q3’26, on weaker price and consumer sentiment. WPIC continued its active market 
engagement efforts, including at JBMA’s annual conference in November, sharing insights into platinum market developments with 
approximately 250 attendees, helping to strengthen market awareness and investor understanding.

In  Singapore,  Oversea-Chinese  Banking  Corporation  Limited  (OCBC)’s  launch  of  a  platinum  trading  product  in August  marked 
an important step in expanding access to platinum through mainstream banking channels, broadening investor participation and 
supporting the further development of the regional platinum investment market.

Platinum  lease  rates  remained  stable  in  Q3’26  and  have  increased  appetite  for  minting  new  products  which  should  help  meet 
demand and increase net sales during the rest of 2026 and into 2027.

2026 THIRD QUARTER PLATINUM MARKET REVIEW

The  third  quarter  unfolded  against  continued  macroeconomic  and  geopolitical  uncertainty,  with  Middle  East  tensions  sustaining 
energy market volatility and inflation concerns. Platinum prices were weaker quarter-on-quarter but remained well up compared  
to Q3’25.

Against this backdrop, the platinum market recorded a deficit of 110 koz in Q3’26, compared with a surplus of 193 koz in the previous 
quarter and a deficit of 367 koz a year earlier. Total supply declined by 6% year-on-year to 1,693 koz, reflecting lower South African 
mine production, partly offset by higher recycling volumes. Total demand fell by 17% to 1,803 koz, driven by weaker automotive 
and  jewellery  consumption  and  substantially  lower  net  investment  demand.  Industrial  consumption,  however,  increased  by  6%, 
supported by growth in chemical, electrical and glass applications.

Supply

Global refined mine supply fell 12% year-on-year to 1,240 koz in Q3’26, its lowest level since Q1’25, as lower production from South 
Africa and Zimbabwe more than offset increases in Russia and North America.

South African output declined 20% to 827 koz, primarily reflecting lower refined volumes from Valterra Platinum. The rescheduling 
of processing maintenance and annual stock counts from the first to the third quarter reversed the benefit recorded earlier in the 
year. Valterra’s refined PGM production consequently fell, although processing operations returned to normal operating rates during 
September. Own-mined production also declined, as safety stoppages at Amandelbult and lower milling throughput at Mogalakwena 
outweighed growth at Mototolo. At Implats, scheduled processing maintenance and safety stoppages reduced smelter throughput, 
leading to a renewed build in excess semi-finished inventory. Mining activity was also affected by safety stoppages at Rustenburg 
and Marula. Elsewhere, Phase 1 output at Ivanhoe’s Platreef mine increased quarter-on-quarter, although its contribution to Q3’26 
remained modest. The concentrator continued to batch process lower-grade development ore, while underground materials handling 
and tailings filtration bottlenecks constrained the ramp-up.

Zimbabwean supply declined 10% year-on-year to 113 koz. Planned furnace maintenance at Zimplats began in September, resulting 
in lower smelter output. At Unki, lower grades and planned plant maintenance reduced mined production, while Mimosa was affected 
by power interruptions, concentrator maintenance and lower grades.

Russian  production  increased  28%  year-on-year  to  199  koz,  recovering  after  weaker  output  during  the  first  half.  Nornickel  also 
completed commissioning work to consolidate precious metals processing at its Kola site.

North American supply is estimated to have increased 2% year-on-year to 51 koz, as higher Canadian by-product output offset 
disruption  to  US  mining.  Vale’s  platinum  production  rose,  supported  by  improved  smelting  performance  at  Sudbury  following  
second-quarter maintenance. US mining activity was disrupted by a strike lasting around five weeks at Sibanye-Stillwater’s Stillwater 
East mine.

PLATINUM QUARTERLY Q3 2026Recycling

Global platinum recycling edged higher by 1% year-on-year (+4 koz) to 418 koz in Q3’26, as growth in autocatalyst recycling was 
largely offset by weaker jewellery recycling. However, total recycling declined by 10% quarter-on-quarter.

Autocatalyst recycling increased by 9% year-on-year (+28 koz) to 334 koz, although volumes fell by 10% quarter-on-quarter. Despite 
higher PGM prices year-on-year, recycling flows slowed during Q3’26, with average PGM prices declining by between 6% and 10% 
quarter-on-quarter following stronger prices in H1’26. This discouraged the release of scrap into the recycling pipeline. Recyclers 
also reported a deterioration in incoming material quality, including a growing proportion of silicon carbide autocatalyst substrates, 
which complicated processing and affected recovery yields. Regionally, North America and Europe recorded year-on-year increases 
of 13% and 10%, respectively, although both experienced weaker volumes quarter-on-quarter.

Jewellery recycling fell by 27% year-on-year and 9% quarter-on-quarter to just 62 koz, largely reflecting weaker activity in China, 
where  volumes  declined  by  43%  year-on-year  to  34  koz.  Softer  platinum  prices  during  the  quarter  and  subdued  local  jewellery 
demand weighed on recycling activity. Although more showrooms and manufacturers shifted away from platinum, this did not trigger 
widespread scrapping of existing inventories. Instead, proceeds from routine platinum sales were increasingly redirected towards 
building gold inventories rather than replenishing platinum stocks. Electronics recycling remained broadly unchanged year-on-year 
at 21 koz, although volumes declined by 9% quarter-on-quarter.

PLATINUM QUARTERLY Q3 2026Automotive demand

Global  automotive  platinum  demand  fell  by  2%  (-15  koz)  year-on-year  to  714  koz  in  Q3’26.  This  reflected  a  2%  drop  in  global  
light-duty vehicle (LDV) production to 22.6m units, which was driven by an 11% decline in internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicle 
production. Only a handful of countries such as India, Brazil and Russia recorded production increases, failing to offset the declines 
elsewhere. Hybrid vehicle (HEV, PHEV, EREV) production increased by 7%, insufficient to offset the decline in platinum demand 
from lower ICE output, while battery electric vehicle (BEV) production maintained its upward trajectory, rising by 17%.

In Europe, automotive platinum demand fell by 8% (-18 koz) year-on-year to 199 koz, outpacing the 4% decline in light vehicle 
production.  This  largely  reflected  a  sharper  contraction  in  diesel  vehicle  production,  which  carries  significantly  higher  platinum 
loadings than gasoline vehicles. Heavy-duty ICE vehicle production declined by 1%. Meanwhile, catalysed non-road vehicle (NRV) 
output  rose  by  10%,  while  BEV  production  increased  across  all  vehicle  segments.  North  American  demand  increased  by  9%  
(+11 koz) to 126 koz as ICE LDV production edged down, but hybrid output rose by 13%. BEV production weakened further despite 
the  reintroduction  of  California’s  MyFirstEV  incentive  in August.  ICE  heavy-duty  vehicle  (HDV)  output  jumped  by  44%  in  Q3’26. 
Higher ICE and fuel-cell electric vehicle (FCEV) non-road output further supported demand, despite BEVs gaining market share in 
this segment.

In Japan, automotive platinum demand fell by 13% (-9 koz) year-on-year to 64 koz. Growth in light-duty hybrid production, alongside 
increased catalysed and fuel-cell NRVs, was insufficient to offset the contraction in pure-ICE LDV production. Meanwhile, BEVs 
continued to gain market share, supported by the Clean Energy Vehicle (CEV) purchase incentives. 

In China, automotive platinum demand fell by 9% (-11 koz) year-on-year to 115 koz, driven by weaker catalysed LDV and HDV 
production, which declined by 17% and 8%, respectively. This reflected subdued domestic vehicle sales following the reduction of 
incentives and amid weak consumer confidence. However, growing exports across all powertrain types helped cushion the decline 
in vehicle production, limiting further downside to platinum demand.

Automotive platinum demand in Rest of World increased by 6% (+12 koz) year-on-year to 209 koz in Q3’26, with India accounting 
for the largest share of the growth. India bucked the broader trend, supported by GST-led improvements in vehicle affordability, 
resilient rural demand, stronger SUV sales and improving financing conditions, alongside pre-festive season stocking. Across the 
region, sustained growth in ICE and hybrid vehicle production, alongside increased heavy-duty FCEV output, supported platinum 
demand despite rising BEV penetration.

Jewellery demand

Global  platinum  jewellery  demand  fell  by  17%  during  the  quarter  with  three  of  the  major  markets  recording  lower  fabrication. 
Following a strong start to 2026, Swiss platinum watch hallmarking declined by 2% year-on-year and 10% quarter-on-quarter in 
Q3’26,  marking  a  second  consecutive  quarterly  decline.  Gold  watch  hallmarking,  meanwhile,  fell  by  10%  year-on-year  over  the 
same period. For the first nine months of 2026, platinum watch hallmarking was down 5% year-on-year, albeit against a record-high 
base in 2025. UK jewellery hallmarking in Q3’26 was also down by 20%, while German and Italian fabrication is estimated to have 
contracted by 6% and 10% respectively, driven chiefly by reducing fine weight demand due to higher prices. This left Europe as a 
whole down by 8% versus Q3’25. In North America, platinum jewellery fabrication is estimated to have grown by 3% year-on-year, a 
second consecutive quarter of growth, though slowing from the 5% growth in Q2’26, as platinum jewellery continues to outperform 
gold, despite the latter metal’s far larger market share.

Japanese jewellery fabrication fell by 25% in Q3. Higher prices were the main culprit – although the yen-denominated platinum 
price has been trending downwards since its January peak, its average over the period was roughly one-third higher year-on-year. 
Anecdotally, we understand that wedding and daily wear jewellery is benefitting from platinum’s discount to gold and that appetite 
for jewellery in the Japanese market in general remains healthy, with turnover in value terms holding up.

Weakness in Chinese platinum jewellery fabrication persisted in Q3’26, with volumes down 52% year-on-year and 13% quarter-on-
quarter, the weakest quarter in our series. Impaired consumer sentiment continued to weigh on China’s overall jewellery market. 
In  addition,  rising  competition  from  gold  jewellery  added  a  further  headwind.  Leading  retailers’  strong  product  innovation  and 
promotional efforts, along with more attractive gold prices, refuelled local consumers’ interest in gold jewellery. We continued to see 
manufacturers and showrooms shifting capital investment from platinum to gold jewellery, feeding a vicious cycle.

Industrial demand

Industrial  platinum  demand  increased  by  6%  year-on-year  and  7%  quarter-on-quarter  to  681  koz  in  Q3’26,  driven  by  stronger 
chemical demand, with additional support from electrical applications.

Petroleum

Platinum  petroleum  offtake  partially  recovered  by  5%  quarter-on-quarter  in  Q3’26,  although  demand  remained  28%  below  the 
previous year’s level. The modest quarterly rise reflected some improvement in refinery operations following the severe disruptions 
experienced in Q2, although the lingering effects of the US-Iran conflict continued to weigh on Middle Eastern crude supply and 
refining activity. Meanwhile, ongoing attacks on Russian refining infrastructure added further uncertainty to global output given this 
region’s  significant  share.  Elsewhere,  exceptionally  strong  refining  margins  encouraged  US  refiners  to  maintain  historically  high 
utilisation rates, with Energy Information Administration data showing that refinery runs between May and September reached their 
highest levels since 1998. While this supported refinery throughput, it also provided an incentive to defer non-essential maintenance 
and catalyst replacement activity, limiting the recovery in platinum demand.

While there was no material change in platinum demand from sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) production during Q3, several project 
developments during the quarter are expected to shape future demand. Notably, in August, SkyNRG marked the start of construction 
of  its  100,000t  SAF  facility  in  Delfzijl,  the  Netherlands,  with  production  expected  to  commence  in  2028.  Meanwhile,  some  SAF 
projects have faced cancellations or delays, particularly in the US, Brazil and Indonesia, though the broader project pipeline remains 
active, supporting further capacity expansion and potential platinum demand growth over the medium to long term.

Chemical

Platinum chemical demand recovered in Q3’26, rising by 15% year-on-year and 31% quarter-on-quarter to its highest quarterly level 
since Q1’24. The increase was driven by improving demand from the Chinese petrochemical industry. Following a period with no 
new capacity additions, Q3’26 saw progress on new paraxylene (PX) projects in mainland China. In August, a major petrochemical 
complex in northeastern China, incorporating 2 Mt of PX capacity, achieved mechanical completion across 32 processing units. The 
project received its first crude oil shipment in early October, bringing it closer to starting up. Silicone demand also strengthened, 
supported by growth in consumer, electronics and home care applications. By contrast, demand from the fertiliser industry declined 
year-on-year, reflecting ongoing supply disruptions associated with the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Medical

In Q3’26, platinum medical demand increased by 3% year-on-year to 83 koz. Medical device growth remained a key driver, while 
shortages of platinum-based chemotherapy drugs constrained oncology demand. In India, elevated platinum costs and government-
regulated drug prices prompted manufacturers to curtail cisplatin and carboplatin production, contributing to shortages that persisted 
into Q3’26 despite a 50% increase in regulated prices in June. In the US, carboplatin shortages reflected product discontinuations 
by some pharmaceutical manufacturers placing increased pressure on remaining manufacturers unable to deliver against growing 
demand. Separately, dental demand declined as non-metal restorations continued to gain market share.

Platinum glass demand increased by 5% year-on-year to 153 koz in Q3’26, although offtake moderated by 3% quarter-on-quarter.

Electrical

Electronics  demand  rose  by  33%  year-on-year  (+18  koz)  to  72  koz  in  Q3’26,  traditionally  a  peak  quarter  for  the  sector.  The 
largest  contributor  to  growth  was  demand  for  platinum  crucibles  used  in  crystal  growth,  driven  by  capacity  expansion  in  optical

HDDs  provided  a  further  boost  to  platinum  demand  through  greater  consumption  of  sputtering  targets.  While  AI  infrastructure 
investment continues to support underlying demand, platinum crucible purchases are concentrated around new capacity additions, 
making quarterly consumption sensitive to the timing of facility commissioning.

Hydrogen Stationary and Other

Hydrogen-related  platinum  demand  outside  road  transport  increased  by  15%  year-on-year  to  19  koz  in  Q3’26,  while  remaining 
broadly stable quarter-on-quarter. Growth continued at a more measured pace than initially anticipated, following particularly strong 
equipment deliveries and project commissioning in 2025. Nevertheless, commercial momentum remained evident, with Plug Power 
securing a 50 MW electrolyser order in Australia, while Thyssenkrupp Nucera reported stronger order intake. Chinese manufacturers 
also continued to expand internationally, including Sungrow’s 21 MW proton exchange membrane (PEM) electrolyser contract in 
Poland. Although project delays continued to constrain deployment, the steady level of activity suggests that underlying demand 
remains resilient, with new orders supporting future growth.

Other

Other industrial demand declined by 3% year-on-year to 139 koz (-4 koz) in Q3’26, primarily due to weaker automotive applications. 
Platinum consumption in spark plugs fell on the back of lower ICE vehicle production and subdued aftermarket replacement demand. 
Continued  substitution  towards  higher-performance  iridium-  and  ruthenium-based  electrodes  added  to  the  decline.  Aerospace 
applications provided a partial offset, with platinum demand rising across uses including turbine blades, fuel nozzles and ozone 
catalytic converters. Following further industry research, we have marginally lowered the platinum loading and penetration rates in 
oxygen sensors.

Investment demand

Global bar and coin demand more than halved year-on-year (-42 koz) during Q3’26, falling to just 40 koz, the lowest third quarter 
total in at least nine years. The decline was broad-based, led by continued disinvestment in Japan, alongside lower demand in China 
and the Rest of the World. 

Looking first at the US, retail demand is estimated to have fallen by 9% (-1 koz) to 13 koz. The decline may look modest, but this 
compares with a particularly weak Q3’25. In terms of this year, the losses reflect the overall weakness in North American bar and 
coin demand. That said, the losses were far more acute for gold and silver, with both markets having to contend with ongoing retail 
liquidations, a feature that was less prevalent for platinum.

In China, bar and coin investment fell by 27% year-on-year and edged slightly lower quarter-on-quarter to 29 koz. Overall investment 
sentiment toward precious metals was cautious in the quarter. Platinum’s continued high volatility and range-bound prices further 
weighed on investor sentiment. 

The lacklustre performance of the local platinum price put pressure on demand for bars and coins in the Japanese market. The 
market went into net disinvestment, however perhaps the more interesting development we saw in the country was the collapse in 
turnover – both sales and purchases. Volumes were a fraction of those seen late last year and in early 2026 and at a multi-year low.

2026 OUTLOOK

Developments during 2026 have materially altered the platinum market outlook. Substantial investor disinvestment, weaker jewellery 
fabrication and declining automotive consumption now point to the first annual surplus since 2022. ETF liquidations and exchange-
stock outflows contrast with substantial accumulations in 2025. 

The platinum market is forecast to record a surplus of 468 koz in 2026. Total supply is expected to increase by 2% to 7,366 koz, 
with mine production broadly stable and recycling rising by 6%. Total demand is forecast to decline sharply by 21% to 6,898 koz to a  
four-year  low,  driven  primarily  by  net  disinvestment  of  398  koz.  Jewellery  fabrication  is  projected  to  fall  by  20%  and  automotive 
demand by 3%, while industrial consumption increases by 9%.

Recycling

Platinum recycling is forecast to increase by 6% year-on-year to 1,764 koz in 2026. The increase is driven by a 13% recovery 
in autocatalyst recycling to 1,393 koz, while jewellery recycling is expected to decline by 21% to 282 koz, its lowest level in this 
series. Industrial recycling is forecast to rise by 9% to 89 koz, supported by increased recovery from electronic equipment and 
other industrial applications.

The recovery in autocatalyst recycling remains concentrated in North America and Western Europe. Higher PGM prices encouraged 
the  release  of  previously  stockpiled  material  during  H1’26,  while  improving  vehicle  replacement  activity  and  aftertreatment 
replacement  programmes  provided  additional  support  in  Europe.  However,  as  indicated  in  our  quarterly  review  above,  the 
recovery has lost momentum during the second half. In particular, weaker prices discouraged the release of material into the 
recycling pipeline. Recyclers also reported a deterioration in feedstock quality, including a growing proportion of silicon carbide 
substrates, complicating processing and affecting recovery yields. Consequently, the growth in recovered PGMs continues to lag 
the increase in physical catalyst volumes.

Jewellery recycling is forecast to decline by 21% year-on-year to 282 koz in 2026, almost entirely reflecting a 32% contraction in 
China to 158 koz. The exceptional supply-chain destocking recorded in 2025 is unlikely to be repeated, while subdued jewellery 
consumption continues to limit scrap generated through product exchanges and trade-ins. During the first nine months of 2026, 
jewellery scrap volumes in China fell by 26% year-on-year. Although we expect a modest improvement in Q4’26, the anticipated 
gains are unlikely to offset the impact of sluggish jewellery demand and weaker trade-in activity. Consequently, jewellery recycling 
is expected to remain broadly flat quarter-on-quarter in Q4’26. Industrial recycling is forecast to increase by 9% year-on-year to  
89 koz, with North America and Western Europe recording growth of 19% and 23%, respectively. Rising volumes of decommissioned 
servers and other data-bearing equipment are supporting electronic scrap availability as equipment installed during the 2020–22 
cloud expansion approaches replacement.

Demand

Global platinum demand is forecast to decline by 21% year-on-year to 6,898 koz in 2026, its lowest since 2022, primarily reflecting 
substantial ETF liquidations and declining exchange inventories. Jewellery and automotive demand are also expected to weaken, 
outweighing strong industrial growth, particularly in glass and electrical applications, alongside recovering chemical demand.

Industrial Demand

Automotive demand

GlobalData has revised its 2026f light-duty vehicle (LDV) production forecast upwards by approximately 1m units to 92.8m units, 
broadly flat year-on-year. The upgrade is broadly evenly split between catalysed vehicles and BEVs. Vehicle demand has proved 
more resilient than anticipated, except in China, despite elevated energy prices. Growth in India is supported by stronger domestic 
sales  and  expanding  manufacturing  capacity.  Increased  localisation  by  Chinese  OEMs  has  also  supported  production  upgrades, 
particularly in ASEAN countries and Latin America.

Global automotive platinum demand is nonetheless projected to decline by 3% year-on-year (-90 koz) to 2,964 koz this year. Global 
LDV production is expected to remain broadly flat in 2026, while heavy-duty and non-road vehicle output are expected to rise by 4% 
and 5% year-on-year, respectively. 

In Europe, platinum demand is forecast to decline by 6% year-on-year (-60 koz) to 900 koz, reflecting shrinking catalysed vehicle 
production. Although hybrid output continues to provide some support, rising competition from Chinese new energy vehicle (NEV) 
imports are constraining regional production.

In North America, platinum demand is expected to increase by 2% year-on-year (+11 koz) to 471 koz, supported by resilient catalysed 
production. Amid  elevated  and  volatile  fuel  prices,  consumers  increasingly  favour  hybrids  for  lower  running  costs. A  recovery  in 
predominantly diesel-powered HDV production, following a weak 2025, provides additional support to platinum demand.

Japan’s automotive platinum demand is projected to decline by 10% year-on-year (-30 koz) to 261 koz. Although demand for Japanese 
hybrids remains relatively strong, supported by domestic order backlogs and export markets, this is insufficient to offset declining 
pure-ICE volumes.

In  China,  platinum  demand  is  forecast  to  decline  by  8%  year-on-year  (-44  koz)  to  498  koz.  This  primarily  reflects  a  substantial 
contraction in pure-ICE LDV output amid electrification and weaker domestic vehicle demand. Expanding export markets continue to 
support NEV production.

Platinum demand in Rest of World is projected to increase by 4% year-on-year (+34 koz) to 835 koz. India is the primary driver, 
with resilient domestic demand and expanding manufacturing capacity sustaining diesel LDVs and HDVs production. There is also 
emerging  heavy-duty  FCEV  production,  particularly  in  South  Korea.  Growth  in  Brazil,  Russia  and  parts  of  the ASEAN  region  is 
underpinned by localisation by Chinese manufacturers. The relocation of ICE vehicle output by European OEMs to Morocco and 
stronger output from South Africa also support growth. Affordability constraints for BEVs and uneven charging infrastructure continue 
to sustain ICE and hybrid production across emerging markets, thus supporting platinum demand.

Industrial demand

Industrial platinum demand is forecast to rise by 9% year-on-year to 2,561 koz in 2026, reaching historically high levels. Strong 
growth in glass and electronic applications are the main growth drivers for this year.

Glass

Platinum glass demand is forecast to increase by 30% year-on-year to 557 koz in 2026, primarily driven by renewed investment 
in  fibreglass  manufacturing  capacity.  China  remains  the  principal  source  of  growth,  with  expansions  spanning  conventional 
reinforcement glass and higher-specification electronic fibres used in printed circuit boards (PCBs) for AI servers and datacentre 
infrastructure. However, capacity additions in China are increasingly being offset by rationalisation across Europe and North America, 
as higher-cost producers struggle to compete. Meanwhile, liquid crystal display (LCD) demand is expected to recover modestly, 
although investment remains below the levels recorded during China’s previous expansion cycle.

Petroleum

Although Middle Eastern crude oil exports have recovered significantly from the lows seen in Q2, regional refining activity continues 
to  lag  behind  the  improvement  in  oil  flows,  reflecting  ongoing  refinery  outages,  infrastructure  damage  and  logistical  constraints. 
Combined with fewer expected catalyst changeouts at gas-to-liquids (GTL) plants, platinum petroleum offtake is forecast to decline 
by 28% in 2026. As a full recovery in regional refinery throughput is unlikely before 2027, disruptions to maintenance schedules and 
catalyst replacement activity are expected to persist. Meanwhile, Russia’s refining sector is likely to remain under significant pressure 
from continued Ukrainian attacks, further constraining refinery operations. Elsewhere, strong refining margins have encouraged high 
utilisation rates, particularly in the US, potentially delaying non-essential maintenance and catalyst changeouts.

Chemical

Platinum chemical offtake is on track to rise by 9% in 2026 to 613 koz, reversing two consecutive years of declines. A rebound in the 
petrochemical industry remains the key driver, with several new PX and PDH projects expected to come on stream in H2’26 (and 
2027). While China accounts for the bulk of these gains, new projects in India and Belgium will also contribute. Meanwhile, silicone 
demand is also expected to strengthen this year. However, these gains will be partly offset by weaker demand from the fertiliser 
sector, reflecting conflict-related disruptions to operations and catalyst maintenance schedules.

Medical

In  2026,  platinum  medical  demand  is  expected  to  increase  by  3%  year-on-year  (+10  koz)  to  331  koz.  Medical  device  demand 
will remain supported by growth in cardiovascular and electrophysiology procedures and continued product innovation. Oncology 
demand  should  also  improve  as  cisplatin  and  carboplatin  availability  recovers,  although  increasing  use  of  targeted  therapies, 
antibody-drug conjugates and immunotherapies, all of which are platinum-free, will constrain growth.

Electrical

Platinum demand in the electronics sector is forecast to rise by 35% to 280 koz in 2026, led by optical communications capacity expansion 
linked to AI infrastructure investment. New co-packaged optics (CPO), which integrate optical connections alongside semiconductor 
chips, and high-speed optical module projects are driving investment in optical crystal production, lifting platinum crucible purchases 
ahead of commissioning. Several lines scheduled to come on stream during the year are expected to make these purchases the largest 
contributor to the annual increase.

Higher HDD output and a rising share of mass-capacity drives are also boosting platinum sputtering target consumption. Strong cloud 
storage  requirements  and  longer-term  supply  agreements  are  improving  production  visibility,  despite  competition  from  SSDs  and 
constraints in magnetic head and substrate supply. Meanwhile, increasing semiconductor capacity and wafer output are underpinning 
demand for high-purity platinum and platinum alloy sputtering targets.

Hydrogen Stationary and Other 

Platinum demand from hydrogen applications is forecast to increase by 8% year-on-year to 77 koz in 2026, following particularly 
strong growth in 2025, when demand surged by 78%. The more moderate increase was largely anticipated following substantial 
equipment deliveries and project commissioning in 2025, with the transition to new developments constraining near-term growth.

Nevertheless, activity continues to broaden across several applications and regions. In North America, growing demand for reliable 
power  from  datacentres  supports  interest  in  stationary  fuel  cells,  particularly  in  California,  while  South  Korea  has  continued  to 
expand its fuel cell generation capacity. Europe has also seen early trials of hydrogen-powered backup systems for datacentres. 
Meanwhile, electrolyser investment continued to progress, with major projects advancing in Europe and Australia, alongside growing 
international  orders  for  Chinese  manufacturers.  However,  project  delays,  uncertain  economics  and  uneven  order  intake  have 
continued to weigh on deployment, resulting in the more measured pace in 2026.

Other  industrial  demand  is  forecast  to  decline  by  2%  (-12  koz)  to  571  koz  in  2026,  as  structural  pressures  on  platinum  use  in 
spark plugs outweigh gains in sensor applications. Lower ICE vehicle production is reducing OEM requirements, while subdued 
aftermarket  replacement  activity  and  continued  substitution  towards  iridium-  and  ruthenium-based  electrodes  are  adding  to  the 
weakness. Growth in hybrid vehicle production and tighter emissions standards are expected to increase platinum consumption in 
sensors, while aerospace demand remains steady. Together, these applications are unlikely to offset the reduction in spark plug 
consumption. 

Investment demand

This year, bar and coin demand is forecast to drop by 28% (-112 koz) to 290 koz. The losses will be driven by China, but it is equally 
important to remember that 2025 saw an unprecedented high for that country. To reflect the worse-than-expected Chinese demand 
in Q3, we have revised our forecast for China in 2026 down by 41% year-on-year compared to 38% in the last edition. 

On the assumption that the year-end rally that Metals Focus’ projections call for materialises, we should see quite a dramatic pick-
up in platinum bar purchases by Japanese investors. This could offset much of the net selling seen in Q2 and Q3, leaving the total 
for the year at 23 koz.

Turning to North America, we forecast a 9% drop to the lowest total since 2010. As touched on above, in the quarterly review, the 
full-year total has been revised markedly lower compared with the previous Platinum Quarterly. This reflects a far weaker Q2’26 
than expected, across the precious metals complex, which spilled over into Q3. Nevertheless, we do expect to see a partial recovery 
before year-end, reflecting high prices and the uncertainty brought about by the US’ midterm elections outcome. 

With platinum prices currently under pressure, a lack of investor conviction has kept European retail investment subdued so far in 
Q4, a trend that is expected to persist through the remainder of the year. Combined with weak demand over the first nine months, 
this is forecast to bring full-year investment down by 29% year-on-year to a three-year low of 24 koz.

Turning to India, for the full year, we expect platinum investment demand to fall by 49% year-on-year as fear of missing out (FOMO)-
driven buying of precious metals slows. Nevertheless, demand would still be the second highest in our series, driven by the strong 
performance of retail investment in the early part of the year. 

Platinum exchange-traded fund (ETF) holdings are forecast to decline further by approximately 490 koz in 2026, with North America 
and  South  Africa  accounting  for  most  liquidations.  In  North  America,  investment  accumulated  during  2025  amid  tariff-related 
uncertainty has progressively unwound, with higher prices encouraging profit-taking. South African holdings have also contracted 
sharply, although the remaining investor base is increasingly concentrated among longer-term institutional holders, potentially limiting 
further selling. European holdings are expected to stabilise following earlier liquidations, suggesting that much of the adjustment to 
higher prices and trade uncertainty has already occurred. Elsewhere, flows are expected to remain comparatively modest.

ABOVE GROUND STOCKS

The forecast market surplus of 468 koz in 2026 is expected to lift above ground stocks to 2,092 koz, equivalent to approximately 
three and a half months of demand cover. The WPIC definition of above ground stocks is the year-end estimate of the cumulative 
platinum holdings not associated with exchange-traded funds, metal held in exchange-approved vaults or working inventories of 
mining producers, refiners, fabricators or end-users.

Supply 

Mine supply is expected to decline by 1% to 5,571 koz in 2027, with lower South African output partly offset by growth in Zimbabwe. 
South  African  production  is  forecast  to  fall  by  1%,  despite  incremental  contributions  from  Northam  and  Ivanhoe.  Zimbabwean 
output is expected to increase by 6%, supported by the drawdown of semi-finished inventory at Zimplats, while Russian production 
remains broadly stable. North American supply is projected to edge higher by 1%. Sibanye-Stillwater is targeting growth from its US 
operations, although our forecast assumes a more gradual recovery in its operating performance.

Global recycling is forecast to rise by 5% to 1,847 koz. Autocatalyst recycling is expected to increase by 5% to 1,465 koz, supported 
by  higher  PGM  prices  encouraging  scrap  flows.  Jewellery  recycling  is  projected  to  recover  by  1%  to  285  koz,  as  higher  prices 
encourage scrap sales despite subdued jewellery demand and wide buy-sell spreads. Industrial recycling is forecast to grow by 9% 
to 96 koz, supported by expanding electronics recycling activity as investment in processing capacity and datacentre equipment 
replacement is expected to continue.

Demand

Total platinum demand is forecast to increase by 12% year-on-year to 7,696 koz in 2027, primarily driven by a recovery in investment 
demand and further growth in industrial consumption. This is expected to more than offset continued weakness in automotive and 
jewellery demand.

PLATINUM QUARTERLY Q3 2026Automotive platinum demand is projected to slip by 5% year-on-year (-151 koz) to 2,813 koz in 2027. Western Europe is forecast to 
record the largest regional decline of 11%, followed by Japan and China, with declines of 8% and 6%, respectively. LDV production 
is expected to remain broadly stable, while HDV and NRV production increase by 3% and 2%, respectively. BEV penetration is 
expected to weigh on catalysed vehicle production, particularly in the US, Europe, Japan and China, where declining ICE production 
weighs on platinum requirements. Despite a noticeable pullback in EV incentives in the US in 2026, regional purchase tax incentives 
elsewhere and the growing availability of affordable Chinese models through exports and localisation will maintain growth in EV 
share.  That  said,  rising  trade  barriers,  localisation  requirements  and  persistent  charging  infrastructure  constraints  in  emerging 
markets will curtail BEV adoption.

In  contrast,  resilient  ICE  and  hybrid  production  in  emerging  markets,  particularly  India,  should  support  platinum  demand.  The 
recovery in US vehicle production is also expected to provide some support, although the extent will depend on the powertrain mix. 
Growth in FCEV output, although from a low base, is expected to support platinum demand. Slower hybrid growth, particularly in 
Europe and Japan, will weigh on platinum consumption. The predominantly gasoline-hybrid production mix and rising palladium-for-
platinum substitution in catalyst formulations further compound the impact of declining diesel vehicles.

Platinum jewellery fabrication is expected to decline by a further 3% to 1,710 koz in 2027, as elevated prices and subdued consumer 
spending continue to weigh on demand. Chinese fabrication is forecast to fall by 5%, as economic uncertainty is expected to persist 
and consumer preference for gold jewellery prevails. North American demand is also projected to decline by 5%, as higher platinum 
prices increasingly offset the benefits of its price discount to gold. European fabrication should remain broadly stable, supported 
by relatively healthy demand for bridal and high-end jewellery. Japanese fabrication is forecast to decline by a further 5%. Despite 
this, jewellery sales are expected to remain relatively resilient in value terms, while further gains in gold prices should provide some 
support  to  platinum  jewellery  demand.  Indian  demand  is  expected  to  be  little  changed  as  domestic  retail  growth  offsets  weaker 
exports. Organised retail expansion and growing demand for lightweight jewellery should support domestic consumption, although 
elevated manufacturing costs and a narrowing price advantage over gold will constrain growth.

Industrial demand is forecast to increase by 4% to 2,660 koz, supported by a recovery in chemical and petroleum applications. 
Chemical  demand  is  projected  to  jump  by  29%  (+179  koz)  to  792  koz,  accounting  for  the  largest  increase  across  industrial 
applications, driven by new petrochemical capacity, silicone production and a recovery in fertiliser-related activity. China is expected 
to account for much of the petrochemical capacity expansion, while a gradual normalisation of Middle Eastern operations should 
support fertiliser-related demand.

Petroleum  demand  is  expected  to  rebound  by  30%  (+39  koz)  to  171  koz,  as  deferred  maintenance  and  catalyst  replacement 
resume, particularly in the Middle East. Much of this increase represents demand postponed from 2026 rather than new refining 
capacity, with the pace of recovery dependent on the restoration of damaged infrastructure and refinery operations. Conversely, 
glass  demand  is  forecast  to  decline  by  11%  (-60  koz)  to  497  koz,  due  to  fewer  new  fibreglass  capacity  additions,  although 
investment  in  higher-value  electronic  applications  remains  supportive.  Continued  production  rationalisation  in  Europe  and  North 
America is also expected to increase China’s share of global fibreglass manufacturing. Electrical demand is projected to fall by 35%  
(-97 koz) to 183 koz, as we do not expect a repeat of platinum crucible installations in 2027 following strong optical crystal capacity 
expansions in 2026. Underlying semiconductor and storage applications should continue to grow, with consumption outside crucible 
applications expected to increase at around a double-digit rate. Medical demand is expected to rise by 3% to 342 koz, driven by 
growth  in  cardiovascular  and  electrophysiology  procedures  and  a  recovery  in  platinum-based  oncology  applications.  Hydrogen-
related platinum demand outside road transport is forecast to increase by 49% year-on-year (+38 koz) to 115 koz in 2027, driven 
primarily by stronger demand from PEM electrolysers as projects progress into equipment delivery and commissioning. Following a 
more measured pace of deployment in 2026, improving order intake among PEM electrolyser manufacturers and the advancement 
of previously committed projects are expected to support renewed growth. Stationary fuel cell demand is also projected to increase, 
supported by expanding applications in distributed generation, datacentres and backup power, particularly in North America and 
Asia. Other industrial demand is expected to decline by 2%, primarily due to lower platinum consumption in spark plugs as ICE 
production contracts and substitution towards alternative metals continues.

Investment demand is forecast to recover to 513 koz in 2027, compared with net disinvestment of 398 koz in 2026. Global bar and 
coin demand is expected to surge by 55% to 448 koz, a seven-year high. This reflects gains in every key market, each benefiting 
from expectations of higher platinum prices. The outcome is all the more impressive given that Chinese demand will still fall well 
short of 2025’s record high.

PLATINUM QUARTERLY Q3 2026North American bar and coin demand is forecast to jump by 52% to 131 koz, a four-year high. The increase may look dramatic but 
follows this year’s subdued performance. Even so, the gains reflect both price upside and the impact of far lower financing costs 
for the entire supply chain. The five-fold increase in Japanese physical investment demand projected for 2027 may also appear 
sizeable. However, this reflects expectations for strong price performance throughout the year and the nature of Japan’s very active 
two-way market, which can experience significant swings. At 120 koz, the absolute figure forecast for 2027 remains a fraction of 
the 2015-2016 peaks in net investment demand. In China, the positive price outlook is expected to support a 5% recovery in bar 
and coin demand, taking it to the second-highest level in the series. Turning to large bars in China, which are reported separately, 
our price outlook should incentivise further growth, with demand forecast to increase by 2%. Bar and coin demand is expected to 
recover in Europe but moderate in markets such as India and Australia as the exceptionally strong buying interest seen in 2025 and 
2026 begins to fade. 

ETF  holdings  are  forecast  to  decline  by  a  further  150  koz  in  2027,  despite  expectations  of  higher  platinum  prices.  Following 
substantial liquidations in 2026, further profit-taking and a rotation towards PGM mining equities are expected to sustain outflows. 
Stronger  producer  earnings  and  substantially  higher  dividend  distributions  have  improved  the  relative  attractiveness  of  mining 
equities, offering investors both income and potential share-price appreciation. However, the growing concentration of longer-term 
institutional ETF holdings is expected to limit the scale of further liquidations.

Exchange warehouse stocks are forecast to increase by 110 koz in 2027, on the back of further inventory accumulation at GFEX. 
As  the  exchange  matures,  greater  participation  by  industrial  consumers  and  increased  use  of  its  physical  delivery  facilities  are 
expected to support warehouse stock growth. In contrast, NYMEX inventories are expected to remain broadly stable as tariff-related 
uncertainty continues to ease.

ABOVE GROUND STOCKS

The return to a market deficit in 2027 is expected to partially reverse the previous year’s inventory build, with above ground stocks 
declining to 1,813 koz by year-end and demand cover declining to just under three months’ supply. The WPIC definition of above 
ground stocks is the year-end estimate of the cumulative platinum holdings not associated with exchange-traded funds, metal held 
in exchange-approved vaults or working inventories of mining producers, refiners, fabricators or end-users.
