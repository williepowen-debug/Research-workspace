London, 06.00 18 November 2026  

Platinum market to return to deficit in 2027 with a 279 koz 
shortfall, as investment demand rebounds and industrial 
growth continues 

Trevor Raymond, CEO of the World Platinum Investment Council, comments: 

“Our first forecast for 2027 indicates a return to a market deficit. This is largely driven by investment 
demand, supported by strong bar and coin growth, on both expectations of higher prices and increased 
product availability. In addition, we are expecting a third consecutive year of industrial demand growth, 
highlighting  why  this  segment  remains  the  dominant  driver  of  overall  platinum  long-term  demand 
growth.  

“Platinum  above ground stocks remain critically depleted with the forecast surplus in 2026 following 
three consecutive years of significant deficit. Platinum’s strategic importance continues to accelerate, 
driving a new phase of demand growth and underpinning platinum’s investment case – most recently 
evidenced by its use in AI-related applications and its inclusion on critical minerals lists across major 
economies.”

The World Platinum Investment Council - WPIC® - today publishes its Platinum Quarterly for the 
third  quarter  of  2026  with  an  updated  full  year  2026  forecast  and  a  first  forecast  for  full  year 
2027.

In Q3’26, the platinum market recorded a deficit of 110 koz. Total supply declined by 6% year-on-year 
to 1,693 koz, reflecting lower South African mine production, partly offset by higher recycling volumes. 
Total demand fell by 17% year-on-year to 1,803 koz, driven by weaker automotive and jewellery demand 
and  substantially  lower  net  investment  demand.  Industrial  demand,  however,  increased  by  6%, 
supported by growth in chemical, electrical, glass and hydrogen applications. 

For full year 2026, the platinum market surplus is forecast to widen from our previous outlook to 468 
koz (+203 koz) due mainly to investment demand which has been downgraded by 315 koz since Q2’26. 
Total supply is projected to increase 2% (+135 koz) year-on-year to 7,366 koz. Total demand is forecast 
to  fall  21%  year-on-year  (-1,879  koz)  to  6,898  koz,  principally  due  to  exchange  traded  fund  (ETF) 
liquidations and exchange stock outflows. 

The impact of the 2026 surplus on above ground stocks (AGS) is mitigated by a prior-year adjustment, 
which has resulted in the estimated 2025 deficit deepening to 1,547 koz. As a result, in 2026, AGS are 
forecast to increase to 2,092 koz by year end, providing just over 3½ months of global demand cover. 

The initial full year 2027 forecast is for total supply to remain broadly stable year-on-year at 7,418 koz 
(+1%,  +52  koz).  Total  demand  growth  is  set  to  rise  12%  to  7,696  koz  (+798  koz)  on  the  back  of  a 
rebound  in  investment  demand,  which  is  expected  to  reach  513  koz,  primarily  due  to  bar  and  coin 
demand growth of 55% (+158 koz) to 448 koz. Record industrial demand of 2,660 koz (+4%, +99 koz) 
will partially offset weaker automotive demand (-5%, -151 koz) and jewellery demand (-3%, -61 koz). In 
2027, AGS are forecast to reduce to 1,813 koz by year end, providing just under 3 months of global 
demand cover. 

Meagre total supply growth of 1% facilitated by recycling supply in 2027 
In 2026, global platinum mine supply is forecast to increase 1% year-on-year (+41 koz) to 5,602 koz, 
as  gains  from  South  Africa  more  than  offset  declines  elsewhere.  Recycling  supply  is  expected  to 
increase  by  6%  (+95  koz)  year-on-year  to  1,764  koz.  The  increase  is  driven  by  a  13%  recovery  in 
autocatalyst recycling to 1,393 koz, while jewellery recycling is expected to decline by 21% to 282 koz, 
its lowest level in this series. Industrial recycling is forecast to rise by 9% to 89 koz. 

In 2027, global mine supply is forecast to be 1% lower year-on-year (-31 koz) at 5,571 koz, with lower 
South African output partly offset by growth in Zimbabwe. Recycling supply is forecast to rise by 5% 
(+83 koz) to 1,847 koz, largely on the assumption of higher platinum group metal prices. Autocatalyst 
recycling is expected to increase by 5% to 1,465 koz, while jewellery recycling is projected to be almost 
flat,  rising  1%  to  285  koz.  Industrial  recycling  is  forecast  to  grow  by  9%  to  96  koz,  supported  by 
expanding electronics recycling activity as investment in AI-related processing capacity and data centre 
equipment upgrades is expected to continue. 

Automotive demand remains resilient despite higher oil prices 
Automotive demand is projected to decline by 3% year-on-year (-90 koz) to 2,964 koz this year.  

In 2027, automotive demand is expected to reduce by 5% (-151 koz) to 2,813 koz. While the advent 
of new emissions legislation and hybridisation trends continue to provide support for platinum 
automotive demand, erosion continues due to ongoing drivetrain electrification, with some substitution 
from platinum to palladium also now in evidence.  

Price and subdued consumer spending continue to weigh on platinum jewellery demand  
In 2026, jewellery demand is forecast to contract by 20% year-on-year (-443 koz) to 1,771 koz. The 
contraction  is  concentrated  in  China,  where  the  reversal  of  last  year’s  strong  inventory  build,  weak 
consumer demand and a renewed trade preference for gold are driving a sharp fall in fabrication. All 
other markets also experienced weakness, with the exception of North America. 

The 2027 forecast anticipates a further decline in jewellery demand of 3% (-61 koz) to 1,710 koz, with 
platinum jewellery fabrication reducing in China, North America and Japan. Fabrication in Europe and 
India is expected to be broadly flat year-on-year.

Record industrial demand forecast for 2027 
In full year 2026, a 9% (+203 koz) year-on-year increase in industrial demand to 2,561 koz is forecast 
as AI applications provide a significant boost to both glass (+30%) and electrical (+35%) demand.  

In 2027, industrial demand is forecast to reach a record 2,660 koz (+4%, +99 koz). While the absence 
of capacity additions will see a cyclical reduction in glass demand (-11%) and electrical demand (-35%), 
this will be more than offset by strong chemical demand growth (+29%) and a recovery in petroleum 
demand (+30%). Hydrogen-related platinum demand is forecast to increase by 49% year-on-year to 
115  koz,  driven  primarily  by  stronger  demand  from  PEM  electrolysers  as  projects  progress  into 
equipment delivery and commissioning.   

Platinum investment demand to make a strong recovery in 2027 
The full year 2026 forecast is for net disinvestment of 398 koz. The geopolitical and macroeconomic 
landscape has hampered platinum and, at times, the broader precious metals complex during 2026, 
and  markets  have  experienced  price  volatility  amid  uncertainty  over  the  direction  of  interest  rates, 
among other considerations. This has impacted the 2026 investment demand forecast, with 490 koz of 
ETF liquidations and 300 koz in outflows from exchange stocks now expected. In addition, a significant 
year-on-year drop in bar and coin demand to 290 koz (-28%, -112 koz) is anticipated.  

Looking to 2027, the forecast for investment demand indicates net investment of 513 koz, predicated 
on bar and coin demand reaching 448 koz (+55%, +158 koz). Conversely, ETF holdings are forecast to 
decline by a further 150 koz in 2027, despite market expectations of higher platinum prices. Further 
profit-taking and a rotation towards PGM mining equities are expected to sustain outflows. 

Trevor Raymond, CEO of the World Platinum Investment Council, adds: 

“Looking  beyond  the  near-term  investment  cycle,  platinum  market  fundamentals  remain  strong, 
characterised by constrained supply growth, historically low above ground inventories and a return to 
deficit forecast for 2027 and beyond. Taken together, these factors point to tight market conditions that 
continue to reinforce platinum's long-term investment appeal.”

About Platinum  
Platinum is one of the rarest metals in the world with distinctive qualities making it highly valued across 
a  number  of  diverse  demand  segments.  Platinum’s  unique  physical  and  catalytic  properties  have 
established its value in industrial applications. At the same time, its unique precious metal attributes 
have made it the premier jewellery metal.  

Platinum’s supply comes from two main sources: primary mining output and recycling, which typically 
comes from end-of-life autocatalysts and jewellery recycling. Over the last five years, between 75% and 
79% of total annual platinum supply (in refined ounces) has come from primary mining output.  

Global demand for platinum is robust and becoming more diverse. There are four core segments of 
platinum  demand:  automotive,  industrial,  jewellery  and  investment  demand.  Platinum  demand  from 
autocatalysts has equated to between 36% and 44% of total demand in the last five years. Platinum’s 
diverse non-automotive industrial uses account on average for 30% of total global demand (five-year 
average). Over the same period, global annual jewellery demand has averaged 27% of total platinum 
demand. Investment demand is the most variable category over the past five years, ranging between -
8% and 14% of total demand (excluding movements in unpublished vaulted investor holdings).
