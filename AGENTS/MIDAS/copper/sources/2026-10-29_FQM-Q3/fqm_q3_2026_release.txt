Toronto, Ontario (October 28, 2026)
– First Quantum Minerals Ltd. (“First Quantum” or the “Company”) (TSX: FM) today reports results for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 (“Q3 2026” or the “third quarter”).
“We are pleased with the significant improvement in our financial performance this quarter, supported by higher production and full exposure to stronger copper prices, which clearly demonstrates the underlying earnings potential of our business. We delivered another quarter of production growth, supported by continued strong performance from S3 at Kansanshi and the processing of stockpiles at Cobre Panamá, where the recommissioning progressed successfully, with all three trains brought online sequentially during the quarter,” said Tristan Pascall, Chief Executive Officer of First Quantum. “In Panama, we are encouraged by the recommendation made by the Interministerial Commission and remain ready to engage constructively with the Government of Panama to achieve a fair and durable resolution that serves the best interests of the country and its people, as well as our stakeholders.”
Q3 2026 HIGHLIGHTS
Financial results for the third quarter include gross profit of $678 million (Q2 2026: $297 million), EBITDA
[1]
of $844 million (Q2 2026: $400 million), net earnings attributable to shareholders of $0.25 per share (Q2 2026: $0.16), and an adjusted earnings per share
1
of $0.29 (Q2 2026: $(0.13)). Relative to the second quarter of 2026, third quarter results benefitted from higher sales volumes, improved realized metals prices
1
, lower fuel costs and the absence of derivative contracts following the completion of the hedging program on June 30, 2026.
Total copper production was 108,959 tonnes (Q2 2026: 100,487 tonnes) at C1 cash costs
1
of $2.59 per lb (Q2 2026: $2.54). Copper sales totalled 109,841 tonnes. During the quarter, 47,095 ounces of gold (Q2 2026: 32,029 ounces) and 9,831 tonnes of nickel (Q2 2026: 11,246 tonnes) were produced.
Copper production guidance has been narrowed from a range of 405,000 – 475,000 tonnes to 405,000 – 435,000 tonnes to reflect lower grades experienced to date at Sentinel and Kansanshi while guidance for Cobre Panamá has been maintained. Gold production guidance has been narrowed from a range of 150,000 – 175,000 ounces to 150,000 – 170,000 ounces. Nickel production guidance has increased from a range of 30,000 – 40,000 tonnes to 40,000 – 45,000 tonnes.
Previously provided sensitivity of $0.25 per lb has been incorporated into updated copper C1 cash cost
[10]
guidance of $2.50 – $2.65 per lb to reflect elevated fuel prices and the strong Zambian kwacha to date, which are expected to continue in the fourth quarter. Guidance for total capital expenditure has been narrowed based on spend to date and the latest expected timing of capital expenditures.
In Panama, the Interministerial Commission delivered its final report, recommending an assessment of the feasibility of a new agreement that could address outstanding arbitrations and provide for a potential restart of Cobre Panamá to support an orderly, self-financed closure, recognizing that closure and rehabilitation will be a long-term undertaking extending over decades. The President is reviewing the report and will communicate a decision directly to the country.
The authorized stockpile processing program (“Processing Program”) at Cobre Panamá advanced materially during the quarter. Following the initial start-up of Train 3, Train 2 commenced operations in July. Train 1 progressed through refurbishment, testing, and commissioning, which was completed without major issues. There are sufficient stockpiles to support the Processing Program until mid-2027.
With respect to the environmental findings from the Comprehensive Audit, environmental actions were incorporated into the compliance tracking process at Cobre Panamá, supported by improved evidence management, traceability, and georeferencing.

COBRE PANAMÁ UPDATE
On September 30, 2026, the Interministerial Commission formally delivered its final report on Cobre Panamá to President José Raúl Mulino (the “President”), marking the Government of Panama’s (“GOP”) transition from technical assessment to the decision-making phase. The report sets out 17 recommendations and six guiding criteria for consideration in determining the future of the mine. The Interministerial Commission recommended assessing the feasibility of entering into a new agreement with Cobre Panamá / First Quantum that would provide for the settlement of the outstanding arbitration proceedings and establish conditions for a potential restart designed to achieve an orderly, self-financed closure, while complying with the ruling of the Supreme Court of Justice and prioritizing Panama’s environmental, social, and economic interests. The report recognized that the site continues to require active management and monitoring, which is currently being funded by Cobre Panamá / First Quantum at no cost to the Panamanian State, and that maintaining and ultimately closing the site represents significant long-term obligations extending over decades. It recommends that any new framework provide for the site’s activities to fund its own closure, with no appropriation from the General State Budget for either the operation or closure of the mine. The report further documents the economic and social impacts of the shutdown, including effects on employment, Panamanian businesses, exports, and government revenues, and cites public-opinion findings indicating support for a resolution under conditions that include environmental and social safeguards, transparency, and independent oversight. The President is reviewing the report with the Cabinet and legal advisers and will communicate a decision directly to the country. The Company remains ready to engage constructively with the GOP with the objective of achieving a fair and durable resolution for all stakeholders of Cobre Panamá.
Follow-up to the SGS Comprehensive Audit progressed from assessment into corrective-action implementation. Environmental actions were incorporated into the compliance tracking process, with field work focused on reforestation maintenance, compensatory reforestation verification, and protected-flora translocation, supported by improved evidence management, traceability, and georeferencing. Environmental stewardship remains a core priority. All processing activities will continue to be conducted in accordance with the approved Preservation and Safe Management plan, applicable permits, and environmental commitments, while maintaining close coordination with the GOP.
Engagement with the municipalities surrounding the project also advanced during the quarter. The Company is supporting the municipal authorities of Donoso, Omar Torrijos Herrera, and La Pintada to identify and structure priority local development initiatives. These include coordination with relevant Government institutions to explore pathways for projects to be incorporated into formal public investment and municipal development mechanisms. This work is intended to create a more sustainable framework for addressing local development priorities.
Additionally, the Participatory Environmental Monitoring Program was launched, which directly involves communities surrounding the project in water-quality monitoring, from sample collection through to access to laboratory results, strengthening transparency and community participation in environmental oversight.
The Company also graduated the first participants from its community technical training program, with more than 125 people, particularly from coastal communities, completing 100 hours of training in trades including plumbing and electrical work.
Progress continues on the “Suma tu Talento” recruitment initiative to support the hiring of staff for preservation activities. Cobre Panamá participated in a major employment fair organized by the Municipality of Panama, receiving 426 in-person candidate profiles, approximately 64% of which were for operational, technical, and supervisory trades.
Public outreach efforts also continued in the third quarter through Cobre Conecta and other direct-engagement platforms, generating approximately 447,000 in-person stakeholder engagements across Panama since 2024. Digital engagement also expanded, with the Cobre Panamá Virtual Tour surpassing 405,000 visits.
On October 9, Panama experienced an earthquake and subsequent aftershocks. All First Quantum personnel in the country were reported safe and Cobre Panamá’s site inspections have identified no material impacts to site infrastructure or environmental protection systems. Following the earthquake, safety protocols were immediately activated and checks were carried out across the operation. Inspections indicated that facilities and infrastructure at Cobre Panamá remained stable and secure, including the site’s tailings management facilities and water management systems. The site’s infrastructure incorporates design criteria for high-intensity seismicity and this event remained well below design parameters. The processing of stockpiled ore was temporarily suspended following the event while precautionary checks were completed, and operations were returned to service and re-stabilized on October 10. Power generation systems remained operational throughout the event. There was no impact to the Company’s environmental protection and monitoring systems from the event. The earthquake impacted many communities across Panama, particularly in the Penonomé area, and Cobre Panamá was involved in supporting relief efforts, in coordination with local authorities and emergency services, for the provision of essential supplies.

GUIDANCE
Guidance provided below is based on a number of assumptions and estimates as of September 30, 2026, including among other things, assumptions about metal prices and anticipated costs and expenditures. Guidance involves estimates of known and unknown risks, uncertainties, and other factors, which may cause the actual results to be materially different.

Gold production guidance has been narrowed to reflect lower grades at Kansanshi. Gold production guidance for Guelb Moghrein has been increased to reflect the transition to production of a gold-copper concentrate from August 2026. Guidance for Cobre Panamá has been maintained.

guidance to reflect elevated fuel prices and the strong Zambian kwacha to date, which are expected to continue in the fourth quarter. UPDATES
Fuel Supply and Prices
Diesel supply conditions improved during the third quarter with stronger supply into all major regional ports as traders and oil marketing companies continued to bring diesel cargos into the region, incentivized by higher regional prices and sustained demand. Governments also took measures to moderate delivered prices through reductions in taxes and VAT, although this fiscal support is expected to be withdrawn over the coming months. The Company maintained uninterrupted fuel supply to its Zambian operations through alternative southern African supply routes, contingency inventories, and other supply arrangements. Diesel prices nevertheless remained elevated and impacted operating costs during the third quarter.
Sulphuric Acid
During the third quarter, the Company continued to manage feed blends and acid inventories to make surplus volumes available for third-party sales where operational requirements permitted, supported by strong regional demand and favourable sulphuric acid pricing. The higher gangue acid consuming oxide ore in the plant feed and acid stocks have been actively managed to enable the sale of acid during the third quarter, providing additional opportunistic profit generation and allowing Kansanshi to realize $33 million from acid sales. The Company will continue to monitor the market and opportunistically sell when profitable.
Zambian Power Supply
During the third quarter of 2026, Zambia’s national power supply remained in a materially improved position, although the force majeure declared by ZESCO, the state power utility, in early 2024 remained in effect. Lake Kariba ended the quarter at approximately 39% usable storage, compared with approximately 15% at the same time in 2025, following seasonal drawdown from the levels reached at the end of the wet season. The 2026/2027 rainy season is expected to be influenced by a very strong El Niño, increasing uncertainty around the pace of further hydrological recovery.
The Company experienced no material power-related production impact during the quarter and maintained its diversified power-sourcing strategy. Approximately 80% of the Company’s Zambian power requirements was sourced from imports and domestic independent power producers, with the balance supplied by ZESCO. This diversified approach supports operational reliability and limits reliance on Kariba-based generation.
Progress continued on medium- and long-term power sourcing solutions. Development of the previously announced wind and solar power project, from which the Company intends to offtake power, remains on track. Joint grid-stability initiatives with the state power utility also advanced, including the Kansanshi STATCOM project, for which procurement and factory acceptance testing were completed during the quarter. Site installation is scheduled to commence in early 2027.
The Company’s Trident mining operation is finalizing a power offtake agreement with Solarcentury Africa for the proposed 112 Megawatt-peak (“MWp”) Kusoka Solar Plant. The plant will be owned and operated by Solarcentury Africa, with Trident serving as the anchor offtaker. The project will diversify electricity supply to the Company’s Sentinel copper and Enterprise nickel mines.

Quantum Electra-Haul is a clear example of the Company’s practical approach to improving operational efficiency while delivering reduced greenhouse gas emissions through innovation and tangible implementation of technology in its mining activities.

CASH COST, ALL-IN SUSTAINING COST, TOTAL COST
The consolidated cash cost (C1), all-in sustaining cost (AISC) and total cost (C3) presented by the Company are measures that are prepared on a basis consistent with the industry standard definitions by the World Gold Council and Brook Hunt cost guidelines but are not measures recognized under IFRS. In calculating the C1 cash cost, AISC and C3, total cost for each segment, the costs are measured on the same basis as the segmented financial information that is contained in the financial statements.
C1 cash cost includes all mining and processing costs less any profits from by-products such as gold, silver, zinc, pyrite, cobalt, sulphuric acid, or iron magnetite and is used by management to evaluate operating performance. TC/RC and freight deductions on metal sales, which are typically recognized as a component of sales revenues, are added to C1 cash cost to arrive at an approximate cost of finished metal.
AISC is defined as cash cost (C1) plus general and administrative expenses, sustaining capital expenditure, deferred stripping, royalties and lease payments and is used by management to evaluate performance inclusive of sustaining expenditure required to maintain current production levels.
C3 total cost is defined as AISC less sustaining capital expenditure, deferred stripping and general and administrative expenses net of insurance, plus depreciation and exploration. This metric is used by management to evaluate the operating performance inclusive of costs not classified as sustaining in nature such as exploration and depreciation.
