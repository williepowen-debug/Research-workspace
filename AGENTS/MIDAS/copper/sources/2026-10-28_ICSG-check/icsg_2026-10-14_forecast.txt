ICSG PRESS RELEASE                                                  Date Issued: 14th October 2026 

                   Copper Market Forecast 2026/2027 

The International Copper Study Group (ICSG) met in Lisbon, Portugal, on 13-14th October 2026.  Government delegates 
and industry advisors from most of the world’s leading copper-producing and using countries participated to discuss key 
issues affecting the global copper market.   At the meeting of the Statistical Committee, the  ICSG's view of the world balance of refined 
copper production and use was developed.  
World copper mine production is expected to increase by 1.3% in 2026, with growth of about 3.1% forecast in 2027:   

•  2026 world mine production growth was revised down to 1.3% from the 1.6% anticipated 
in  the  Group’s  April  2026  forecasts,  mainly  reflecting  downward  revisions  made  for 
Chilean, Chinese and Indonesian production.  

•  Global growth this year is mainly attributed to additional production from the ramp-up of 
Oyu Tolgoi (Mongolia), Batu Hijau (Indonesia) and Malmyz (Russia) mines; the expansion 
at Julong (China) and Svaytogor (Russia) mines; higher output at Antamina (Peru); the 
processing of stockpile ore at Cobre Panama and an expected recovery from the previous 
year’s constrained output at other mines.  

•  Although production is expected to increase in a number of countries, global growth will be partially offset by a decline in concentrate 
production in Indonesia and the DRC - Democratic Republic of Congo (output at Grasberg and Kamoa-Kakula mines continues to be 
constrained as a result of the incidents that occurred in 2025) and a decline in Chilean mine production. 

•  A higher growth of about 3.1% is forecast for 2027, supported by the continued ramp-up of new/expanded capacity in a number of 
countries, an expected improvement in Chilean and Zambian output and a continued recovery of operational rates in Indonesia and 
the DRC.  

• 

In both years, a series of smaller expansions and the start-up of some small/medium-sized mines will also contribute to the increase 
in global production, notably in Angola, Botswana, Brazil, DRC, Ecuador, Eritrea, the Philippines, Greece, Mongolia, Turkey and the 
United States.  

World refined copper production is forecast to rise by about 0.4% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027: 

• 

• 

In 2026, world refined copper production is expected to rise by a modest 0.4%.  Although 
global refined production will continue to benefit from new and ramp-up capacity, primary 
electrolytic refined production is expected to decline as a result of constrained availability 
of concentrates, partially offsetting growth in SX-EW and secondary (from scrap) output.   

In 2027, global refined output is forecast to grow by 3.4%, supported by the ramp up of 
smelters/refineries in Indonesia, India and the EU; expected improvement of operating 
rates in Chile; and expansion of SX-EW and secondary production capacity. 

•  Primary refined production (electrolytic and SX-EW) is forecast to rise by around 3%, 
and secondary refined production (from scrap) is expected to grow by about 4.7%. 

World apparent refined copper usage is expected to increase by about 1.5% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2027: 

• 

ICSG’s  projection  of  refined copper  usage  growth  for  2026 was  revised  slightly down 
compared to the Group’s April 2026 forecast, mainly reflecting downward revisions for 
the Middle East, North America and EU. 

•  Global refined usage growth for 2026 is now anticipated to be 1.5%.  Chinese apparent 
usage is expected to rise by 2.5%, and usage in the rest of the world is anticipated to 
remain essentially unchanged.  China’s share of world refined copper usage is currently 
59%. 

• 

In  2027,  world  refined  copper  usage  is  expected  to  increase  by  2.5%,  supported  by 
recovery  in  world  economic  growth,  planned  startup  and  expansion  of  semis  plants  in  a  number  of  countries  and  an  expected 
improvement in semis plant operating rates in the Middle East.  

•  Asia will continue to be the main driver of global growth, with demand in other key copper-using regions remaining subdued, notably 

in the EU and Japan.  

• 

In general, however, global usage is expected to continue to be supported by improvements in manufacturing activity in some of the 
key  copper  end-use  sectors,  continued  demand  from  the  energy  transition,  urbanisation,  digitalisation  (data  centres/AI)  and  the 
development of new semis production capacity in a number of countries. 

World refined copper balance projections indicate a surplus of about 104,000 tonnes for 2026 and 368,000 tonnes for 2027:  

• 

• 

ICSG recognises that global market balances can vary from those projected owing to numerous factors, notably the current conflict in 
the  Middle  East,  that could  alter  projections  for both  production  and  usage.   In  this context, it  should  be noted  that  actual  market 
balance outcomes have, on recent occasions, deviated from ICSG market balance forecasts due to unforeseen developments.  

In  developing  its  global  market  balance,  ICSG  uses an apparent  demand  calculation  for  China  that  does  not  consider  changes  in 
unreported  stocks  (State  Reserve  Bureau  (SRB),  producer,  consumer,  merchant/trader,  bonded),  which  can  be  significant  during 
periods of stocking or de-stocking and which can markedly alter global supply-demand balances.  Apparent copper demand for China 
is based only on reported data (production + net trade +/- SHFE stock changes).  

•  For 2026, a surplus of about 104,000 tonnes is expected, slightly higher than the 96,000 tonnes anticipated at the Group’s April 2026 

forecast. 

• 

ICSG currently expects the market to present a higher surplus of about 368,000 tonnes in 2027, in line with the April 2026 forecast.  

The next Meetings of the International Copper Study Group will be held in Lisbon in April 2027.
