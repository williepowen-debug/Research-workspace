Copper: Preliminary Data for August 2026 

The International Copper Study Group (ICSG) released preliminary data on world copper supply and demand for August 
in its October 2026 Copper Bulletin. The Bulletin and ICSG online statistical database provide data, on a country basis, 
for copper mine, smelter, refined, and semis production; copper refined usage; trade; stocks; and prices. The Bulletin is 

available for sale (annual subscription €600/€1200 for orders originating from/outside institutions based in ICSG member countries). 

Preliminary data indicates that world copper mine production declined by 0.9% in the first 8 months of 2026, with a decline in 
concentrate production of 1.9% more than offsetting an increase in solvent extraction-electrowinning (SX-EW) of 2.7%. 

• Although global mine production benefited from additional output from projects ramping up to capacity in a number of countries, 

global growth was offset by important declines in concentrate production in Chile, D.R. Congo (DRC) and Indonesia. 

• Chilean mine production declined by 7% mainly as a result of reduced output at El Teniente, Escondida and Spence mines. Chilean 

concentrate production was down by 9.3% and SX-EW output was up by 0.5%. 

• In Indonesia, concentrate production is estimated to have declined by 29% as output at the Grasberg mine remains constrained by 

the impact of a severe mud rush incident that occurred in September 2025.  

• Mine production in the DRC is estimated to have declined by 1%: growth of about 4.5% in SX-EW output has been offset by a 26% 

decline in concentrate production, due to reduced output at Kamoa mine (impact of a seismic event in 2025). 

• In Peru, copper mine production rose by  1.3% mainly due to  significantly higher production at Antamina. Output at other major 
mines  (Las  Bambas,  Antapaccay,  Southern  Peru  Copper,  Quellaveco,  Cerro  Verde  and Marcobre)  either declined  or  remained 
essentially unchanged.  

• Mongolian copper concentrate has grown by about 20% as a consequence of the Oyu Tolgoy UG project ramp-up; Australian mine 

output is estimated to have declined by 10% mainly due to the closure of Mount Isa mine in July 2025. 

Preliminary data indicate that world refined copper production grew by about 1.7% in the first 8 months of 2026, with primary 
production (electrolytic and electrowinning from ores) growing by 1% and secondary production (from scrap) increasing by 5.3%. 

• Production in China and the DRC, which currently represent about 59% of global production, is estimated to have increased by a 
combined 4.3% (China 4.2% and DRC 4.5%). World refined copper output excluding these two countries declined by about 1.7%. 

• Chilean refined copper production declined by 7.6%, with electrolytic production (from concentrates) down by 24%, impacted by 

smelter operational constraints and maintenance, and electrowinning (SX-EW) output up by 0.5%. 

• Output in Asia (ex-China) is estimated to have declined by 1.7%, as lower output in Japan, Indonesia and the Philippines offset 
growth in other Asian countries. Production in India is estimated to have increased by 23% due to improved operating capacity rates 
and the ramp-up of the Adani refinery.  

• Global secondary refined production (from scrap) increased by 5.3% mainly due to growth in China. 

Preliminary data suggests that world apparent refined copper usage increased by 1.4% in the first 8 months of 2026. 

• World  ex-China  usage  is  estimated  to  have  declined  by  0.6%,  and  Chinese  apparent  demand  (excluding  changes  in 

bonded/unreported stocks) is estimated to have increased by 2.8%. Chinese net refined copper imports declined by 12%. 

• China's share of total world refined copper usage is currently about 59%. 

Preliminary world refined copper balance indicates an apparent surplus of 218,000 t in the first 8 months of 2026. 

• In developing its global market balance, ICSG uses an apparent demand calculation for China that does not  consider changes in 
unreported  stocks  (State  Reserve  Bureau  (SRB),  producer,  consumer,  merchant/trader,  bonded).  To  facilitate  global  market 
analysis, however, an additional line item - Refined World Balance Adjusted for Chinese Bonded Stock Changes - is included in the 
attached  table  that  adjusts  the  world  refined  copper  balance  based  on  an  average  estimate  of  changes  in  bonded  inventories 
provided by two consultants with expertise in China’s copper market. 

• In  the  first  8  months  of  2026,  the  world  refined  copper  balance,  based  on  Chinese  apparent  usage  (excluding  changes  in 
bonded/unreported stocks), indicated a preliminary surplus of about 218,000 t. This compares with a surplus of about 158,000 t in 
the same period of 2025. The world refined copper balance adjusted for estimated changes in Chinese bonded stocks suggested 
a market surplus of about 181,000 t. 

Copper Prices and Stocks: 

• Based on the average of estimates provided by two independent consultants, China’s bonded stocks are thought to have declined 

by about 37,000 t in the first eight months of 2026 compared to the year-end 2025 level. 

• As  of the  end  of  September  2026, copper stocks  held at  the  major metal  exchanges  (LME,  COMEX,  SHFE)  totalled  992,865  t 
(highest level since June 2003). Stocks increased by 248,750 t (+33%) compared with stocks held at the end of December 2025, 
and were up at the LME (+102,750 t) and COMEX (+252,598 t) and down at SHFE (-106,598 t) 

• The average LME cash price for September was US$ 14,483.39 /t, up 0.9% from the August average US$ 14,353.40 /t. The 2026 
high and low copper prices until 22nd October were US$ 14,850 /t (on 17th August) and US$ 11,826 /t (on 19th March), respectively, 
and the year-to-date average is US$ 13,519.85 /t (+36% above the 2025 annual average).
