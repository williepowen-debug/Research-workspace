Generalizes to any of the fleet's "vs normal/baseline" bands. | round 3 |

| L-10 | 2026-07-17 | **Pre-register prediction branches for BOTH directions of the anchor variable, and anchor a date-test to the EVENT, not a calendar guess.** MIDAS-03's two sign-branches both assumed "yields UP" on CPI day; the actual print had real yields −3bp DOWN, so neither literal branch fired and I had to grade on the underlying v2 mechanism (premium-reassertion vs re-coupling). | L-11 | 2026-08-07 | **Audit a detector's WINDOW against the window its registered spec names — a lookback several times wider than the detection window is a structural false negative, and it fails silently in the exact case it was built for.** `metals_watch.py` returned **rc=0 "CONVERGE"** on the session my registered v2 kill-cond #3 (**3-week** gold-through-rising-yields) actually fired. The classifier's window is a fixed trailing **~90d** anchored to the April blow-off (gold $4,761.90): over 90d gold was −7.6% vs yields +50bp, so a 3-week decoupling *at the end* of the window is arithmetically invisible — it is ~4× too wide to resolve the event. Since the 7/17 polarity flip, catching kill-cond #3 was this script's **entire job**. The generalisable trap: the instrument was *working correctly* on its own terms and printing a confident verdict, so nothing looked broken — a wrong-window detector is far more dangerous than a crashed one, because it manufactures a reassuring null. **Fix pattern: for every registered threshold that carries a duration ("sustained N weeks"), the instrument must expose a window matching N — and when two windows disagree, print BOTH rather than letting one silently gate the verdict. The disagreement IS the signal.** Note this is the *second* rc-blindness in my boot chain in eight days (DAEDALUS fixed one in `boot.py` on 7/31) — worth treating rc-plumbing as a standing audit axis, not a one-off. | gold-rise adjudication session |
| L-12 | 2026-08-07 | **A duration threshold needs a stated CONTINUITY rule, or it has two defensible answers and the grader picks one under pressure.** "Gold rises through rising real yields **sustained 3+ weeks**" does not say whether the joint condition must hold *continuously* or *endpoint-to-endpoint*. Endpoint (7/17→8/7: +12bp yields, +9.68% gold) = **satisfied**; continuous = yields rose for 2 of 3 weeks then eased 4bp = **not satisfied**. I graded FIRED because the alternative reading's escape hatch was arithmetically unavailable (−4bp cannot produce +8.70% at a −0.05%/bp beta) — a *substantive* resolution, not a definitional one, which is exactly the tell that the definition was doing no work. Related trap avoided: the temptation to pick whichever reading matched the conclusion I already believed. **Every duration spec should ship with: continuous-vs-endpoint, the measurement frequency, and an explicit NO-VERDICT band.** Registered in MIDAS-06 (numeric boundaries + an explicit INDETERMINATE branch). Fixing the *existing* spec is Will-gated — flagged, not self-amended. | gold-rise adjudication session |
| L-13 | 2026-08-07 | **Check whether your bands can score the regime you're actually in — a one-sided threshold set reads a live signal as "nothing happening."** Every registered I1 trigger is a *downside* (copper −5/−12/−20%, LME inventory +25/+50/+100% vs median). In this session copper rose 4.6% while LME stocks crossed from **+16.4% above** the 2yr median to **−9.2% below** it in 15 days — a fast physical **tightening**. My matrix scored I1 **1 ⚪ "benign,"** which is *correct per the registered bands* and *wrong as a description of the tape*. A band set built during one regime silently assumes that regime's failure direction. **Audit question to run on every channel: "what would a signal in the OPPOSITE direction look like, and could my scores show it?"** If the answer is no, say so in the matrix rather than letting ⚪ imply quiet. Band changes are Will-gated — flagged, not added. | gold-rise adjudication session |
| L-14 | 2026-08-07 | **A search snippet can fuse an old article with fresh figures scraped from its "related posts" — always date the ARTICLE, not the number.** Hunting a current sulphur price, the top result (Argus, "Sulphur's rally pre-empts Middle East price spike") carried what looked like live OSP/KSP quotes in its snippet. | L-15 | 2026-08-07 | **A threshold that grades a period ONCE, at publication, is blind to the publisher revising that period — and the revision can move the figure across your kill line retroactively.** My v2 kill-cond #2 ("WGC Q2 <100t = CB-floor collapse") graded Q1 as a comfortable pass at **243.7t** and moved on. This session's Q2 pull revealed WGC had revised **Q1 from 244t to 57t (−76.8%)** — **below my 100t kill line.** The condition did not fire only because a threshold has no memory: it evaluated the pre-revision print and never looked back. Q2 itself (288.9t) passes decisively, so nothing is mis-graded *today* — but I carried a **CONF** figure that was wrong by 187t for a month, and the "structural floor intact" reassurance built on it went into a note to Will about a 15%-of-account position. **Two rules: (a) for any threshold keyed to a REVISABLE estimate (WGC, BLS, NBS, BEA — anything with "estimate" in its lineage), re-grade prior periods when a new vintage publishes, don't just grade the new one; (b) a CONF stamp certifies the PULL, not the permanence of the underlying number — estimates need a vintage, not just a confidence tier.** Same defect *shape* as L-11: the instrument silently not matching what it is trying to measure, and producing a confident reading either way. Fix is Will-gated — flagged, not self-applied. | B/A session |
| L-16 | 2026-08-14 | **Two rules quoting the same NUMBER are not necessarily keyed to the same CLOCK — verify each mechanism separately, or a merge silently deletes one of them.** WALTER's N5 fleet rule carries my clause **(ii-b)** (refuse a futures bar labelled with the current calendar day past **18:00 ET**) and the new clause **(i-b)** (a reading taken before its instrument's settlement clock has run is PROVISIONAL). Both mention 18:00 ET, and WALTER explicitly warned against merging them. Chasing my own open ask at the CME primary showed *why* the warning was right, with numbers: **18:00 ET is the CME Globex trade-date roll** (Sun–Fri 18:00–17:00 ET) — so (ii-b) is correct for metals and is an **exchange-clock fact, not the vendor-behaviour observation I registered it as**. But **COMEX metals settlements are struck at 13:30 ET (gold, copper; silver 13:25)** — **4h30m earlier**. So on metals the two clauses carry **two different hours**: (i-b) binds at 13:30, (ii-b) at 18:00. Merging at the shared "18:00" would have destroyed the capture-time test and licensed pre-settlement bars as closes all afternoon. **General rule: when two rules share a constant, the constant is a coincidence until you can name a distinct mechanism for each. Derive both from their own primary; never let numeric coincidence stand in for mechanical identity.** Corollary bought the same day: BRENT proposed crude boundaries at session-end (18:00/17:00 ET) when crude settles at 14:30 ET — **metals settle an hour earlier still**, so anyone generalising the crude number to metals is late by an hour and anyone using the session-end is late by four and a half. | catch-up + MIDAS-07 grade session |
| L-17 | 2026-08-14 | **A registered threshold anchored to "the [date] close" inherits that print's revisability — and a vendor bar can move under a frozen boundary weeks after you froze it.** `MIDAS-06`'s branch (a) is literally `gold closes >= $4,401.30 (the 8/7 close)`. Re-pulled at T+7, the vendor serves **$4,340.70** for 8/7 — **−$60.60 / −1.38%**. The boundary now names a close that, on the corrected record, **never printed**; the same correction re-bases the kill-cond-#3 window from +9.68% to **+8.17%** and the melt-up from +7.5% to **+5.99%**. Nothing about the *finding* changes (the fire survives at the revised magnitudes, and the real-yield leg moved the *wrong way* for the alternative explanation) — but a numeric boundary that was supposed to be frozen turns out to have been **defined in terms of a moving quantity**. This is L-15's shape (revision-blindness) crossed with N5 clause (ii) (a bar is provisional until T+1), landing this time on my *own* prediction ledger rather than on a publisher's estimate. **Two rules: (a) freeze a boundary as a NUMBER standing on its own ("$4,401.30"), never as a DESCRIPTION that re-resolves ("the 8/7 close") — the description is a live query, not a constant; (b) any boundary derived from a futures bar should be set from a settlement-sourced or T+1-confirmed print, and if it cannot be, say so at registration.** Detection is cheap and nobody was running it: the check is one re-pull of the anchor date at grade time. **Recorded, NOT repaired — MIDAS-06 is frozen and the boundary fix is Will's, not mine.** | catch-up + MIDAS-07 grade session |
