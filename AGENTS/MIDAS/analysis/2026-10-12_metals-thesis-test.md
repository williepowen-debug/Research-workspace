## China: what the two declines actually measure

It excludes the commercial-truck/bus channel. Chart 2 defines wholesale as domestic plus foreign sales and labels the monthly-report source an initial draft. Exports include complete vehicles and completely knocked-down kits (CKD).

The 43% claim is therefore verified for its stated category and September-on-September comparison. It is neither all-vehicle production nor a pure-ICE-only decline. The more precise total-retail-minus-NEV residual is 0.534m versus 0.945m, −43.49% from rounded chart levels; the separately reported 0.53m/−43% has coarser precision. **Do not substitute either retail percentage for catalyst-metal demand.**

The [October 13, 2025 first release](https://www.cada.cn/Trends/info_91_10339.html) printed September 2025 production 2.838m, wholesale 2.803m and retail 2.241m. Using those older levels produces −8.74%, −9.06% and −27.80%, respectively. Current charts 3/4 directly supply updated comparators, which reconcile the 2026 reported rates within displayed precision. Chart 7 similarly uses 0.122m prior range-extender wholesale, rather than the older 0.129m. This closes the apparent wholesale comparison discrepancy; it does not explain every revision's cause.

**One issuer-table defect remains:** chart 7 prints prior range-extender *production* as 0.012m (1.2 wan), inconsistent with its stated +13.3% rate and prior NEV component sum. That cell and the range-extender production growth rate are excluded, with no guessed repair. Total production and BEV production are independent, reconciled rows used below.

## Catalyst bridge: platinum and palladium are different tests

[PRIMARY Johnson Matthey's May 2026 report](https://matthey.com/documents/161599/509428/pgm-market-report-26.pdf/a2d115af-bf7c-f589-29e9-6beacf8a4452?t=1778750383760), printed pages 4, 12, 21, 24 and 27/29, accounts automotive metal at vehicle production and manufacturing region. Its automotive universe includes more than CPCA passenger cars. Its **2025 estimates** put automotive at 8.227m/10.140m oz palladium (**81.13%**) versus 3.329m/8.019m oz platinum (**41.51%**). These mix shares explain the different exposure; they are not China-specific current sensitivities.

Hybrids still require catalysts, including plug-in/range-extender vehicles ([JM issuer release, May 14](https://matthey.com/media/2026/johnson-matthey-publishes-2026-pgm-market-report1)). A universal higher-loading multiplier is unsupported. JM describes aggressive thrifting by Chinese domestic manufacturers and often higher loadings on export models. Larger engines, regulatory durability/testing requirements and manufacturer choices matter. More hybrids cannot be translated automatically into either more metal or full replacement of lost pure-fuel units.

Substitution is also time-dependent. JM's May report describes platinum-rich systems selected for some European Euro 7 models when palladium was more expensive, with later reversal anticipated as thrifting progresses. That is a dated, regional forecast, **not observed September Chinese substitution or an immediate response to today's relative price**. No current substitution quantity is estimated here, and this report does not certify changes in emissions law.

The [PRIMARY WPIC Q2 2026 report, **September 9 publication**](https://platinuminvestment.com/files/354715/WPIC_Platinum_Quarterly_Q2_2026.pdf), printed pages 8 and 14–16, supplies an independent metal-demand bridge: Q2 Chinese automotive platinum demand estimated **114 koz/−11%**, while pure-ICE production contracted 22%, with hybrids/exports and heavy-duty output partly offsetting it. Its **2026 forecast** is Chinese automotive platinum **489 koz/−9%**, global automotive **2,904 koz/−4%**, industrial **2,385 koz/+5%**, and overall platinum **+265 koz surplus**. September 9 precedes this month's release: the annual forecast already contains a China auto headwind, so September is corroboration rather than an automatically additive downgrade.

**Forecast disagreement retained:** JM's May model still has a 2026 platinum deficit of 371 koz, versus WPIC's later 265 koz surplus. They are different models, scopes and vintages; no stitched consensus is constructed. WPIC is the desk's existing latest quarterly baseline. Its September report says its first detailed 2027 forecast arrives in November; the separate October 9 longer-term outlook is not a new quarterly 2027 estimate.

## Silver: total stock is not freely available metal

[PRIMARY LBMA September total](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/london-vault-data) remains **28,985 tonnes/+1.95% MoM**. It challenges depletion of London's aggregate pool. The [LBMA methodology explanation](https://www.lbma.org.uk/alchemist/issue-86/demystifying-londons-gold-and-silver-vault-holdings) explicitly includes physically London-vaulted ETF backing regardless of exchange listing. Its older quantitative examples are historical methodology, **not October 2026 allocation estimates**.

| Concept | What is established | What remains missing |
|---|---|---|
| Total London inventory | Monthly custodian-vault aggregate rose in September. | Ownership, encumbrance and willingness to lend/sell are not in that total. |
| Allocated holdings | [PRIMARY SLV 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1330568/000143774926006059/slv20251231_10k.htm): bars chiefly allocated, no more than 1,100 oz unallocated at day-end; trust and custodian cannot lend the trust's silver. Metal can be held in London or New York. | Matched August/September London-only fund holdings and other allocated/encumbered positions. Global ETF totals cannot simply be subtracted from London totals. |
| Available supply | Allocated fund bars can re-enter circulation through authorized redemptions; they are not automatically dealer lending inventory. | A complete, date-matched available-float estimate and transaction prices for access to metal. Non-ETF bars are not necessarily all unencumbered either. |

Accessible tightness check: existing named-contract access retrieved **December 2026 SIZ26 and March 2027 SIH27** raw unadjusted Yahoo vendor histories. March-minus-December was **+$0.769/oz on September 30** ($61.335−$60.566) and **+$0.759/oz on October 9** ($61.807−$61.048): **contango at both endpoints**, with positive reported volumes. That pair supplies no backwardation corroboration for scarcity over those US delivery months. These are **historical vendor Close, uncertified settlements**, not London spot/forward quotes. Financing, storage, regional basis and distant maturity mean contango cannot exclude near-term London stress or determine a lease rate. No current positioning recommendation is made.

[PRIMARY public LBMA trade data](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/lbma-daily-trade-reporting-data) supplies turnover, currently a 12-week average ending September 11, not loan prices or available bars. Full daily reporting requires subscription; none was purchased. Earlier SHFE warranted September 30→October 9 decline and COMEX one-day withdrawal remain valid for their separate categories/clocks, not proof of a worldwide squeeze. The existing April World Silver Survey 2026F deficit 46.3 Moz is not overturned by an inventory increase.
