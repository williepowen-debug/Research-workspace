## ⚠️ HAWK's country discriminator — ADOPTED, but the metric it runs on is a FORK and my own surface carries both

HAWK's §1: SA is **~70%** of platinum and Russia **~10%**, while Russia is **~40%** of palladium ⇒ an **SA** event is **Pt-led or joint** (SA cannot move Pd without moving Pt harder); a **Russian** event is **Pd-led with Pt ≈ flat**; **non-supply** flow is typically Pd-led (thinner market).

⛔ **Applying it to 8/4 gives OPPOSITE answers depending on what "led" means, and `reports/2026-08-27_pgm-surge-attribution.md` states BOTH:**

| Metric | 8/4 reading | Country verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw return** | Pd **+8.35%** vs Pt **+7.99%** (−0.35pp) → *"On 8/4, palladium LED platinum"* | Pd-led ⇒ looks RU-shaped |
| **Residual σ** | Pt **3.11σ** vs Pd **2.69σ** → Pt leads | Pt-led ⇒ looks SA-shaped |

🔑 **RULE THE DEFINITION FIRST — and for HAWK's test the correct metric is RAW RETURN, not σ.** The argument is *supply-share transmission*: a shock moves price in proportion to a country's share of that metal. **σ normalises by each metal's own volatility, which is exactly the quantity you must NOT divide out when asking "which metal moved more in supply terms."** HAWK used σ. *(`[[finding_definition_change_moves_the_evidence_for_the_level]]` — rule the definition before the level.)*

**On the corrected metric, HAWK's conclusion SURVIVES and its route improves:**
- **8/4 — Pt moved +7.99%.** A Russia-only event should leave the 10%-share metal near flat; **it did not.** ⇒ **SA-shaped, JOINT, or non-supply. NOT Russia-only.** *(And "Pd-led" is a misleading label here: the two metals moved within 0.35pp of each other — this is a JOINT move, not a led one.)*
