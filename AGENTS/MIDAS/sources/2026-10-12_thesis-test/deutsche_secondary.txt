Deutsche Bank Calls Time on the Silver Squeeze, Sees $70 Silver by Q2 2027
London's spare silver is up 70% in under a year, solar is using a fifth less of the metal, and Deutsche Bank thinks gold will leave silver behind.
Team @ Exencial Research
Oct 08, 2026
3
Share
Deutsche Bank’s
Head of Metals Research,
Daniel Ghali, CFA
, published
Beyond Peak #Silversqueeze
on
2 October 2026
. It’s a thematic commodities note, so there’s no rating or target. The call is in the forecasts. Deutsche Bank sees silver averaging
$70/oz by Q2 2027
, about
16% above the $60.5 spot
printed in the note. Over the same stretch it sees gold at
$5,250/oz
, about
26% above its $4,165 spot
. The basis is the supply and demand balance plus a new yardstick the bank calls inventory cover.
The logic is straightforward.
A year ago the world ran short of silver that was free to move. Now there’s plenty of it, and the biggest new buyer, solar, is cutting back. So silver should lag gold, borrowing silver should stay cheap, and price swings should calm down.
Where did last year’s silver shortage go?
It went away because the metal came back to London.
The squeeze peaked in
mid October 2025
. One month lease rates (the fee to borrow silver for a month) hit extreme levels. The odd part is that global vaults never held much less than
1.3bn ounces
. Deutsche Bank estimates the 2025 deficit at about
245mn oz
.
The gap was in what traders call the
free float
. That’s silver sitting in London vaults that isn’t tied up backing an ETF, so anyone can buy it. More than
914mn oz
sits in London commercial vaults. At end August, more than
300mn oz
of that was free. Deutsche Bank says that’s
up 70% since end October 2025
.
Why does 300mn oz matter? Average daily trading in LBMA spot silver is about
275mn oz
. Free float has topped that every month since at least
April 2026
. It now beats trading volume by its widest margin since
November 2024
.
The refill wasn’t just metal shuffled from one city to another.
CME
warehouse stocks in the US are still about
25mn oz above November 2024 levels
. That’s even as CME open interest fell by the equivalent of
225mn oz
. Shanghai vaults have added about
35mn oz
since running low in early 2026. Deutsche Bank reads this as fresh metal from private vaults, recycling and buyers walking away.
India helped too. After
Prime Minister Modi
asked people not to buy gold and silver, India raised import duties and tightened silver imports. Imports for the year are running
25% below
last year. August suggests the buyers’ strike may be over. Imports usually peak in October before Diwali, at about
40mn oz
a month over the past five years. Deutsche Bank says London and Shanghai have enough stock to handle that.
The caveat:
the CME pile is a backstop only if dealers ship it. If London’s free float drains again, the bank assumes CME metal flows back across the Atlantic. That worked in 2026. It isn’t guaranteed.
Why is solar buying less silver when panels still get built?
Because panel makers are using less silver per cell, and fast.
Deutsche Bank expects global silver used in solar to fall
more than 20% in 2026
. Chinese solar use falls about
33%
. That’s the worst year for silver in solar in a decade.
Fewer panels explain only a sliver of it. BNEF sees global solar capacity additions shrinking just
4.7%
this year. The rest is
thrifting
, which means engineering silver out of each cell. Silver per solar cell should drop about
17% in 2026
. Deutsche Bank lists four reasons: laser contact tweaks, copper coated silver pastes, ultra fine cell fingers and new busbar designs.
On TOPCon, the most common cell type, the fingers are already
70% smaller
than in 2022. Back contact cells were meant to need more silver. They’re cutting it too, with copper plating and copper paste. Copper paste could reduce silver loadings by
up to 80%
over the next several years.
Price did this. Silver was
more than 30%
of the cost of a solar module earlier this year. It was
below 10%
at the start of 2025. It’s back to about
14%
now. But inside the cell itself, silver is still
38%
of the cost, against under 25% at the start of 2025. So the pressure to thrift hasn’t eased.
The caveat:
thrifting is a one way door only if the copper alternatives hold up in the field. A reliability problem with copper pastes would slow the switch. Deutsche Bank doesn’t model that case.
How much cover does the market have now?
About 45 years, up from one.
Deutsche Bank introduces a measure it calls inventory coverage. It asks how many years London’s free float could fund that year’s shortfall. The answer was
2.4 years in 2023
and
1.0 year in 2025
. For 2026 the chart shows
45.3 years
.
That number explodes for two reasons at once. The pile got bigger, and the hole got smaller. Deutsche Bank’s balance sheet puts the 2026 physical deficit at just
16.6mn oz
, or
2% of demand
. In 2022 it was
254.3mn oz
. Then in
2027
the physical market flips to a
surplus of 18.5mn oz
, even with generous bar and coin buying assumed.
Add ETFs and the 2027 surplus widens to
43.5mn oz
. ETF flows tend to follow the US Federal Reserve. Based on past Fed hiking cycles, Deutsche Bank expects silver ETFs to shed about
40mn oz by December 2027
. Its table pencils in
25mn oz
of ETF selling for 2027.
The caveat:
the ETF call rests on just
two
hiking cycles since silver ETFs began. Deutsche Bank says so itself. In a better case, ETFs keep buying and add up to
55mn oz
by Q4 2027. The bank says even that wouldn’t strain London.
Why is Shanghai paying up if Chinese demand is weak?
This is the one fact that doesn’t fit, and Deutsche Bank says it can’t fully explain it.
Every Chinese demand signal it tracks looks soft.
Chinese solar use is down about
33%
this year. Withdrawals from the Shanghai Gold Exchange (SGE) are a proxy for wholesale demand. They’ve averaged about
70mn oz a year
over five years. This year’s pace has slowed to
42% below
that average, after a burst of retail buying in January to March. Shanghai Futures Exchange stocks are up too.
Yet the Shanghai price keeps running close to London’s, or above it. For most of 2020 to 2025, shipping silver into China lost an importer
$2 to $4 an ounce
. Now it roughly breaks even.
How do both hold at once? Deutsche Bank’s partial answer is speculative buying of physical metal in China. But that should drain SGE vaults, and it hasn’t. So the bank stays sceptical and calls this an
upside risk
to its view. If China launched investment products that hold physical silver, the import route to London is already built. Metal could leave London fast.
The resolution, for now:
the soft demand data outnumber one stubborn price signal. Deutsche Bank sides with the data and flags the price.
What does this mean for silver’s price, gold and lease rates?
Silver drifts higher, gold runs faster.
Deutsche Bank forecasts silver at
$66.7
in Q4 2026 and about
$70
through mid 2027. It doesn’t see silver getting back to the
$83.9
Q1 2026 average. Gold climbs to
$5,500
by Q3 2027 and
$6,750
on average in 2028.
Do the arithmetic on those forecasts. The gold to silver ratio is about
69
at the printed spot. It rises to about
80
by Q4 2027 and about
85
in 2028. That’s what Deutsche Bank means by a weaker XAGXAU (silver priced in gold). It even suggests silver could be sold to fund gold buying.
Borrowing costs should stay low. Deutsche Bank expects lease rates to remain
structurally suppressed
. Short term lease rates already track the inventory rebuild. Longer ones lag. London’s
one year lease rate
is still about
180 basis points above November 2024
. The bank expects that gap to close over the coming year. It also expects calmer price swings.
The caveat:
a $70 call with a $60.5 spot still implies a rising silver price. This is a relative call against gold, not a crash call.
What could prove Deutsche Bank wrong?
Three things, and the first is the one to watch.
The
Shanghai premium
is the cleanest warning sign. If it reflects real Chinese hoarding, a new physical silver product in China could pull metal out of London quickly.
Retail bar and coin buying
is the second. Retail bought heavily in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026. Deutsche Bank thinks that hangover drags on demand and that surpluses could be larger than forecast. A fresh retail rush would flip that.
ETF flows
are the third. The forecast rests on a two cycle sample, and ETF flows in 2025 hit
200mn oz
. A swing of that size in either direction would swamp a 2027 surplus of 18.5mn oz.
There are also loose ends in the note itself. The text puts 2026 inventory cover at
46.3 years
while the chart shows
45.3
. Shanghai’s stock build is given as
30mn oz
in one heading and
35mn oz
in the body. The ETF outflow is about
40mn oz
in the text and
25mn oz
in the table. None of these changes the direction of the call.
