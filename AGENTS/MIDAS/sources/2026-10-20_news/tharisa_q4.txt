"FY2026 was the year Tharisa's growth moved from planning into construction. 

As always, we begin with safety. Our Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate for the year was 0.03 at Tharisa

Operationally, we met our market guidance for both commodities, producing 153.7 koz of 6E PGMs

with Valterra Platinum. On 24 September, we issued US$300 million senior secured bond, supported 
by institutional investors across Europe, the United Kingdom, the Middle East, North America, and Asia 
which were oversubscribed.

Processing performance strengthened in Q4 FY2026, with reef milled increasing by 7.3% quarter on 
quarter to 1 428.1 kt. The 88.3 kt difference between reef mined and reef milled was supplemented 
by purchased ore. Higher throughput supported a 3.5% increase in 6E PGM production to 41.0 koz 
and a 5.8% increase in chrome concentrate production to 416.7 kt. PGM recovery decreased to 82.6% 
amid slightly softer feed grades, while chrome recovery improved to 70.4%, supported by Vulcan’s 
recovery of chrome from the processing circuit and a slightly higher ex-pit chrome grade from the 
mining of the MG4A, MG4 and MG2 seams. 

Underground progress 

The underground transition remains on schedule to reach steady state for Apollo shaft by start of our 
FY2029. A total of 450 development metres was advanced during the quarter, while 52 kt was mined, 
including  30.8  kt  of  reef.  Sunday  labour  permission,  approval  for  twice-daily  blasting  and 
implementation  of  a  full-calendar  operating  roster  were  secured  during  the  quarter,  enabling 
continuous operations.  

Karo Platinum 
Karo  maintained  its  strong  safety  performance  during  the  quarter,  while  mine  development  and 
construction activities continued in line with the phased funding profile. Pre-stripping advanced to 
1.91 million BCM and the project transitioned from bulk earthworks towards fabrication, structural 
steel erection, and preparation for mechanical installation. First ore to the mill remains targeted for 
Q4 CY2027.

The life-of-mine mining contractor is fully mobilised, with 44 of the planned 82 fleet units currently on

Construction 

Design  and  engineering  activities  are  complete,  with  construction  having  progressed  from  bulk 
earthworks  into  fabrication  and  structural  steel  erection.  The  focus  for  the  next  phase  is  the 
completion of fabrication and steel installation ahead of the planned commencement of mechanical 
installation in Q1 2027 and electrical and instrumentation installation in Q2 2027. 

Construction  of  the  milling  structures  has  advanced,  while  cleaner  flotation  installation  is  73% 
complete. Construction of the Chirundazi Dam is 75% complete. Logistics activities continued during 
the period. Deliveries of the substation, thickener and clarifier commenced from mid-September, and 
the on-site bonded warehouse is operating as planned. 

Funding 

Cumulative investment in the project stands at US$240 million of the estimated US$516 million project 
capex requirement. Funding to date comprises US$204 million of Tharisa equity and US$36 million 
from the KMH  bond. The US$300 million bond has been sized to fund the remaining construction 
programme, with proceeds held in escrow pending fulfilment of the customary conditions precedent. 
Project drawdowns are expected to commence from November 2026. A US$75 million Zimbabwean 
working-capital  facility  is  also  being  finalised.  Bond  proceeds  are  ring-fenced  for  Karo,  with  debt 
service settled through offshore accounts.

Market review 

Platinum Group Metals (PGM) prices partially recovered during our Q4, although most of the gains 
had reversed by quarter-end. Following the sharp sell-off in the first half of the year, platinum led a 
rally  through  July  and  August,  supported  by  a  softer  US  dollar  and  renewed  investor  interest  in 
precious  metals.  Macroeconomic  pressures  intensified  in  September:  oil  prices  remained  elevated 
amid the US–Iran conflict, the US Federal Reserve maintained a hawkish stance as support for a rate 
increase grew, and the dollar strengthened. Prices consequently weakened across the PGM basket. In 
its  September  update,  the  WPIC  revised  its  2026  platinum  forecast  to  a  small  surplus,  principally 
because of ETF selling and weaker Chinese jewellery demand. However, it still expects deficits during 
the second half of the year, while above-ground stocks remain critically low.  

The  chrome  ore  market  remained  well  supplied  during  the  quarter,  with  Chinese  port  inventories 
remaining elevated at approximately ten weeks of consumption. Prices came under pressure towards 
the end of the  seasonal  lull,  with  transactions for UG2 chrome ore at approximately US$265/t CIF 
China.  Stainless  steel  producers  continued  to  prioritise  cost  control  and  scheduled  maintenance. 
Elevated  alloy  inventories  weighed  on  ferrochrome  market  sentiment,  with  Chinese  ferrochrome 
tender  prices  declining  by  RMB200/t  towards  the  end  of  the  quarter.  Looking  ahead,  improved 
electricity tariffs for South African ferrochrome producers are facilitating the resumption of previously 
curtailed  smelting  operations,  with  potential  implications  for  both  ferrochrome  supply  and  the 
availability of chrome ore for export. A reallocation of ore from exports to domestic processing could 
support chrome ore prices, while increased ferrochrome output may constrain alloy prices. Market 
conditions  will  also  be  influenced  by  Chinese  port  inventory  levels,  stainless  steel  production,  and 
trends in chrome ore production and logistics costs.

About Tharisa – funded growth, a second operating asset, commercialising technology 

Tharisa  plc  is  an  integrated  resource  group  producing  platinum  group  metals  (PGMs)  and  chrome 
concentrates, commodities central to the global energy transition and the decarbonisation of industry. 
The  Group  operates  across  the  full  value  chain  –  exploration,  mining,  processing,  beneficiation, 
marketing, sales, and logistics. 

The  Tharisa Mine, on  the south-western  limb  of  the  Bushveld  Complex  in  South  Africa,  is  a  multi-
generational operation co-producing PGMs and chrome from a single orebody – a combination that 
underpins  the  Group’s  cost  position  through  the  commodity  cycle.  Downstream  beneficiation, 
including the Vulcan ultra-fine chrome recovery plant and proven chrome and PGM alloy production, 
extends the Group’s reach along the value chain. 

The Karo Platinum Project on Zimbabwe’s Great Dyke is the Group’s second major mining asset and is 
under development. Phase  1  is  designed to produce approximately 226 koz of PGMs a year at full 
capacity, more than doubling Group PGM production, with first ore in mill expected by the end of 
2027. PGM concentrate offtake has been secured with Valterra Platinum for an initial five-year term. 

Through  Redox  One,  Tharisa  is  commercialising  proprietary  iron-chromium  redox  flow  battery 
technology for long-duration energy storage, using the chrome and iron the Group already produces. 

Tharisa targets a 30% reduction in carbon emissions by 2030 and net carbon neutrality by 2050. 
Tharisa plc is incorporated in Cyprus. Its ordinary shares are primary listed on the Main Board of the 
Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE: THA), with listings on the Equity Shares (Commercial Companies) 
category of the London Stock Exchange (LSE: THS) and A2X Markets (A2X: THA).

Information for South African institutional investors: exchange control classification 
Tharisa plc is incorporated in Cyprus, and its ordinary shares are primary listed on the Main Board of 
the JSE. The shares are an approved inward listing, denominated in Rand and settled through Strate. 
In terms of South African Reserve Bank Exchange Control Circular No. 9/2022, approved inward listed 
shares  are  classified  as  domestic  assets  for  exchange  control  purposes.  On  that  basis,  holdings  of 
Tharisa ordinary shares acquired on the JSE are not marked off against the prudential foreign exposure 
limit  applicable  to  South  African  institutional  investors,  and  do  not  consume  the  foreign  asset 
allowance under Regulation 28 of the Pension Funds Act. This treatment differs from that applied to
