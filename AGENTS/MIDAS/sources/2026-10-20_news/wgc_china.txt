Looking ahead The long Mid-Autumn Festival weekend at the end of September and the seven-day National Day Holiday in early October – both of which are anticipated sales boosts – saw demand for gold jewellery, bars and coins notably improve as price pullbacks ahead of the holidays encouraged dip buying 1 The current macro conditions should remain supportive for bullion investment going forward, while the traditional peak season in Q4 should provide a seasonal boost for jewellery consumption, although affordability issues and low consumer confidence may continue to weigh on tonnage demand. Gold’s September decline trimmed Q3 gain Gold pulled back in September ( Chart 1 ), with the LBMA Gold Price PM in USD and the SHAUPM in RMB falling 8.5% and 5.5%, respectively. According to our gold return attribution model (GRAM) , soaring US Treasury yields amid the Fed’s tightening moves and the strengthening dollar weighed on gold. As the dollar appreciates, the international gold price denominated in USD is generally weaker than in other currencies. The RMB gold price ended Q3 with a 3% gain, trimming its y-t-d loss to 7%. The intensifying US-Iran conflict kept investors’ inflation concerns elevated and raised expectations that more hikes from the Fed are to come – rates were lifted by 25bps in September. 2 Combined with surprisingly resilient US growth, short-term US Treasury yields surged; long-term yields also rose, primarily driven by fiscal concerns via term premia. Amidst these offsetting forces, gold concluded the quarter with a small rise. Heading into Q4, gold stabilised in early October, with the local gold price in RMB hovering around RMB900/gram and the international peer price holding above US$4,100/oz. 3 Geopolitical tensions and their impact on energy prices, as well as investor expectations around inflation and the Fed’s future rate path, remain key to gold’s short-term moves. Source: Shanghai Gold Exchange, ICE Benchmark Administration, World Gold Council Chinese gold ETF demand persisted Chinese gold ETFs expanded by 11t in September, the third consecutive monthly rise. Total holdings reached 304t, a month-end record. But Chinese gold ETFs’ total AUM was down 2% to RMB276bn (US$41bn) as the RMB11bn (US$1.6bn) inflow was offset by a lower gold price in the month. Inflows of RMB27bn (US$3.9bn) were recorded in Q3, lifting the quarter’s total AUM by 13% and holdings by 27t. Demand in Q3 has been influenced by rising portfolio diversification needs in the face of plunging local equities. Reduced opportunity costs also boosted demand as government bond yields have continued to decline. And further encouragement for gold ETF demand has come from the official sector’s non-stop gold buying announcements. Notably, the positive Q3 offset Q2 losses, setting Chinese gold ETFs’ y-t-d inflows on course for a record year ( Chart 2 ). Demand for gold ETFs carried into early October as local investors continued to diversify portfolios amid sustained equity market weakness. 4 Chart 2: Chinese gold ETF inflows on track for a record year Chinese gold ETFs’ monthly cumulative flows* *Data to 30 September 2026. Source: Company filings, World Gold Council Gold futures trading active despite a mild decline Gold futures market activity cooled in September ( Chart 3 ). The average daily trading volume on the SHFE contracted 9% m/m to 360t/day and net longs held by the top 20 market participants – based on available data – fell 5t from end-August, to 149t. This moderation in trading activity and net positioning reflected softer market sentiment as both gold prices and volatility eased during the month. Despite September’s retreat, net longs surged 59% in Q3 while volumes rose 18% during the quarter. The improving gold price performance after Q2 – particularly in August – has been a major factor driving these changes. Source: Shanghai Futures Exchange, World Gold Council A seasonal pick up in wholesale gold demand Banks, jewellers and other market participants withdrew 92t of gold from the SGE in September, up 48% m/m ( Chart 4 ). The jewellery sector typically benefits from seasonal replenishment ahead of the National Day Holiday in early October – often viewed as the start of Q4’s peak jewellery sales season - and the gold price pullback has helped. Anecdotal evidence suggests bullion sales also improved as many investors viewed the lower gold price as a better entry point. Plunging local equities presented Chinese investors with limited options and fired their quest for safe assets; this was further bolstered by the PBoC’s non-stop gold purchase announcements. Despite periodic rebounds, notably weaker jewellery consumption than in previous years meant that September SGE withdrawals were subdued from historical norms and 22% lower y/y. Wholesale gold demand in China totalled 234t in Q3, down 21% y/y and 44% below the ten-year average of 418t. Persistently weak jewellery consumption was the main drag. Source: Shanghai Gold Exchange, World Gold Council Chinese central bank ramped up gold purchases The PBoC reported a 23t gold purchase in September, the largest monthly buying since October 2023 ( Chart 5 ). September’s addition brings China’s official gold holdings to 2,410t, 8.5% of total reserves – down 2% in the month to US$3.8tn, due mainly to a pullback in foreign exchange assets. 5 September extended the official sector’s gold accumulation streak to 23 months and pushed the Q3 total to 63t, the strongest quarter since Q3’23. Y-t-d, the PBoC has accumulated an additional 103t of gold and is one of the most active gold-purchasing central banks globally.
