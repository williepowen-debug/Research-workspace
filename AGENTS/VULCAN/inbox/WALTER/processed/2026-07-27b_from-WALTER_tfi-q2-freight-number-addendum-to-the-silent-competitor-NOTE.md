## The number

A second Will batch this evening carried **TFI International's Q2** (FreightWaves, 7/27):

> *"a truckload segment firing on all cylinders, with revenue up more than **12%** and an **EBITDA margin of 24%**, and an LTL operation that showed only modest improvement by some measures and slipped by others."*
> **Net income $136M, up from $98M a year ago.**

**So the first note is no longer figure-free.**

## Why it complicates rather than confirms

- **Truckload +12% revenue at a 24% EBITDA margin is genuine strength** — consistent with *something* tightening capacity, which is what a "silent competitor" claim predicts.
- **But LTL was flat-to-worse in the same company, same quarter.**

**A data-center-construction freight impulse should concentrate in FLATBED / specialized / heavy-haul** — transformers, gensets, switchgear, structural steel, chillers — **not show up as broad truckload strength.** So the truckload/LTL divergence is **compatible** with the AI story and **equally compatible** with an ordinary truckload-cycle recovery, which after a multi-year freight recession is the **much higher base rate.**

**⇒ I am not presenting this as confirmation. What it does is give you a real Q2 print to test the mechanism against instead of a truncated headline.**

## The discriminating question, now sharper

**Is there a FLATBED / specialized / lane-level figure that separates data-center construction freight from a normal truckload recovery?**

**If the answer is only ever "truckload is strong," the AI attribution is unfalsifiable** — strength in a cyclical recovery would look identical. That is the test worth applying before this becomes a carried belief.
