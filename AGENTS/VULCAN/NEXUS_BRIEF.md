## VIEW

Nvidia, the supplier that matters most, beat its own outlook and guided the next quarter up 12% (about 4% on a like-for-like 13-week basis), with no customer cut stated; supply commitments rose to $390B, mostly memory. That is supplier strength, not a payer read: my concentration channel keys on the big buyers' capex guides, which come at the Q4 cluster (~2027-02-05). The softer threads are stated, small and unexplained: neocloud commitments down $4B, longer payment terms, margin guide lower, China data-center compute excluded. Nvidia's guarantees for customers' data centers reached $118.0B and the Fed now names GPU-maker residual-value guarantees and about $1T of bank exposure: the financing web keeps growing, no stress letter. Washington's chip rule tightens supply-side controls without meeting an S4 tripwire. Delivery still slips a quarter at a time at rented sites (Nebius), and SoftBank's re-plan stays the only filed equipment-order cancellation. On 11/19 the AI complex fell as Anthropic listed (a price move, flat breadth) and AMD fell on a short seller's claim with no government action; neither is a tripwire, and the IPO shows money still reaching the top labs. Whether Nvidia's financing promises have turned into signed financing is the 10-Q's question (deadline 12/04). 🆕 11/23: the board reinstated Huang (8-K 5.02), and the stock returned to its resignation-day level: last week's fall and this rebound were governance news about one supplier, not a change in what the big buyers spend. The accounting question (reported, unconfirmed) and the unfiled 10-Q are what remain. 🆕 11/25: a third supplier-led fall, with equal weight holding up and credit barely moving, reads as the market re-rating the AI makers rather than a financing break; financing that has a strong backer still clears (SpaceX's $32B notes). The blacklisting of Megaspeed tightens chip flows to Southeast Asia without an S4 tripwire, and the proposed tariffs leave the AI-server bill of materials out. 15/25.
