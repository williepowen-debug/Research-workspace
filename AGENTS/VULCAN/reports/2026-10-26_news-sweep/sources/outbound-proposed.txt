SUMMARY: This proposed rule sets forth 
regulations that would implement 
Subtitle C of the Comprehensive 
Outbound Investment National Security 
Act, which is codified at Title VIII of the 
Defense Production Act. The proposed 
rule would require United States 
persons to provide notification to the 
U.S. Department of the Treasury 
regarding certain transactions involving 
foreign persons that are engaged in 
notifiable technologies; and prohibit 
United States persons from engaging in 
certain other transactions involving 
foreign persons that are engaged in 
prohibited technologies. Where 
consistent with Title VIII, the proposed 
rule incorporates certain definitions and 
concepts from the current regulations 
governing the Outbound Investment 
Security Program. The proposed rule 
also would add applicability dates to 
the regulations, such that those 
regulations would continue to apply to 
activities that occurred within the 
relevant timeframe.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: 

I. Background 

A. Executive Order 14105 and Current 
Outbound Investment Security Program 

On August 9, 2023, Executive Order 

14105 (88 FR 54867), ‘‘Addressing 
United States Investments in Certain 
National Security Technologies and 
Products in Countries of Concern’’ (the 
Outbound Order), was issued. The 
Outbound Order described the threat 
posed to the United States by countries 
of concern that exploit, or have the 
ability to exploit, certain U.S. outbound 
investments, including certain 
intangible benefits that often accompany 
U.S. investments and that help 
companies succeed. The Outbound 
Order identified the People’s Republic 
of China (PRC), along with the Special 
Administrative Regions of Hong Kong 
and Macau, as a country of concern, and 
identified three technology areas for 
coverage. 

On November 15, 2024, the 

Department of the Treasury (Treasury 
Department) published regulations to 
implement the Outbound Order. These 
regulations are found at 31 CFR part 850 
(Part 850 Regulations), and they 
established the Outbound Investment 
Security Program (OISP), which went 
into effect on January 2, 2025. Among 
other things, the Part 850 Regulations 
(1) require U.S. persons to provide 
notification to the Treasury Department 
regarding certain transactions involving 
persons of a country of concern that are 
engaged in specified activities in the 
semiconductor and microelectronics 
and artificial intelligence (AI) sectors 

that may contribute to the threat to the 
national security of the United States; 
and (2) prohibit U.S. persons from 
engaging in certain other transactions 
involving persons of a country of 
concern that are engaged in other 
specified activities in the semiconductor 
and microelectronics, quantum 
information technologies, and AI 
systems sectors that pose a particularly 
acute national security threat to the 
United States. 

In the time since the OISP went into 
effect (approximately 21 months), the 
Treasury Department has received 
approximately 100 notifications of 
investments in the semiconductor and 
AI industries from a range of transaction 
parties, including venture capital firms, 
asset managers, investment banks, and 
individual investors and entrepreneurs. 
Many other transaction parties and their 
counsel have engaged with the Treasury 
Department during this time. The 
Treasury Department also has been 
closely monitoring investment activity 
that may be subject to the OISP, because 
such investment was either potentially 
prohibited or not notified. Additionally, 
the Treasury Department has engaged 
with a wide range of stakeholders across 
industries, issued guidance and 
responses to more than 50 Frequently 
Asked Questions, and launched a 
website dedicated to the OISP that 
includes an online portal for submission 
of notifications. 

B. Comprehensive Outbound Investment 
National Security Act of 2025 

On December 18, 2025, Congress 
enacted the Comprehensive Outbound 
Investment National Security (COINS) 
Act as part of the National Defense 
Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2026 
(FY26 NDAA). In passing the COINS 
Act, Congress made clear that restricting 
certain U.S. outbound investments into 
dual-use strategic technologies that 
benefit a foreign adversary’s military 
modernization efforts, surveillance 
states, and human rights abuses is 
necessary to prevent harm to U.S. 
national security and foreign policy 
interests. In addition, Congress stated 
that the President should exercise the 
authorities granted in the COINS Act to 
prevent countries of concern from 
exploiting U.S. capital to undermine 
U.S. national security and foreign policy 
interests. 

Subtitle C of the COINS Act adds Title 

VIII to the Defense Production Act of 
1950, 50 U.S.C. 4581–4589 (Title VIII), 
and establishes a statutory basis for the 
OISP. In the COINS Act, Congress 
codified the framework for the Secretary 
of the Treasury (Secretary) to (1) require 
United States persons to provide

regarding certain transactions involving 
foreign persons engaged in notifiable

other transactions involving foreign

technologies. Title VIII maintains 
various aspects of the current OISP— 
such as its application to certain 
transactions involving the

systems, and quantum information

including the Hong Kong and Macau

broadening the program to address 
threats to U.S. national security posed

Specifically, Title VIII expands the 
OISP’s jurisdiction to include two new 
technology sectors (in addition to the 
three already covered under the Part 850 
Regulations): (1) high-performance 
computing and supercomputing; and (2) 
hypersonic systems. Title VIII also 
authorizes the Secretary—in 
consultation with the appropriate 
congressional committees and subject to 
notice and comment—to identify 
additional categories of notifiable and 
prohibited technologies beyond those 
specifically listed in the law that enable 
the military, intelligence, surveillance, 
or cyber-enabled capabilities of a 
country of concern, and to define the 
technical parameters of the technologies 
within those categories as reasonably 
needed for national security purposes. 

Title VIII includes an expanded list of 
countries of concern. In addition to the 
PRC (including Hong Kong and Macau), 
Title VIII includes as countries of 
concern the Republic of Cuba, the 
Islamic Republic of Iran, the Democratic 
People’s Republic of Korea, the Russian 
Federation, and the Bolivarian Republic 
of Venezuela under the regime of 
Nicola´s Maduro Moros. Title VIII uses 
the terms notifiable technology and 
prohibited technology to capture the 
relevant technologies for coverage 
largely in place of the terms ‘‘notifiable 
transaction’’ and ‘‘prohibited 
transaction’’ found in the Part 850 
Regulations. Title VIII uses the term 
covered national security transaction (in 
place of the term ‘‘covered transaction’’ 
found in the Part 850 Regulations) and 
explicitly excepts from its coverage, 
among other things, underwriting 
services (including where such services 
involve the acquisition of equity), bank 
lending, processing or clearing of 
payments, prime brokerage, and global 
custody services. Title VIII also includes 
exceptions for de minimis transactions, 
specifically for (1) any transaction the 

value of which the Secretary determines 
is de minimis and (2) certain 
investments made by a limited partner 
or the equivalent where committed 
capital is not more than a de minimis 
amount, as determined by the Secretary. 
Title VIII does not set such de minimis 
values and instead grants the Secretary 
the discretion to do so. Title VIII 
additionally defines a covered foreign 
person only in terms of such person’s 
relationship to a country of concern and 
not in connection with the technology 
or technologies in which such person 
engages (in contrast to the Part 850 
Regulations, where ‘‘covered foreign 
person’’ has a different meaning). 

In addition to certain changes that 
expand the scope of the OISP, Title VIII 
includes certain new processes. In 
particular, Title VIII requires the 
Treasury Department to implement 
regulations establishing a process under 
which a person can request confidential 
non-binding feedback or anonymized 
public guidance for a potentially 
prohibited transaction. Title VIII also 
requires the Secretary to establish a 
mechanism for the public to submit 
evidence on a confidential basis with 
respect to whether a foreign person is a 
covered foreign person engaged in a 
prohibited technology or a notifiable 
technology. Title VIII further provides 
the Secretary with the discretionary 
authority to establish a publicly 
accessible, non-exhaustive database that 
identifies covered foreign persons that 
are engaged in a prohibited technology 
or a notifiable technology. 

II. The Proposed Rule 

A. Scope and Structure of the Proposed 
Rule 

The United States remains committed 

to maintaining a strong, open 
investment environment and supporting 
cross-border investment where 
consistent with the protection of U.S. 
national security. Certain U.S. 
investments, however, may be exploited 
by countries of concern to advance the 
development of sensitive technologies 
and products that contribute to those 
countries’ military, intelligence, 
surveillance, or cyber-enabled 
capabilities. 

This current rulemaking seeks 
comment on the draft regulatory 
framework (Proposed Rule) to 
implement Subtitle C of the COINS Act. 
Consistent with the sense of Congress, 
in implementing the COINS Act, the 
Treasury Department seeks to (1) restrict 
certain U.S. outbound investments into 
certain technologies in countries of 
concern as is necessary to prevent harm 
to United States national security and 

foreign policy interests; and (2) balance 
the priority of protecting the national 
security interest of the United States 
while, to the extent practicable, 
minimizing the cost and complexity of 
compliance, adopting the least 
burdensome alternative that achieves 
regulatory objectives, and prioritizing 
transparency and stakeholder 
involvement in the process of issuing 
the rules. 

To implement Title VIII, Congress has 
directed the Secretary to issue or update 
current regulations that, as necessary, 
amend, terminate, supersede, revoke, or 
streamline the existing requirements in 
the Part 850 Regulations. Where 
consistent with Title VIII, the Proposed 
Rule incorporates certain definitions 
and concepts from the Part 850 
Regulations. Where Title VIII uses new 
or different terminology, the Treasury 
Department has made adjustments 
while seeking, to the extent consistent 
with congressional intent, to preserve 
relevant aspects of the Part 850 
Regulations. This approach is intended 
to minimize the cost and complexity of 
compliance for affected parties by 
maintaining certain definitions and 
concepts with which the regulated 
community is already familiar. 

The Proposed Rule, therefore, 
includes detailed definitions and 
descriptions of terms and elements to 
clearly delineate the scope of coverage 
and facilitate compliance by U.S. 
persons. At the same time, the Proposed 
Rule seeks to avoid loopholes that could 
undermine the intent of the COINS Act. 
The Treasury Department seeks input 
on how the Proposed Rule can best meet 
these objectives. 

Consistent with Title VIII, the 
Proposed Rule would place certain 
obligations upon any U.S. person in 
connection with a covered national 
security transaction in a prohibited 
technology or in a notifiable technology. 
A covered national security transaction 
would involve a U.S. person’s 
investment in, or other specific 
activities in connection with, a covered 
foreign person that ‘‘engages in’’ a 
prohibited technology or notifiable 
technology. The proposed definition of 
covered foreign person is based on the 
person’s relationship with a country of 
concern. A covered national security 
transaction relating to a prohibited 
technology—i.e., where the relevant 
covered foreign person ‘‘engages in’’ a 
prohibited technology—would be a 
prohibited transaction, meaning it could 
not legally be undertaken. As set forth 
in the Proposed Rule, a covered foreign 
person ‘‘engages in’’ a prohibited 
technology when it undertakes an 
activity specified in the definition of

foreign entity. These and other terms are 
defined in Subpart B of the Proposed 
Rule. 

Under the Proposed Rule, a U.S. 

person undertaking a transaction would 
have the obligation to determine 
whether the given transaction is 
prohibited, permissible but subject to 
notification requirements, or not 
covered by the Proposed Rule because it 
is an excepted transaction, it is exempt, 
or it is not within the jurisdiction set 
forth under the Proposed Rule. A U.S. 
person could seek a national interest 
exemption from the prohibition set out 
in the Proposed Rule by following the 
process described in § 851.502 and 
further discussed below. 

As directed by Title VIII, the 

provisions contained in the Proposed 
Rule will not have retroactive effect. 
The Part 850 Regulations, however, will 
continue to apply to activities occurring 
between January 2, 2025, and the 
effective date of the final regulations 
implementing Title VIII. Nothing in this 
Proposed Rule limits the authority of 
the Treasury Department or any other 
department or agency of the U.S. 
Government to take action, including 
enforcement action, in connection with 
conduct that would be a violation of the 
Part 850 Regulations, as applicable. 

In Section III, the Treasury 
Department seeks comment from 
stakeholders regarding the provisions of 

the Proposed Rule. The Treasury 
Department asks commentors to 
provide, where possible, empirical data 
or other specific information in support 
of their comments. 

B. Discussion of the Proposed Rule 

The following discussion provides an 

overview and narrative description of 
the key substantive provisions of the 
Proposed Rule to implement Title VIII. 
Because the Part 850 Regulations will 
remain in effect for transactions 
occurring during the relevant period, 
the Treasury Department views 
establishing a new Part 851 within Title 
31 of the Code of Federal Regulations 
with the full process, procedures, and 
requirements under Title VIII as the 
most administrable approach. To make 
clear the applicability of the Part 850 
Regulations, the Proposed Rule also 
would add a new § 850.105 to the Part 
850 Regulations that sets the specific 
dates for the applicability of those 
regulations. 

Many provisions of the Proposed Rule 

are substantively identical to the 
corresponding provisions in the Part 
850 Regulations. The Proposed Rule 
should be read in its entirety as many 
terms are related and various features 
are discussed in different parts. The 
Treasury Department will carefully 
consider all comments provided to the 
Proposed Rule and is particularly 
interested in receiving comments 
relating to the new provisions and 
revisions described below. 

Subpart A—General 

This subpart includes a general 

discussion of the Proposed Rule’s scope, 
how the Proposed Rule relates to other 
laws and regulations, the similarities 
and differences between the Proposed 
Rule and the Part 850 Regulations, 
proposed guidance on how to interpret 
and apply certain terms and provisions, 
the application of the knowledge 
standard in certain circumstances, and, 
consistent with Title VIII, the role of the 
heads of other relevant Federal 
departments and agencies in the 
implementation and administration of 
Title VIII and the Proposed Rule. 
Subpart A of the Proposed Rule would 
generally be identical in substance to 
§§ 850.101 through 850.104 in Subpart 
A of the Part 850 Regulations, with most 
differences focused on conforming the 
language of the regulatory text to terms 
used in Title VIII. Proposed § 851.101(e) 
would also revise the Secretary’s 
responsibilities to conform to Title VIII’s 
requirements. 

The most significant change in 
Subpart A compared to the Part 850 
Regulations is an additional element 

explicitly added to the knowledge 
standard. The Treasury Department 
proposes to clarify in § 851.104 that it 
will take into consideration whether a 
U.S. person appropriately considered 
any information that the Treasury 
Department has provided to such U.S. 
person when assessing whether the U.S. 
person conducted a reasonable and 
diligent inquiry. 

§ 851.104—Knowledge Standard 

Under the Proposed Rule, certain 
provisions, including the definition of 
covered national security transaction, 
would apply only if a U.S. person knows 
of a relevant fact or circumstance. As 
with the Part 850 Regulations, § 851.104 
of the Proposed Rule would provide 
explanation and guidance regarding the 
application of the knowledge standard. 
It would state that if a U.S. person fails 
to conduct a ‘‘reasonable and diligent 
inquiry’’ at the time of a transaction and 
undertakes the transaction where a 
particular fact or circumstance 
indicative of a covered national security 
transaction is present, the Treasury 
Department may find in the course of 
determining compliance with the 
Proposed Rule that the U.S. person had 
reason to know of such fact or 
circumstance (and therefore, for 
purposes of the Proposed Rule, knew). 
The articulation of the application of the 
knowledge standard in § 851.104 would 
be similar to that in the Part 850 
Regulations. This is because Title VIII’s 
definition of knowledge is substantially 
identical to that in the Part 850 
Regulations, which indicates 
congressional intent to maintain the Part 
850 Regulations’ general approach to the 
knowledge standard. Further, 
consistency in this provision minimizes 
the cost and complexity of compliance 
for affected parties because the 
regulated community is already familiar 
with it. As such, the Proposed Rule 
would maintain both the definition of 
knowledge from the Part 850 
Regulations (see the discussion of 
§ 851.217 below), as well as the 
important explanatory language in 
§ 851.104, with certain modifications 
discussed below. 

The Treasury Department recognizes 
that for some potential covered national 
security transactions, a U.S. person’s 
ability to conduct diligence may be 
somewhat limited in certain situations, 
including in the context of certain 
secondary market transactions. The 
Treasury Department reiterates that its 
assessment of whether a U.S. person has 
undertaken a reasonable and diligent 
inquiry would under the Proposed Rule 
be based on a consideration of the 
totality of the relevant facts and

Subpart B—Definitions 

The Proposed Rule would preserve 
several definitions from the Part 850 
Regulations in their entirety; amend 
certain definitions from the Part 850 
Regulations; and add several new 
defined terms both to implement the 
requirements in Title VIII and to make 
adjustments based on feedback received 
during implementation of the Part 850 
Regulations. To assist in understanding 
this Proposed Rule, the discussion 
below notes proposed changes from the 
Part 850 Regulations and, in certain 
cases, highlights that the Proposed Rule 
would make no such substantive 
changes. 

§ 851.202—Affiliate 

The Treasury Department proposes 
defining the term affiliate to mean, in 
the context of a transaction or activity 
covered under the Proposed Rule, a 
person that directly, or indirectly 
through one or more intermediaries, 
controls or is controlled by, or is under 
common control with, a person subject 
to this part. Under the Proposed Rule, 
if an affiliate of a covered foreign person 
is included on a list enumerated in 
§ 851.226(v), then a covered national 
security transaction involving a covered 
foreign person engaged in a notifiable 
technology would be a prohibited 
transaction. 

§ 851.206—Controlled Foreign Entity 
Title VIII refers to the actions of a 

controlled foreign entity of a U.S. person 

when explaining the application of the 
prohibition and notification 
requirement but does not define the 
term. The Treasury Department 
proposes using the definition of 
controlled foreign entity from the Part 
850 Regulations. As noted in the 
preamble to the Part 850 Regulations, a 
controlled foreign entity is an entity 
incorporated in, or otherwise organized 
under the laws of, a country other than 
the United States of which a U.S. person 
is a parent. The definition is intended 
to draw a clear line so that it is 
straightforward for a U.S. person to 
ascertain whether an entity is a 
controlled foreign entity. Of note, Title 
VIII also uses the term ‘‘controlled 
foreign person’’ in reference to the 
exception for certain intracompany 
transfers of funds. The term ‘‘controlled 
foreign person’’ is not used in the Part 
850 Regulations, and the context for its 
use in Title VIII is substantially similar 
to that of a controlled foreign entity in 
the Part 850 Regulations. Therefore, the 
Proposed Rule would continue only to 
refer to a controlled foreign entity. 

§ 851.207—Country of Concern 

Title VIII defines country of concern 

as the PRC (including the Hong Kong 
and Macau Special Administrative 
Regions), which is consistent with the 
Part 850 Regulations, and adds Cuba, 
Iran, North Korea, Russia, and the 
Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela under 
the regime of Nicola´s Maduro Moros. 
The Proposed Rule would define 
country of concern consistent with the 
definition in Title VIII. 

With respect to inclusion of the 

Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela under 
the regime of Nicola´s Maduro Moros in 
Title VIII, that regime no longer exists. 
On March 5, 2026, the United States 
recognized Delcy Rodrı´guez as the head 
of state of Venezuela and her 
government is not the regime of Nicola´s 
Maduro Moros. 

§ 851.208—Covered Foreign Person 

Title VIII defines a covered foreign 

person as, subject to regulations, a 
foreign person that (i) is incorporated in, 
has a principal place of business in, or 
is organized under the laws of a country 
of concern; (ii) is a member of the 
Central Committee of the Chinese 
Communist Party (CCP) or is a member 
of the political leadership of a country 
of concern; (iii) is directly or indirectly 
subject to the direction or control of a 
country of concern, as defined by 
regulation, an entity described in (i) or 
(ii), or the state or the government of a 
country of concern (including any 
political subdivision, agency, or 
instrumentality thereof); or (iv) is 

owned in the aggregate, directly or 
indirectly, 50 percent or more by a 
country of concern, an entity described 
in (i) or (ii), or the state or the 
government of a country of concern 
(including any political subdivision, 
agency, or instrumentality thereof). 
Many aspects of the definition of 
covered foreign person in Title VIII 
generally align with the definition of 
‘‘person of a country of concern’’ in the 
Part 850 Regulations, and Title VIII 
provides no indication that it was 
intended to limit the application of the 
program in ways that would permit easy 
circumvention of its restrictions. The 
Treasury Department therefore proposes 
defining covered foreign person in the 
Proposed Rule in a manner consistent 
with the definition of ‘‘person of a 
country of concern’’ from the Part 850 
Regulations. The Proposed Rule would 
maintain the Part 850 Regulations’ 
coverage of certain individual foreign 
persons who are citizens or permanent 
residents of a country of concern. This 
inclusion is necessary to prevent 
circumvention that would undermine 
the effectiveness of the Proposed Rule. 
For example, if an individual national of 
a country of concern (who is not a 
member of the CCP Central Committee 
or the political leadership of a country 
of concern) chose to redomicile an 
entity that such individual wholly 
owned to engage in a prohibited 
technology or notifiable technology 
outside of a country of concern, the 
proposed definition would treat such 
entity as a covered foreign person. 
Including such individuals also 
minimizes the cost and complexity of 
compliance for affected parties, in this 
case by maintaining a definition with 
which the regulated community is 
already familiar. 

Title VIII includes as a category of 
covered foreign person certain foreign 
persons who are, directly or indirectly, 
subject to the direction or control of a 
country of concern or certain entities 
associated with a country of concern. 
For the purpose of the proposed 
definition of a covered foreign person, 
the Proposed Rule would define 
‘‘subject to the direction or control’’ to 
mean that the government of a country 
of concern (including any political 
subdivision, political party, agency, or 
instrumentality thereof, or any person 
acting for or on behalf of the 
government of a country of concern), 
relevant individual, or relevant entity 
has the power, directly or indirectly, to 
direct or cause the direction of the 
management or policies of the subject 
entity (whether through the ownership 
of voting securities, by contract, or 
otherwise).

§ 851.209—Covered National Security 
Transaction 

Title VIII defines the term covered 
national security transaction as, subject 
to regulations, a U.S. person’s direct or 
indirect: 

• Acquisition of an equity interest or 
contingent equity interest in a covered 
foreign person that the U.S. person 

knows at the time of the acquisition is 
a covered foreign person; 

• Provision of a loan or similar debt 
financing agreement to a covered foreign 
person that the U.S. person knows at the 
time of the provision is a covered 
foreign person and where the provision 
affords or will afford the U.S. person 
lender certain financial or governance 
rights characteristic of an equity 
investment in the covered foreign 
person but not typical of a loan; 

• Entrance into a joint venture, 

wherever located, that is formed with a 
person of a country of concern where 
the U.S. person knows at the time of 
entrance into the joint venture, that the 
joint venture will engage, or plans to 
engage, in a prohibited technology or 
notifiable technology; 

• Conversion of contingent equity 
interest (or equivalent) or debt into an 
equity interest in a covered foreign 
person; 

• Acquisition, leasing, or other 
development of operations, land, 
property, or other assets in a country of 
concern that will result in, or the U.S. 
person plans to result in, the 
establishment of a covered foreign 
person or the engagement of a person of 
a country of concern in a prohibited 
technology or notifiable technology; 
• Direction of a transaction by a 
foreign person that the U.S. person 
knows at the time of the transaction 
would be a covered national security 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person, other than an acquisition of a 
limited partner interest or equivalent in 
a non-U.S. person pooled investment 
fund; 

• Acquisition of a limited partner or 

equivalent interest in a pooled 
investment fund where the fund is not 
a U.S. person and the U.S. person knows 
at the time of the acquisition that such 
fund likely will invest in a person of a 
country of concern that is in an 
enumerated prohibited technology or a 
notifiable technology sector, and where 
such fund undertakes a transaction that 
would be a covered national security 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person; and 

• Any other transaction identified by 
the Secretary, in consultation with the 
appropriate congressional committees 
and subject to public notice and 
comment, that is contributing to the 
military, intelligence, surveillance, or 
cyber-enabled capabilities of a country 
of concern. 

The Treasury Department proposes to 

implement Title VIII’s definition of 
covered national security transaction in 
a way substantively consistent with the 
statutory text, but with a number of 
important modifications to ensure 

clarity and consistency with other 
provisions of Title VIII. Specifically, the 
Title VIII definition of covered national 
security transaction refers in multiple 
places to a ‘‘person of a country of 
concern.’’ The term ‘‘person of a country 
of concern’’ is not defined in Title VIII 
but is used in the Part 850 Regulations. 
Title VIII also uses the term covered 
foreign person, which appears to be the 
equivalent of ‘‘person of a country of 
concern.’’ As previously discussed, the 
Proposed Rule would include within 
the definition of covered foreign person 
some provisions currently included in 
the definition of a person of a country 
of concern in the Part 850 Regulations. 
Accordingly, the Proposed Rule would 
replace all references to a ‘‘person of a 
country of concern’’ in the definition of 
covered national security transaction 
with the term covered foreign person. 
Additionally, in each of the relevant 
provisions of the definition of covered 
national security transaction, the 
Treasury Department proposes to clarify 
that the relevant knowledge that the U.S. 
person must possess is not merely that 
the transaction involves a covered 
foreign person, but rather that it 
involves a covered foreign person that 
engages in a notifiable technology or 
prohibited technology. As reflected in 
the Proposed Rule, this would include 
specified circumstances in which that 
engagement is indirect because the 
covered foreign person has one of 
certain specified relationships with a 
covered foreign person engaged in a 
notifiable technology or prohibited 
technology. Without this clarification, 
the definition of covered national 
security transaction would extend to 
transactions without any nexus to 
relevant technologies. 

In addition, as noted in the discussion 

regarding the definition of covered 
foreign person, Title VIII and the 
Proposed Rule do not define that term 
in connection with activity that meets 
the definition of notifiable technology or 
prohibited technology. A covered 
national security transaction, however, 
involves a covered foreign person 
engaged in a notifiable technology or 
prohibited technology. For the Proposed 
Rule to reach a U.S. person investment

relationship to one or more covered 
foreign persons that engage in a 
notifiable technology under § 851.218(a) 
through (g) or a prohibited technology 
under § 851.226(a) through (u), the 
Treasury Department would propose to 
define covered national security

§ 851.210—Develop 

Title VIII does not define develop and 

the Treasury Department proposes to 
maintain the definition of develop as it 
exists in the Part 850 Regulations with 
three substantive clarifications. First, 
the Proposed Rule would make explicit 
that the term extends to activities 
engaged in through or in collaboration 
with affiliates, consistent with Title 
VIII’s coverage of indirect covered 
national security transactions. Second, 
the Proposed Rule would also 
supplement the list of example stages 
prior to serial production provided in 
the Part 850 Regulations by adding the 
act of transforming blueprints, schema, 
or other plans into a product. Finally, 
the Proposed Rule would clarify in 
§ 851.210(b) that the term develop 
includes the provision of high-level 
oversight, direction, approval, 
conceptual input, or strategic planning 
in connection with any stage prior to 
serial production. 

§ 851.212—Excepted Transaction 

Title VIII identifies certain 

‘‘exceptions and clarifications’’ to the 
term covered national security 
transaction but does not use the term 
excepted transaction as that term is 

used in the Part 850 Regulations. The 
Treasury Department proposes to retain 
the term excepted transaction in the 
Proposed Rule to refer to a transaction 
that is not a covered national security 
transaction because it meets specified 
conditions. See the discussion in 
Subpart E below for more information. 

§ 851.214—Foreign Person 

Title VIII introduces the term foreign 

person but leaves the definition to 
regulations. Accordingly, the Treasury 
Department proposes to define foreign 
person as any person that is not a U.S. 
citizen or alien lawfully admitted for 
permanent residence in the United 
States or an entity organized under U.S. 
law, including any foreign branch of 
such entity, except that for the purposes 
of § 851.208(a)(5) through (a)(8), the 
term foreign person means any person. 
The carveout related to § 851.208(a)(5) 
through (a)(8) is intended to preserve 
Title VIII’s introduction of the term 
foreign person while continuing to 
capture in the proposed definition of 
covered foreign person those persons 
that have significant financial or other 
material relationships with covered 
foreign persons. This approach is 
consistent with the Part 850 Regulations 
and is intended to prevent 
circumvention of the Proposed Rule and 
strengthen its effectiveness. 

§ 851.215—Hypersonic System 

Title VIII adds hypersonic systems as 

a technology sector in which the 
Secretary may identify prohibited 
technology or notifiable technology. The 
Treasury Department proposes adding 
the term hypersonic system as a defined 
term in the Proposed Rule. The term 
would mean a vehicle or weapon 
delivery system capable of sustained 
atmospheric flight or very low Earth 
orbit flight at speeds of Mach 5 or 
greater. The Proposed Rule would 
identify hypersonic glide vehicles, 
hypersonic cruise missiles, hypersonic 
air-launched or aero-ballistic missiles, 
maneuvering reentry vehicles, and 
reusable hypersonic vehicles as 
examples of a hypersonic system. 
Notably, the examples include both 
military and civilian hypersonic 
systems, which may include systems 
intended for spaceflight. The Treasury 
Department believes that this definition 
is consistent with industry usage and 
with other regulatory regimes, including 
export controls, specifically items with 
Export Control Classification Number 
(ECCN) 9A011 on the Commerce 
Control List and defense articles within 
Category IV of the United States 
Munitions List.

§ 851.216—Intracompany Transfer of 
Funds 

Title VIII provides an exception to the 

definition of covered national security 
transaction for an intracompany 
transfer of funds from a U.S. person 
‘‘parent company’’ to a ‘‘subsidiary’’ 
located in a country of concern, where 
each of ‘‘parent,’’ ‘‘subsidiary,’’ and 
‘‘intracompany transfer of funds’’ is left 
undefined. As explained above, the 
Proposed Rule would define a 
controlled foreign entity to be the non- 
U.S. subsidiary of a U.S. person parent. 
The Treasury Department proposes to 
define the term intracompany transfer

or credit by a U.S. person parent to (1)

finalized at a later time. This definition

§ 851.217—Knowledge 

Title VIII defines the terms knowledge 

and variants such as know as they are 
currently defined in the Part 850 
Regulations. The Treasury Department 
proposes to maintain such definition in 
the Proposed Rule. 

§ 851.218—Notifiable Technology 

Title VIII introduces the term 

notifiable technology and, subject to 
regulations, defines it as a technology 
within the areas of (1) semiconductor 
technology and microelectronics; (2) 
artificial intelligence systems; (3) 
quantum information technologies; (4) 
high-performance computing and 
supercomputing; or (5) hypersonic 
systems, in each case where not already 
specified in regulations for prohibited 
technology. This statutory definition 
makes clear that the Secretary, in 
consultation with appropriate 
congressional committees and subject to 
notice and comment, may prescribe 
regulations defining the technical 
parameters of notifiable technologies as 
reasonably needed for national security 
purposes and may add and define 
categories to the list of technology 
sectors that enable the military, 
intelligence, surveillance, or cyber- 
enabled capabilities of a country of 
concern. 

The Proposed Rule would define 
certain notifiable technology sectors 
with reference to specific activities and 

technical parameters consistent with the 
activities and technical parameters in 
the Part 850 Regulations, with specific 
changes and additions that reflect both 
Title VIII’s requirement to consider 
notifiable technology in the areas of 
high-performance computing and 
supercomputing and hypersonic 
systems, as well as recent advances and 
developments in the semiconductor 
technology and microelectronics sector, 
which is currently covered by the Part 
850 Regulations. 

With respect to changes to the three 
technology sectors covered by the Part 
850 Regulations, the most notable 
involves what was defined as AI 
systems in the Part 850 Regulations and 
is referred to as ‘‘super intelligence 
systems’’ (SI systems) in the Proposed 
Rule. As discussed in more detail 
below, the Treasury Department, in 
consultation with interagency partners 
and the Executive Office of the 
President, has determined that the rapid 
advances in super intelligence 
capabilities of countries of concern 
warrant a broad prohibition on U.S. 
person investment in that sector. 
Accordingly, the Proposed Rule lists the 
development of SI systems within the 
definition of prohibited technology, and 
not within notifiable technology. The 
Treasury Department particularly 
welcomes comment on this approach, as 
discussed in further detail below. 

Semiconductor Technology and 
Microelectronics 

As with the Part 850 Regulations, the 

Treasury Department proposes 
including within the definition of 
notifiable technology the design, 
fabrication, or packaging of any 
integrated circuit that is not included 
within the definition of prohibited 
technology (i.e., that does not meet the 
performance parameters and criteria 
identified in § 851.226(c), (d), (e), and 
(f), as applicable, although these criteria 
differ in certain respects from those in 
the Part 850 Regulations). Additionally, 
the Treasury Department proposes to 
include as notifiable technology the 
development or production of certain 
equipment not described in § 851.226(b) 
that is designed or intended for use in 
the design, fabrication, or packaging of 
any integrated circuit. The Proposed 
Rule would separately define the terms 
fabricate and package, adding further 
technical detail as to both notifiable 
technology and prohibited technology. 
The Treasury Department assesses that 
the scope of this notification 
requirement would aid the U.S. 
Government’s visibility into the volume 
and nature of investments of U.S. 

person transactions involving these 
technologies and products. 

SI Systems 

In contrast to the Part 850 

Regulations, the Proposed Rule would 
not include development of any SI 
system within the definition of 
notifiable technology. Instead, the 
Treasury Department would include 
development of any SI system within 
the definition of prohibited technology, 
as discussed below. 

Quantum Information Technologies 

Similar to the Part 850 Regulations, 
the Proposed Rule would not include 
any quantum information technologies 
within the definition of a notifiable 
technology. The Treasury Department 
would include quantum information 
technologies within the definition of 
prohibited technology as set forth below. 

While Title VIII includes both 
artificial intelligence systems and 
quantum information technologies in 
the definition of a notifiable technology, 
Title VIII explicitly states that such 
technologies are to be included in the 
regulatory definition of a notifiable 
technology to the extent ‘‘not already 
captured by the technical thresholds 
specified’’ in the regulatory definition of 
a prohibited technology. Title VIII also 
explicitly directs the Secretary, in 
consultation with the appropriate 
congressional committees and subject to 
notice and comment, to define the 
parameters of those technologies as 
reasonably needed for national security 
purposes. For the reasons discussed 
below, the Treasury Department has not 
proposed any technical parameters 
within which the U.S. Government can 
permit a covered national security 
transaction into the SI systems or 
quantum information technologies 
sectors consistent with U.S. national 
security. 

High-Performance Computing and 
Supercomputing

a notifiable technology the development

latency interconnects for high- 
performance computing, including 
interconnects for system-on-chip, 
system-in-package, scale-up networks, 
and scale-out networks that are not 
otherwise proposed to be prohibited 
under the Proposed Rule. These 
interconnect technologies and 
approaches to integration contribute to 
the capability of super intelligence 
clusters, high-performance computing 
environments, and modern data centers 
through high-speed data transfer

§ 851.219—Notifiable Transaction 

Title VIII does not define the term 
notifiable transaction. The Treasury 
Department proposes using this term to 
describe the relevant language of Title 
VIII and to promote consistency with 
the Part 850 Regulations, in which this 
term is used, to minimize the cost and 
complexity of compliance for affected 
parties. Accordingly, the Treasury 
Department proposes to define 
notifiable transaction as a covered 
national security transaction (that is not 
a prohibited transaction) in which the 
relevant covered foreign person or 
relevant joint venture (1) engages in a 
notifiable technology or (2) meets the 
conditions set forth in § 851.208(a)(8) 
because of its relationship to one or 
more covered foreign persons that 
engage in a notifiable technology. 

§ 851.221—Parent 

The Proposed Rule would define 
parent, with respect to an entity, as (1) 
a person that directly or indirectly holds 
more than 50 percent of the outstanding 
voting interest in an entity or the voting 
power of the board of the entity; (2) the 
general partner, managing member, or 
equivalent of the entity; or (3) the 
investment adviser to any entity that is 
a pooled investment fund, with 
‘‘investment adviser’’ as defined in the 
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (15 
U.S.C. 80b–2(a)(11)). 

The Treasury Department is 

considering whether to expand the 

definition of parent to incorporate 
certain sub-advisers to a non-U.S. 
person fund as well as the general 
manager (or equivalent) to a non-U.S. 
person entity. The Treasury Department 
welcomes comments on this definition. 

§ 851.222—Party 

Title VIII introduces the term party 

with respect to a covered national 
security transaction and states that it 
should have the meaning provided in 
regulations. The Treasury Department 
proposes defining party to mean any 
U.S. person, any covered foreign person, 
and any foreign person relevant to the 
covered national security transaction, 
including in the context of joint 
ventures or controlled foreign entities. 
The term would be used in proposed 
§ 851.902 concerning submission of 
information in connection with a 
request for non-binding feedback. The 
Proposed Rule would require 
identification of ‘‘each party to the 
transaction,’’ to include relevant 
intermediaries, financing sources, and 
controlled foreign entities. Party would 
also be used in § 851.104(c) concerning 
a U.S. person’s reasonable and diligent 
inquiry. 

§ 851.223—Person 

Title VIII defines person as ‘‘an 
individual, corporation, partnership, 
association, or any other organized 
group of persons, or legal successor or 
representative thereof, or any State or 
local government or agency thereof.’’ 
The Treasury Department proposes 
modifying the definition to include any 
entity—where entity is separately 
defined in the Proposed Rule—rather 
than listing specific types or 
organizations. The Proposed Rule 
otherwise would implement Title VIII’s 
definition without substantive changes. 

§ 851.224—Principal Place of Business 
The term principal place of business 

is used in Title VIII’s definition of 
covered foreign person but is not 
defined, leaving it to the Treasury 
Department to do so in regulations. The 
Treasury Department proposes defining 
principal place of business in a way that 
is similar to the corresponding 
definition in the Part 850 Regulations, 
but also specifically includes the 
location of an entity’s headquarters and, 
separately, the location where an entity 
conducts its main business activities. 
For example, if a company is 
incorporated and has its official 
headquarters in the United States, but 
its chief executive officer resides and 
primarily manages the business in a 
country of concern, then the company’s 
principal place of business would be in 

both the United States and the country 
of concern. 

§ 851.226—Prohibited Technology 
Title VIII introduces the term 

prohibited technology, which is defined, 
subject to regulations, as a technology 
within one of the five technology areas 
enumerated in Title VIII: advanced 
semiconductor technology and 
microelectronics; artificial intelligence 
systems; quantum information 
technologies; high-performance 
computing and supercomputing; and 
hypersonic systems. As with notifiable 
technology discussed above, Title VIII 
provides the Secretary with the 
authority, in consultation with the 
appropriate congressional committees, 
to prescribe regulations that define the 
technical parameters of such technology 
as reasonably needed for national 
security purposes. The Secretary may 
also add and define technology 
categories beyond those listed in Title 
VIII that enable the military, 
intelligence, surveillance, or cyber- 
enabled capabilities of a country of 
concern. As discussed further below, 
the Secretary, in consultation with 
interagency partners and the Executive 
Office of the President, has determined 
that the biotechnology sector in 
countries of concern enables the 
military, intelligence, surveillance, or 
cyber-enabled capabilities of those 
countries and, therefore, pursuant to his 
authority under Title VIII to prescribe 
regulations, has added that sector to the 
proposed definition of prohibited 
technology. 

Advanced Semiconductor Technology 
and Microelectronics

Regulations, the Treasury Department 
proposes including within the

development or production of any 
electronic design automation software 
for the design of integrated circuits or 
advanced packaging and the 
development or production of any of the

semiconductor fabrication equipment

fabrication of integrated circuits (to 
include extreme ultraviolet and deep 
ultraviolet lithography equipment), 
equipment for performing volume

designed or intended for use in or with 
deep ultraviolet or extreme ultraviolet 
lithography fabrication equipment.

SI Systems 

Since publication of the Part 850 

Regulations nearly two years ago, 
technology has undergone rapid 
transformation, with breakthrough 
advances in the capabilities and 
development of SI systems. A range of 
SI systems developed by covered foreign 
persons present significant national 
security risks—from the ability to 
autonomously conduct cyberattacks to 
the potential use by malign actors and 
foreign adversaries to develop weapons 
of mass destruction. SI systems can also 
provide countries of concern with the 
potential to substantially enhance 
military modernization efforts, 
surveillance, and real-time targeting and 
decision-making capabilities. A range of 
SI systems may pose novel national 
security risks in areas such as 
cyberattacks and the development of 
chemical, biological, radiological, 
nuclear, or explosive weapons, as well 
as expose novel security vulnerabilities. 
The Treasury Department has observed 
advances in the capabilities and 
development of SI systems directly 
through its market monitoring activity, 
receipt of notifications as part of the 
OISP, and other sources. 

The Part 850 Regulations 

distinguished among ‘‘AI systems’’ for 

purposes of the prohibition, the 
notification requirement, or absence 
from OISP coverage based on the 
quantity of computing power for 
training, designed end uses, and 
intended end uses. Over the past two 
years, SI models have achieved notable 
performance gains through additional 
means other than the quantity of 
training compute. Therefore, no single 
measure, benchmark, or threshold is 
currently sufficient to distinguish 
advanced models. Although the 
quantity of training compute may 
continue to indicate a degree of SI 
model capability, developers 
increasingly rely on additional methods 
to improve model capability, including 
other research and development 
activities such as synthetic data 
generation, improved algorithmic or 
architectural efficiencies, and novel 
post-training techniques or increasing 
inference-time compute. 

Similarly, the proliferation and 

adoption of SI capabilities have made SI 
system end uses more challenging to 
isolate. General-purpose SI models that 
were not necessarily designed to be 
used for relevant national security 
applications nevertheless may exhibit 
that functionality, and investors may 
lack insight into the capabilities of such 
models if model developers have not 
evaluated or advertised these 
capabilities. Additionally, the 
availability of capable open-source SI 
models that have fewer safeguards and 
oversight may lead to their utilization 
and incorporation into SI systems in 
contexts outside their intended scope. 

Accordingly, the Secretary, in 

consultation with interagency partners 
and the Executive Office of the 
President, has determined that it is 
necessary for national security purposes 
to define prohibited technology to 
include the development of any SI 
system. The Treasury Department notes 
that this approach is consistent with the 
COINS Act’s direction to balance the 
priority of protecting the national 
security interest of the United States, 
while, to the extent practicable, 
minimizing the cost and complexity of 
compliance for affected parties and 
adopting the least burdensome 
alternative that achieves regulatory 
objectives. Specifically, this approach 
seeks to protect U.S. national security 
and streamline compliance through 
clear proposed regulatory text that does 
not rely on a U.S. person ascertaining 
the technical capabilities or thresholds 
of SI systems being developed by a 
covered foreign person (which was the 
case in the Part 850 Regulations). 

Importantly, the Treasury Department 
proposes to include clarifying language 

regarding when the use or incorporation 
of certain third-party SI systems would 
or would not constitute the 
development of an SI system. This 
language generally corresponds to Notes 
2 and 3 to § 850.224 in the Part 850 
Regulations. 

The Treasury Department particularly 

welcomes feedback regarding the 
potential implications arising from 
defining prohibited technology to 
include the development of any SI 
system, as well as feedback relating to 
the definition of an SI system. Where 
possible, feedback should discuss 
impacts, possible alternative approaches 
aimed at the same policy intent 
described above, and where relevant, 
should include suggestions for how the 
Proposed Rule could be scoped to 
address the national security concerns 
described above accounting for the 
potential impact on U.S. businesses and 
consumers. To the extent that a U.S. 
person believes engaging in a covered 
national security transaction is in the 
national interest of the United States, 
the Treasury Department notes that the 
Proposed Rule provides a process by 
which the U.S. person may request an 
exemption from the Proposed Rule’s 
coverage, provided that the Secretary, in 
coordination with other relevant Federal 
departments and agencies, determines 
the transaction to be in the U.S. national 
interest. See Subpart E for a further 
discussion on exceptions and 
exemptions.

Subpart C—Prohibited Transactions and 
Other Prohibited Activities 

Title VIII authorizes the Secretary to 

prohibit a U.S. person, including its 
controlled foreign entities, from 
knowingly engaging in a covered 
national security transaction in any 
prohibited technology. To promote 
consistency with the Part 850 
Regulations and to minimize the cost 
and complexity of compliance for 
affected parties, this subpart of the 
Proposed Rule closely aligns with the 
text of the Part 850 Regulations and 
describes activities that would be 
prohibited. Such activities would 
include a U.S. person engaging in a 
prohibited transaction unless an 
exemption has been granted. A U.S. 
person would also be required to take 
all reasonable steps to prohibit and 
prevent any transaction by its controlled 
foreign entity that would be a prohibited 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person. 

Subpart D—Notifiable Transactions and 
Other Notifiable Activities 

This subpart of the Proposed Rule 
would require a U.S. person to notify 
the Treasury Department in any of the 
following circumstances: (1) if the U.S. 
person knowingly undertakes a 
notifiable transaction; (2) if a controlled 
foreign entity of the U.S. person 
knowingly undertakes a transaction that 
would be notifiable if undertaken by a 
U.S. person; and (3) if the U.S. person 
learns following the completion date 
that a transaction that it undertook 
would have been a covered national 
security transaction if the U.S. person 
had known of certain facts or 
circumstances at the time of the 
transaction. In each case, the U.S. 
person would be required to meet 
certain deadlines and follow specified 
procedures that include requirements to 
submit detailed information to the 
Treasury Department, certify the 
completeness and accuracy of the 
information submitted, and maintain 
records. A U.S. person would also be 
required to promptly notify the Treasury 
Department of any material omission or 
inaccuracy that the U.S. person learns 
about following any information 
submission. 

The Treasury Department notes that 

§ 851.402 would clarify that a U.S. 
person must submit a notification when 
its controlled foreign entity undertakes a 
transaction that the controlled foreign 
entity or the U.S. person knew at the 
time of the transaction would be a 
notifiable transaction if engaged in by a 

U.S. person. Section 851.403 would 
clarify that a U.S. person’s notification 
obligations in connection with post- 
transaction knowledge apply both to 
transactions undertaken by a U.S. 
person and to transactions undertaken 
by a controlled foreign entity of a U.S. 
person. As discussed above, to reflect 
the proposed changes to §§ 851.402 and 
851.403, the Treasury Department 
proposes to clarify in § 851.104 that the 
knowledge standard would also apply to 
the knowledge of a controlled foreign 
entity for the purposes of applying 
§§ 851.402 and 851.403. 

Section 851.405 would expand the 

information from the Part 850 
Regulations that is required as part of a 
notification. The items that would be 
added are those that typically have been 
part of follow-on requests for 
information by the Treasury 
Department. Including these items in 
the regulatory text is intended to 
streamline any follow-on requests once 
a notification has been submitted.

to exempt from prohibition any activity

of a loan made by a lending syndicate; 
(3) receipt and exercise of employment 
compensation in the form of equity or 
options to purchase equity in a covered 
foreign person; and (4) a transaction 
with or involving a country or territory 
outside the United States designated by 
the Secretary in accordance with criteria 
that relate to the country or territory’s 
own measures to address risks related to 
outbound investment. To further 
Congress’s direction to balance 
protecting national security with 
minimizing the cost and complexity of 
compliance for affected parties, and to 
minimize impacts on the smooth 
functioning of U.S. capital markets 
while still achieving the national 
security objectives of the COINS Act, 
the Secretary has determined, pursuant 
to the authority provided in Title VIII, 
that maintaining these excepted 
transactions, with which the regulated 
public is already familiar, is in the 
national interest of the United States, 
and therefore includes these exceptions 
in the Proposed Rule. Further 
explanation regarding the Secretary’s 
determination is provided below. 

Fulfilment of a binding, uncalled 
capital commitment. Including this 
exception in § 851.501 is in the national 
interest because it is intended to avoid 
disruption to a U.S. person that entered 
into a binding commitment prior to the 
effective date of the final regulations. 
Such a transaction, however, may be a 
‘‘covered transaction’’ pursuant to the 
Part 850 Regulations. 

Acquisition of voting interest upon 
default of a syndicated loan. Including 
this exception is in the national interest 
because it would avoid potential 
disruption to private credit markets in a 
narrow set of circumstances where a 
U.S. person lender has passively 
received an interest in a covered foreign 
person and, even after receiving such 
interest, lacks a role in the lending 
syndicate that could create the 
opportunity for a U.S. person lender’s 
intangible benefits to transfer to the 

covered foreign person debtor. The 
Treasury Department welcomes 
comments regarding whether this 
exception is redundant given Title VIII’s 
language regarding the exception for 
secondary transactions, which includes 
bank lending. 

Receipt of employment compensation 
in the form of stock or stock options and 
the exercise of such options. Including 
an exception for receipt of employment 
compensation in the form of equity or 
the option to acquire equity (and the 
exercise of such an option) in a covered 
foreign person is in the national interest 
because it avoids interference with 
certain individual U.S. workers’ 
employment prospects and potentially 
personal finances. Moreover, U.S. 
person employees are generally not 
providing capital to a covered foreign 
person employer. 

Transactions in certain designated 
countries or territories. Title VIII directs 
the Secretary, in coordination with the 
Secretary of State, Secretary of 
Commerce, and the heads of other 
relevant Federal departments and 
agencies, to engage on both a bilateral 
and multilateral basis with allies and 
partners to encourage the development 
of mechanisms similar to the OISP to 
prevent the development of prohibited 
technologies by a country of concern. 
Including an exception for certain 
transactions occurring in countries and 
territories that develop such similar 
mechanisms is in the national interest 
because it incentivizes allies and 
partners to take such steps, which helps 
protect U.S. national security and 
foreign policy interests. 

Details regarding the other exceptions 

pursuant to Title VIII are provided

Publicly traded securities. Title VIII 

includes as an excepted transaction 
investment in a security, as defined in

78c(a)(10), that trades on a securities

market in any jurisdiction. The 
Proposed Rule incorporates the 
exception with minor, non-substantive 
clarifications. The Treasury Department 
considers this exception to be 
substantively equivalent to the 
corresponding exception in the Part 850 
Regulations. As a result, the Treasury 
Department considers that existing 
guidance available on the OISP website

exception would likely be re-issued in 
a manner that applies similarly to this 
exception under Title VIII. 

Securities issued by an investment 
company. Title VIII also includes an 
exception for investment in a security 

issued by an investment company, as 
defined in the Investment Company Act 
of 1940, that is registered with the 
Securities and Exchange Commission. 
Consistent with Title VIII and with the 
Part 850 Regulations, the Proposed Rule 
also includes such an exception, along 
with illustrative language from the 
corresponding exception in the Part 850 
Regulations, i.e., ‘‘such as an index 
fund, mutual fund, or exchange traded 
fund.’’ 

The Part 850 Regulations also provide 

for an exception to the definition of a 
covered national security transaction for 
investment by a U.S. person in any 
company that has elected to be 
regulated or is regulated as a business

80a–53. Title VIII does not provide for 
a corresponding exception to the 
definition of covered national security 
transaction, and the Proposed Rule 
likewise does not include it. The 
Treasury Department welcomes 
comment on whether extending the

Proposed Rule to an investment in a 
business development company is in 
the national interest. Title VIII 
additionally provides the Secretary 
discretion to except investment in a 
‘‘security issued by a non-United States 
investment company that is registered 
with a foreign regulator with 
comparable oversight standards and 
regulatory jurisdiction to the Securities 
and Exchange Commission as 
determined by the Secretary.’’ The 
Treasury Department does not include 
this exception in the Proposed Rule but 
invites comment regarding which 
foreign regulators may qualify under 
Title VIII’s standard and the potential 
scope of such an exception.

also contains an exception from the 
coverage of limited partner investments

where the limited partner or equivalent 
obtains a binding contractual assurance 
that its capital in the fund will not be 
used to engage in a transaction that 
would be a covered national security 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person. With respect to the first prong, 
to promote consistency with the Part 
850 Regulations to minimize the cost 
and complexity of compliance for 
affected parties, and in light of the

would help ensure that the exceptions 
in the Proposed Rule are consistent with 
the policy objectives of Title VIII, and 
that U.S. persons cannot undertake a 
transaction that has a higher likelihood 
of advancing the technologies that are a 
core concern of Title VIII. The Treasury 
Department further believes this 
carveout would be important to protect 
against potential evasion of the 
Proposed Rule’s notification 
requirements and prohibitions. 
Additionally, the Proposed Rule would 
include clarifying language noting that 
the relevant ‘‘rights’’ of the U.S. person 
are governance-related rights. 

Buyout exception. Title VIII creates an 

exception similar to the buyout 
exception in the Part 850 Regulations. 
The Title VIII exception appears broader 
than the exception in the Part 850 
Regulations in that it applies to a U.S. 
person’s acquisition of all the equity or 
other interests owned or held by a single 
covered foreign person in an entity or 
assets located outside of a country of 
concern, while the corresponding 
exception in the Part 850 Regulations 
requires for its application that a U.S. 
person acquire all equity or interests 
owned or held by all ‘‘persons of a 
country of concern.’’ 

The wording of the statutory 
exception, if left unclarified, could 
introduce administrative challenges for 
the OISP and create interpretive 
ambiguity. This is so because in the case 
of an entity or assets outside of a 
country of concern in which multiple 
covered foreign persons hold equity or 
other interests, excepting a U.S. person’s 
buyout of a single covered foreign 
person’s interest in such entity or assets 
could effectively permit U.S. person 
investment in a covered foreign person 
entity that is engaged in a prohibited 
technology (or allow for such a 
transaction to go unnotified where the 
covered foreign person is engaged in a 
notifiable technology) even while such 
covered foreign person continues to 
have significant, or even majority, post- 
transaction ownership by other covered 
foreign persons. And although Title 
VIII’s language would on its face permit 
such a circumstance, its emphasis on 
excepting a U.S. person’s acquisition of 
the ‘‘totality of the interest’’ indicates 
that Congress did not contemplate this 
scenario. The Proposed Rule, therefore, 
would apply the buyout exception only 
where a U.S. person acquires all equity 
or interests owned or held by all 
covered foreign persons such that, 
following the acquisition, the entity no 
longer constitutes a covered foreign 
person. 

Intracompany transfer exception. 
Title VIII provides an exception for the 

intracompany transfer of funds, as 
defined by the Secretary, ‘‘from a United 
States parent company to a subsidiary 
located in a country of concern or a 
transaction that, but for this clause, 
would be a covered national security 
transaction between a U.S. person and 
its controlled foreign person that 
supports operations that are not covered 
national security transactions or that 
maintains covered national security 
transactions that the controlled foreign 
person was engaged in prior to the 
effective date of the regulations 
implementing’’ Title VIII. (Emphases 
added for clarity.) 

The Proposed Rule would clarify the 
statutory exception and harmonize its 
terminology with the rest of the 
Proposed Rule. By operation of the 
proposed definitions of ‘‘parent,’’ 
‘‘subsidiary,’’ ‘‘controlled foreign 
entity,’’ and ‘‘intracompany transfer of 
funds’’ in the Proposed Rule, a U.S. 
person parent’s subsidiary located in a 
country of concern would be a 
controlled foreign entity, such that the 
relevant exception in the Proposed Rule 
would refer to the intracompany 
transfer of funds by a U.S. person to a 
controlled foreign entity. The Treasury 
Department notes that the intracompany 
transfer exception in Title VIII refers to 
transactions between a U.S. person and 
its ‘‘controlled foreign person.’’ As 
discussed above, the term ‘‘controlled 
foreign person’’ is used only in the 
intracompany transfer exception and 
does not otherwise appear in Title VIII, 
and the Proposed Rule therefore instead 
uses the term controlled foreign entity, 
consistent with the other parts of Title 
VIII and with the Part 850 Regulations. 

The Title VIII statutory exception also 
is limited to supporting ‘‘operations that 
are not covered national security 
transactions.’’ On its face, such 
language would apply the intracompany 
transfer exception only to investments 
in entities that themselves engage in 
covered national security transactions. 
Given the statutory definition’s 
similarity to the corresponding 
exception in Part 850 Regulations, it is 
unlikely Congress intended such a 
result. To ensure the proper and 
intended operation of Title VIII and to 
promote consistency with the Part 850 
Regulations to minimize the cost and 
complexity of compliance for affected 
parties, the proposed exception (via the 
definition of an intracompany transfer 
of funds) would refer instead to 
supporting operations that are not in a 
prohibited technology or a notifiable 
technology.

considering whether and how it should

De minimis transaction. Title VIII also 

includes an exception for transactions, 
the value of which the Secretary 
determines is de minimis. Based on the 
Treasury Department’s experience 
administering the OISP, the Secretary 
has determined that the de minimis 
value should be $50,000, aggregated 
across the covered national security 
transactions or provision of funds to the 
relevant covered foreign person or joint 
venture in the preceding 12 months. 
The $50,000 threshold would refer to 
the value of the transaction or 
transactions, which can include the 
provision of funds as well as the fair 
market value of any non-cash 
consideration. Pursuant to the authority 
delegated to the Secretary in Title VIII 
and as explained in the proposed 
definition of an excepted transaction, 
the Treasury Department would not be 
bound by the U.S. person’s 
determination of the value of a 
transaction. A U.S. person investor 
considering the applicability of this 
exception to a transaction involving 
non-cash consideration is encouraged to 
consider valuing such consideration in 
ways consistent with the equity or other 
interest acquired in exchange for such 
non-cash consideration. 

The Proposed Rule would further 

limit the application of this exception to 
transactions that would otherwise be 
notifiable transactions. A prohibited 
transaction below this threshold could 
raise national security concerns given 
that the technology at issue may enable 
the military, intelligence, surveillance, 
or cyber-enabled capabilities of a 
country of concern. Therefore, the 
Secretary does not believe that any such 
transaction should be classified under 
Title VIII as de minimis. 

Secondary transaction. Title VIII 
includes a new category of excepted 
transaction: a transaction secondary to a 
covered national security transaction. 
The Proposed Rule would incorporate 
the relevant text from Title VIII without 
modification. The Treasury Department 
invites comment on the scope of 
‘‘contractual arrangements;’’ what 
‘‘other similar services’’ may exist that 
would constitute a secondary 
transaction; whether ‘‘bank lending’’ 
would except all ‘‘banks’’ from covered 
national security transactions involving 
loan provision or similar debt financing; 
and whether other ‘‘secondary 
transactions’’ are duplicative of any 
ancillary transactions, including the 
exception for underwriting services or 
the processing, clearing, or sending of 
payments by a bank. 

Ordinary or administrative business 

transaction. Title VIII includes an 
exception for any ordinary or 
administrative business transaction as 
may be defined in regulations. The 
Treasury Department has opted not to 
include a definition of an ordinary or 
administrative business transaction in 
the Proposed Rule. Given the broad 
scope of the exceptions for ancillary 
transactions and secondary transactions, 
the Treasury Department believes that 
transactions that may fall within the 
category of ordinary or administrative 
business transaction likely would 
already be within the scope of those 
exceptions or would not be covered 
national security transactions at all. The 
Treasury Department welcomes 
comment on what may constitute an 
ordinary or administrative business 
transaction that (1) would meet the 
definition of a covered national security 
transaction; and (2) is not otherwise 
included in another exception in the 
Proposed Rule. 

Ancillary transaction. Title VIII 

creates a new exception for any 
ancillary transaction undertaken by a 
financial institution, as ‘‘financial 
institution’’ is defined in 31 U.S.C. 
5312. The Proposed Rule would 
incorporate Title VIII’s statutory 
definition and clarify that such 
transactions must be undertaken in 
connection with, or in certain 
situations, on behalf of, a covered 
foreign person. 

Transaction completed before the 

effective date of Title VIII. Title VIII also 
includes an exception for any 
transaction completed before the date of 
the enactment of Title VIII, i.e., 
December 18, 2025. The Proposed Rule 
would implement this provision but 
limit the application to any covered 
national security transaction not 
otherwise subject to the Part 850 

Regulations. This additional language is 
intended to ensure that parties 
understand that the exception does not 
permit them to avoid any prohibition or 
notification requirements under the Part 
850 Regulations for any covered 
national security transaction that would 
be a ‘‘covered transaction’’ under the 
Part 850 Regulations. 

§ 851.502—National Interest Exemption 
Title VIII authorizes the Secretary to 

exempt from prohibition any activity 
determined by the President, in 
consultation with the Secretary, or 
delegated to the Secretary, in 
coordination with the Secretary of 
Commerce, Secretary of State, and, as 
appropriate, the heads of other relevant 
Federal departments and agencies, to be 
in the national interest of the United 
States. Any determination to grant a 
national interest exemption will be 
based on a consideration of the totality 
of the relevant facts and circumstances. 
Instructions for submitting a request 
for a national interest exemption can be 
found on the OISP website. 

The Treasury Department is 

considering whether a streamlined 
process for seeking a national interest 
exemption would be appropriate for 
covered national security transactions 
in one or more technology sectors. The 
Treasury Department requests comment 
on what the criteria and timelines 
should be if any such process were to 
be considered. 

§ 851.503—Intelligence Activities 
Exemption 

Title VIII adds an exemption for any 
authorized intelligence activities of the 
United States. Section 851.503 of the 
Proposed Rule incorporates this 
exemption.

Subpart G—Penalties and Disclosures 
This subpart of the Proposed Rule 
describes the penalties that would be 
applicable to violations of the Proposed 
Rule. The Treasury Department notes 
that the current Part 850 Regulations 
contain text that allows for civil and 
criminal penalties. Title VIII, however, 
includes language regarding civil 
penalties but does not include language 
regarding criminal penalties. As such, 
the Treasury Department proposes to 
retain previous civil penalty language 
from the Part 850 Regulations in the 
Proposed Rule and to omit language that 
allows for criminal penalties from the 
Proposed Rule. The Proposed Rule 
would include civil penalties up to the 
maximum amount set forth in section 
206 of the International Emergency 
Economic Powers Act, 50 U.S.C. 1701 et 
seq. The Treasury Department may 
impose a civil penalty on any person 
that violates the Proposed Rule. Further, 
to implement Title VIII, the Proposed 

Rule would also state that the Secretary 
may compel the divestment of a covered 
national security transaction in a 
prohibited technology. 

This subpart would also describe the 

process for submission of a voluntary 
self-disclosure by a person that may 
have violated applicable rules. The 
Treasury Department may take self- 
disclosures into account as a mitigating 
factor in determining any appropriate 
response, including the potential 
imposition of penalties, if the Treasury 
Department determines that there was a 
violation. Consistent with Title VIII, the 
Proposed Rule would use corresponding 
language from the Part 850 Regulations 
but would modify the scope of a 
voluntary self-disclosure to require that 
such disclosures contain a proposal for 
mitigation of any harms associated with 
the violation, including divestment or a 
voluntary disgorgement of profits. The 
Proposed Rule would further specify the 
information that must be included in a 
voluntary self-disclosure letter. 

Subpart H—Provision and Handling of 
Information 

This subpart of the Proposed Rule 
would implement the section of Title 
VIII that provides that information or 
documentary material provided to the 
Treasury Department under this part is 
exempt from disclosure under section 
552(b)(3) of Title 5 of the United States 
Code. Title VIII also identifies 
exceptions to this rule. 

The Proposed Rule would incorporate 

this provision of Title VIII with certain 
clarifications. Title VIII excepts from the 
disclosure exemption certain 
information ‘‘[g]athered by the Secretary 
or the Secretary’s designee where the 
disclosure is determined to be in the 
national interest.’’ Section 851.801(b)(6) 
would clarify that the determination is 
made by the Secretary or the Secretary’s 
designee. Factors that the Secretary or 
the Secretary’s designee may consider 
when determining if disclosure is in the 
national interest may include whether 
such disclosure will further national 
security interests, address law 
enforcement needs, or promote 
compliance with the rule. 
Circumstances in which the Secretary 
may determine the disclosure of 
information to be in the national interest 
can include, for example, in the context 
of notifying the public regarding an 
action taken by the Treasury 
Department to enforce compliance with 
the Proposed Rule. The Treasury 
Department anticipates this exception to 
be invoked only in circumstances in 
which the Secretary identifies a national 
interest that disclosure could help to 
address.

of the products, services, technologies, 
or activities implicated by the 
transaction; all operative documents 
and material term sheets, draft 
agreements, side letters, and other 
written materials reflecting the 
transaction; and any other information 
that the requester believes would be 
relevant to the application of the 
Proposed Rule to the transaction. 

The Proposed Rule would outline 
additional features of the submission 
process for non-binding feedback, 
including a required certification that 
the request contains true, correct, and 
complete disclosure of all material facts 
and circumstances known to the 
requester at the time of submission and 
that the requester will supplement the 
request if the requester learns of 
material changes or omissions. The 
Proposed Rule notes that the Treasury 
Department may request additional 
information or a meeting with the 
requester and any relevant parties to the 
transaction. 

The Proposed Rule would state that 
receipt of a complete request for non- 
binding feedback would not obligate the 
Treasury Department to issue such 
feedback and would also state that the 
Treasury Department may, in its sole 
discretion, respond to a complete 
request by providing anonymized public 
guidance, issuing confidential, written 
non-binding feedback, or declining to 
provide feedback. The Proposed Rule 
would further state that non-binding 
feedback provided pursuant to this 
subpart would address only whether a 
transaction, as described in the request, 
would constitute a prohibited 
transaction, and that the Treasury 
Department may provide feedback that 
is responsive to some, but not all, of the 
questions or information presented by 
the requester. The Proposed Rule would 
also describe the non-binding nature of 
the feedback, noting that feedback 
provided pursuant to this subpart would 
not bind the Treasury Department, any 
other Federal or local department or 
agency, or any court; would not 
constitute an authorization, license, 
exemption, or waiver; and would not 
preclude the Treasury Department from 
taking any action authorized by the law 
with respect to the transaction, the 
requester, or any other person. 

To further limit frivolous requests as 

authorized under Title VIII, the 
Proposed Rule would describe types of 
requests that the Treasury Department 
may identify as frivolous, including 
requests that concern hypothetical 
conduct, are incomplete or lack 
sufficient information to permit a 
meaningful review, or seek general 
advisory views or policy guidance that 
is not specific to the transaction that is 
the subject of the request. 

The Treasury Department notes that 

this non-binding feedback process 
would be a new addition to the OISP, 
as the Part 850 Regulations do not 
include such a process, and welcomes 
comment on this subpart. 

Subpart J—Information on Covered 
Foreign Persons 

Subpart J of the Proposed Rule would 

implement Title VIII by establishing a 
mechanism by which the public can 
submit evidence, on a confidential 
basis, regarding whether a foreign 
person is a covered foreign person 
engaged in a prohibited technology or 
notifiable technology. The Proposed 
Rule would describe the information 
that the Treasury Department requires to 
determine whether a foreign person is a 
covered foreign person. 

The Treasury Department is actively 

considering whether and how to 
establish a publicly accessible, non- 
exhaustive database of covered foreign 
persons as described in Title VIII. Items 
under consideration include: (1) 
whether to limit such a database to only 
covered foreign persons engaged in 
prohibited technology; (2) the 
evidentiary standard that the Treasury 
Department should meet before 
determining that a foreign person is (or 
is no longer) a covered foreign person 
engaged in a prohibited technology or a 
notifiable technology and should be 
included in (or removed from) a 
database; (3) how to communicate the 
limited temporal nature of inclusion in 
any such database, given that a covered 
foreign person’s engagement in a 
prohibited technology or notifiable 
technology can change over time; and 
(4) the administrative burden this may 
place on the Treasury Department. The 
Treasury Department welcomes 
comment on these and any other issues 
that may be relevant to the 
establishment of the public database. 

Subpart K—Other Provisions 

This subpart of the Proposed Rule 
would implement provisions related to 
the delegation of the Secretary’s 
authorities under Title VIII, any 
amendment or modification of the 
Proposed Rule, severability of the 

Proposed Rule, and a requirement for 
certain information regarding any 
transaction to be furnished upon 
demand. The Proposed Rule would also 
state that, consistent with Title VIII, the 
Treasury Department has the power to 
investigate conduct that may constitute 
a violation, hold hearings, call 
witnesses, and require in-person 
testimony or production of documents.

The primary direct costs to the public

if applicable, preparing and submitting 
a mandatory notification of certain

Treasury Department pursuant the 
Proposed Rule. The Treasury 
Department requests comments on any

The Proposed Rule would apply to all

national security transaction with or 
involving a covered foreign person. To

transactions subject to the Proposed

from this data over the relevant three-

that these average figures undercount 
transactions. For example, commercially

Proposed Rule, or such data may not

subject to the Proposed Rule. Thus, the

on the assumption that, annually, 
around 150 different investors 
collectively engage in 200 transactions 
that would be potentially covered by the 
Proposed Rule.

Treasury Department should consider.

Additional Indirect Costs Associated 
With Prohibited Transactions 

With respect to prohibited 

transactions, the Treasury Department 
does not believe that the Proposed Rule 
will have additional direct economic 
costs to U.S. investors beyond those 
described above. There may, however, 
be additional indirect costs associated 
with prohibited transactions. Investors 
who would have otherwise engaged in 
a prohibited transaction, absent the 
Proposed Rule, may have to seek less 
promising investment opportunities 
since they would be precluded from 
undertaking and benefiting from 
prohibited transactions that would be 
foreclosed to them by the Proposed 
Rule. These indirect costs amount to the 
difference between the return that 
would have been generated by the 
prohibited transaction and the return 
that would result from the best 
alternative transaction. In addition, 
while the Proposed Rule may have an 
economic impact on investment targets 
that are covered foreign persons because 
certain transactions would be 
prohibited, the Proposed Rule is not 
designed to, nor does it, prohibit all U.S. 
person investments into such persons, 
due to the several exceptions provided 
for in the Proposed Rule.

C. Benefits 

As Congress noted in the COINS Act, 

there are countless known and 
unknown entities in countries of 
concern that are developing dual-use 
strategic technologies that benefit a 
foreign adversary’s military 
modernization efforts, surveillance 
states, and human rights abuses. 
Advancement by countries of concern in 
sensitive technologies and products 
critical for the military, intelligence, 
surveillance, human rights abuse, or 
cyber-enabled capabilities of such 
countries constitutes a threat to the 
national security of the United States. 
Restricting certain U.S. outbound 
investments into certain technologies in 
countries of concern is necessary to 
prevent harm to U.S. national security 
and foreign policy interests. 

The Proposed Rule is designed to 
complement existing tools of the U.S. 
Government and effectively address the 
threat to the national security of the 
United States described in Title VIII. 
While the benefit of protecting national 
security is difficult to quantify, the 
Treasury Department has identified 
specific benefits below. The notification 
function of the Proposed Rule is 
intended to provide key information 
that the Treasury Department could use 
to better inform the development and 
implementation of the OISP. Such 
notifications received under the existing 
OISP have increased the U.S. 
Government’s visibility into 
transactions by U.S. persons, or their 
controlled foreign entities, in the SI and 

the semiconductor and microelectronics 
sectors in a country of concern. These 
have also assisted the U.S. Government 
by highlighting trends with respect to 
related capital flows and investment by 
non-U.S. persons in these technology 
sectors in a country of concern. Finally, 
these notifications have informed policy 
development for the Proposed Rule as 
well as other U.S. Government efforts to 
protect U.S. national security. The 
Treasury Department expects to receive 
similar benefits from notifications filed 
under the Proposed Rule. The Treasury 
Department does not currently propose 
a breakeven analysis for the Proposed 
Rule, as it continues to collect 
information on potential costs and 
benefits of the Proposed Rule through 
the rulemaking process. 

D. Alternatives 

The Proposed Rule is promulgated 
pursuant to Title VIII, which directs the 
Treasury Department to issue 
regulations to implement its 
requirements. However, the Treasury 
Department considered some alternative 
approaches that would be consistent 
with Title VIII. Specifically, the 
Treasury Department considered the 
following potential alternatives to the 
Proposed Rule: 

• Limited exceptions. In the Proposed 

Rule, the Treasury Department would 
define the term ‘‘covered national 
security transaction’’ largely as defined 
in Title VIII (as set forth in Subpart B) 
without further permitted additions and 
would provide for certain exceptions (in 
Subpart E), some of which are explicitly 
required by Title VIII and others that are 
permitted, but not required, under Title 
VIII. Therefore, as an alternative 
consistent with Title VIII, the Treasury 
Department could have proposed a 
broader definition of covered national 
security transaction and/or fewer 
exceptions. This alternative likely 
would result in the gathering of more 
information on U.S. investments in 
sensitive technologies and products in 
countries of concern through notifiable 
transactions, which would expand the 
information available to assist the 
development and implementation of the 
OISP. But the alternative approach also 
likely would impose higher compliance 
costs on U.S. persons. 

• Prohibited and notifiable 

technology using broad definition of 
sectors rather than specific activities 
and technologies. In the Proposed Rule, 
the Treasury Department would define 
notifiable technology and prohibited 
technology in § 851.218 and § 851.226, 
respectively, by reference to certain 
technologies and activities, and in some 
instance, end uses. Alternatively, and 

consistent with Title VIII, the Treasury 
Department could have proposed broad 
sectoral categorizations, such as, for 
example, defining a prohibited 
technology to include ‘‘all super 
intelligence’’ in a country of concern. If 
the Treasury Department had proposed 
that approach, the Treasury Department 
estimates that it would create ambiguity 
and uncertainty for U.S. persons about 
what investments were subject to the 
prohibition and notification 
requirements because of the lack of a 
clear, articulated nexus between a 
covered foreign person and the 
technology being described. This in turn 
would result in significant compliance 
costs and overall economic 
consequences on U.S. persons and the 
U.S. economy that could undermine 
potential national security benefits.

§ 851.101 Scope. 

(a) This part implements Title VIII of 
the Defense Production Act of 1950, as 
amended (50 U.S.C. 4581–4589) (Title 
VIII), which directs the Secretary of the 
Treasury (the Secretary), in consultation 
with the Secretary of Commerce, the 
Secretary of State, and, as appropriate, 
the heads of other relevant Federal 
departments and agencies (as defined in 
§ 851.229), to issue, subject to public 
notice and comment, regulations to 
require a U.S. person to provide written 
notification on behalf of itself or its 
controlled foreign entity regarding 
certain covered national security 
transactions in any notifiable 
technology and, where the Secretary has 
exercised the authority under Title VIII 
to prohibit certain transactions, to 
prohibit a U.S. person and its controlled 
foreign entities from engaging in certain 
covered national security transactions in 
any prohibited technology. 

(b) This part identifies certain types of 

transactions that are covered national 
security transactions—which are certain 
transactions by a U.S. person into or 
with a covered foreign person. 
Additionally, this part identifies other 
instances where a U.S. person has 
obligations with respect to certain 
transactions. The regulations prescribe 
exceptions to the definition of covered 
national security transaction. A 
transaction that meets an exception is 
not a covered national security 
transaction and is referred to as an 
excepted transaction. Finally, this part: 

(1) Exempts any authorized 

intelligence activities of the United 
States; and 

(2) Prescribes a process to consider 
exempting certain activities from the 
definition of a covered national security 
transaction on a case-by-case basis. 

(c) This part identifies categories of 
covered national security transactions 
that are notifiable transactions. A 
notifiable transaction is a covered 
national security transaction by a U.S. 
person or its controlled foreign entity 
with a covered foreign person or a 
relevant joint venture involving a 
notifiable technology. A notifiable 
transaction includes a U.S. person’s 
direction of a transaction that the U.S. 
person knows would be a notifiable 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person (other than an acquisition of a 
limited partner interest or equivalent in 
a pooled investment fund that is not a 
U.S. person). This part requires a U.S. 
person to notify the Department of the 

Treasury of each such notifiable 
transaction by such U.S. person or its 
controlled foreign entity. This part also 
requires a U.S. person to provide 
prompt notice to the Department of the 
Treasury upon acquiring actual 
knowledge after the completion date of 
a transaction of facts or circumstances 
that would have caused the transaction 
to be a covered national security 
transaction if the U.S. person had such 
knowledge on the completion date. 
Additionally, any person who makes a 
representation, statement, or 
certification to the Department of the 
Treasury under this part is required to 
promptly notify the Department of the 
Treasury upon learning of a material 
omission or inaccuracy in such 
representation, statement, or 
certification. 

(d) This part identifies categories of 
covered national security transactions 
that are prohibited transactions. A 
prohibited transaction is a covered 
national security transaction by a U.S. 
person with a covered foreign person or 
relevant joint venture involving a 
prohibited technology. This part 
prohibits a U.S. person from knowingly 
engaging in a prohibited transaction, 
which includes a prohibition on a U.S. 
person’s direction of a transaction that 
the U.S. person knows would be a 
prohibited transaction if engaged in by 
a U.S. person (other than an acquisition 
of a limited partner interest or 
equivalent in a pooled investment fund 
that is not a U.S. person). This part also 
requires a U.S. person to take all 
reasonable steps to prohibit and prevent 
any transaction by its controlled foreign 
entity that would be a prohibited 
transaction if undertaken by a U.S. 
person.

§ 851.103 Rules of construction and 
interpretation. 

(a) As used in this part, the term 
‘‘including’’ (or variations such as 
‘‘include’’) means ‘‘including but not 
limited to.’’ 

(b) Any term in the singular includes 

the plural, and the plural includes the 
singular, if such use would be 
appropriate. 

(c) Section headings are included for 
convenience of reference only and shall 
not affect the interpretation of this part. 

§ 851.104 Knowledge standard. 

(a) Certain provisions of this part 
apply only if a U.S. person knows of a 
fact or circumstance. The term 
knowledge is defined in § 851.217. In 
determining whether a U.S. person is 
complying with this part or has violated 
any obligation under this part, the 
Department of the Treasury will assess 
whether such person has or had 
knowledge of the relevant facts and 
circumstances at the specified time. 

(b) Such assessment as to whether, at 

the time of a given transaction, a U.S. 
person has or had knowledge of a given 
fact or circumstance will be made based 
on information a U.S. person had or 
could have had through a reasonable 
and diligent inquiry. A U.S. person that 
has failed to conduct a reasonable and 
diligent inquiry by the time of a given 
transaction may be assessed to have had 
reason to know of a given fact or 
circumstance, including facts or 
circumstances that would cause the 
transaction to be a covered national 
security transaction in a notifiable 
technology or a prohibited technology. 

(c) In assessing whether a U.S. person 

has undertaken such a reasonable and 
diligent inquiry, the Department of the 
Treasury’s considerations will include 
the following, as applicable, among 
others that the Department of the 
Treasury deems relevant, with respect to 
a particular transaction: 

(1) The inquiry a U.S. person has 
made regarding an investment target or 
other relevant party, including 
questions asked of the investment target 
or relevant party, as of the time of the 
transaction; 

(2) The contractual representations or 
warranties the U.S. person has obtained 
or attempted to obtain from the 
investment target or other relevant party 
with respect to the determination of a 
transaction’s status as a covered 
national security transaction in a 
notifiable technology or a prohibited 
technology and the status of an 
investment target or other relevant party 
as a covered foreign person; 

(3) The efforts by the U.S. person as 
of the time of the transaction to obtain 
and consider available non-public 
information relevant to the 
determination of a transaction’s status 
as a covered national security 
transaction in a notifiable technology or 
prohibited technology and the status of 
an investment target or other relevant 
party as a covered foreign person; 

(4) Available public information, the 
efforts undertaken by the U.S. person to 
obtain and consider such information, 
and the degree to which other 
information available to the U.S. person 
as of the time of the transaction is 
consistent or inconsistent with such 
publicly available information; 
(5) Whether the U.S. person 
purposefully avoided learning or 
seeking relevant information; 

(6) The presence or absence of 
warning signs, which may include 
evasive responses or non-responses 
from an investment target or other 
relevant party to questions or a refusal 
to provide information, contractual 
representations, or warranties; 

(7) The use of available public and 
commercial databases to identify and 
verify relevant information of an 
investment target or other relevant 
party; and 

(8) Whether the U.S. person 
appropriately considered any 
information made available by the 
Department of the Treasury prior to the 
completion of the transaction, including 
any assessments by the Department of 
the Treasury that the relevant covered 
foreign person may be engaged in a 
notifiable technology or a prohibited 
technology. 

(d) An assessment of whether a U.S. 
person has undertaken a reasonable and 
diligent inquiry shall be based on a 
consideration of the totality of relevant 
facts and circumstances. 

(e) Paragraphs (a) through (d) of this 

section shall also apply to the 
knowledge of a controlled foreign entity 
for the purposes of applying §§ 851.402 
and 851.403.

Subpart B—Definitions 

§ 851.201 Advanced packaging. 

The term advanced packaging means 

to package integrated circuits in a 
manner that supports the two-and-one- 
half-dimensional (2.5D) or three- 
dimensional (3D) assembly of integrated 
circuits, such as by directly attaching 
one or more die or wafer using through- 
silicon vias, die or wafer bonding, 
heterogeneous integration, or other 
advanced methods and materials. 

§ 851.202 Affiliate. 

The term affiliate means, in the 
context of a transaction or activity 
covered under this part, a person that 
directly, or indirectly through one or 
more intermediaries, controls or is 
controlled by, or is under common 
control with, a person subject to this 
part. 

§ 851.203 Certification. 

(a) The term certification means a 
written statement signed by the chief 
executive officer or other duly 
authorized designee of the person filing 
a notification or providing other 
information that certifies under the 
penalties provided in the False 
Statements Accountability Act of 1996, 
as amended (18 U.S.C. 1001), that the 
notification or other information filed or 
provided: 

(1) Fully complies with the 

regulations in this part; and 

(2) Is accurate and complete in all 
material respects to the best knowledge 
of the person filing a notification or 
other information. 

(b) For purposes of this section, a duly 

authorized designee is: 

(1) In the case of a partnership, any 

general partner thereof; 

(2) In the case of a corporation, any 

officer thereof; and 

(3) In the case of any entity lacking 
partners and officers, any individual 
within the organization exercising 
executive functions similar to those of a 
general partner of a partnership or an 
officer of a corporation or otherwise 
authorized by the board of directors or 
equivalent to provide such certification. 

(c) In each case described in 
paragraph (b) of this section, such 
designee must possess actual authority 
to make the certification on behalf of the 
person filing a notification or other 
information. 

Note 1 to § 851.203: A template for 

certifications may be found at the 
Department of the Treasury’s Outbound 
Investment Security Program website. 

§ 851.204 Completion date. 

The term completion date means: 

(a) With respect to a covered national 

security transaction other than under 
§ 851.209(a)(7), the earliest date upon 
which any interest, asset, property, or 
right is conveyed, assigned, delivered, 
or otherwise transferred to a U.S. 
person, or as applicable, its controlled 
foreign entity; or 

(b) With respect to a covered national 

security transaction under 
§ 851.209(a)(7), the earliest date upon 
which any interest, asset, property, or 
right in the relevant covered foreign 
person is conveyed, assigned, delivered, 
or otherwise transferred to the 
applicable fund. 

§ 851.205 Contingent equity interest. 

The term contingent equity interest 
means a financial interest (including 
debt) that currently does not constitute 
an equity interest but is convertible into, 
or provides the right to acquire, an 
equity interest upon the occurrence of a 
contingency or defined event or at the 
discretion of the U.S. person that holds 
the financial interest. 

§ 851.206 Controlled foreign entity. 

(a) The term controlled foreign entity 

means any entity incorporated in, or 
otherwise organized under the laws of, 
a country other than the United States 
of which a U.S. person is a parent. 
(b) For purposes of the term 

controlled foreign entity, the following 
rules shall apply in determining 
whether an entity is a parent of another 
entity in a tiered ownership structure: 

(1) Where the relationship between an 

entity and another entity is that of 
parent and subsidiary, the holdings of 
voting interest or voting power of the 
board, as applicable, of a subsidiary 
shall be fully attributed to the parent. 

(2) Where the relationship between an 

entity and another entity is not that of 
parent and subsidiary (i.e., because the 
holdings of voting interest or voting 
power of the board, as applicable, of the 
first entity in the second entity is 50 
percent or less), then the indirect 
downstream holdings of voting interest 
or voting power of the board, as 
applicable, attributed to the first entity 
shall be determined proportionately. 
(3) Where the circumstances in 

paragraphs (b)(1) and (2) of this section 
apply (i.e., because a U.S. person holds 
both direct and indirect downstream 
holdings in the same entity), any 
holdings of voting interest shall be 
aggregated for the purposes of applying 
the definition in this section, and any 
holdings of voting power of the board 
shall be aggregated for the purposes of 
applying the definition in this section. 
Voting interest shall not be aggregated 
with voting power of the board for the 

purposes of applying the definition in 
this section.

any of the following: 

(a) The People’s Republic of China, 
including the Hong Kong and Macau 
Special Administrative Regions; 

(b) The Republic of Cuba; 
(c) The Islamic Republic of Iran; 
(d) The Democratic People’s Republic 

of Korea; 

(e) The Russian Federation; and 
(f) The Bolivarian Republic of 

Venezuela under the regime of Nicola´s 
Maduro Moros.

§ 851.210 Develop. 

Except as used in § 851.209(a)(5), the 

term develop means to: 

(a) Engage, including through or in 
collaboration with any affiliates, in any 
stages prior to serial production, such as 
design or substantive modification; 
design research; design analyses; design 
concepts; assembly and testing of 
prototypes; pilot production schemes; 

design data; transforming design data,

any stage prior to serial production. 

§ 851.211 Entity. 

The term entity means any branch, 
partnership, association, estate, joint 
venture, trust, corporation or division of 
a corporation, group, sub-group, or other 
organization (whether or not organized 
under the laws of any State of the 
United States or foreign state). 

§ 851.212 Excepted transaction. 

The term excepted transaction means 

a transaction that meets the criteria in 
§ 851.501.

The term foreign person means: 
(a) A person that is not: 
(1) A United States citizen or alien 

lawfully admitted for permanent 
residence to the United States; or 

(2) An entity organized under the 

laws of the United States or of any 
jurisdiction within the United States, 
including any foreign branch of any 
such entity; except that: 

(b) For the purposes of § 851.208(a)(5)

§ 851.215 Hypersonic system. 

The term hypersonic system means a 

vehicle or weapon delivery system

§ 851.217 Knowledge. 

Knowledge of a fact or circumstance 

(the term may be a variant, such as 
‘‘know’’) means: 

(a) Actual knowledge that a fact or 
circumstance exists or is substantially 
certain to occur; 

(b) An awareness of a high probability 

of a fact or circumstance’s existence or 
future occurrence; or 

(c) Reason to know of a fact or 

circumstance’s existence. 

Note 1 to § 851.217: See the discussion of

Subpart C—Prohibited Transactions 
and Other Prohibited Activities 

§ 851.301 Undertaking a prohibited 
transaction. 

A U.S. person may not engage in a 

prohibited transaction unless an 
exemption for that transaction has been 
granted under § 851.502 or the 
transaction qualifies for the exemption 
under § 851.503. 

§ 851.302 Actions of a controlled foreign 
entity. 

(a) A U.S. person shall take all 

reasonable steps to prohibit and prevent 
any transaction by its controlled foreign 
entity that would be a prohibited 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person. 

(b) If a controlled foreign entity 

engages in a transaction that would be 
a prohibited transaction if engaged in by 
a U.S. person, in determining whether 
the relevant U.S. person took all 
reasonable steps to prohibit and prevent 
such transaction, the Department of the 
Treasury will consider, among other 
factors, any of the following with 
respect to a U.S. person and its 
controlled foreign entity: 

(1) The execution of agreements with 

respect to compliance with the 
regulations in this part between the 
subject U.S. person and its controlled 
foreign entity; 

(2) The existence and exercise of 

governance or shareholder rights by the 
U.S. person with respect to the 

controlled foreign entity, where 
applicable; 

(3) The existence and implementation 

of periodic training and internal 
reporting requirements by the U.S. 
person and its controlled foreign entity 
with respect to compliance with the 
regulations in this part; 

(4) The implementation of appropriate 

and documented internal controls, 
including internal policies, procedures, 
or guidelines that are periodically 
reviewed internally, by the U.S. person 
and its controlled foreign entity; and 

(5) Implementation of a documented 

testing and/or auditing process of 
internal controls, policies, procedures, 
or guidelines. 

Note 1 to § 851.302: Findings of violations 

of this section and decisions related to 
enforcement and penalties will be made 
based on a consideration of the totality of 
relevant facts and circumstances, including 
whether the U.S. person has taken the steps 
described in paragraph (b) of this section and 
whether such steps were reasonable in light 
of the relevant facts and circumstances. 

Subpart D—Notifiable Transactions 
and Other Notifiable Activities 

§ 851.401 Undertaking a notifiable 
transaction. 

A U.S. person that engages in a 
notifiable transaction shall file a 
notification of that transaction with the 
Department of the Treasury pursuant to 
§ 851.404. 

§ 851.402 Notification of actions of a 
controlled foreign entity. 

A U.S. person shall file a notification 

with the Department of the Treasury 
pursuant to § 851.404 with respect to 
any transaction undertaken by a 
controlled foreign entity of the U.S. 
person that the controlled foreign entity 
or the U.S. person knew at the time of 
the transaction would be a notifiable 
transaction if engaged in by a U.S. 
person. 

§ 851.403 Notification of post-transaction 
knowledge. 

If a U.S. person or its controlled 

foreign entity acquires actual knowledge 
after the completion date of a 
transaction of a fact or circumstance 
such that the transaction would have 
been a notifiable transaction or 
prohibited transaction if such 
knowledge had been possessed by the 
relevant U.S. person at the time of the 
transaction, then the relevant U.S. 
person shall promptly, and in no event 
later than 30 calendar days following 
the acquisition of such knowledge, 
submit a notification pursuant to 
§ 851.404.

Subpart F—Violations 

§ 851.601 Taking actions prohibited by this 
part. 

The taking of any action prohibited by 

this part is a violation of this part. 

§ 851.602 Failure to fulfill requirements. 

Failure to take any action required by 
this part, and within the time frame and 
in the manner specified by the 
regulations in this part, as applicable, is 
a violation of the regulations in this 
part. 

§ 851.603 Misrepresentation, concealment, 
and omission of facts. 

With respect to any information 
submission to or communication with 
the Department of the Treasury 
pursuant to any provision of this part, 
the making of any materially false or 
misleading representation, statement, or 
certification, or falsifying, concealing or 
omitting any material fact is a violation 
of this part. 

§ 851.604 Evasions; attempts; causing 
violations; conspiracies. 

(a) Any action that evades or avoids, 
has the purpose of evading or avoiding, 
causes a violation of, or attempts to 
violate any of the obligations set forth in 
this part is prohibited. 

(b) Any conspiracy formed to violate 

the obligations set forth in this part is 
prohibited. 

§ 851.605 Burden of proof. 

In an enforcement action for a 
violation of this part, the burden of 
proof shall be upon the Secretary. 

Subpart G—Penalties and Disclosures 

§ 851.701 Penalties. 

(a) Section 807 of Title VIII applies to 
any person subject to the jurisdiction of 
the United States who violates, attempts 
to violate, conspires to violate, or causes 
a violation of any order, regulation, or 
notification requirement, or prohibition 
under Title VIII. 

(b) A civil penalty may be imposed on 

any person who violates, attempts to 
violate, conspires to violate, or causes a 
violation of any order, regulation, 
notification requirement, or prohibition 
issued under Title VIII, including any 
provision of this part in amounts 
equivalent to amounts provided for 
under section 206(b) of IEEPA. Those 
amounts are not to exceed the greater of: 

(1) $250,000, as such amount is 
adjusted pursuant to the Federal Civil 
Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act of 
1990, as amended (Pub. L. 101–410, 28 
U.S.C. 2461 note); or 

(2) An amount that is twice the

(c) The civil penalties provided for in

pursuant to the Federal Civil Penalties

(d) The penalties available under this 

section are without prejudice to other 
penalties, civil or criminal, and 
forfeiture of property, available under 
other applicable law. 

(e) Pursuant to 18 U.S.C. 1001,

jurisdiction of the executive, legislative, 
or judicial branch of the Government of

willfully falsifies, conceals or covers up

false, fictitious, or fraudulent statement

false writing or document knowing the 
same to contain any materially false, 
fictitious, or fraudulent statement or 
entry shall be fined under title 18, 
United States Code, or imprisoned not 
more than 5 years, or both. 

§ 851.702 Administrative collection; 
referral to United States Department of 
Justice. 

The imposition of a monetary penalty 

under this part creates an immediate 
debt due to the U.S. Government. The 

Department of the Treasury may take 
action to collect the penalty assessed if 
not paid. In addition or instead, the 
matter may be referred to the 
Department of Justice for appropriate 
action to recover the penalty.

Subpart I—Non-Binding Feedback 

§ 851.901 Eligibility to request feedback. 

(a) This subpart establishes 

procedures by which an eligible person 
may request non-binding feedback from 
the Department of the Treasury as to 
whether a transaction would constitute 
a prohibited transaction under this part. 

(b) Only a person with a direct 

financial interest in the subject 
transaction may submit a request under 
this subpart. 

(c) The transaction that is the subject 

of the request must be an actual—not 
hypothetical—transaction and involve 
only prospective conduct (i.e., conduct 
that has not yet occurred). An executed 
contract is not required, but the material 
terms of the prospective transaction 
must be identified in a binding written 

agreement or other binding document to 
demonstrate that the transaction is not 
hypothetical. 

Note 1 to § 851.901: Because feedback may 

only be requested for prospective conduct, 
submission of a voluntary self-disclosure 
under § 851.704 does not constitute a request 
under this subpart. 

§ 851.902 Submission requirements. 

(a) A request for non-binding feedback 

must be submitted in writing. 

(b) A request must be submitted in 
accordance with the instructions found 
on the Department of the Treasury’s 
Outbound Investment Security Program 
website or as otherwise specified by the 
Department of the Treasury. 

(c) A request must address a single 

transaction. 

(d) A request must include all 
relevant and material information 
bearing on whether the subject 
transaction would constitute a 
prohibited transaction, including: 

(1) Identification of the requester and 
any other persons joining in the request, 
including name, address, place of 
incorporation or organization (if 
applicable), principal place of business, 
citizenship, and points of contact; 

(2) Identification of each party to the 

transaction (including relevant 
intermediaries, financing sources, and 
controlled foreign entities), and each 
covered foreign person, if known; 
(3) A detailed description of the 

contemplated structure of the 
transaction, including the form of 
investment, governance and information 
rights, timing, and completion date; 
(4) A detailed description of the 
products, services, technologies, or 
activities implicated by the transaction

(i) Anonymized guidance to the

§ 851.903 Additional information; 
completeness. 

(a) If a request does not contain all 

information the Department of the 
Treasury deems necessary to review the 
matter, the Department of the Treasury 
may request additional information or 
documents, or a meeting with the 
requester and any other relevant parties 
to the transaction. 

(b) Failure to provide requested 
information within the time frame 
specified by the Department of the 
Treasury may result in the closing of the 
request without providing feedback. 
(c) A request will not be deemed 
complete until the Department of the 
Treasury receives all additional 
information it has requested.

Treasury may request additional information 
from other relevant parties to conduct its 
analysis. 

§ 851.904 Issuance of non-binding 
feedback. 

(a) Receipt of a complete request for 

non-binding feedback by an eligible 
person under this subpart does not 
obligate the Department of the Treasury 
to issue such feedback. 

(b) The Department of the Treasury 
may, in its sole discretion, respond to a 
complete request by providing 
anonymized public guidance, issuing 
confidential, written non-binding 
feedback stating the Department of the 
Treasury’s view as to whether the 
transaction, as described, would 
constitute a covered national security 
transaction in a prohibited technology, 
or declining to provide feedback. When 
providing feedback, the Department of 
the Treasury may, in its sole discretion, 
respond to some, but not all, of the 
questions or information provided by 
the requestor (e.g., non-binding 

feedback limited to whether the foreign 
person is a covered foreign person). 

(c) No oral clearance, release, or other 

statement purporting to provide non- 
binding feedback under this subpart 
may be given. 

(d) Non-binding feedback does not 

express the Department of the 
Treasury’s views on any legal or factual 
issues that may arise under other 
statutes or regulations, or before any 
other department or agency. 

(e) Non-binding feedback may be 

invalidated, modified, or revoked by the 
Department of the Treasury at any time, 
including if it was based on inaccurate, 
incomplete, or outdated information. 

§ 851.905 Effect of non-binding feedback. 
(a) Any non-binding feedback issued 
under this subpart is not binding on the 
Department of the Treasury, any other 
Federal or local department or agency, 
or any court; does not constitute an 
authorization, license, exemption, or 
waiver; and does not preclude the 
Department of the Treasury from taking 
any action authorized by law with 
respect to the transaction, the requester, 
or any other person. 

(b) In determining an appropriate 
response to a potential violation under 
this part, the Department of the 
Treasury may consider, among other 
factors, whether the requester sought 
and reasonably acted consistent with 
non-binding feedback, provided that the 
requester made a true, correct, and 
complete disclosure of all material facts 
and circumstances known to the 
requester at the time of submission. 

§ 851.906 Anonymized public guidance. 

Anonymized public guidance issued 

pursuant to this subpart may take the 
form of answers to frequently asked 
questions, standalone regulatory 
guidance, or anonymized versions of the 
feedback provided to a requestor. 

§ 851.907 Limitations; frivolous feedback 
requests. 

(a) In establishing and administering 

processes under this subpart, the 
Department of the Treasury may reject 
or otherwise limit any request identified 
as frivolous. The Department of the 
Treasury may identify a request as 
frivolous in its sole discretion and based 
on a totality of the circumstances, 
including whether the request: 
(1) Lacks sufficient detail or 
supporting materials to permit 
meaningful review; 

(2) Duplicates, without material 
change, a prior request by the same 
requester; 

(3) Seeks general advisory views or 

policy guidance not specific to the 
transaction described; 

(4) Is submitted in bad faith, for 
purposes of delay, harassment, or to 
burden the Department of the Treasury; 
or 

(5) Otherwise fails to further the 
administration or enforcement of this 
part. 

(b) The Department of the Treasury, in 
its sole discretion, may place limitations 
on a requester that submits a frivolous 
request. Such limitations may include 
declining to accept the request, closing 
the request, requiring consolidation of 
multiple requests, limiting the number 
of requests submitted by a requester 
within a specified period, or requiring 
pre-submission consultation, as 
specified by the Department of the 
Treasury through public notice or 
instructions published on the 
Department of the Treasury’s Outbound 
Investment Security Program website. 

§ 851.908 Signature requirement. 

Confidential non-binding feedback 

must be signed by the Assistant 
Secretary of the Treasury for Investment 
Security, a Deputy Assistant Secretary 
for Investment Security, or another 
official acting pursuant to § 851.1101 or 
other applicable delegation.

Subpart K—Other Provisions 

§ 851.1101 Delegation of authorities of the 
Secretary of the Treasury. 

Any action that the Secretary is 

authorized to take pursuant to Title VIII 
may be taken by the Assistant Secretary 
of the Treasury for Investment Security 

or their designee or by any other person 
to whom the Secretary has delegated the 
authority so to act, as appropriate. 

§ 851.1102 Amendment, modification, or 
revocation. 

(a) Except as otherwise provided by 
law, the Secretary, in consultation with 
the Secretary of Commerce, the 
Secretary of State, and, as appropriate, 
the heads of other relevant Federal 
departments and agencies, may amend, 
modify, or revoke provisions of this part 
at any time. 

(b) Except as otherwise provided by 

law, any instructions, orders, forms, 
regulations, or rulings issued pursuant 
to this part may be amended, modified, 
or revoked at any time. 

(c) Unless otherwise specifically 

provided, any amendment, 
modification, or revocation of any 
provision in this part does not affect any 
act done or omitted, or any civil or 
criminal proceeding commenced or 
pending, prior to such amendment, 
modification, or revocation. All 
penalties, forfeitures, and liabilities 
under any such instructions, orders, 
forms, regulations, or rulings pursuant 
to this part continue and may be 
enforced as if such amendment, 
modification, or revocation had not 
been made. 

§ 851.1103 Severability. 

The provisions of this part are 
separate and severable from one 
another. If any of the provisions of this 
part, or the application thereof to any 
person or circumstance, is held to be 
invalid, such invalidity shall not affect 
other provisions or application of such 
provisions to other persons or 
circumstances that can be given effect 
without the invalid provision or 
application. 

§ 851.1104 Reports to be furnished on 
demand. 

(a) Any person is required to furnish 

under oath, in the form of reports or 
otherwise, at any time as may be 
required by the Department of the 
Treasury, complete information 
regarding any act or transaction subject 
to the provisions of this part, regardless 
of whether such act or transaction is 
effected pursuant to a national interest 
exemption under § 851.502. Except as 
provided otherwise, the Department of 
the Treasury may, through any person 
or agency, conduct investigations, hold 
hearings, administer oaths, examine 
witnesses, receive evidence, take 
depositions, and require by subpoena 
the attendance and testimony of 
witnesses and the production of any 
books, contracts, letters, papers, and 
other hard copy or electronic documents 
relating to any matter under 
investigation, regardless of whether any 
report has been required or filed under 
this section. 

(b) For purposes of paragraph (a) of 

this section, the term document 
includes any written, recorded, or 
graphic matter or other means of 
preserving thought or expression 
(including in electronic format), and all 
tangible things stored in any medium 
from which information can be 
processed, transcribed, or obtained 
directly or indirectly. 

(c) Persons providing documents to 

the Department of the Treasury 
pursuant to this section must do so in 
a usable format agreed upon by the 
Department of the Treasury.
