## Bottom line

Unchanged in direction, firmer in evidence: **the chain is breaking from the bottom, at delivery, not at the paying hyperscalers.** NextDC's own release (primary, read today) confirms the guidance cut and shows *why* the developer tier is the weak link — the power distributor suspended works on community opposition, A$140M of contracted revenue left FY27 guidance, the customer holds termination rights and NextDC is weighing force majeure — while its balance sheet is not the problem (A$8.7B liquidity, nothing due before December 2029). **CoreWeave's unsecured parent stays the largest single weak node** (stock −9.75% today to $55.79, no new bond print found). **Meta's 10-Q, filed today, puts a primary number on the top of the chain: $810.25B of uncommenced leases plus non-cancelable commitments, $121.41B of it due in 2027** — that is the collateral the neoclouds borrow against. Securitised data-centre paper moves **down** the ranking: DEWEY finds non-investment-grade AI tenants are a sliver of a ~$58–61B market and the stress there is pricing, not credit losses. **S5 implication: no new qualifying event — S5 4, n=2, composite 15/25.**

## Why the order moved where it did

- **Rows 7 and 8 swap.** BDC shares trade at ~0.70× June NAV — an observed, priced discount on a holder class that DEWEY finds also lends to CoreWeave and Crusoe (BDC filings, amounts not extracted) — while securitised DC paper has an IG-tenant base, granular colocation pools, and no credit event on record (DEWEY). Securitisation takes only stabilised, leased buildings; construction and power risk stays with rows 1, 3 and 7.
- **Row 1 holds #1 on a sharper reason.** NextDC is not short of money; it is short of electricity. The weak link is the gap between a contracted customer and a grid connection — service-level credits, termination rights, and project-level lenders behind it. Lam's supply-capped guide is the same pattern one layer up.
- **Rows 9–10 run real funding gaps but are not where it breaks.** All five payers are cash-negative after capex and shareholder returns in 2026E (DEWEY), yet they owe only ~$34B of principal through 2027 and are funding at investment grade, at a rising price. The gap is a *demand on the bond market*, not a default risk; the long-duration buyer is the constraint to watch. Oracle is the exception that keeps it at #4: its gap is the widest relative to its cash, and its cash flow is flattered by $16.0B of customer prepayments.
- **Row 9 is louder, not weaker.** Meta's CDS record and the −7% day are real, but the 10-Q shows no principal due in 2026, $2.75B in 2027 and $82.45B of cash (8-K). Its $121.41B of 2027 commitments is what makes **row 2's** counterparties creditworthy; a Meta pullback would hit rows 1–2 first.
