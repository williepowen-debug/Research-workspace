# Siemens Energy FY2026 — supplier tell for SoftBank's SB7 write-off [KB-VULCAN-422]

**Written:** 2026-11-11 ~08:50 ET, VULCAN (Claude Code, Opus 5.5), on PROME prome-2b's wake of catalyst row 2026-11-11 (WQ-184). Follow-up to `reports/2026-11-10_softbank/REPORT.md` (known-unknown 8) and KB-VULCAN-419.

## Bottom line

Siemens Energy's release, deck and call contain **no** wording about a data-center customer cancelling, deferring, re-planning or settling a gas-turbine or grid-equipment order, and **no** forfeited deposits. No customer is named: SoftBank, Silver Bands 7, SB Energy, Stargate, OpenAI and Ohio/Piketon do not appear in the release, the deck or the verbatim transcript text. That is a clean negative. **It does not tell us who SB7's supplier was:** Siemens Energy remains UNKNOWN, neither confirmed nor excluded.

Two softer facts stand beside the negative:
1. **Q4 conversions stopped.** In Q4 Gas Services booked about 2 GW of new units and converted 0 GW of slot reservations. In Q3 it booked 15 GW and converted 12 GW. Over the same quarter it *added* 14 GW of new reservations. Morgan Stanley asked about "cancellations on some of those slot reservations in this week" (Texas, Ohio). The CEO answered: "Maybe also with the 2 GW, to put this into perspective, that is really a timing effect."
2. **Expired reservations go back to the supplier quietly.** CEO: "whenever timing runs out on the SRAs, that wherever we can, we also take back the slots to us". Deck p.10: Siemens Energy can give the slots to another customer if a project does not progress on time. So a buyer that walks away from a reservation would show up here as a slot handed back, not as a cancellation. A clean negative on the word "cancellation" therefore cannot rule out a reservation that was walked away from.

The roll-forward below shows at most a few GW of reservations leaving other than by conversion. Rounding is too large to tell whether any did.

**Roll-forward test.** Backlog: 69 + 2 − 6 = 65, which closes exactly. Reservations: 26 + 14 − 0 = 40 against 39 reported, leaving 1 GW unexplained. All four inputs are rounded to whole GW, so the true residual lies somewhere between about −1 and +3 GW. A small take-back or lapse can be neither shown nor excluded. A whole-campus walk-away would not fit; SB7's write-off was $2.9B of advances, size in GW unknown.

**Other wording, release and deck.** The only "reservation" text is release p.1: "the recent quarter continued to benefit from customer advance payments and reservation fees on the back of continued high order intake" (repeated p.1–2). Deck p.10: the 39 GW of SRAs are "expected to be converted into firm orders in FY27". Data-center customers are about €15bn of the €164bn backlog (CFO, call); about one-fifth of grid demand (deck p.12 / CFO).
