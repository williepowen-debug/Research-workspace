## Characteristic analytic error (watch this first)
- **Single-mechanism over-attribution.** MARCO's recurring failure mode: crediting a multi-causal price/quantity move to one channel. Twice-burned: **produce CPI** (v2.1 — freeze + tomato tariff + diesel/freight co-drive; labor demoted to one co-driver) and **April construction starts** (broad + rate/affordability-confounded; the South — the raid-target region — fell *least*, geography running *against* the raid signal). **Before crediting a multi-causal move to one channel: run the multi-causal check, and separate mechanism-intact from threshold-breached** ([[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]). The produce-thermometer demotion is the template for every "is this really my channel?" call. *(The June-2026 LFPR-collapse temptation — "the labor-force contraction IS the immigration shock" — is the same trap: it's also aging + discouraged workers + degraded CPS response. Immigration is a named material co-driver, not sole.)*
- **Load-bearing figure with a mis-attributed authoritative source (the "2.2M CBO" lesson, v2.6 2026-07-02).** MARCO's *founding* Channel-1 magnitude — "2.2M self-deportations (CBO)" — was **wrong on both count and source** and propagated fleet-wide for months. It was a *disputed DHS* claim (CMS "Two Million Deportation Myth"), NOT CBO (CBO removals ≈290K+30K); realized was ~1.0M foreign-born LF / ~1.5M pop. The attribution to an authoritative body (CBO) is exactly what made it sticky and un-questioned. MARCO's own NOTES.md/MAINTENANCE T1-B had flagged the tension; it took a PROME Tier-2 workflow-verify to force the reconcile. **Lesson: a number's *source attribution* is as load-bearing as the number — verify "who actually said this" for any smoking-gun figure, especially one attributed to a neutral authority; a self-flagged data-quality tension is a debt, resolve it, don't carry it.** (General principle → [[finding_number_carries_threshold_unit_source]] / root CLAUDE.md "agent data can be hallucinated — PSEC PIK was 8.6% not 35%".)

- **Universal claims about MARCO's own metrics, written in prose, escape the rigor MARCO applies to its own tests (2026-07-25, session 18; general principle → [[finding_prose_claims_escape_test_rigor]]).** Same session, two standards: ES-MARCO-08 (pre-registered test) got a BLS-API primary pull, a series-label catch (carried "+6.1% fresh F&V" was actually `SAF113` aggregate, not `SAF1131`), and a result reported *against* the thesis — while the sentence *"every FL metric I own is improving"* shipped in the same summary without `VX.tsv` ever being opened. - **🔴 THE HELPER I BUILT TO FIX ONE SILENT-CORRUPTION CLASS INTRODUCED ANOTHER, AND IT COMPOUNDED FOUR TIMES BEFORE ANYTHING CAUGHT IT (2026-08-21).** `tsvutil.read_tsv` was written to stop banner-related silent breakage — and its own body did a **raw `split("\t")`**, which is not CSV-aware. So a quoted field came back with its **escaping as literal characters**; `csv.writer` then escaped what was already escaped. **Every read→edit→write pass DOUBLED the quotes.** Five VX rows went from **12 decoded quotes to 3,574** across four commits in one session (46 → 214 → 3,574 → 7,158 raw). **Three of the five were rows I never intended to touch** — collateral from rewriting the whole file each pass.
  - ⚠️ **Nothing surfaced it.** Every check I ran stayed green: field counts uniform, row counts right, dates parsed, boot clean, `version_drift` clean, staleness output byte-identical. **The corruption lived inside a field, and no check I owned looked inside a field.** It took a **byte-level `git diff`** on an unrelated question ("why is a 2-ID renumber a 105-line diff?") to expose it.
  - ⚠️ **And my first read of the evidence was WRONG in the reassuring direction:** I compared quote counts against `HEAD~8`, which already contained the damage, concluded "identical, so I didn't cause it," and nearly stopped. **Only re-checking against the actual pre-session commit showed 32 → 7,158.** *A baseline that post-dates the damage will always exonerate you* — the same class as [[finding_apparent_confabulation_is_often_a_baseline_mismatch]], committed while investigating that very lesson.
  - **Two other things the same trace exposed:** `csv.writer` defaults to `lineterminator="\r\n"` on every platform, so routine edits had turned VX into a **mixed CRLF/LF file** (58 CRLF rows under 26 LF banner lines); and a raw split **silently truncates fields containing embedded newlines** — VX has several, which is why its 57 rows occupy 91 physical lines.
  - **Fixes:** `read_tsv` is now `csv.reader`-based; `write_tsv`/`banner_of` added and are the only sanctioned write path (LF-pinned, banner-preserving); the 5 rows repaired (3 restored verbatim from the pre-session commit, 2 collapsed and re-authored); **round-trip stability is now an asserted test** across VX/KB/FLOW, and all three boot scripts verified byte-identical before and after.
  - **Rule: for any ledger you edit programmatically, assert `read(write(read(x))) == read(x)` ONCE. A stable round-trip would have caught this on the first pass; row counts and field counts never will, because they are blind to what is inside a cell.**

- **BASIS ERROR #6, and it is a NEW sub-type: I did not use a wrong baseline — I ASSUMED an asymmetry that wasn't there, and called a real published figure an artifact (2026-08-21, caught on my own review pass in the same session).** NV Gaming headlined percentage-fee tax collections at **−6.99% YoY** under a footnote reading *"collections are through July 21."* I concluded partial-vs-full, **refused the number**, and wrote that into `VX-TAX-01`, STATUS, SCRATCH, the archive doc **and the NEXUS brief** — a consumer surface. **The footnote is a standing convention, not an asymmetry:** the prior month's release carries the identical note (*"through June 23"*). ⚠️ **A limitation disclosed on ONE side is not evidence the other side lacks it.**
  - **The discriminator I should have run first, and it takes one pass:** a real one-sided truncation produces a **systematic directional skew**. Twelve FY26 monthly prints: **mean +7.09%, 6 of 12 negative, FY total +5.07%** — no skew ⇒ no asymmetry ⇒ the figure is real.
  - **The correct finding was STRONGER than the one it replaced:** those prints carry **σ = 14.04pp** (−12.35% to +33.80%), so −6.99% is a **−1.0σ move with 2 of 12 months at least as negative — no signal.** *Base-rating the series answered what "is this fake?" could not.* And the stale text I was replacing had made the **mirror-image error** — three prints called "clear deterioration" in that same 14pp-σ series. **Both directions are the same root: a BASIS question answered by inspection instead of by measurement.**
  - ⚠️ **The uncomfortable part, recorded because it is the actual lesson: this error was produced BY my correction discipline, not despite it.** I was pattern-matching to the basis-trap family at the top of this section — and the pattern fired on a case where it did not apply. **Having a good failure-mode library makes false-positive corrections cheaper to reach.** Before withdrawing a figure, apply the same standard to the withdrawal that you would to an adoption ([[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]] — *"also points INWARD: verify the number that makes you RETRACT"*). *(General principle → [[finding_apparent_confabulation_is_often_a_baseline_mismatch]], now carrying this case as its inverse direction.)*

- **MARCO applies its own base-effect discipline to one instrument and not the other — and the gap is invisible until a threshold fires (2026-08-11, session 21).** MARCO has written, repeatedly and correctly, that *"the April recovery was base-effect"* and frames **TOUR-01 on a 2-yr stack** precisely so a flattering YoY cannot break it.
