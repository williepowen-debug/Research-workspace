### #1 — The U.S. Job Market Just Went Negative

**The finding:** In February 2026, the U.S. economy lost 92,000 jobs — the first negative payroll print of this entire cycle, months ahead of most forecasts.

**Why it matters:** This isn't a "slowdown" — it's a reversal. The job market was supposed to be fine. Instead, federal workers are being laid off, healthcare hiring is contracting, and the professional services sector (staffing, temps, business support) shed 30,000 jobs in February alone. These are not lagging indicators. The labor market is cracking now. This feeds directly into consumer spending stress (CARL's domain) and accelerates the case for Fed rate cuts — which reprices everything.

**Source:** Bureau of Labor Statistics, Nonfarm Payrolls February 2026 (released March 6, 2026); ADP National Employment Report February 2026

**Strength:** 🔴 Smoking gun

> **Slide sentence:** *"The U.S. economy shed 92,000 jobs in February — the first negative payroll print of the cycle, arriving months ahead of consensus."*

### #2 — 2.2 Million People Quietly Left the Workforce

**The finding:** An estimated 2.2 million people self-deported from the United States in 2025, on top of 675,000 formal deportations — a total of nearly 3 million people removed from the labor pool.

**Why it matters:** The visible job losses in agriculture (-155,000) and construction (-93% growth rate vs. national) are the tip of the iceberg. The full labor supply shock is 10–15x larger. These aren't people who were replaced — these are workers in essential industries (farms, job sites, restaurants, meatpacking) who vanished, leaving gaps no one is filling. The domestic applicant pool for H-2A agricultural visas was literally 182 people out of 415,000 slots. Americans are not taking these jobs.

**Source:** DHS data via WSJ/EADaily aggregates; USDA/OFLC H-2A certification data (FY2025)

**Strength:** 🔴 Smoking gun

> **Slide sentence:** *"2.2 million people self-deported in 2025 — creating a labor supply shock 10–15x larger than what shows up in agricultural employment data."*

### #3 — Farmers Are Running Out of Hands During Planting Season

**The finding:** The H-2A agricultural guest worker program — the legal channel farms use when they can't find domestic workers — just issued a federal "Harvest Alert" warning of "catastrophic" shortages heading into the 2026 planting season.

**Why it matters:** Planting season runs March through May. That window is now. Farms that can't plant don't harvest. Harvests that don't happen create food shortages and price spikes. The DOL's own new streamlined system for H-2A is failing internally. Wage floors are spiking (Michigan and Illinois above $18.50/hr for entry-level farm work). Midwest farms are already deploying autonomous drone swarms as emergency substitutes. This feeds directly to California produce prices projected up 15%+ in H2 2026 — before the oil shock and tariffs layer on top.

**Source:** DOL "Harvest Alert" 2026 (March 2026); Investigate Midwest (March 3, 2026); DOL AEWR data; DJI Agras T40 deployment reports

**Strength:** 🔴 Smoking gun

> **Slide sentence:** *"The federal government just issued a 'Harvest Alert' — catastrophic farm labor shortages are arriving during the exact weeks farmers need to plant."*

### #4 — Canadians Aren't Coming Back, and the Airlines Know It

**The finding:** Canadian visitors to the U.S. fell 28% in 2025 — and airlines have already physically moved their planes to other routes, meaning capacity is gone for years.

**Why it matters:** This isn't a sentiment shift that reverses next quarter. Flair Airlines cut U.S. routes by 58% and grew domestic Canadian routes by 34%. WestJet shifted to Mexico (+40%). Air Transat canceled ALL 11 Canada-to-U.S. summer routes including Fort Lauderdale. When airlines restructure their fleets around new routes, they don't snap back in one season. The Canada Tourism Index hit 0.72 — the crisis threshold is 0.75. And 62% of Canadians say they are less likely to visit the U.S. in 2026. This isn't a boycott. It's a structural rerouting of travel dollars.

**Source:** Statistics Canada; OAG airline capacity data; Flair/WestJet/Air Transat route announcements; Angus Reid polling (Canada)

**Strength:** 🔴 Smoking gun

> **Slide sentence:** *"Canadian airlines didn't just pull passengers — they physically moved their planes. That capacity takes years to come back."*

### #5 — Florida Just Lost 93% of Its Population Growth

**The finding:** Florida's net domestic migration collapsed from 311,000 people in 2022 to just 22,500 in 2025 — a 93% drop — and the state fell from #1 to #8 destination in the country.

**Why it matters:** Florida doesn't have a state income tax. Its government runs on property taxes, sales taxes, and the spending of new arrivals and tourists. When migration collapses, that revenue pipeline dries up. Simultaneously, international migration (which was masking the domestic decline in 2024) has been severed by the termination of CHNV and TPS programs. Florida condo inventory is at 8.8 months supply — near the 9-month threshold that signals a distressed market. Days on market are up 41% year-over-year. The math is simple: fewer people, more inventory, declining prices, lower tax revenue.

**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau, Net Domestic Migration Estimates (February 2026); Florida Realtors Association; BLS State Employment Data

**Strength:** 🔴 Smoking gun

> **Slide sentence:** *"Florida went from the #1 domestic migration destination to #8 in three years — and the international pipeline that was masking it has been cut off."*

### #6 — Mexico Remittances Are Falling for the First Time Since the Financial Crisis

**The finding:** Money sent from the U.S. to Mexico fell 4.6% in FY2025 — the worst decline since the 2009 financial crisis — and January 2026 marked the first January decline since 2015.

**Why it matters:** Remittances are the most direct, real-time signal that immigrant workers are leaving the U.S. economy. These aren't surveys — they're actual wire transfers. A decline this large and sustained means the workers are gone, not just scared. Mexico received $61.8 billion in remittances in 2025; a 4.6% drop is $2.8 billion removed from the circuit. The January 2026 figure ($4.594B, -1.4% YoY) arriving with no seasonal bounce confirms this is structural. The 1% U.S. remittance tax that took effect January 1, 2026 adds further friction going forward.

**Source:** Banco de México (Banxico) official monthly data; Mexico Business News/Mexico News Daily (published March 2, 2026)

**Strength:** 🟠 Strong signal

> **Slide sentence:** *"Remittances to Mexico — the most direct measure of whether immigrant workers are still here — just posted their worst decline since the 2008 financial crisis."*

### #7 — Food Prices Were Already Rising Before the Shocks Arrived

**The finding:** Food CPI ran at +3.1% year-over-year and +0.4% month-over-month in February 2026 — and that's the baseline before oil prices, farm labor shortages, or tariff pass-through have shown up in the data.

**Why it matters:** February CPI has zero contamination from the oil spike (Brent +21%), zero contamination from the H-2A planting-season crisis, and zero tariff pass-through yet measured. The March and April CPI prints — released in mid-April and mid-May — will be the first reads on all three stressors hitting simultaneously. Apparel was already up 1.3% month-over-month in February (tariff signal). The February food number is the floor, not the ceiling. Consumers are already under pressure; the compounding hasn't started yet.

**Source:** Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index February 2026 (released March 2026)

**Strength:** 🟠 Strong signal

> **Slide sentence:** *"February food inflation — 3.1% annually — is the baseline before oil prices, farm labor shortages, and tariffs have even shown up in the data."*

### #8 — Border Cities Are Watching Their Tax Base Evaporate

**The finding:** Mexican shoppers — once the economic backbone of U.S. border cities — have collapsed from 51% of Laredo's retail to 13%, and Nogales has seen residential property values fall 43%.

**Why it matters:** These aren't abstract fiscal projections — they're real cities with real bond obligations and underfunded pensions. El Paso is running a $55–62 million deficit with a pension that's only 60% funded. Imperial County, California — 20–31% unemployment, already carrying BBB credit — is one shock away from a downgrade. Nogales, Arizona, earns 60% of its revenues from cross-border economic activity that has effectively stopped. When municipalities can't pay their bills, they cut services, raise taxes, or both — accelerating the out-migration that caused the problem in the first place.

**Source:** Municipal fiscal disclosures; EMMA (Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board); Texas A&M Border Economy reports; Arizona Department of Revenue; Imperial County credit reports

**Strength:** 🟠 Strong signal

> **Slide sentence:** *"Border cities that built their budgets on cross-border shoppers are now watching the shoppers disappear — and the bond market hasn't fully priced it yet."*

### #9 — Americans Are Leaving Too — First Time Since the Great Depression

**The finding:** The United States posted a net migration figure of negative 150,000 people in 2025 — meaning more people left the country than arrived — the first time that's happened since the 1930s.

**Why it matters:** This is the thesis going recursive: it's not just immigrants leaving, it's Americans. Top destinations include Spain, Netherlands, Ireland, and Czech Republic — the number of Americans moving to Ireland alone has doubled in a decade. Brookings Institution projects this outflow to increase in 2026. A country that can't retain its own population is in a different category of stress than one experiencing a cyclical immigration slowdown. This amplifies the Sun Belt reversal thesis — it's not domestic reshuffling, it's absolute population loss.

**Source:** WSJ, "Americans Are Emigrating" (February 26, 2026); Brookings Institution; 15-country sample analysis

**Strength:** 🟠 Strong signal

> **Slide sentence:** *"For the first time since the 1930s, more people left the United States than arrived — and economists expect the trend to accelerate in 2026."*

### #10 — The Data We'd Need to Track This Is Being Deleted

**The finding:** USDA canceled its Agricultural Labor Survey. DOL canceled the National Agricultural Worker Survey. The primary tools for measuring the farm labor shortage have been eliminated at the exact moment the shortage is peaking.

**Why it matters:** This isn't a caveat — it's a signal amplifier. When you eliminate the instruments that would measure the shock, the shock becomes harder to price. Markets can't react to data that doesn't exist. The H-2A tracking system is already straining (the DOL's own "one-stop shop" is failing internally per leaked emails). If the labor crisis deepens invisibly — no surveys, no measurements, no published data — the first time the market sees it will be in food prices, in earnings misses from agricultural companies, in municipal revenue shortfalls. By then, it's already priced in for anyone paying attention now.

**Source:** USDA Agricultural Labor Survey cancellation notice; DOL National Agricultural Worker Survey cancellation; Investigate Midwest (March 3, 2026); DOL internal communications

**Strength:** 🟡 Supporting evidence

> **Slide sentence:** *"The government just eliminated the surveys that would have told us how bad the farm labor shortage is — meaning the market will be flying blind when the data finally arrives."*
