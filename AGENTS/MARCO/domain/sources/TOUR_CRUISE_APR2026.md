# CRUISE INDUSTRY — FL TRANSMISSION LAYER (2026-04-20)

## HEADLINE
Canadian cruise demand to FL ports is softening but is numerically small (~5% of NA source) — not yet a thesis-mover in aggregate port data. The real signal is operator-level: NCLH guided Q1 2026 Caribbean net yield -1.6% on "execution missteps" against a 40% capacity surge, and Air Transat's Winter 2026-27 schedule restores FL routes at a reduced footprint (11 weekly flights from Eastern Canada) after cancelling all summer 2026 US routes. Cruise is the most-insulated FL tourism layer because it's a global source market — but the pre/post-cruise hotel and airport transfer layer at PortMiami / Fort Lauderdale is more Canadian-exposed than headline port volumes suggest.

## CANADIAN CRUISE SHARE (BASELINE)
- **Canada as a cruise source market:** ~1.0M cruisers in 2023 (CLIA 2024 report). North America total was 18.1M in 2023 and 20.53M in 2024 — so Canada is roughly **5% of North American passenger source**, ~3% of global source market of 34.6M (2024) / 37.2M (2025).
- **NA dominates the Caribbean:** ~75% of Caribbean cruise passengers are from North America (Hope Research Group, CLIA 2024 Global Market Report). Canadian specifically is estimated 3-6% of FL-embarking pax — not disclosed publicly by operators, estimated via NA share × Canada/NA ratio.
- **Key baseline point:** Cruise is MUCH less Canadian-exposed than FL hotel/condo tourism. The marginal transmission is in **ancillary spend** (pre/post-cruise hotel nights in Miami/Fort Lauderdale, airport transfers at MIA/FLL/MCO), not the cruise ticket itself.

## ITINERARY REDEPLOYMENT

**Caribbean capacity is INCREASING, not decreasing:**
- Order book adds +28% Caribbean capacity by 2030 (58 new ships, Hope Research Group)
- RCL 2026: Caribbean = 57% of capacity, +8% growth. Harmony of the Seas repositioning to Port Canaveral winter 2026; Legend of the Seas (largest-ever Icon-class) to Port Everglades Nov 2026
- CCL 2026-27: Carnival Valor/Liberty anchoring New Orleans Caribbean; Panorama/Radiance/Firenze in Long Beach Mexican Riviera (3 ships, diversifying away from FL embark)
- NCLH: 40% Caribbean capacity increase at Norwegian brand Q1 2026 (the execution problem)

**Alaska / Europe capacity also growing (not a Caribbean redeployment):**
- RCL Ovation of the Seas → Alaska spring 2026 (Quantum-class first)
- RCL Liberty of the Seas → Europe summer 2026 (Southampton, Norwegian fjords)
- RCL Harmony of the Seas → Europe summer 2026, FL winter 2026

**Signal:** Operators are ADDING Caribbean supply (RCL +8%, NCLH +40%), not redeploying away. This means the Caribbean yield test comes from supply absorbing demand — not demand collapse. NCLH already failing that test.

## FL DOWNSTREAM ECONOMICS

**Per-passenger ancillary spend (est.):**
- Pre/post-cruise hotel: 40-50% of cruisers take 1-2 nights pre-cruise in embarkation city (CLIA / industry consensus)
- Airport transfer: MIA/FLL/MCO handle cruise pax via rental / shuttle / Uber — $30-80 per head
- Est. ancillary spend per cruiser: $200-400 in hotel + transport in FL
- Combined FL ports 21.9M pax × 40% hotel-night uptake × ~$300 ancillary = **~$2.6B annual FL downstream cruise-adjacent economy**

**Canadian slice (estimated):**
- If Canadians = 4-5% of FL-embarked cruisers and Canadian bookings -20% (PAX Feb 2025 reading), incremental lost FL downstream = ~**$25-50M annually** — NOT thesis-material at aggregate level
- BUT: geographically concentrated in Fort Lauderdale (Port Everglades) where Air Transat historically flew from Montreal/Quebec City. This is the Broward canary mechanism MARCO already tracks.

**Hotel REIT exposure:**
- Pebblebrook (PEB): Only one named FL property — LaPlaya Naples (Gulf Coast, not cruise-adjacent). Portfolio-wide Q4 2025 RevPAR +2.9%. Not a clean FL cruise play.
- Apple Hospitality (APLE): 84 markets, Tampa/Brandon presence, 2025 RevPAR -1.6%, 2026 guide flat. Broad exposure, not FL-concentrated.
- **No pure-play FL cruise-adjacent REIT in the public sphere.** Operator equities (CCL/RCL/NCLH) are more direct. Cruise Canada Hollywood Beach etc. are privately held.

## CROSS-REFERENCE WITH AIR TRANSAT/WESTJET ROUTE EXITS

**This is the real transmission signal, not cruise operator earnings:**

- **Air Transat (4/17/26 announcement):** Winter 2026-27 restores FL with **11 weekly flights from Eastern Canada** (MTL 7, QC 3, HAL 1) — starting Nov 15-Dec 16, 2026. This is a REDUCED footprint vs prior years (though exact prior-year capacity not disclosed in article).
- Air Transat cancelled ALL US summer 2026 routes, describing "cutting its full-year transborder flying by close to 10 percent."
- **WestJet** suspended 16 Canada-US routes for summer 2026; spokesperson cited "notable decline in transborder demand throughout 2025 and no sign of near-term recovery"; ~10% transborder capacity cut.
- **Aggregate:** WestJet + Air Canada removed ~450,000 seats from US routes Q1 2026 (Campspot CEO Scheinman cited 32% YoY drop in Canadian travelers to US Q1 2026).

**Flight+cruise bundle implication:** Canadians booking pre/post-cruise FL hotel nights are heavily dependent on seasonal Air Transat and WestJet FLL/MCO/MIA service. With summer 2026 FL routes cancelled and winter 2026-27 restored at likely reduced gauge, the **pre/post-cruise hotel night in Broward/Miami-Dade is the transmission point** — Canadians who still cruise are likely to either skip the FL hotel night (fly in day-of) or substitute to non-US embarkation (MSC Mediterranean pattern). This is consistent with MARCO's $600M-$1.2B winter 2026-27 FL loss projection and the Broward TDT canary thesis.

## CONFIDENCE + CAVEATS

**HIGH confidence:**
- NCLH Q1 2026 -1.6% Caribbean yield (direct guidance)
- FL port FY2025 records (all three ports, audited volumes)
- Air Transat/WestJet route cuts and winter 2026-27 schedule (press releases, dated)
- Canadian = ~5% of NA cruise source (CLIA 2023/2024 reports)

**MEDIUM confidence:**
- Attribution of NCLH Caribbean softness to Canadian demand specifically — NCLH cites execution missteps, NOT Canadian demand. Could be coincident rather than causal.
- $25-50M Canadian ancillary FL-cruise loss estimate — built from estimates of % Canadian + % hotel-night uptake + ancillary spend; none of these are disclosed by operators.
- 32% Q1 2026 Canadian US travel drop (Campspot CEO, not government data).

**LOW confidence / blind spots:**
- Operators don't disclose source-market breakdown. CCL/RCL/NCLH Q1 2026 calls contain ZERO specific Canadian commentary despite the trade war context — suggests either (a) Canadian exposure is genuinely small, or (b) operators are avoiding the topic.
- FY2026 FL port monthly data not yet published — we're flying blind on whether Oct 2025 - Mar 2026 pax volumes slipped.
- No CLIA Canada 2025/2026 country-specific passenger figure found.

**Key challenge to MARCO's Canadian thesis:** Cruise is proving to be a highly substitutable vacation category — Canadians are switching to MSC Mediterranean / Japan / Antarctic instead of cancelling. So cruise does NOT amplify the Canadian softness; it arguably absorbs it. The Canadian-FL stress transmits via flight bookings and hotel nights, NOT via cruise embarkation counts. This argues for maintaining the winter 2026-27 snowbird thesis but NOT expanding to cruise operator shorts (except NCLH on company-specific execution).

**Summary:** Aggregate cruise data is a distraction from the real transmission — which is Canadian air capacity cuts → pre/post-cruise hotel nights → Broward/Miami-Dade TDT. NCLH is the only cruise equity with a tradeable negative setup, and its problem is self-inflicted, not Canadian.
