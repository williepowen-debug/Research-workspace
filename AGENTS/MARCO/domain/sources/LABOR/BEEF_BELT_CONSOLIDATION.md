# BEEF-BELT CONSOLIDATION — Cattle Cycle vs Self-Deportation Untangle

**Owner:** MARCO | **Built:** 2026-04-21 | **Data:** `baselines/beef_belt_layoffs.tsv`
**Purpose:** Test Will's hypothesis that 75-yr-low cattle herd is driving packer consolidation → concentrated layoffs in immigrant-heavy beef-belt towns → SDL-01 compound effect.

---

## VERDICT

**Hypothesis PARTIALLY supported, but the SDL-01 compound claim is NOT.** The first half of Will's thesis holds decisively: Tyson alone has announced ~6,500 beef/pork-belt job losses 2024–2026 (Perry IA 1,276, Emporia KS 809, Lexington NE 3,212 + Lexington extension 294 + Fortrex sanitation 139, Amarillo TX 1,761 B-shift). These are concentrated in towns with 40–65% foreign-born or Hispanic populations. Tyson's FY25 beef segment loss of $426M and FY26 guidance of –$400–600M make further capacity rationalization likely. Cattle inventory (86.2M head Jan 2026, 75-year low) plus packer capacity utilization dropping to 77% (Oct 2025) confirms this is a structural cycle event, not a one-off. The Big 4 (Tyson, JBS, Cargill, National Beef) control 83-85% of U.S. beef; when one moves, the others follow with roughly a quarter lag.

**However, the SDL-01 compound mechanism fails on the evidence available.** The Flatwater Free Press on-the-ground interviews with six laid-off Lexington workers found ZERO planning to return to Mexico, Guatemala, or elsewhere — they are relocating within the U.S. (Omaha, Kearney, North Platte, South Sioux City, Kansas). Tyson is actively offering internal transfers (Emporia → Holcomb KS absorbed ~200). The workers being displaced by cycle-driven consolidation are disproportionately **long-tenured, documented or mixed-status families rooted in their towns for 20-35 years**; they resemble the 1990-wave immigrants who built these plants, not the newly-arrived undocumented cohort most at risk of self-deportation under SDL-01. Cattle-cycle displacement therefore produces **intra-U.S. labor reshuffling**, not international outflows. The SAME observable (empty houses in Lexington, school enrollment drops) can emerge, but the transmission is different: cycle-driven layoffs feed REGINALD (rural CRE / regional bank loan stress) and CARL (consumer stress in beef-belt counties), not SDL-01 remittance/border flows. MARCO should open a **separate vector** — tentatively VX-MARCO-BEEFBELT-01 — rather than amend SDL-01, and downgrade expectations that meatpacking throughput is a SDL-01 leading indicator. Watch the parallel ICE raid track (Glenn Valley Foods Omaha June 2025, 76 arrests) as the actual SDL-01 signal path.

## 2 — COMPANY EARNINGS-CALL EXTRACTION

### Tyson Foods (TSN) — most complete signal

- **FY2025 Q4 (Nov 2025):** Beef segment operating loss $94M (Q4) and $426M (full year), up from $291M loss prior year. Sales $21.6B on 1.9% volume decline. CEO Donnie King: "The beef segment remains our only soft spot." [[TT News Q4 2025](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/tyson-earnings-q4-2025)]
- **FY26 outlook:** Projected beef operating loss of **$400M–$600M**. Chicken is expected to offset. [[Supermarket Perimeter](https://www.supermarketperimeter.com/articles/13407-tyson-foods-shares-q4-results)]
- **Q1 FY2025 call:** Direct immigration question asked about deportation impact on workforce. CEO: "Everyone that works at Tyson Foods is legally authorized to do so." Noted company has DACA, refugee, parolee, TPS workers. [[Motley Fool Q1 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/02/03/tyson-foods-tsn-q1-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)]
- **Workforce composition:** Company discloses ~35% of workforce are immigrants; 60% of frontline workforce immigrant/refugee per internal docs; IASJ filed a 2025 shareholder proposal on immigration risk disclosure. [[IASJ](https://iasj.org/timely-shareholder-proposal-on-immigration-impacts-to-tyson-will-go-to-a-vote-at-february-agm/)]

### Pilgrim's Pride (PPC)

- FY24 Q4 results strong (net rev $17.9B, adj EBITDA $2.2B / 12.4% margin). No major U.S. closures disclosed on the call. Hatchability issues with new bird in 2025. USDA projects U.S. chicken production +1.4% in 2025 as beef availability tightens (beneficiary of the cycle). [[PPC Q4 2024 results](https://ir.pilgrims.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pilgrims-pride-reports-fourth-quarter-and-year-end-2024-results)]
- **Not displaced-labor source; poultry is gaining capacity.**

### JBS USA / JBSAY
- No major U.S. beef plant closures in 2024–2026. Greeley strike March 2026 is a labor-cost event; resolved with 99% vote authorization, $0.70/hr + $750 bonus. [[MEAT+POULTRY](https://www.meatpoultry.com/articles/33353-striking-jbs-workers-will-return-to-colorado-plant-negotiations-continue)]
- Closed a CA meat-processing plant in 2025 (size not disclosed in public sources). JBS is relatively insulated — vertically integrated, Brazilian parent.

### Cargill
- **Global 5% workforce cut (≈8,000 jobs) announced Dec 2024** after ~10% annual earnings decline. Milwaukee ground beef plant closed 2026-02 (221 workers) — first Cargill beef-belt closure, cited 75-yr-low cattle supply. Dodge City / Fort Morgan / Friona / Schuyler all flagged as UNAFFECTED by initial restructuring. [[Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/cargill-ground-beef-processing-facility-close-layoff-221-workers-restructure-job-cut-loss/601581097); [Agriculture Dive](https://www.agriculturedive.com/news/cargill-layoffs-profit-decline-restructuring/734564/)]

### Smithfield (SFD, re-IPO'd 2025)
- 2024 closures: Altoona IA (314), Charlotte NC (107). 2023 Missouri hog-farm shutdown 35 sites, 92 workers. Pork segment less exposed to beef cycle; pressure is demand/capacity rationalization, not herd scarcity.

**Common management framing:** Every closure cites "efficiency" or "capacity utilization" as primary; cattle supply mentioned explicitly only in Tyson Lexington and Cargill Milwaukee. No U.S. packer has publicly tied closures to immigration enforcement or self-deportation in earnings disclosures. H-2B is NOT material for meatpacking (H-2B caps at ~66K and meatpackers do not use it at scale — they use legally-authorized long-term residents, refugees, parolees, TPS).

## 4 — UNTANGLING CATTLE CYCLE vs SDL-01

### Share attribution of 2024–2026 beef-belt layoffs

Rough decomposition of the ~8,260 confirmed layoffs:

| Driver | Estimated share | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Cattle herd cycle** (supply-driven) | **60–70%** | Lexington (3,212) + Amarillo (1,761) + Cargill Milwaukee (221) explicitly cite cattle supply. Lexington alone = 4.8% of US daily beef slaughter capacity. |
| **Firm-specific efficiency / aging plant** | 20–25% | Perry IA pre-dated peak cycle; Emporia KS → Holcomb consolidation is primarily footprint rationalization. |
| **Poultry demand / market shifts** | 10–15% | Perdue Monterey TN, Washington IN; Butterball Jonesboro. NOT cattle-cycle. |
| **Listeria / food safety** | ~1–2% | Boar's Head Jarratt VA (one-off). |
| **ICE enforcement direct** | <1% of CLOSURES in this sweep, but see Glenn Valley Foods Omaha June 2025 (~76 arrests, plant 80% capacity drop next-day) as the SDL-01 signal path running in parallel. |

### Duration of cycle-driven layoffs

USDA and industry consensus: **cattle herd rebuild begins no sooner than 2027; meaningful growth 2028–2030; peak inventory likely 500K–1M head BELOW 2019 highs.** A heifer calf born spring 2025 won't have her first calf until spring 2027, and that calf doesn't reach a packer until late 2029. [[Beef Magazine 2025 review](https://www.beefmagazine.com/market-news/a-review-of-2025-beef-and-cattle-markets); [AgInfo](https://www.aginfo.net/report/63603/Land-Livestock-Report/Rebuilding-the-U-S-Cow-Herd-A-Calculated-Climb)]

**Plant reopening is uncertain.** Historical analog: closed packing capacity rarely comes back. Industry estimate: 2025 annual slaughter ~30.5M head vs 34.96M head packing capacity (13% excess capacity). Reopening Lexington is not in Tyson's plan. Even if rebuild happens, post-rebuild capacity utilization at surviving plants is the optimization target, not reopening closed ones.

**Implication:** Cycle-driven job losses are effectively **permanent** on a 5-year horizon. This overlaps fully with SDL-01's 5-year structural window, which blurs the observability question.

### Do the displaced workers self-deport?

**Evidence says NO, overwhelmingly:**
- Flatwater Free Press interviews with six Lexington displaced workers (Somali, Mexican, Nicaraguan, Guatemalan backgrounds): all plan intra-U.S. relocation (Omaha, Kearney, North Platte, S. Sioux City, Kansas). No return-migration plans.
- ~60–80 Somali workers migrated to South Sioux City NE for Tyson employment there.
- Perry IA 1-yr follow-up: workers retrained (CNA, CDL), stayed local; food insecurity elevated but no return-migration wave.
- Emporia KS: Tyson offered ~200 Holcomb transfers; most not taken due to family/schools; workers stayed in region.

**Why:** Beef-belt immigrant populations are long-tenured (1990-wave IBP/Tyson buildout), with U.S.-born children, mixed-status families, home equity, church/community ties. They are structurally different from the newly-arrived undocumented cohort that SDL-01 tracks (freshly-arrived parolees losing TPS, asylum seekers, recent border crossers). Cycle displacement is **NOT a SDL-01 amplifier** for this cohort.

**Caveat:** A parallel ICE enforcement track IS visible (Glenn Valley Foods Omaha June 2025, 76 arrests of identity-theft users; Southeastern Provision TN historical analog showing 90% capacity drop). That runs on a DIFFERENT transmission path and IS an SDL-01 signal.

### Size of potential compound effect

Even under the most generous assumption (10% of cycle-displaced workers self-deport vs a baseline of 0% for rooted workers), cycle-driven 2024–2027 displacements of ~15–25K beef-belt workers would produce **at most 1.5–2.5K incremental self-deportations** — rounding error vs SDL-01's 100K+ workforce-withdrawal thesis. The cycle amplifies observable community effects (empty houses, school decline, rural CRE stress) but does NOT feed remittance or border-crossing signals meaningfully.

### What MARCO should track going forward

1. **Open VX-MARCO-BEEFBELT-01** as distinct vector: transmission target = REGINALD (rural CRE in Dawson/Dallas/Lyon/Potter counties), CARL (regional consumer stress), NOT SDL-01.
2. **Stop treating meatpacking throughput (hog/poultry proxy) as SDL-01 leading indicator.** Cattle throughput is herd-driven; confounded. Use poultry throughput ONLY (less exposed to herd cycle, more exposed to labor).
3. **Watch Big 4 Q1-Q2 2026 earnings:** if JBS/National Beef announce closures to match Tyson, the cycle story escalates; if they don't, Tyson was the outlier and other packers are betting on capacity gain from Tyson's exit.
4. **Watch Dawson County UE realized print** (NDOL releases lagged ~30 days): if it approaches KC Fed's 27% projection, that validates rural CRE stress severity — feed REGINALD.
5. **Track ICE enforcement track separately:** Glenn Valley–style raids, not Lexington-style closures, are the SDL-01 signal.
