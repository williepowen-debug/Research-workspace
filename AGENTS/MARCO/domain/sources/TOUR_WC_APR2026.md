# FIFA WORLD CUP 2026 — TOURISM STRESS TEST (2026-04-20)

## HEADLINE
World Cup 2026 will produce a real but narrower-than-FIFA-projected tourism pulse in Jun-Jul 2026. Game-night hotel rates in host cities (incl. Miami) have already been cut ~30% from peak, Canadian B1/B2 wait times run 10-14 months, and a third of group-stage inventory remains unsold — evidence that WC partially masks but does not reverse underlying weakness. For MARCO's FL thesis, Miami gets a 7-match, ~4-week event boost that is compressed enough to NOT mask the Q2 2026 baseline Canadian collapse or structural condo/insurance stress; the Q3 post-WC air pocket is the higher-probability asymmetric trade.

## FIFA VISA PROGRAM STATUS
**FIFA PASS (Priority Appointment Scheduling System)** — launched early 2026, jointly with US State Dept. Confirmed active as of Apr 2026.

- Voluntary opt-in for verified FIFA ticket holders who bought directly from FIFA.
- Provides **priority interview scheduling only** — does NOT guarantee approval or bypass standard screening. Sec. Rubio: *"Your ticket is not a visa."*
- US deploying **500 additional consular officers** to process WC-related B1/B2.
- Does nothing for applicants who don't have FIFA-direct tickets (i.e., StubHub/resale buyers) or for walk-up B1/B2 queues.

**Critical gap:** FIFA PASS only helps ticket holders. Family members, secondary travelers, and would-be fans who haven't bought yet are still stuck in the normal 10-14-month Canadian / 12-month Bogotá queues.

Signal: Marquee demand intact (stars + finals); breadth of demand thin. Group stages featuring non-marquee national teams are the canary.

## AIRLINE CAPACITY RAMP
- **+727,000 seats** reallocated to MEX host cities (CDMX, Monterrey, Guadalajara) during Jun-Jul 2026.
- **+57,000 additional seats** on US-MX routes during tournament.
- **Mexico City** (AICM + AIFA combined) gets +373,000 additional seats — largest single hub ramp.
- **American Airlines**: +27,000 seats across 12 routes, including LAX-SEA, BOS-DFW, ATL-MIA uplift.
- **Mexicana de Aviación**: Requested US DOT authorization for charter flights MX–US host cities (pending).
- **Sojern (data provider)**: June 2026 flight bookings to US host cities pacing **+7% YoY** — meaningful since overall inbound US flight bookings are -1% to -6% YoY over the last 9-10 months. UK is #1 international source (19.5% share); European share rising 12% → 14%; APAC 6% → 7%.

**Read:** Airlines committing real capacity, but the ramp is concentrated in MX and US-MX corridors (low visa friction) — not in LHR/FRA/GRU/YYZ long-haul. Capacity ramp supports WC pulse BUT June's +7% is barely above normal baseline given the tournament magnitude. A "+7%" WC month is a disappointment dressed as growth.

## HOTEL PRICING READ
**Source:** Lighthouse Intelligence (per Financial Times, Apr 15 2026).

- **Game-day room rates in Miami, Atlanta, Dallas, Philadelphia, SF have fallen ~30–33% from peak earlier in 2026.**
- Initial post-draw (Dec 2025) surge had lifted rates up to +300% vs prior year; most of that has now been unwound.
- Game-night vs non-game-night premium still exists: **+31.4% on average.** Miami Aloft Aventura example: $385/night Jun 13-16 vs $152 the prior weekend.
- Miami specifically: -5.43% average rate decrease (roughly -$14/night).
- Drivers cited: weak demand, high travel costs, visa/political concerns.

**Read:** Hotels are the cleanest high-frequency demand proxy. A 30% game-day rate cut 2 months pre-event = hospitality operators see demand undershooting. Post-draw pricing peak → April collapse is the key signal that overrides FIFA's bullish narrative.

## SENTIMENT & BOYCOTT SIGNALS
- **Head coaches / federations from ~20 European federations** have expressed boycott sentiment (team-level boycott unlikely; fan-level already evident).
- **Former FIFA president Blatter (Jan 2026):** "Stay away from the USA."
- **boycottusa2026.org** exists as organized fan campaign.
- **Florida Immigration Coalition** issued formal travel advisory: "Florida is no longer a safe destination for international tourists" (racial profiling / rights risk). Directly aimed at Miami host city.
- **Mexico protests (Mar 28, 2026)** at Azteca Stadium — local opposition to hosting amid housing/water/electricity issues; not travel-suppressing for visitors.
- **Reported individual ticket resales** by fans who changed their minds — but data is anecdotal.
- **US admin posture**: 19-country full/partial travel ban (Jun 2025) + Jan 2026 suspension of immigrant-visa processing for 75 countries. FIFA's Infantino has publicly reassured fans.

**Read:** Formal boycott: unlikely to materialize at scale. Ambient deterrence: real, measurable in hotel and group-stage ticket data, concentrated in Canadian and European source markets.

## SUPER BOWL LX CROSS-READ
SB LX, Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara, Feb 2026 — closest recent mega-event comparable.

- **~90,000–100,000 out-of-market visitors** (heavily domestic — football is an NFL/US audience, not international like soccer).
- Bay Area hotel RevPAR: **+47% for Feb**. Room rates: **+143% vs same weekend 2025** (~$510/night avg).
- Total economic impact: **~$500M** regional (BCG estimate range $370M-$630M).
- **400,000+ event-related room nights**; SF captures ~70% of regional impact despite being 40mi from stadium.

**Read:** SB LX was a robust, domestic-demand mega-event that saw NO hotel-rate softening pre-event. Rates were up 143% YoY into event weekend. This confirms that when US domestic demand for a mega-event is intact, pricing holds. World Cup's 30% hotel rate CUT in April — with 50 days to go — is a cleanly bearish divergence from the SB LX playbook and is specifically international-demand driven.

## MIAMI-SPECIFIC EXPOSURE
**What WC delivers to Miami:**
- 7 matches over ~5 weeks (Jun 15 – Jul 18).
- Draw includes Brazil, Portugal, Colombia, Uruguay — strong Latin American fan corridors where visas work (MX 1.5mo, Colombia 12mo is the friction point).
- Host-committee claim: 1M visitors, $1.5B impact. Realistic: likely $500-800M per independent economists.
- Quarterfinal (Jul 11) and Bronze Final (Jul 18) as knockout anchors. **No semifinal** (goes to Atlanta/Dallas).

**What WC does NOT fix for Miami:**
- Canadian snowbird/tourism collapse: -28% YoY is a structural Q1/Q4 story; WC is Q2/Q3. **Zero Canadian visa-dependent benefit** to Miami from the event (most Canadians traveling to WC will go to Toronto/Vancouver or not travel).
- $678.8B FL Citizens exposure (insurance) — unaffected by WC.
- Condo inventory at 8.8 months — unaffected by WC; in fact, a short-term event pulse that pulls summer rentals into event nights will not clear the condo oversupply.
- Workforce displacement / DHS enforcement — ongoing independent of WC.
- **Q3 2026 post-WC air pocket**: Aug-Oct 2026 will see hard comp against a stimulated Jun-Jul; if underlying travel demand is still weak (Canadian + European), Q3 tourism prints could be especially poor and flow to REGINALD/CORAL FL bank exposure.

**Miami tourism math (rough):**
- Baseline Jun-Jul visitor spend (non-event years): ~$3B/month equivalent in Miami-Dade.
- WC direct visitor spend (best case, credible estimate): +$550M over 5 weeks = +$100M/week ≈ +15% uplift on baseline.
- That +15% is NOT enough to mask a simultaneous -28% Canadian collapse if Canadians are a >20% share of Miami inbound. Miami's Canadian share is concentrated in Oct-Apr (snowbird), so the offsetting effect on Q2/Q3 is muted — the Canadian collapse shows up hardest in Nov 2026-Mar 2027, well after WC closes.

## NET VERDICT
**WC delivers a REAL but BOUNDED pulse that does NOT mask the underlying weakness thesis. Base case: WC underdelivers FIFA projections by 15-30% on international visitor spend, driven by visa friction (Canada, Colombia, select markets), ambient boycott sentiment, and high ticket prices. Pulse is heavily domestic + Mexican.**

**Confidence: 65%** on "underdelivers FIFA projection" (supported by: hotel rate -30%, Canadian B1/B2 14mo, unsold group-stage inventory, FIFA PASS only helps ticket holders, airline capacity ramp is modest outside MX corridor).

**Confidence: 75%** on "does not mask MARCO's FL thesis" (supported by: WC seasonality vs Canadian seasonality mismatch, structural issues unrelated to tourism flows, short duration of pulse).

**Key upside risk to bearish read:** If knockout-round drama drives last-minute demand surge AND FIFA PASS clears ~100k visa backlog, June-July bookings could surprise +10-15% vs current pace. Monitor mid-May booking pace for this.

**Key downside asymmetry:** Q3 2026 (Aug-Sep) is the cleaner short window for MARCO — post-WC air pocket + Canadian Labour Day weakness + DHS/condo structural issues converging. WC stimulation peaks Jul 19 then falls off a cliff.
