## S — SYNTHESIS

### Key Mental Model

**Core Finding This Session:** The MARCO thesis is **not Florida-specific** — it's a **regional stress pattern** with different exposure combinations across states:

| Exposure Type | Primary States | Mechanism |
|---------------|----------------|-----------|
| Tourism (Canadian) | FL, NV, AZ | Political boycott → spending loss → job loss |
| Workforce Displacement | TX, CA, FL, AZ | Enforcement → labor shortage → wage/price pressure |
| Insurance/Climate | FL, CA | Climate risk → cost spiral → affordability crisis |
| Housing Distress | FL, TX | Above stresses → demand decline → price/inventory stress |

**Florida's uniqueness is the COMBINATION**, not any single vector. Texas is closest to replicating the FL pattern but lacks insurance exposure. California has insurance + workforce but no tourism exposure. Nevada has severe tourism exposure but nothing else.

### What Matters Most
The geographic expansion transforms MARCO from a "Florida case study" to a "regional stress thesis." This has significant implications:
1. **Not idiosyncratic** — Can't dismiss as "Florida problems"
2. **Different transmission speeds** — Some states (TX) showing housing stress, others (CA) not yet
3. **Policy relevance** — Immigration enforcement effects are measurable across multiple states
4. **Validation path** — Can cross-check patterns across states

### Biggest Uncertainties
1. **Food price transmission:** Still absent despite -155K ag workers. USDA Jan 23 is key test.
2. **Texas trajectory:** Will TX housing follow FL path or stabilize?
