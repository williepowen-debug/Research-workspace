## Finding 1: about 11pp of the CRE ratio's fall is the merger, not de-risking (KB-FLG-080)

Bank-level Call Report, $M (FDIC BankFind, cert 32541):

| | 6/30/25 | 9/30/25 | **12/31/25** | 3/31/26 | 6/30/26 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tier 1 capital | 8,835 | 8,832 | **8,348** | 8,385 | 8,441 |
| Tier 2 capital | 800 | 790 | **1,572** | 1,553 | 1,562 |
| **Total RBC (SR 07-1 denominator)** | 9,635 | 9,622 | **9,921** | 9,938 | 10,003 |
| CRE numerator (constr + MF + non-OO NFNR) | 40,008 | 38,436 | 35,858 | 34,209 | 32,763 |
| **SR 07-1 ratio** | 415.2% | 399.5% | **361.4%** | 344.2% | 327.5% |

- At the merger quarter, Tier 2 roughly doubled (+$783M, the holding company's sub-debt moving into the bank) and Tier 1 fell $484M (the holding company's debt-funded equity in the bank unwinding). **Net +$299M = +3.1% on the denominator.**
- 2025Q3 → Q4 the ratio fell **38.1pp**. Holding the 9/30 denominator, Q4 would be **372.7%**: **−26.8pp is numerator run-off, −11.3pp (30% of that quarter's move) is the basis change.**
- **What survives:** the ratio still fell in every quarter, and the numerator did most of the work (−32.2% since 2023Q3). **What changes:** the −143pp headline (REGINALD matrix §3b, `fb1f68659`) includes ~11pp of merger basis. Going forward both legs are on the merged basis, so the "~2 quarters to 300%" pace is not inflated by this from here on; it was inflated in the quarters that included 2025Q4.
- ⚠️ **The issuer's own comparatives now show holding-company figures under the "Bank" label** for pre-merger periods (Q4-2025 release, 9/30/25 column = 15.92% vs bank-level 15.31%). Any FLG-vs-history capital comparison read from a post-merger release is silently mixed-entity. This is exactly charter rule (b)'s case, at the issuer's hand.
- Also: 4.125% notes due 2030 and 5.90% notes due 2028 now count in bank Tier 2. As sub-debt approaches maturity its Tier 2 credit amortises (20% per year in the final 5 years). Both tranches (due 2028 and due 2030) are already inside that window as of 10/2026, so **the denominator has a built-in slow leak** that this desk has not modelled. Sizes per tranche are not in this filing; the 10-K debt note is the place to read them. Owed, not urgent.

## Finding 2: management has cut its 2026 plan at every print since first giving it (three cuts), and halved the provision budget (KB-FLG-081)

| Issuer forecast slide | 2026 adj. EPS | 2026 NII | **2026 provision** | 2027 adj. EPS | 2027 provision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q3-25 deck (10/24/25) | $0.75–0.80 | $2,250–2,300M | **$200–250M** | $2.10–2.20 | $150–200M |
| Q4-25 deck (1/30/26) | $0.65–0.70 | $2,150–2,200M | $200–250M | $1.90–2.00 | $150–200M |
| Q1-26 deck (4/24/26) | $0.60–0.65 | $1,950–2,050M | **$100–150M** | $1.80–1.90 | $100–150M |
| Q2-26 deck (7/24/26) | **$0.40–0.50** | $1,860–1,960M | **$90–140M** | $1.60–1.70 | $100–150M |

- Actual provisions: Q4-25 **$3M**, Q1-26 **$0**, Q2-26 **$18M** → H1-2026 $18M against a $90–140M year. The frozen frame's provision bands (>$62M / <$37M in Q3) already encode the July plan; this table is the history behind them.
- **So what for the thesis:** the falling reserve this desk treats as its live signal (coverage 31.04%, specific reserve 5.8%) was **budgeted by management**. They halved the 2026 provision plan in April, while the non-accrual book was ~$2.8–3.0B and being cleared mostly by payoffs. That does not prove the reserve is wrong; it does show the drawdown is a management choice, not an accident. A provision guide raised back up would be management conceding the budget.
- **So what for the print:** adjusted EPS H1-2026 = $0.09 (Q1 $0.04 + Q2 $0.05). The $0.40–0.50 full-year range implies **$0.31–0.41 in H2, i.e. ~$0.15–0.20 a quarter**. The Street's Q3 ~$0.07 (aggregator, KB-077) sits far below that. **The market already disbelieves the guide; a fourth cut is the base case the consensus prices, not a surprise.** See the surprise note `reports/2026-10-22_preprint-surprise-threshold-NOTE.md`.
