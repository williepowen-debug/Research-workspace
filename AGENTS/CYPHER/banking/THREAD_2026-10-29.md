# Bank cyber / vendor risk — thread note 2026-10-29

**Source session:** CYPHER, 10/29, Will's questions 12:39–13:49 ET. Evidence records: `research/2026-10-29_news-sweep/RECORD.md` (both passes) and `research/2026-10-29_wave-recheck/RECORD.md`. Every figure below carries the basis given in its ledger row; most are SECONDARY (news reports quoting the company or regulator).

## The reading, in four sentences

US bank suppliers suffer a steady drip of cyber and technology failures: roughly one confirmed incident a month in 2026, and three in August (KB-CYP-076; a floor, because the count comes from a sweep). So far they have cost customer data, short outages and legal bills, not deposits, funding or a bank's solvency. That is consistent with 0 of 5 major cyber waves since 2017 causing a bank run or failure (KB-CYP-001). Officials say the same thing on the record: the Fed's supervisor said on 9/29 that "a number of community banks have experienced significant cyber events", and an executive order of 6/2 names community banks for defensive help. The thing to watch is not the drip; it is one of two shapes that would move money.

## How to read it

1. **The cost is an expense line, not a credit event.** NCR Voyix's 2023 ransomware cost $47M over three years, of which $37M was insured. Marquis exposed ~672k people across 70+ banks without moving a balance sheet.
2. **Two shapes would matter:**
   - **A core processor down at many banks for days.** Sharetec showed the mechanism at small scale, by accident; at Fiserv, FIS or Jack Henry scale it reaches customers' access to money and payments (LIQUID, REGINALD).
   - **Money moved through payment messaging.** The 2016 Bangladesh Bank theft was ~$81M via SWIFT; India's 10/15–17 case was small.
3. **The MSP route is real.** The 10/23 read "wide reach, little traffic" was wrong on traffic; N-central is the evidence (KB-CYP-020 revised).
4. **CYPHER sees bank-vendor trouble late.** These events surface by press release, trade press and state breach lists, not SEC filings: five were found in two searches. Hence the weekly watch's wider search and the late-victim (MOVEit) checks on 11/04 and 11/20.
5. **AI is the accelerant officials name.** It makes flaw-finding cheap, so more flaws, faster; community banks, with thin IT staff and heavy vendor reliance, absorb it worst. The October PHP flaw was itself AI-found. Policy response so far is defensive and voluntary (EO 14409; Treasury clearinghouse; whether it is running NOT checked).

## Not supported by the record

No official says community banks are failing or that a large attack is being hidden; no sign of government foreknowledge of the PHP flaw. ⚠️ The April Treasury/Fed meeting concerned Anthropic's Mythos model; Anthropic also makes the model running CYPHER.
