Claim Summary
The ransomware group tracked as
qilin
has allegedly listed Power Funding, a United Kingdom-based financial services organization operating at
www.powerfundingltd.com
, on its dark web leak site. According to the threat actor, the attack was purportedly carried out on 26 October 2026. The group claims to have exfiltrated data from the victim, though the specific data volume is listed as undisclosed and no sample data categories have been publicly described.
This claim remains entirely unverified. Power Funding has not publicly confirmed the incident, and no independent security researchers or incident response firms have corroborated the threat actor’s assertions at the time of writing. The listing should be treated as an allegation only.
Threat Actor Profile
Qilin is a ransomware operation that has been active since approximately 2022 and is widely assessed by security researchers as a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) affiliate model. The group is also tracked under alternate names including “Agenda” by some vendors. Public reporting has linked Qilin affiliates to double extortion tactics, in which data is allegedly stolen before encryption and then used as leverage for payment demands.
Qilin has been associated with attacks across multiple sectors, with healthcare, manufacturing, and financial services among those reportedly targeted. The group’s affiliates are believed to have used a range of initial access techniques documented in public research, including exploitation of known vulnerabilities in edge devices and VPN appliances, as well as valid credential abuse. Specific tooling attributed to this campaign against Power Funding is not publicly available, and no YARA rules or detection signatures specific to this incident have been released. Organizations should rely on general ransomware detection guidance and threat hunting for known Qilin TTPs rather than incident-specific indicators.
Alleged Data Exposure
The threat actor claims to have obtained data from Power Funding, but the leak site entry provides no data volume figure and no description of the categories of information allegedly taken. No samples, screenshots, or proof-of-compromise artifacts have been publicly reported in connection with this listing. Given the financial services context, any purported exposure would theoretically raise concerns around client records, financial documentation, and internal communications, but none of this has been substantiated. We are not reproducing or linking to any leaked material.
Potential Impact
If the claim is accurate, a financial services firm could face regulatory scrutiny under UK data protection and financial conduct frameworks, potential client notification obligations, and reputational harm. Operational disruption from encryption, if it occurred, could affect lending or funding workflows. However, these are hypothetical consequences contingent on verification. Ransomware groups frequently exaggerate scope, inflate data volumes, and occasionally list victims based on incomplete or recycled information to pressure payment.
What to Watch For
Any official statement from Power Funding or its regulators confirming or denying an incident.
Independent confirmation from incident response firms or breach notification services.
Updates to the leak site entry, such as added samples or revised data claims, which may indicate escalation.
Signs of credential stuffing or phishing targeting Power Funding clients, a common follow-on risk.
Monitoring of Qilin affiliate activity for related UK financial sector targeting.
