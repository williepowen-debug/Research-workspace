Claim Summary
The Qilin ransomware group has allegedly added Delta Electric Power Association, a US energy and utilities organization (deltaepa.com), to its dark web leak site. According to the threat actor, the attack occurred on October 26, 2026. The group has not disclosed the volume of data it purportedly holds, and no data samples or proof-of-compromise artifacts have been publicly described in the listing data reviewed by Yazoul Security.
This claim remains entirely unverified. At the time of writing, there is no independent confirmation that a breach occurred, that data was exfiltrated, or that the organization has engaged with the threat actor. Ransomware operators frequently post victims prematurely, sometimes based on limited initial access rather than confirmed exfiltration.
Threat Actor Profile
qilin
is a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) operation that has been active since approximately 2022 and is widely assessed by researchers as a rebrand or successor of the earlier BlackCat/ALPHV and, by some accounts, Agenda lineage. Qilin has targeted a broad range of sectors globally, with notable emphasis on healthcare, manufacturing, and critical infrastructure.
Public reporting on Qilin indicates the group commonly employs double extortion tactics, exfiltrating data before encryption to pressure victims. Known tooling referenced in third-party research includes Cobalt Strike for post-exploitation, various credential-dumping utilities, and legitimate remote monitoring and management (RMM) tools for lateral movement. The group has been observed exploiting exposed VPN appliances and valid credentials as initial access vectors.
Note: The intelligence feed accompanying this claim lists the group’s total known victims as unknown and its tools as unknown, and cites no public research references. This is a data gap in the source feed, not an indication that the group is inactive or low-capability. Analysts should treat Qilin as an established, operationally mature threat actor based on broader industry reporting, while acknowledging the specific feed lacks corroborating detail.
Alleged Data Exposure
The leak site listing does not specify a data volume, data categories, or provide samples. The “Claimed Data” field is listed as not applicable and the volume as undisclosed. Because no proof-of-compromise has been described, the scope of any alleged exposure is unknown. Claims of this nature should not be treated as evidence that customer records, operational technology data, or employee information were accessed.
Potential Impact
If the claim is accurate, a US electric power association could face risks including operational disruption, exposure of member or customer information, regulatory scrutiny from energy sector oversight bodies, and reputational harm. Energy utilities are attractive targets due to the criticality of service and the sensitivity of grid-adjacent data. However, these are hypothetical risks contingent on the claim being substantiated.
What to Watch For
Official statements from Delta Electric Power Association confirming or denying an incident.
Regulatory or industry notifications from energy sector bodies.
Appearance of the organization’s data on the leak site, which would raise confidence in the claim.
Any reported operational impacts to member utilities or grid services.
Organizations in the energy sector should review detection coverage for Cobalt Strike beacon activity, unusual RMM tool installations, and anomalous VPN authentication patterns. Where available, YARA rules targeting Qilin ransomware binaries and Cobalt Strike configurations can support hunting; consult your threat intelligence provider for current signatures.
