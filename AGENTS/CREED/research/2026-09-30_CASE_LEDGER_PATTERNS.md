# Patterns in Will's CRE loss-sales workbook (v3): what holds, what doesn't, and the test for each

**Written:** 2026-09-30 23:26 ET (rewritten after cold read; first draft 2026-09-30 23:20 ET) · **Directed by:** Will, in-session ("write up the patterns") · **Source:** `cases/sources/2026-09-30_will_CRE_Loss_Sales_v3.xlsx` (Will's research; adopted into `cases/` the same night as CASE-CREED-056..134) · **Figures:** printed by `scripts/workbook_patterns.py` (read-only; set definitions in its header) **unless tagged [KB]** (CREED's own KB rows, cited by ID). · **Reviewed:** a cold Opus reader checked the first draft against the script and the workbook: about 95 figures matched; 6 serious and 9 lesser defects, all re-checked at the artifact and fixed here (§Correction). · **No score, band, trigger or prediction moved.** The case ledger is not a trigger instrument (`cases/README.md` rule 9).

## Bottom line

**One pattern holds, two are weak, and the one I first called most important runs the other way on the rows that meet the workbook's own comparison rule.**
- ✅ **Lenders lose more than the sale price implies.** In the four cases with a loan, a price and a loss, the loss ran **8–22 points of the loan balance beyond the price**. Named causes include liquidation expenses (One City Centre: $8.0M on $24.6M of proceeds) and servicer advances (315 W. 36th: a $5M trust loss from advances alone, before any sale). **Whether the gap grows with time in workout is UNTESTED.** One disputed counter-case: 3000 Post Oak.
- 🟡 **Failed buildings track their old value less, but part of that is arithmetic.** Splitting on the size of the drop manufactures some of the contrast by itself. The rest survives a null test, but on sourced rows only the gap is small (0.57 vs 0.72).
- 🟡 **Named-case loan losses are a tail.** Median **76%** across six CMBS losses vs **35.2% all types / 49.3% office** for 2026 dispositions year to date [KB-CREED-041, JPM, SECONDARY].
- 🔄 **The purchase-year pattern is OPEN, and its direction is reversed.** On v2 I said peak-era buyers lose most. Under the workbook's own filter (Include = Yes, verified prior-purchase benchmark, n=9), **older purchases fell MORE**: rank correlation **−0.66** (−0.50 without the 410 Townsend resale). That is thin, and it is a different claim from the one I made.

## The limits that govern every line below

1. **A sample of deals someone chose to report, not a sample of the market** (the workbook's methodology sheet and `cases/README.md`). **75 of 101 rows carry an office label.**
2. **A "discount" is a PRICE change versus an earlier mark, never a loan loss.** Losses come only from 8 rows: 6 final CMBS losses, 1 partial CMBS trust loss (315 W. 36th, advances) and 1 bank charge-off (OZK San Carlos). OZK's second row (a recapitalization) discloses no loss.
3. **The workbook's own comparable-sale rule** (Include = Yes, same benchmark type, verified benchmark) **admits 9 sales.** Patterns 2, 5 and 6 use a wider 46-row set: 16 Include = Yes + 30 Include = Unverified, across 8 benchmark types (31 unverified prior purchases, 9 verified, 6 appraisals or valuations). **That pooling is what the methodology warns against.** The 46-row figures are shown because the 9-row set is too small to test anything, but **every 46-row figure is a lead, not a measurement.** Sourced-only figures are shown wherever they were computed.
4. **Nothing here was re-verified by CREED.** Source tiers carry through from the workbook as `PRIMARY-CITED` / `SECONDARY` / `LEAD`.

## So what, for CREED

1. **Pattern 3a is the usable one: for a lender, a property price overstates the recovery.** Across four cases the gap ran 8–22 points of the loan. A loss estimate built from property values alone (an appraisal or a comparable sale) will understate the credit loss by roughly that much, before any question of timing. This is loan-level evidence, so it can go to REGINALD if its next office loss work asks. It is n=4 with a disputed counter-case.
2. **Do not yet tie the gap to extend-and-pretend.** "Delay costs money" (3b) is the version with thesis consequences, and it is untested: the evidence names expenses as well as advances, and carries no workout durations. The remittance test below is what would decide it. Glancy (2026, Fed), known so far only through Trepp's summary (F5), points the other way on extension quality. Neither moves THESIS wording.
3. **Pattern 2 is a price pattern, not a loss pattern.** Do not carry it into loss-rate language. "Failed buildings form a separate group" is partly an artifact of where the 80% line was drawn.
4. **The purchase-year question is open, and if anything runs opposite to the first read.** Nothing here supports focusing on 2015–2019 purchases. The maturity mechanism stands on its own evidence (`CREED-T-02`; F1's $10.7B of hard maturities by 2029 [refi-screens page]) and does not need this workbook.
