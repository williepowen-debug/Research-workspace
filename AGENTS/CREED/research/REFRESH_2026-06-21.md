## Executive Read

CREED is revivable, but the current data argues for a **selective CRE recognition / extend-and-pretend still absorbing** frame rather than an already-confirmed broad CRE→bank cascade.

- **CMBS stress remains elevated:** Trepp May 2026 overall CMBS delinquency rate was **7.55%**, up 1bp MoM; office remained severe at **11.53%** and multifamily **6.95%** even after May declines/cures.
- **Special servicing is still high, despite a May pullback:** Trepp May 2026 special servicing fell **51bps to 10.86%**, but office was still **16.75%**, retail **13.00%**, mixed-use **11.62%**, multifamily **8.51%**. The decline was partly large-loan return/denominator effects, not clean repair.
- **Maturity-wall thesis remains alive:** Morningstar DBRS expected **>$100B** fixed/floating CMBS maturities in 2026, with **more than half** failing to repay at maturity; Trepp identified **$76.6B** of 2026 hard maturities, with **39% in Q4** and **36%** at debt yield ≤8%.
- **Bank transmission is not yet broad-cascade confirmed:** FDIC Q1 2026 said overall PDNA declined to **1.53%**; non-owner CRE and multifamily remained elevated. Large-bank non-owner CRE PDNA declined for a sixth consecutive quarter to **3.40%**, below the Q3 2024 peak **4.99%** but far above pre-pandemic **0.58%**.
- **Office remains the core CREED problem; multifamily is more mixed:** office is maturity/refi/value impairment; multifamily has real property-level stress, but May Trepp showed a cure-driven delinquency pullback after March spike.

**Working classification:** Phase 3 evidence supports **selective recognition accelerating in CMBS/office**, while banks remain cushioned by concentration differences, lower large-bank exposure, mods/forbearance, reserves, and still-favorable system liquidity/capital. CREED should test whether Q2/Q3 bank filings convert CMBS stress into bank provisions.

## Source Facts

### 1. Trepp — May 2026 CMBS delinquency

Source: Trepp, “CMBS Delinquency Rate Increased One Basis Point in May 2026.”  
URL: https://www.trepp.com/trepptalk/cmbs-delinquency-rate-increased-one-basis-point-in-may-2026

Key facts:
- Overall CMBS delinquency rate: **7.55%** in May 2026, up **1bp** MoM.
- Newly delinquent loans: **$4.04B** total; top five accounted for **$1.86B**.
- Status mix of newly delinquent balances: **70% non-performing matured balloon**, **28% 30-days delinquent**, **2% mostly foreclosure**.
- Property-type rates:
  - Office: **11.53%**, down **16bps** MoM.
  - Multifamily: **6.95%**, down **76bps** MoM.
  - Retail: **6.61%**, up **30bps** MoM.
  - Industrial: **1.31%**, up **35bps** MoM.
  - Lodging: **6.01%**, down **51bps** MoM.

CREED read:
- The maturity-default mechanism is visible: non-performing matured balloons dominate newly delinquent balances.
- Office is still deeply stressed even when the rate ticks down.
- Multifamily is not a clean one-way deterioration line; cures can temporarily swamp new stress.

### 2. Trepp — May 2026 special servicing

Source: Trepp, “CMBS Special Servicing Rate Declines in May, Led by Office Pullback & Denominator Growth.”  
URL: https://www.trepp.com/trepptalk/cmbs-special-servicing-rate-declines-in-may

Key facts:
- Overall CMBS special servicing rate fell **51bps** in May to **10.86%**.
- Trepp attributed the decline mainly to a massive office loan returning to master servicer and denominator effects.
- New transfers remained elevated; May transfers totaled approximately **$2.9B across 59 loans**.
- Property-type special servicing rates:
  - Office: **16.75%**, down **91bps**.
  - Retail: **13.00%**, up **1bp**.
  - Mixed-use: **11.62%**, down **59bps**.
  - Multifamily: **8.51%**, down **57bps**.
  - Lodging: **8.45%**, down **121bps**.
  - Industrial: **1.28%**, up **5bps**.

CREED read:
- May improvement is not enough to declare repair. Office special servicing at 16.75% remains a red CREED signal.
- Watch loan-level returns/cures carefully; large loans can move headline rates without changing underlying market health.

### 3. Morningstar DBRS / Commercial Observer — 2026 maturity wall

Primary source reference: Morningstar DBRS, “U.S. CRE 2026 Outlook: Momentum Is Healthy, but Office Dynamics and CMBS Maturity Wall Bring Challenges.”  
URL: https://dbrs.morningstar.com/research/471472  
Accessible corroborating source: Commercial Observer, “Five- and 10-Year CMBS Loans: Which Vintage Might Crash the Office Market?”  
URL: https://commercialobserver.com/2026/03/cmbs-loans-office-distress-2026/

Key facts from accessible source quoting Morningstar DBRS:
- More than **$100B** fixed/floating CMBS loans come due in 2026.
- Morningstar DBRS expected **more than half** of those loans to fail to repay at maturity.
- Office maturity defaults were described as the primary driver of elevated delinquency and expected to continue in 2026.
- Office may account for as much as one-third of scheduled maturities.
- Commercial Observer / Trepp context: office CMBS delinquency hit **12.34%** in Jan 2026, dipped to **11.4%** in Feb; later Trepp May print showed **11.53%**.

CREED read:
- The old hard-maturity thesis is not dead. The live mechanism is “cash-flowing but cannot refinance at current value/rates,” not necessarily missed monthly debt service.
- CREED should track maturity defaults separately from term defaults.

### 4. Trepp — hard maturities

Source: Trepp, “March 2026 CMBS Hard Maturities Show Contained Stress, Led by Mixed-Use, Retail, & Office Exposure.”  
URL: https://www.trepp.com/trepptalk/tbc-cmbs-hard-maturity-blog

Key facts:
- Trepp defines hard maturity as no remaining contractual extension options beyond the period.
- 2026 hard maturities: **$76.6B**, more than either prior two years.
- Back-loaded profile: **39%** due in Q4.
- **36%** of 2026 hard-maturity loans have debt yield ≤8%, the segment likely to face refinancing friction.
- Highest concentration of low-debt-yield exposure: office, retail, multifamily.
- March cohort: **$3.18B** reaching hard maturity across 87 whole loans / 93 loan pieces; **$78.7M**, or **2.5%**, non-performing.

CREED read:
- Q1 hard-maturity stress looked contained in March, but 2026 risk is back-loaded. Do not over-extrapolate March calm.
- Q4 hard maturities and low-debt-yield cohorts are CREED’s key timing rails.

### 5. FDIC — Q1 2026 bank CRE / asset quality

Source: FDIC, “FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile First Quarter 2026.”  
URL: https://www.fdic.gov/news/speeches/2026/fdic-quarterly-banking-profile-first-quarter-2026

Key facts:
- Industry net income: **$80.5B**, up **3.6%** QoQ; ROA **1.26%**.
- Provision expense: **$21.4B**, up **2.3%** QoQ but down **4.6%** YoY.
- Overall PDNA rate declined to **1.53%**.
- FDIC said PDNA rates for **multifamily CRE** and **non-owner-occupied CRE** remained elevated.
- Non-owner-occupied CRE PDNA for banks >$250B declined for the sixth consecutive quarter to **3.40%**, down from **4.99%** in Q3 2024, but above pre-pandemic **0.58%**.
- Industry volume of PDNA non-owner CRE loans declined **$300.7M**, or **1.3%**, QoQ.
- Reserve coverage ratio fell to **166.8%** because noncurrent loans increased more than allowance; community-bank reserve coverage declined to **146.6%**.
- Problem banks declined by 6 to **54**; FDIC characterized strong capital/liquidity but noted weakness in certain portfolios and elevated unrealized losses.

CREED read:
- Bank channel is elevated but not breaking. Large-bank non-owner CRE PDNA improved, which weakens a near-term broad-bank-cascade claim.
- Community-bank reserve coverage deterioration is worth REGINALD follow-up, especially among high-CRE-concentration banks.

### 6. Multifamily — March spike, May pullback

Source: Multifamily Dive summarizing Trepp March 2026 and Trepp May 2026 direct article above.  
URL: https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/multifamily-cmbs-delinquency-apartment-loan-default/816842/

Key facts:
- March multifamily delinquency increased **30bps** MoM to **7.15%**, above prior high **7.12%** in Oct 2025; one year earlier **5.44%**, two years earlier **1.84%**.
- March distress concentration: New York/New Jersey **48%** of delinquent balances; Houston **30%**.
- March multifamily special servicing increased **45bps** MoM to **8.75%**.
- Trepp comment: most new multifamily delinquencies in March were **term defaults**, not maturity defaults, pointing to property-level fundamentals rather than pure refi friction.
- May Trepp showed multifamily delinquency down to **6.95%** and special servicing down to **8.51%**.

CREED read:
- Multifamily is a two-channel problem: property-level stress/expense/occupancy and maturity/refi stress.
- It is not just Sunbelt; March showed NY/NJ and Houston concentration. CORAL gets Florida-specific overlap only.
