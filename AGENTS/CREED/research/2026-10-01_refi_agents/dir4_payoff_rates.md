# Direction 4: payoff at maturity by property type, and a base rate for low-debt-yield loans

**Commissioned by:** CREED (Will-approved 2026-09-30) · **Written:** 2026-10-01 13:07 EDT · **Author:** research subagent
**Status:** RESEARCH PACK. These are raw findings, not CREED state. Nothing here has been entered into VX, KB or THRESHOLDS.

---

## Bottom line (5 lines)

1. **No public source gives a payoff rate for each debt-yield (DY) bucket.** The one study built for that question, Trepp's *CMBS Hard Maturity Playbook* (Feb 2026), is behind a form, which was not submitted. What is public are **average DYs split by outcome**: office loans that paid off averaged **11–12.6% DY (conduit)**, while those that did not pay off averaged **7–7.2%**. That places the Sept-2026 cohort's <8% DY half among the loans that historically did not pay off.
2. **Office CMBS payoff at or near maturity, 2026 YTD, by balance: about 39–49%.** Conduit office was **49%** (maturities through July). SASB (single-asset, single-borrower) office was **39%** (through August). Source is BofA, quoted by CREFC on 9/11/26. A stricter "on time" cut from KBRA for Q1 2026 conduit office gives about **32%** (68% failed). For comparison, full-year 2025 office was **58.3%** by balance, but measured as "paid off by year-end."
3. **These series use different definitions and cannot be compared or combined.** In 2023 the same market showed **~94% by count and 71.8% by balance** (KBRA, paid off by year-end) but only **~50% YTD** in DBRS's maturity payoff rate. The window (on time vs. by year-end) and the weighting (count vs. balance) each move the number by 20–40pp.
4. **Bank side (Glancy, FEDS 2026-025):** in 2023–25, about **20% of office loan balances** paid off at maturity, against **>40% before the pandemic**. Loans with DY under 8% were **+5.46pp more likely to be delinquent at maturity** and **−6.91pp less likely to be extended** than before the pandemic. Glancy publishes **no payoff rate by DY bucket**.
5. **Base rate CREED can defend for the Sept-2026 office hard-maturity cohort ($1.48B):** about **35–50% of balance paid off near maturity** for the whole cohort. For the **45.67% with DY <8%**, the only sourced statement is that its rate is **below the cohort rate**. A working figure of **≤25% (ESTIMATE, not sourced)** is the most that can be attached, and **the <6% slice (14.21%) lower still**.

## 5. What base rate CREED can attach to the Sept-2026 office hard-maturity cohort

**Cohort** (Trepp, 2026-09-02; anchor already held): office $1.48B. **45.67% of balance has DY <8.0%; 14.21% has DY <6.0%.** Delinquency 3.20%; 36.50% already in special servicing.

**Perimeter mismatch, stated before any number.** The cohort is **hard maturities only** (no extension options left; excludes loans already past maturity or in REO). Every payoff series above counts **all** maturing loans, including those with extension options and the defeased. A hard-maturity cohort should pay off **less** than an all-maturities rate, because the borrower has no contractual extension to use. Every rate below is therefore a likely **upper** bound for this cohort.

| Slice | Attachable base rate | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole office cohort, payoff near maturity (within ~1–3 months) | **~35–50% of balance** | BofA 2026 YTD conduit office 49% / SASB office 39%; KBRA Q1-26 conduit office ~32% on time; Trepp Playbook 2024 office 43%; CRED iQ 2015/16-vintage office 47%/44% | Moderate. Five independent-perimeter sources cluster at 32–49% |
| Whole office cohort, paid off by year-end | **~45–60%** | KBRA FY2025 office 58.3% bal (strong-office year); FY2024 29.6% (weak year) | Low–moderate; year-to-year range is 29.6→58.3 |
| **<8% DY slice (45.67%)** | **Directional only: below the cohort rate.** If a number is forced: **≤25% near maturity (ESTIMATE / ANALYTICAL, not sourced)** | Average DY of office non-payers is 7.0–7.2% (conduit) and 5.3% (SASB), against 11–12.6% / 9.2% for payers. The <8% bucket is centred on the non-payer distribution. Glancy shows low-DY bank loans' maturity DQ rose +5.46pp in stress | Low. Inferred from averages-by-outcome, which **cannot** be inverted into a bucket rate without the distributions (Playbook-gated) |
| **<6% DY slice (14.21%)** | **Lower than the <8% slice;** no figure attachable | SASB office non-payer average DY 5.3% sits inside this bucket | Low |

**Uncertainty, in plain terms.**
1. Every payoff-rate series uses a different window (on time, by YE, within 6Q) and weight (count or balance). The 20–40pp spreads between them are definitional, not market moves.
2. The ≤25% figure for the <8% DY half is my inference from average-DY-by-outcome. It is **not** a published rate and must be logged as ESTIMATE.
3. Office payoff ran 29.6% in 2024, 58.3% in 2025 and 39–49% in 2026 YTD. A one-year swing of ~29pp means any single-year anchor risks the seasonal or regime trough trap (CREED standing trap #4: anchor to a distribution).
4. "Not paid off" ≠ default. In BofA's 2026 conduit office, 49% was **outstanding**, much of it performing matured balloons or extended, and only 2% liquidated. The Trepp Playbook shows unresolved 2025 hard maturities spent only 45% of post-maturity time non-performing. **A payoff base rate is not a delinquency base rate.**
5. **The cheapest way to upgrade this is the Trepp Playbook's DY-threshold tables.** They require a form submission or a client login, which is Will's call. Second cheapest: the DBRS Q3-2026 summary (~late Oct), which gives the realized quarterly office print for the quarter that includes this cohort.
