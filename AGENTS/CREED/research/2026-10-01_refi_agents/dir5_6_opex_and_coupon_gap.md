# Directions 5 + 6: office operating-cost growth by region, and the office coupon gap at refinancing

*Research agent commissioned by CREED. Written 2026-10-01T17:08Z. Approved by Will on 2026-09-30. Tier key: PRIMARY-READ = I read the primary document. PRIMARY-CITED = a secondary that quotes a named primary. SECONDARY = press. SEARCH-SUMMARY = seen only in a search snippet. DERIVED = my arithmetic on the cited inputs.*

## Bottom line (5 lines)

1. **Office opex outgrew revenue in every year from 2021 to 2025 and in every Census division Trepp could measure.** The gap ran from 0.8pp a year (New England) to 1.6pp (East North Central). East North Central NOI fell 1.4%, and the Pacific had the fastest insurance growth at 9.2% a year. The division-level detail comes from secondaries quoting Trepp; Trepp's own report is form-gated.
2. **The cost driver has already turned.** US property insurance rates fell 13% in Q2 2026, the eighth straight quarterly decline (Marsh, 2026-07-23). Trepp's office insurance growth slowed to 3.3% in 2025, but utilities sped up to 6.7%. Office real-estate taxes grew only 1.1% a year over 2021–25, less than revenue. So the opex problem now sits more in the revenue line than in the cost line.
3. **The coupon gap for 2016-vintage conduit office is +253bp (DERIVED, primary-read from EDGAR).** In-place: 4.34% balance-weighted average (168 loans, 26 deals, 2016 annexes). New issue: 6.87% (20 office loans originated Jun–Aug 2026, 14 deals). On the same basis, the medians give +203bp. CRED iQ's independent read is +172bp (5.14% → 6.86%) for office loans maturing Sep 2026–Jun 2027.
4. **On an interest-only loan, that gap raises debt service by about 58%.** At an unchanged DSCR, the new loan can carry only about 63% of the old balance, which is consistent with Trepp's finding that 63% of H2-2026 office maturing balance needs fresh equity. For 2021-vintage office (WA 3.31%), the gap is +357bp and debt service rises about 108%.
5. **⚠️ Every published "current coupon" predates the September rate move.** The 5Y Treasury went from a 4.31% average in Jun–Aug to 5.01% on 10/1, and the 10Y was 5.24% on 10/1. Holding spreads constant, the scenario office coupon is about 7.57%, which would put the 2016 gap near +323bp. That figure is a scenario, not an observation.

**Which regions saw opex outgrow revenue:** all seven Census divisions that met Trepp's sample threshold. Secondaries quoting Trepp name the extremes: East North Central was worst (largest gap at 1.6pp and NOI −1.4%), and New England had the smallest gap (0.8pp). The other five divisions, and their NOI except West South Central, are inside the gated report. By metro, Chicago was worst and LA best.

**Read-through for refinancing.** Office real-estate taxes are the one cost line that grew slower than revenue (1.1% vs 1.3%). In Chicago and Boston, falling assessed values are moving tax burden off office and onto residential owners. That is a modest tailwind for office NOI in exactly the metros where revenue is weakest. Insurance is now falling at the rate level (Marsh −13%), but it reaches property P&Ls on a 12-month renewal lag. 2025 utilities (+6.7%) are the new pressure. *No CMBS-reported regional opex series was found for retail or industrial: Trepp says its line-item series will cover "additional property types … soon".*

## Direction 6: office in-place coupon vs current refinance coupon

### Method for the primary-read legs (EDGAR, done by this agent)

I parsed the Annex A-1 loan-level tables in the SEC FWP filings, using `pandas.read_html` with chunks joined on Loan ID. I kept loan-level rows only (no property rows), filtered to General Property Type = Office, and **de-duplicated pari passu pieces by (property name, rate)**. Each coupon is the **fixed note rate at securitization, weighted by trust cut-off balance**.

- **2016 sample: 26 conduit deals.** CGCMT 2016-C1/C2/C3/GC36/GC37/P3/P4/P5/P6 · CSAIL 2016-C5/C6/C7 · CSMC 2016-NXSR · GSMS 2016-GS2/GS3/GS4 · MSC 2016-BNK2/UBS9/UBS11/UBS12 · SGCMS 2016-C5 · WFCM 2016-C32/LC24/NXS5/BNK1 · BACM 2016 (CIK 1673542). MSBAM 2016 deals were not parsed (a file-name collision).
- **2021 sample: 28 conduit deals.** BANK 2021-BNK31–38 · BBCMS 2021-C10/C11/C12 · BMARK 2021-B23/B25–B31 · CSAIL 2021-C20 · GSMS 2021-GSA3 · MSC 2021-L5/L6/L7 · 3650R 2021-PF1 · WFCM 2021-C59/C60/C61.
- **2026 sample: 14 conduit deals.** BANK5 2026-5YR22/23/24 · BANK 2026-BNK52 · BBCMS 2026-5C42 · BMO 2026-5C15/5C16/C15 · BMARK 2026-V22/V23 · MSBAM 2026 (CIK 2145539) · WFCM 2026-C66/5C9/5C10. Annexes were filed 2026-05-04 to 2026-08-12; loans were originated Nov 2025 to Aug 4 2026.
- Index: EDGAR full-text search, FWP filings containing "Annex A-1" and "General Property Type". Example annex: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2136643/000153949726002080/n6057_x3-annexa.htm (BMO 2026-5C16). The rest are listed in the efts results for those dates.
- **Caveats:** these are samples, not the vintage universe (2016 had more conduit deals than 26). The coupons are at origination: a loan modified since then may now carry a different rate. 2016 loans are mostly 10-year terms (maturing about 2026); **2021 conduit office is mostly 10-year (maturing about 2031), so its gap is hypothetical at today's rates**. 2026 new issue is mostly 5-year (Trepp: 91% of conduit loan count in 2026 is 5-year). **The term mismatch (10-year in place vs 5-year replacement) is a real basis difference**, but it reflects the take-out the market actually offers.

- **Debt-yield lens:** 2016 office was originated at a WA UW NCF DY of 10.29%; 2026 office clears at WA 12.6%. If NCF were flat since origination (Trepp office NCF was +1.1% cumulative over 2021–25, though a different sample), DY sizing would support about **81.7%** of the original balance. **The DSCR/coupon constraint (63%) binds harder than the DY constraint (82%).** Breakeven DY at 1.25x DSCR, interest-only: 8.59% at 6.873% and 9.47% at 7.57%. This brackets Trepp's 8% "highest-risk" threshold.
- **Rate check, primary-pulled via FORGE `fetch.py` / yfinance on 2026-10-01:** 10Y UST was 5.24% (avg Jun 4.47, Jul 4.59, Aug 4.68, Sep 4.99). 5Y UST was 5.01% (Jun–Aug avg 4.31%; Q1 avg 3.78%). 2016-average 10Y was 1.83%, so the 2016 office coupon (4.34%) was about 250bp over the 10Y. Today's office coupon at Trepp's 287bp 5-year spread plus the 5.01% 5Y would be about 7.9%, again a scenario.
