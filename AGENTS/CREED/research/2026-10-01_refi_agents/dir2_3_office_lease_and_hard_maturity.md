## Bottom line (5 lines)

1. **Direction 2: no publisher has a NATIONAL office "largest tenant's lease expires before loan maturity" share that I could reach.** Trepp has run that screen only for LA office (8/21, held) and industrial (9/15, held). The closest national figures measure lease-expiry VOLUME and are not tied to loan maturity: Trepp says 85.5M sf of CMBS office leases expire in 2025 (5.8% of CMBS office inventory, 2025-02-04), and CompStak says more than 32% of office leases expire between Q2-2026 and YE-2028 (11 markets, 2026-04-03).
2. **Direction 3: no office STATED-vs-HARD split for 2026–28 is publicly reachable.** Trepp publishes the split only across all property types: for 2026, $146.2B stated, $76.6B hard and $69.7B extension-eligible; for 2H-2026, $65B, $37B and about $28B. The office cut sits inside two form-gated Trepp reports (the Hard Maturity Playbook and the 2H-2026 Refinance Gap). I did not submit either form.
3. **What IS public on a hard basis for office:** Trepp's monthly private-label hard-maturity cohorts. Office was $1.07B in April, $0.74B in May, $0.84B in July, $1.81B in August and $1.48B in September 2026. In August, 54.6% of the office cohort was in special servicing and all non-performing balance was office. In September the office cohort had 36.5% in special servicing, 45.67% below an 8% debt yield (DY) and 14.21% below 6%. Add the held sub-1.0x DSCR hard schedule and that is the whole public hard-basis office record.
4. **Stated-basis office maturity walls disagree because their perimeters differ, and they must not be mixed:**
   - CRED iQ: $39B of office CMBS matures in the next 12 months on an initial-maturity basis. CRED iQ itself warns that this overstates floating-rate maturities.
   - Yardi Matrix: $289.2B of all-lender office loans are recently matured or mature by YE-2028 (33.5% of office loan volume).
   - MBA: 17% of office mortgage BALANCES mature in 2026. That is a percent only.
   - **The "$167B / $123B / $76B" and "$148B" office-dollar figures attached to MBA are unattributed or contradictory. DO NOT CITE (trap #5).**
5. **Named loans:**
   - BSREP 2021-DC is the cleanest weak-coverage HARD 2026 case: $377.6M, fully extended 2026-08-09 with no options left, KBRA LTV (KLTV) 142.2%, and no payoff indication from the sponsor as of 6/16.
   - 280 Park Ave ($1.075B, DSCR 0.68x) is the basis trap in one loan. CRED iQ lists it among maturities due in the next 12 months. Trepp and KBRA put its HARD maturity at Sept-2028.
   - One New York Plaza shows that a "hard" maturity can be re-extended by modification: hard 2026-01-09, then modified to 2028-01-09 with two more one-year options.

**Structural read (ANALYTICAL, not a sourced figure):** these are inferences, not sourced facts.
- In conduit CMBS (fixed-rate balloon), stated maturity is mostly equal to hard maturity. That is an ASSUMPTION; ARD loans and modifications are exceptions.
- The extendable office pool is mostly floating-rate SASB and CRE CLO (initial 2-year term plus three 1-year options; see BSREP 2021-DC and 280 Park below).
- So "office maturities in the next 12 months" measured at initial maturity (3.11) overstates the hard wall by exactly the SASB/CLO extendable share. Trepp knows that share for office and has not published it.
- The industrial mirage (held: 14.4% hard by YE-2028) cannot be ported to office. Office SASB extension tests (DSCR/DY hurdles, cap purchases) bind harder, so the office hard share is likely HIGHER than industrial's. **That is untested.**

## Could CREED build these screens itself from free data?

**Direction 2: YES, for public conduit CMBS, with known holes.** I verified the fields against SEC EDGAR ABS-EE EX-102 for Benchmark 2024-V7 (CIK 2016841, filing 0001888524-26-013329, July-2026, 27 asset records). The schedule carries:
- `propertyTypeCode`
- `largestTenant`, `squareFeetLargestTenantNumber` and `leaseExpirationLargestTenantDate`, plus the 2nd and 3rd tenant
- `netRentableSquareFeetNumber`
- `maturityDate` and `postModificationMaturityDate`
- `mostRecentDebtServiceCoverageNetCashFlowpercentage` and `mostRecentPhysicalOccupancyPercentage`
- `reportPeriodEndActualBalanceAmount`
- `mostRecentAnnualLeaseRolloverReviewDate`

That is the full Trepp method: the largest tenant's lease date vs loan maturity, the largest tenant's share of NRA (>30% and >50% buckets), and balance-weighting. Holes:
1. Only Reg AB II **registered** deals (2017+ vintages) file ABS-EE. **144A SASB and CRE CLO are off EDGAR**, and so are pre-2017 conduits.
2. Pari-passu split loans appear in several trusts. De-duplicate by property and loan identity, or balances double-count (Trepp uses TreppREAL ID).
3. Tenant fields refresh only at the annual rollover review, so check the review date before trusting them.
4. It covers no bank balance sheets.

Scale: one EX-102 per trust per month, a few hundred trusts. That is feasible with the existing `.venv` plus EDGAR (User-Agent "Research CREED williepowen@gmail.com").

**Direction 3: NO, not from free data.** The ABS-EE schema has **no extension-option or fully-extended-maturity field**: a grep for "extens" across every tag in the EX-102 returned nothing; only `maturityDate` and `postModificationMaturityDate` exist. The extendable office pool (floating SASB and CRE CLO) is 144A anyway. The only free route is loan-by-loan: KBRA and Morningstar DBRS surveillance press releases state "fully extended maturity" dates in prose (as for BSREP 2021-DC, ONYP and 280 Park above). That is a hand-built named-loan tracker, not a national split. A national office hard/extendable split means buying Trepp (or CRED iQ), or getting the gated Playbook through a sanctioned channel. **That is Will's call, not this agent's.**
