## Key Concerns & Bear Case

### 1. ⚠️ Q3 2025 Loan Fraud / "Cockroach Risk" Incident
- **October 2025:** WAL stock dropped ~11% after disclosing a borrower **failed to provide collateral** on a loan.
- The borrower (linked to Andrew Stupin, a California real estate investor) allegedly **forged title insurance policies** to obscure the fact that other banks already had a claim on the pledged properties (Cantor fund entity).
- Same borrower tied to ~$270M in troubled debt across multiple banks (Zions, Banc of California, Enterprise Bank, Nano Banc, WAL).
- Oct 22, 2025: WAL said they found **no further irregularities** in the loan portfolio.
- This event re-activated the "cockroach" narrative — Jamie Dimon's warning about one-off bad loans being the first of many.
- **Trepp specifically cited WAL** in its "cockroach-like one-offs" Q3 2025 banking review alongside First Citizens and Zions.
- **What it means for our thesis:** The collateral fraud involved real estate. Even if classified under C&I or "lender finance," the underlying collateral is real property. This is exactly the type of hidden CRE risk that standard loan classification misses.

### 2. ⚠️ C&I Portfolio Growth — Potential Reclassification Risk
- C&I loans grew from 39% to 44% of HFI portfolio in one year. This is management's narrative: "we're diversifying away from CRE."
- **However:** WAL's C&I includes "lender finance" (loans to non-bank financial companies), which can be backed by real estate collateral while classified as C&I. The Stupin/Cantor incident is exactly this — real estate underlying a nominally non-RE loan.
- **No sell-side analyst has explicitly flagged C&I-to-CRE reclassification risk in recent notes.** This is a blind spot in consensus.
- The Q1 2025 earnings transcript notes: "Our $8 billion total [reserves] compares to the peer median of just $69 million with several having no exposure at all" — this language suggests management is aware of the reserve gap and is defensive about it.

### 3. ⚠️ CRE Exposure — Still Present in 10-K
- WAL's 2025 10-K (filed ~Feb 2026) explicitly flags CRE market stress as a risk factor.
- ACL on funded loans: **$460.6M**; unfunded commitments: **$49.6M**; OREO: **$137M**
- These are elevated but management argues coverage is conservative ("weighted to scenarios more pessimistic than economists forecast," including peak unemployment above 6% and significant CRE valuation stress).
- The residential portfolio ($14B) is management's defense: high FICO, low LTV, covered by $8.5B in credit-linked notes.

### 4. ⚠️ Non-Bank Financial Institution Exposure
- StockAnalysis notes WAL as "one of the regional banks facing concerns over loans made to non-bank financial players."
- This is the "lender finance" vertical — loans to private credit funds, BDCs, and other shadow banking entities.
- Direct linkage to our APO/private credit thesis thread.

### 5. JP Morgan Hold Downgrade (Jan 2026)
- Alexopoulos at JPM downgraded after Q4 2025 beat — unusual to downgrade on a beat.
- JPM's concern likely valuation (stock was near $90+ pre-downgrade) combined with macro uncertainty.
- With stock now at $80, the downgrade case may be priced in — or JPM saw something in guidance that others didn't.
