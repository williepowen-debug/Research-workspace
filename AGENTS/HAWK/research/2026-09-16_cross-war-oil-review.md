## Decision summary

**Supply-delivery disruption is real enough to monitor, but a combined lost-crude total is not established.** Saudi pipeline shutdown and reported cancelled/suspended loadings are a crude-transport problem. Russia's latest refinery campaign now has stronger operational outage evidence, while a newly recovered Bloomberg crude-export observation is counterevidence to continuous deterioration. Adding the two theaters' facility capacities would get both the quantity and, for refinery strikes, potentially the crude-price direction wrong.

**The strongest near-term counter-signal is a reported partial Petroline restart objective within days.** It is a plan, not resumed pumping; a longer full-repair horizon can coexist with earlier partial flows. Reported east-coast Saudi loading and uninterrupted deliveries at one buyer further refute an all-exports-stopped claim, but do not measure net replacement. Territorial gains around Bab do not establish a new enforced closure. Current insurance repricing remains unmeasured.

The actionable research distinction for BRENT is **delivery loss versus delayed/rerouted cargo, products shortage versus crude loss, and actual restart versus a repair objective**. Existing owner grades remain unchanged. This report proposes no trade.

## Strongest evidence against a worsening supply thesis

1. Partial-restart objective, inventory buffering and some continuing loading/delivery provide mechanisms for interruption to reverse before full repairs finish. All have measurement limits; no current “supply intact” conclusion follows.
2. Refinery damage is not destroyed crude production; products-terminal fire is not automatically a crude-berth closure. Repeated attacks add severity only if a repaired/operating asset loses incremental output.
3. Bab's observed tanker series does not support declaring a new generalized zero-flow closure. It cannot exonerate every route or ship class.
4. Bloomberg's newly readable3.54mb/d four-week average to September13 is a crude-export counter-signal. Missing intermediate prints still prevent a full path reconstruction, and the observation does not satisfy OSPREY's downgrade rule.
5. No current matched premium measurement supports assigning the whole oil-price move to war-risk insurance. BRENT owns price attribution; no stale quote is refreshed by this report.
