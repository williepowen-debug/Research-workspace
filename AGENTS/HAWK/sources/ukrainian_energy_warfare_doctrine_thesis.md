## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Ukraine has systematically developed and executed an **energy warfare doctrine** targeting Russian oil and gas infrastructure. This is not opportunistic — it is a deliberate, phased campaign that has evolved from isolated strikes to sustained attrition warfare against refineries, pipeline pumping stations, and export terminals. The campaign is **accelerating** in both frequency and geographic depth, with strikes now reaching 2,000+ km from Ukrainian territory.

**Three theses:**

1. **Attack Tempo is Escalating Exponentially** — From ~1–2 strikes/month in early 2025 to 58+ strikes in a single quarter (Aug–Oct 2025), escalating further with Storm Shadow integration in late 2025 and Druzhba pipeline hub targeting in early 2026.

2. **The Kursk Incursion Served as an Energy Warfare Enabler** — Ukraine's territorial hold in Kursk, while militarily costly to maintain, placed Ukrainian forces within close-range drone reach of multiple oil/gas facilities and pipeline infrastructure serving Belgorod, Bryansk, and feeding Druzhba's western branches. This was strategically rational.

3. **Markets Are Mispricing Ceasefire** — "Ceasefire = oil supply restoration" is structurally wrong. Physical damage is cumulative, repair cycles are being disrupted by repeated strikes, and Ukraine has **strategic incentive to continue striking energy targets even during negotiations**. Russian export capacity trajectory is downward through at least mid-2026 regardless of diplomatic outcomes.

## COMPONENT 1: ATTACK TEMPO ANALYSIS — THE ESCALATION CURVE

### 1.1 Chronological Strike Catalog

#### Phase 0: Pre-Doctrine (2022–2023)
- Isolated, opportunistic strikes near front lines
- Targets: fuel depots, railway logistics
- Range: <300 km from front lines
- Frequency: Episodic, non-systematic
- **Strategic character:** Tactical support, not energy warfare

#### Phase 1: Early Campaign (Q1–Q2 2024)
- Sustained but limited campaign against border-proximate refineries
- Primary targets: Krasnodar, Rostov regions
- Equipment: First-generation long-range FPV and loitering munitions
- Typical damage: Atmospheric distillation units, storage tanks
- Repair timelines: Days to weeks
- **Strategic character:** Testing range/capability, learning target vulnerability

**Key tactical evolution observed (Kpler analysis):**
- **Targeting shift:** Early campaign focused on Crude Distillation Units (CDUs). By August 2025, Ukraine shifted to **secondary processing units** — Hydrocrackers (HCK), Fluid Catalytic Crackers (FCC), Hydrotreaters, Reformers.
- **Why this matters:** Secondary units produce finished fuels. They take longer to repair. Many components are **sanctioned Western equipment** with no Russian equivalent available.
- **Repeat-strike strategy:** Strikes timed to hit during repair windows, ~every 21 days. Purpose: prevent full restarts, extend downtime, exhaust repair crews and materials.

**Impact of Phase 3:**
- Russian refinery throughput fell to ~5.0 Mbd (Sep–Nov 2025) — 335 kbd y/y drop
- Gasoline supply down ~120 kbd; export ban extended; Belarus imports emergency fill
- Gasoil supply down ~150 kbd
- Runs ~400 kbd below expectations by December 2025

#### Phase 4: Weapon Escalation (Dec 2025–Present)

**December 25, 2025 (Christmas Strike Package):**
- Ukraine deployed **British Storm Shadow cruise missiles** against Russian oil targets for the first time
- Target: Novoshakhtinsk oil refinery, Rostov Oblast
- Simultaneous drone strikes on: Temryuk port (2,000 m² oil tank fire), Krasnodar region terminal
- **Significance:** Storm Shadow has ~250 km range from launch point — but from Ukrainian airspace, extends effective energy warfare reach significantly. Also signals UK-authorized escalation against energy targets.

**January 1, 2026:**
- Long-range drones struck oil depot **north of Moscow** (extremely deep) + Krasnodar refinery + oil terminal (DW reporting)

**February 23, 2026 — Kaleykino Strike (Strategic Escalation):**
- **SBU strike on Kaleykino pumping station, Tatarstan** (~1,400 km from front)
- Two 50,000-ton storage tanks ignited
- Transneft forced to cut crude intake by **250,000 bpd**
- Target significance: Kaleykino is a **master blending hub** receiving crude from Western Siberia, Tatarstan, Udmurtia, and Bashkortostan — fed directly into Druzhba pipeline main branches
- **This is qualitatively different:** Previous strikes hit refineries (processing). This hits the **pipeline network arterial infrastructure**.

## COMPONENT 2: KURSK INCURSION AS ENERGY WARFARE ENABLER

### 2.1 The Strategic Geometry

Ukraine launched the Kursk incursion in August 2024, establishing a salient approximately 30-50 km deep into Russian territory at peak. The operation drew intense criticism for diverting Ukrainian forces from defensive lines. **Standard military analysis focused on:** prisoner leverage, territory-for-territory exchange, morale/political signaling to Western allies.

**Missing from most analyses:** The energy warfare dimension.

### 2.2 What's Within Drone Range of the Kursk Salient

The Kursk salient, at its deepest penetration (~50 km into Russia), places Ukrainian forces approximately:

**Immediate range (0–150 km from Kursk salient):**
- Kursk Oblast oil infrastructure: fuel depots, rail logistics nodes
- **Voronezh Oblast** — major rail/logistics hub, fuel storage
- Belgorod Oblast — forward military logistics (strikes already occurring pre-incursion)

**Medium range (150–400 km from Kursk salient — standard long-range FPV reach):**
- **Bryansk Oblast:** Unecha pipeline pumping station (struck Aug 21, 2025 — major Druzhba feed station with significant fire)
- **Tula Oblast:** Industrial/energy complex
- **Lipetsk Oblast:** Industrial facilities

**Extended range (400–800 km from Kursk salient — Ukraine's long-range drones):**
- **Ryazan Oblast:** Ryazan refinery (376 kbd, Rosneft's largest) — one of the most-struck facilities
- **Nizhny Novgorod Oblast:** Nizhny Novgorod refinery (359 kbd)
- **Saratov Oblast:** Saratov refinery (150 kbd)
- **Penza Oblast:** Transit infrastructure

**Critical strategic observation:** The Kursk salient is approximately **250 km closer** to Ryazan, Saratov, and other central Russian refinery clusters than launching from Ukrainian-controlled territory. This is operationally significant for drone range/payload optimization.

### 2.3 Druzhba Pipeline Corridor — The Key Strategic Geometry

The Druzhba pipeline runs from east to west across Russia and branches in Belarus. The key arteries:

```
Western Siberia/Tatarstan/Bashkortostan oil fields
         ↓
Kaleykino (Tatarstan) — master blending hub [struck Feb 2026]
         ↓
Unecha pumping station (Bryansk) — key feed station [struck Aug 2025]
         ↓
Belarus junction → Poland/Germany (northern branch)
                → Hungary/Slovakia/Czech Republic (southern branch)
```

**Kursk salient geographic position:** The salient sits directly north of Belgorod and directly west of Voronezh — essentially **straddling the Druzhba western corridor**. Any drone launched from the Kursk salient or nearby liberated Ukrainian territory has substantially reduced flight distance to:
- Unecha pumping station (Bryansk) — ~250-300 km
- Pipeline infrastructure in Kursk, Belgorod, Bryansk corridor
- Military fuel logistics supporting Russian southern/southwestern groupings

### 2.5 The Strategic Motivation Thesis

**Question:** Was energy warfare access a strategic motivation for the Kursk incursion beyond the stated military/political reasons?

**Assessment: PROBABLE SECONDARY MOTIVATION, EXPLOITED FULLY**

Evidence for:
1. **Timing correlation:** Kursk incursion launched August 2024. Ukraine's most intensive energy warfare phase began August 2025 — a 12-month ramp correlating with consolidation of Kursk knowledge/infrastructure.
2. **Unecha struck August 21, 2025** — immediately as the Phase 3 campaign launched, hitting a facility directly accessible from the Kursk corridor.
3. **Ukrainian intelligence assets in the salient** provide ground-truth targeting intelligence for deep-strike drones that cannot use GPS in Russian territory (Russian jamming is intense beyond ~500 km).
4. **Forward staging:** Classified forward drone staging/relay capability cannot be confirmed from open sources, but the operational pattern of reaching 1,400 km (Kaleykino) suggests relay architecture possibly exploiting Kursk corridor.
5. **Druzhba vulnerability:** The western Druzhba branches run through exactly the geographic region where the Kursk salient provides cover/access.

Evidence against:
1. Kursk incursion's primary stated and observable purpose was leverage for negotiations + prisoner exchange.
2. Long-range drone strikes launched from deep inside Ukraine (Zaporizhzhia, Dnipropetrovsk direction) also hit Bashkortostan/Tatarstan without needing Kursk proximity.
3. Tactical cost was high — significant Ukrainian manpower expended maintaining the salient.

**Net assessment:** The Kursk incursion created an energy warfare **option** that Ukraine exercised. Whether it was a primary motivation is uncertain. That Ukraine **exploited** the geographic advantage for energy warfare targeting is **confirmed by the Unecha strike timing and subsequent escalation pattern**.

## COMPONENT 3: OIL SUPPLY IMPLICATIONS — THE CEASEFIRE MISPRICING THESIS

### 3.1 The Market Narrative vs. Physical Reality

**Market consensus:**  
> "Ceasefire → sanctions relief pathway opens → Russian oil floods back → Brent down"

**This thesis argues the market is structurally wrong on three counts:**

### 3.2 Count 1: Physical Damage is Cumulative and Deepening

**IEA Assessment (October 2025):** Drone strikes will suppress Russian oil processing rates until **at least mid-2026**. This is not price-talk — it's an engineering constraint.

**Kpler's "beats to death" framework applies here:**
- No single strike is fatal
- But each strike forces Russia to use spare capacity, cannibalize maintenance, defer upgrades
- Spare capacity buffer (was ~1.5 Mbd at baseline) has been drawn down throughout 2025
- Russia was running ~5.0 Mbd throughput by Q4 2025 vs. 5.5 Mbd pre-campaign

**The compounding math:**
| Quarter | Russia Refinery Throughput | Y/Y Change | Notes |
|---------|--------------------------|-----------|-------|
| Q4 2024 | ~5.4 Mbd | Baseline | Pre-escalation |
| Q1 2025 | ~5.3 Mbd | -2% | Wave 2 begins |
| Q3 2025 | ~5.15 Mbd | -4-5% | Phase 3 peak disruption |
| Q4 2025 | ~5.0-5.1 Mbd | -6-7% | Kpler: 400 kbd below expectation |
| Q1 2026 | ~4.9-5.0 Mbd | -8%+ | Kaleykino strike, winter demand |
| Forecast Q2-Q3 2026 | ~4.7-5.0 Mbd | -8-12% | IEA: suppressed through mid-2026 |

**Key: This is BEFORE ceasefire.** Ceasefire stops new damage. It does not restore already-struck capacity on a fast timeline.

### 3.3 Count 2: Ukraine Has Strategic Incentive to Strike During Negotiations

**This is the critical mispricing vector.**

A ceasefire creates a window for Ukraine to achieve maximum energy warfare impact:
- **Ceasefire ≠ Ukrainian drones stand down on energy targets.** Historical precedent: Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge during ceasefire discussions. Energy targets would be similar.
- **Ukrainian incentive structure during negotiations:**
  - Strikes on energy infrastructure fund Russian war: degrading them is war-shortening
  - Energy infrastructure is not (always) covered by ceasefire terms — Russia's "no strikes on civilian infrastructure" demands vs. Ukraine's "oil = war machine" framing
  - The deeper into negotiations, the greater the asymmetric value of economic warfare vs. kinetic

- **The game theory:**
  - Russia's best outcome: ceasefire → energy recovery → rebuild war chest → resume later
  - Ukraine's counter: maintain energy attrition through and beyond ceasefire
  - Historical parallel: Korean War, WWII strategic bombing continued during Panmunjom negotiations

**Market implication:** Markets price in "ceasefire = peace = oil flows." The correct model is "ceasefire = pause in ground conflict ≠ pause in energy warfare."

### 3.4 Count 3: Pipeline Infrastructure Damage Changes the Export Equation

**Druzhba is the last Russian pipeline to Europe. It's now under attack at the network level.**

**Kaleykino pumping station (struck Feb 23, 2026):**
- Caused 250,000 bpd cut in crude intake
- It is not a refinery — it is a **network node**. Disrupting it cascades across the entire Druzhba supply chain
- Storage tanks (50,000 tons each) take months to rebuild
- This is Ukraine shifting its targeting from processing capacity to **export transport infrastructure**

**What this means for the ceasefire narrative:**
- Even if Russia rebuilds refining capacity, Druzhba network disruption limits what crude can reach those refineries (or western export terminals)
- Druzhba's southern branch (Hungary/Slovakia exemption) depends on Kaleykino-class infrastructure being intact
- Ceasefire does not fast-forward pipeline repair to pre-strike condition

### 3.5 Russian Oil Export Capacity Trajectory: 6–12 Month Forecast

**Scenario A: Ceasefire by Q2 2026, Ukraine halts energy warfare**
- Refinery throughput recovers slowly: ~5.1-5.2 Mbd by Q3 2026
- Druzhba pipeline repairs: 3-6 months post-ceasefire
- Export volumes recover: +150-200 kbd over 6 months
- **Not a supply flood. A slow trickle.**

**Scenario B: Ceasefire by Q2 2026, Ukraine continues energy warfare (Base Case)**
- Continued strikes on secondary processing units, pipeline stations
- Throughput stays suppressed: ~4.8-5.0 Mbd through Q3-Q4 2026
- Druzhba disruptions episodic but persistent
- Russian fuel export ban maintained or re-imposed
- **Net effect: No meaningful export volume recovery**

**Scenario C: No ceasefire, campaign escalates (Bull Case for oil prices)**
- Storm Shadow authorization expands to deeper/hardened targets
- Ukraine targets Druzhba pumping stations systematically (Kaleykino was first)
- Potential targets: Almetyevsk, Chelyabinsk, major Transneft trunk stations
- **Export capacity could fall 500-700 kbd from peak within 12 months**
- This would be material to global balances

**Scenario D: Ceasefire + Western sanctions relief (Bear Case for oil prices)**
- Political resolution enables partial sanctions rollback
- European buyers gradually return to Russian crude (years, not months)
- Druzhba fully repaired and operational
- **Probability: LOW.** EU infrastructure has been rewired. Legislative reversal of the seaborne ban requires qualified majority. Most European refineries no longer configured for Urals crude.

### 3.6 The "Energy Dominance" Framework Integration

From the Energy Dominance strategy context:
> US energy dominance strategy benefits from constrained Russian supply. A weakened Russian energy sector increases US LNG, oil, and energy services market share globally.

**The Ukraine energy warfare doctrine is therefore:**
1. **Aligned with US strategic interests** (constrained Russian supply → higher prices → US energy revenue)
2. **Operating in a political space where US may tacitly permit** continued energy warfare even under ceasefire pressure
3. **Structurally different from Iran disruption:** Ukrainian attacks are predictable, measurable, and non-catastrophic — they suppress supply without triggering a price panic that damages US consumer demand

**Market positioning implication:**
- **"Ceasefire = oil down" trade is structurally short Russia physical supply but long US production**
- The physical damage to Russian refinery and pipeline infrastructure is a **floor under Russian production**
- That floor is ~4.7-5.0 Mbd processing — vs. pre-war ~5.5 Mbd
- Ceasefire does not lift that floor quickly
- **The trade should be: ceasefire = modestly oil negative on sentiment only, not on fundamental supply**

## 7. CONCLUSION

**Ukrainian energy warfare doctrine has crossed a strategic threshold.** What began as opportunistic, limited strikes on nearby refineries has evolved into a systematic, deep-penetration campaign targeting the entire Russian downstream oil supply chain — from processing (refineries) to transport (Druzhba pumping stations) to export terminals.

**The attack frequency data admits no other interpretation:** 58+ strikes in a single quarter in 2025, 180+ strikes across the full year, now reaching 1,400 km into Russia and hitting pipeline network nodes that constrain all downstream operations simultaneously.

**The ceasefire mispricing thesis stands:** Markets conflate "hostilities end" with "supply restores." The correct model requires understanding that:
- Physical damage to Russian refinery secondary units takes 6–18 months to repair
- Druzhba network disruption cascades across all export volumes
- Ukraine has rational incentive to continue energy warfare regardless of ground-war status
- The IEA's own mid-2026 suppression forecast was published *before* the Kaleykino strike

**This is not a bear case. This is the base case.** The bull case for Russian oil supply restoration requires a set of conditions — complete ceasefire, no energy warfare carve-out, rapid repair, EU sanctions rollback, infrastructure reconstruction — that would have to occur simultaneously in a political environment making each individually implausible.

**For the Energy Dominance trade:**  
Russian oil export capacity is structurally impaired through at least mid-2026. Ceasefire sentiment may temporarily compress oil prices on "supply restoration" narrative. That compression is a **buying opportunity**, not a structural shift.
