# Russian Oil Infrastructure: Damage Assessment
**Filed:** 2026-03-03  
**Sources:** Reuters, Carnegie Endowment/Moscow Times, CREA, Kyiv Independent, iSANS  
**Purpose:** Energy Dominance thesis — is "ceasefire = oil flows back" narrative realistic?

---

## 1. STRIKE CATALOG — Major Ukrainian Attacks on Russian Oil Infrastructure

### Wave 1: Early 2024
- Sustained campaign against refineries close to border (Krasnodar, Rostov regions)
- Limited range drones, mostly fire/partial damage, weeks-to-repair timelines

### Wave 2: Q1 2025
Six major refineries hit:
- **Ryazan** (Rosneft's largest refinery) — halted operations, ~1 month offline
- **Volgograd** — hit multiple times, fully repaired by Aug 25 after Aug 13-14 strikes
- **Saratov** — struck twice
- **Tuapse** (Black Sea coast refinery)
- **Ufa** (Bashkortostan, deep inside Russia)
- **Astrakhan** (southeast Russia, ~1,300 km from Ukraine)

### Wave 3: August–October 2025 (Escalation)
At least 58 attacks on Russian energy sites; drones flying 2,000 km into Russia.
- **Novokuibyshevsk (Novokuibyshev)** — struck 3 times (~every 3 weeks), halted processing Oct 2025
- **Kirishi** (near St. Petersburg) — largest unit halted after Oct 2025 strike
- **Salavat** (Bashkortostan petrochemical complex) — struck twice in September
- **Ryazan** — struck again
- **Saratov** — struck again
- **Volgograd** — struck again (twice in August, twice in September)
- **Unecha pumping station** (Bryansk) — August 21, 2025, fire at major Druzhba feed station

### Most Recent: February 2026
- **Kaleykino pumping station** (Tatarstan) — Feb 23, 2026 SBU drone strike; two 50,000-ton storage tanks ignited; forced Transneft to cut crude intake by **250,000 bpd**. Key Druzhba network hub.

**Total: At least 17 major refineries struck as of Nov 2025 (Reuters count)**

## 2. PERMANENT vs. TEMPORARY DAMAGE — The Critical Question

### Bottom Line: Mostly Repairable, But With Compounding Costs

**The 38% figure is MISLEADING:**
- 38% = total nameplate capacity of *attacked* refineries, not actual damage
- Russia's total refinery capacity: ~6.6 million bpd (327 million tons/year)
- Russia's actual throughput before attacks: ~5.3–5.5 million bpd (refineries run ~78% utilization due to old idle equipment)
- Actual output reduction: only ~3% year-on-year Jan–Oct 2025; peak disruption ~6% (300,000 bpd)

**Why repairs are fast (weeks, not months):**
- Drone strikes typically hit atmospheric distillation columns and storage tanks
- This equipment is relatively simple, mostly Russian-manufactured
- Spare parts available domestically or from China
- Russia had significant excess/idle capacity to absorb disruption

**What makes it harder over time:**
- Western sanctions block access to specialized Western refinery equipment (upgrade components, catalyst systems for secondary conversion units)
- Russia claims domestic manufacturing + Chinese imports replace this, but costly
- Multiple strikes on same facility force repeated repair cycles
- Undisclosed: Kirishi is a complex, modern refinery; Salavat is a petrochemical hub — more complex damage may take longer

**Carnegie/iSANS assessment (Oct–Nov 2025):**
> "Drone attacks do not cause irreparable damage to oil refineries: the facilities resume operation after a while."
> "It becomes harder and harder to recover after each subsequent blow. Although no single punch is fatal, [Russia] could end up being beaten to death."

**Repair timelines observed:**
- Simple distillation column damage: 2–4 weeks
- Storage tank fires: weeks to repair/replace
- Complex unit damage (secondary processing, conversion): potentially 3–6 months
- Truly permanent destruction: NOT OBSERVED so far — every struck facility has partially or fully resumed

**Key caveat:** Russia stopped publishing refinery data in 2024. True damage picture is opaque.

## 3. WHAT RUSSIA HAS LEFT TO SELL EUROPE (Ceasefire Scenario)

### Current European Russian Oil Purchases (Pre-Druzhba Outage)
- **Druzhba pipeline (southern branch):** ~200,000 bpd to Hungary + Slovakia (EU exemption)
- **Seaborne to EU:** Minimal — most EU countries banned Russian crude post-2022
- EU only accounts for ~6% of Russian crude exports (mostly residual Druzhba)
- EU buys Russian **LNG** (unsanctioned) — largest global buyer at 49% of Russian LNG exports

### "Ceasefire = Oil Flows Back" — Is it Realistic?

**Short answer: Mostly NO. Here's why:**

1. **EU crude ban (Dec 2022 seaborne ban + exceptions):** Legislative change required, not just political will. Most European infrastructure has already been rewired to non-Russian supply.

2. **Druzhba itself is now physically damaged** (see Section 4). Even if politics resolved tomorrow, pipeline needs repair.

3. **Pre-war Druzhba flows (~1 million bpd) are gone.** Most EU countries built alternative supply infrastructure (Adria pipeline, Baltic terminals, LNG terminals). Reconversion would take years and investment.

4. **Refinery damage compounds crude export problems.** With less refining capacity, Russia exports more raw crude but less product — and its main alternative markets (India dropping volumes Jan 2026, Turkey mixed) are uncertain.

5. **Russia's total fossil fuel revenue is at all-time low** (EUR 464 mn/day Jan 2026 per CREA) despite still selling to China, India, Turkey. Adding Europe back is not a flip of a switch.

## 4. DRUZHBA PIPELINE — Physical Damage vs. Political Suspension

**This is NEW and CRITICAL (as of Feb 2026):**

### Timeline:
- **Jan 27, 2026:** Flows halted. Ukraine says Russian attack damaged a pumping station near Brody (western Ukraine).
- **Feb 12, 2026:** Ukraine officially confirms suspension; notes ongoing repairs but Russian shelling threatens workers.
- **Feb 23, 2026:** Ukraine (SBU) strikes Kaleykino pumping station in Tatarstan — key Druzhba hub, 250,000 bpd cut to Transneft intake. Two 50,000-ton tanks destroyed.
- **Feb 25, 2026:** EU technical meeting receives Ukraine's note confirming physical damage to Brody pumping station and pipeline equipment.
- **Feb 26, 2026:** Reuters explainer — pipeline has been offline since late January.

### Who Did What:
- **Initial damage:** Russian missile/drone attack on Ukrainian Brody pumping station
- **Ukrainian counter-strike:** Kaleykino hub in Tatarstan hit on Feb 23 — now Russia's *own* pipeline infrastructure is damaged
- **Druzhba carried Ukrainian oil too:** Before the disruption, pipeline was also transiting some Ukrainian crude alongside Russian crude (Reuters Feb 27 exclusive)

### Current Status:
- **BOTH ENDS PHYSICALLY DAMAGED** — not a political suspension
- Southern branch throughput in 2025 already at 10-year low (9.7 million tons)
- Hungary/Slovakia have accused Ukraine of delaying repairs; Ukraine says Russian shelling blocks repair workers
- Hungary blocked EU sanctions on Russia + EUR 90bn EU loan for Ukraine in retaliation
- Croatia's Adria pipeline could theoretically replace 200,000 bpd (Hungary/MOL disputes this)

### Repair Timeline:
- Ukrainian side (Brody): Unknown, complicated by ongoing Russian strikes
- Russian side (Kaleykino): Unknown, but storage tanks and pumping station damage; weeks to months
- Political resolution: Requires Ukraine/Hungary negotiation; Hungary elections April 12, 2026

## 5. NET ASSESSMENT: How Much Could Russia Restore to Europe?

### If Sanctions Lifted Tomorrow (Hypothetical)

**Immediate (0–3 months): ~100,000–200,000 bpd**
- Druzhba physically damaged; needs repair at Brody (Ukraine) AND Kaleykino (Russia)
- Even after repair, southern branch capacity limited by Hungary/Slovakia demand
- Seaborne exports to Europe face infrastructure constraints (European terminals rewired)
- Net: Possibly partial Druzhba restoration, no meaningful seaborne volume

**Medium term (6–18 months): ~300,000–500,000 bpd**
- Full Druzhba repair + restoration to pre-war southern branch flows (~200,000 bpd Hungary/Slovakia)
- Some EU countries might restart seaborne imports if political will exists
- BUT: Would require EU legislative changes, re-contracting, infrastructure reversal
- Russia's refinery constraints mean more crude on market but potentially lower product yields

**Long term (2–5 years): ~600,000–900,000 bpd**
- Pre-war Russia exported ~2.5 million bpd to Europe (seaborne + pipeline combined)
- Most of this infrastructure is gone: Nord Stream destroyed, Baltic Pipe alternatives built, LNG terminals expanded across Europe
- Even with full political will, physical infrastructure to receive Russian crude in most EU countries no longer exists or has been decommissioned
- The market has structurally diversified — Norwegian, US LNG, Azerbaijani gas, Middle Eastern oil

**Critical constraint — Refinery Damage:**
- No permanently destroyed refineries (as of current data)
- But: 3-6% throughput reduction persists; western spare parts unavailable; cumulative degradation real
- Russia can continue exporting crude (its export infrastructure is mostly intact for non-European markets)
- The damage is to *domestic processing* more than *export pipelines*

## INVESTMENT THESIS IMPLICATIONS

### "Ceasefire = Oil Down" Trade — Is It Wrong?

**Likely YES, partially.** The narrative assumes Russian supply bounces back quickly. It won't, for structural reasons:

1. **Physical pipeline damage (Druzhba)** — months to repair even with political will
2. **European market is structurally diversified** — demand for Russian crude in Europe is near zero outside Hungary/Slovakia
3. **Sanctioned infrastructure** — EU crude ban requires legislative reversal, not just executive action
4. **Russia's refinery degradation** — reduces product exports regardless of crude export capacity
5. **Geopolitical trust problem** — even in ceasefire, European utilities/refiners won't re-sign Russian contracts (force majeure risk, political risk, regulatory risk)

**The realistic upside scenario for Russian supply:**
- Druzhba southern branch resumes: +200,000 bpd (Hungary/Slovakia only)
- Some seaborne crude to fringe EU buyers: +50,000–100,000 bpd
- **Total realistic near-term European restoration: ~250,000–300,000 bpd**
- vs. pre-war ~2.5 million bpd to Europe

**That's ~10–12% of pre-war flows, in an optimistic 12–18 month scenario.**

**For global oil prices**, the more relevant factor is Russia's exports to China/India/Turkey, which:
- Are NOT capacity-constrained by Ukrainian strikes (mainly seaborne from Novorossiysk/Baltic)
- ARE constrained by US sanctions on the shadow fleet (ongoing)
- China's Urals imports hit all-time high January 2026 despite everything
