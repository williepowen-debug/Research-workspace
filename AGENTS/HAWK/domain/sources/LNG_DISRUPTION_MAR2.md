## 1. WHAT'S DISRUPTED

### Root Cause
- **Feb 28, 2026:** US and Israel launched coordinated strikes on Iran under **Operation Epic Fury** — targeting IRGC facilities, nuclear sites, missile/drone sites, air defense systems, and leadership. Khamenei killed.
- Iran responded with retaliatory missile/drone strikes across the Gulf: UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, US military bases.
- IRGC issued VHF warnings prohibiting vessel passage through the Strait of Hormuz — not legally a formal blockade, but effectively one.

### Strait of Hormuz
- **~70% reduction** in tanker traffic (ship-tracking data)
- **150+ vessels** anchored outside the strait in open Gulf waters
- **20% of global daily oil supply** affected (~20M barrels/day)
- GPS jamming: **1,100+ vessels affected** within 24 hours across the Middle East Gulf (Windward AI)
- **200%+ increase** in dark vessel activity first night of escalation
- Airspace closures across Gulf region

### LNG-Specific Disruption
- **At least 13 empty LNG tankers** diverted away from eastern side of Hormuz chokepoint (Bloomberg, Mar 1)
- Major shipping firms suspending operations in the area

### War Risk Insurance — CRITICAL DEVELOPMENT
- **7 of the 12** International Group of P&I Clubs (which cover ~90% of global ocean-going tonnage) have issued withdrawal notices for war risk coverage
- **Effective: midnight London time, March 5** (Thursday)
- Coverage withdrawal applies to: Persian Gulf, Iranian waters, Gulf of Oman, waters west of Ras al Hadd (Oman), northeast to Iran-Pakistan border
- Named insurers withdrawing: Gard AS, NorthStandard, Steamship Mutual, Skuld, American Club, Swedish Club, London P&I Club
- Pre-strike war risk premiums: 0.125% → 0.2–0.4% of ship value per transit (for VLCCs = ~$250K increase per transit)
- **After March 5:** effectively uninsurable for most operators

### QatarEnergy Facilities — DIRECT PHYSICAL STRIKE
- Iran launched drones, hitting **Ras Laffan Industrial City** and **Mesaieed Industrial City**
- QatarEnergy confirmed: **LNG production HALTED** (statement issued Monday, Mar 2)
- Ras Laffan is Qatar's primary LNG export hub — the world's largest LNG facility
- Qatar's Defense Ministry: 2 Iranian drones struck facilities; **no casualties reported**
- No timeline given for restart

### Saudi Arabia
- Aramco's **Ras Tanura refinery** struck by drone — shut as precautionary measure (Reuters, Mar 2)

## 2. EUROPEAN EXPOSURE

- Qatar supplies **12–14%** of Europe's LNG imports
- Qatar became critical to Europe post-2022 Ukraine war as Russian pipeline gas was phased out
- Europe does NOT primarily depend on Gulf LNG — most European LNG comes from Norway (pipeline), US, Algeria, Nigeria
- However: **indirect impact is severe** — if Qatar/Gulf supply drops off, Asian buyers bid aggressively for US/Australian cargoes, creating global price competition Europe cannot escape
- **EU gas storage at ~30% capacity** — below the ~40% at same point last year (below 5-year average)
  - Germany: **20.5%** full as of March 1
  - France: **21%** full as of March 1
- This is the worst storage trough heading into a disruption scenario in years — **extremely high vulnerability**

### Hormuz % of European gas supply
- LNG through Hormuz = ~20% of global LNG trade; Qatar specifically = 12–14% of European LNG
- European LNG = ~25–30% of total European gas supply (rest is pipelines: Norway, Algeria, Russia residual)
- Therefore Qatari LNG ≈ **3–4% of total European gas supply** direct exposure
- But at current storage levels (<30%), losing even 3–4% of supply over weeks creates acute shortage risk — Germany and France most vulnerable

## 3. QATAR — DIRECT HIT

- QatarEnergy is the **world's largest LNG producer** (overtaken US in volume; some sources list it #2 behind US in 2025/26)
- Ras Laffan Industrial City: produces **~77 million tonnes/year** of LNG (North Field expansion in progress)
- **Production halted as of March 2, 2026** — physical damage to facilities
- All Qatar LNG must pass through Strait of Hormuz to reach Asian/European customers
- Double jeopardy: facilities struck AND strait blocked
- Qatar's North Field is the world's largest natural gas reservoir (shared with Iran's South Pars)
- Restart timeline unknown; Iran drone damage + insurance cliff = no near-term resumption signal

## SYNTHESIS / THREAT ASSESSMENT

**This is not a drill.** The combination of:
1. Physical strike on Qatar's LNG facilities (Ras Laffan)
2. Effective Hormuz closure (IRGC warnings + 70% traffic drop)
3. War risk insurance withdrawal (effective March 5)
4. Europe at 30% storage heading into supply shock

...creates the most severe LNG supply disruption scenario since 2022. Unlike Red Sea disruptions (which allowed rerouting), the Hormuz closure has **no viable alternative route** — Qatar's LNG has nowhere to go.

**Goldman Sachs 1-month scenario: TTF at €74/MWh** — that's 2x current and triggers 2022-style demand destruction in European industry.

**Key variable:** How long does the Hormuz disruption last? If resolved in days (ceasefire, IRGC stand-down), market pulls back. If it extends weeks, Europe faces acute gas emergency.
