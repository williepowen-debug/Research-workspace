# HAWK Phase 3 — Hormuz Traffic / Carve-Out Verification

**Date:** 2026-05-22 12:28 EDT  
**Scope:** Hormuz traffic regime only. Checks current transit category, China/India/Qatar/non-Qatar carve-outs, waypoint/toll regime, backlog/queue status, and whether old “zero crossings” language survives.

## Verdict

**Transit-regime category: SELECTIVE CARVE-OUTS, with controlled-reopening mechanics forming but not yet a controlled reopening.**

**Confidence: 0.84.**

The Strait is **not closed in a literal/zero-crossings sense**. That language should be killed or retained only as a stale May 9 unverified snapshot. Confirmed or near-confirmed data now show multiple permitted movements: Qatari LNG on May 10/12, Chinese VLCCs on May 20, a South Korean VLCC exiting May 20, Indian-flagged vessels moving under an Iran/India waypoint process, and a Chinese container ship among roughly 10 recent daily crossings.

But this is **not normalizing**. Pre-war traffic was about **125-140 daily passages**; current Reuters/Kpler/SynMax-style data show about **10 vessels/day** recently, crude tankers still a small share, hundreds of vessels/seafarers still stuck, and Iran/IRGC/SNSC/Persian Gulf Strait Authority controls vetting, waypoints, inspections, route instructions, and sometimes fees. Iran claims **26 vessels in 24h** after PGSA formation, but that is an Iranian claim and should not override independent tracking until confirmed.

Bottom line: HAWK’s C/Grind-Partial-Thaw thesis is strengthened. The correct phrase is **“Iran-controlled selective carve-outs / trickle flow,” not closure and not reopening.**

## Specific checks

### Chinese tanker egress

**Confirmed.** Yuan Gui Yang and Ocean Lily exited on May 20 with about **4 million barrels**; Reuters/LSEG/Kpler also counted a third supertanker flow set reaching **6 million barrels** including South Korea’s Universal Winner. Earlier Yuan Hua Hu reportedly exited the prior week with **2 million barrels** of Iraqi oil. This is not a single Chinese vessel; it is a China-aligned class of exits.

### India vessels cleared / stranded

**Confirmed as of Reuters investigation date, but timestamped to May 14.** India’s Shipping Ministry count: **13 Indian-flagged cleared / 13 stranded west of the waterway**. The process is embassy/IRGC/Iranian navy/Indian navy waypoint clearance, with captains instructed to obey Iranian commands and sometimes turn off transponders. This proves controlled carve-out, not open passage.

### Qatar / non-Qatar carve-outs

**Qatar: confirmed two-vessel LNG precedent.** Al Kharaitiyat and a second Qatari LNG tanker successfully crossed to Pakistan under Iran-approved route / Pakistan-mediated arrangement.

**Non-Qatar: confirmed.** China, South Korea, India, and Iraq/Vietnam/Greek-managed Agios Fanourios I examples show the carve-out is no longer just Qatar/China. However, breadth is still selective and politically mediated, not commercial normalization.

### Iran waypoint / checkpoint / toll regime

**Confirmed on mechanism; fee totals lower confidence.** Reuters reports IRGC vetting, affiliation documents, inspections, Iranian coordinates/instructions, island checkpoints including Abu Musa / Greater Tunb / Larak, and a newly created Persian Gulf Strait Authority to approve/tax transits. Fees are reported as sometimes **>$150,000** by European shipping sources and as high as **$2m** in Al Jazeera’s feature, but Reuters could not independently confirm total sums or transfer channels.

### Backlog / queue status

**Backlog still large.** U.S. military / Reuters-syndicated reports cite about **1,500 vessels / 22,500 sailors** trapped in early May; Reuters 5/20 tanker piece says **20,000 seafarers remain stranded** on hundreds of ships. Traffic is about **10/day** recently vs **125-140/day** pre-war. Even if Iran’s **26/24h** claim were true, it would still be far below normal and not enough to clear backlog quickly.

### “Zero crossings” language

**Supersede / kill as current-state language.** It may be retained only as “unverified May 9 claim later contradicted by Reuters/Kpler/SynMax and subsequent egress.” Evidence against durable zero: fewer than **60 ships** crossed Apr 18-May 6; Qatari LNG crossed May 10/12; Reuters/Kpler saw ~10/day recent traffic; Chinese/Korean VLCCs exited May 20; Chinese container ship crossed May 21.

## Current transit-regime classification and HAWK weights

**Classification:** Selective carve-outs.  
**Sub-label:** Iran-controlled trickle flow with PGSA/IRGC waypoint/toll mechanics; controlled reopening mechanics are emerging but volume and access remain too low for “Controlled reopening.”

**Scenario implication vs current HAWK C 55 / D 35 / B 10:**

- **C / Grind-Partial-Thaw:** should rise modestly to **57%**. The evidence strongly fits grind: non-zero movement, mediated egress, enforcement/fees/waypoints, no general opening.
- **D / Re-escalation:** hold **35%**. Traffic trickle lowers “closed/zero” escalation pressure, but Iran-control/fees and U.S. blockade friction keep re-ratchet risk alive.
- **B / Deal-Reopen:** trim to **8%**. Multiple carve-outs are constructive, but **~10/day vs 125-140/day**, stranded ships, fees, and deadlocked talks argue against broad reopen.

This is not a huge scenario state-change; it is a regime-precision update: C becomes better defined as **selective carve-outs**, not vague partial thaw.
