## [2026-06-12] Prediction text and promotion-threshold text must be written identically
**What happened:** HAW-10 was registered in PREDICTIONS.tsv as "Houthi commercial-vessel kinetic attack (mechanism) **on Bab al-Mandab** (threshold)" while the same session's STATUS emerging-vector row said "first Houthi **kinetic** commercial-vessel hit = promote to core + fires HAW-10" — no locus restriction. When the Houthis struck two vessels in the **Gulf of Aden** (6/8-9), the two texts gave different answers: vector promotion criterion satisfied, prediction locus unmet.
**Why it bites:** the wedge only surfaces when the event arrives, exactly when you're under pressure to resolve fast — inviting post-hoc rubric drift toward the convenient reading.
**Rule:** when a prediction and a vector threshold describe the same event, write one canonical sentence and reference it from both places. On conflict, the registered PREDICTIONS.tsv text is canonical (pre-registration discipline: fire only on the registered condition). Same-class second instance same session: GULFSTATE-01's Red line ("Gulf-state infra damage") vs its promotion criterion ("energy infra / US base hit") diverge — airport hit fired one, not the other.

## [2026-06-12] Live-war boot needs a day-by-day gap sweep, not topic searches
**What happened:** Jun 12 boot searched the current topic (deal talk) and current escalation, but the kinetic timeline effectively stopped at Jun 9-10 (BOARD dispatch date). Missed until director flagged: Iran's FORMAL Hormuz closure declaration Jun 11 (my own Tier-1 watch item #2 — it fired, and Brent fell anyway, the strongest decoupling datum yet) and the second US strike night (49 Tomahawks, Kharg threat).
**Why it bites:** topic-shaped searches retrieve the narrative peak, not the full sequence; in a fast theater, single days carry trigger-class events, and missing them silently corrupts the matrix and the scenario marks.
**Rule:** during an active conflict, boot must sweep day-by-day from last STATUS date through boot date ("Iran war June 10", "June 11", "June 12"-style queries or equivalent chronology source), and explicitly check each open Tier-1 watch item against the window before writing any re-mark.

## [2026-06-20] A deprioritized secondary vector can go stale for MONTHS — re-sweep dormant vectors, don't carry them forward
**What happened:** HAW-03 ("No US military intervention in Venezuela in 2026," 95%) and VX-HAWK-VEN-01 ("rhetoric only, no military action") were carried OPEN/unchanged from Feb–Apr through Jun 20 — but the US had actually run Operation Absolute Resolve on **Jan 3 2026**, capturing Maduro, with a naval blockade since Dec 17. The boot theater-sweep surfaced it (A1, multi-primary); the vector had been wrong for ~5.5 months because the Iran war "absorbed bandwidth" and the dormant vector was never re-verified.
**Why it bites:** a stale 95%-confidence prediction sitting OPEN reads as live calibration when it's actually a buried miss; and a "rhetoric only" vector understates a realized intervention with live oil-supply implications (US now controls Venezuelan export flows). Carry-forward without re-verification silently rots — and the staleness is invisible precisely because the vector is "quiet."
**Rule:** at boot, dormant/secondary vectors (Venezuela, Taiwan, trade war) get a periodic FULL re-sweep against external primaries — not a carry-forward of the last state. Treat "VX last-updated >30 days ago AND has an OPEN prediction" as a re-sweep trigger, not a skip. Extends the boot predictions-scan ([[finding_boot_predictions_scan]]) from predictions to the vectors behind them.

## [2026-07-12] A prediction's literal wording narrows your evidence sweep — search the MECHANISM, not just the named targets
**What happened:** re-checking HAW-15 ("Ukraine does NOT strike Russian crude-export INFRASTRUCTURE — Primorsk/Ust-Luga/Novorossiysk/Druzhba"), I searched exactly those named terminals, got April-vintage hits, and wrote "no in-window crude-export strike." I entirely missed a week-long, industrial-scale Ukrainian campaign against Russian shadow-fleet **tankers** (Sea of Azov + Black Sea, 7/6-12, 21-42 vessels claimed) — a DIFFERENT channel for the same underlying mechanism (disrupt Russian crude to market). Will surfaced it. Shadow fleet is explicitly in my domain scope.
**Why it bites:** the row's named targets (fixed terminals) became the search scope, so a real, decision-relevant escalation arriving via an UN-named channel (the vessels) was invisible. Same wording-wedge family as HAW-10 (locus) and HAW-14 (catalyst) — but here it corrupted *evidence-gathering*, not just resolution. A "clean CONFIRMED" on the letter would have read as a good call while the mechanism fired next door.
**Rule:** when re-checking a prediction at boot, sweep the underlying **mechanism** broadly (here: "Ukraine attacking Russian oil" — refineries AND terminals AND tankers AND pipeline), not just the prediction's named literal targets. Then map what you find back to letter-vs-spirit. Corollary 1: write prediction labels that name the mechanism, not one channel of it (`[[feedback_forward_discovery_prediction_spirit]]`, `[[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]`).
**Corollary 2 (added 7/12 same day — Will pushed a SECOND time and I was STILL wrong):** my "tighter confirmation" *also* concluded "terminals unstruck in-window" — wrong again. Two compounding causes: (a) a **single-search-per-terminal-name sweep returned only the 'famous' Mar/Apr headlines**, masking the June/July re-strikes (Primorsk ~6/25, NOVATEK-Ust-Luga 7/10, Vysotsk 7/6, Kavkaz 6/20); (b) my **own "canonical" ledger was stale (last updated 6/20) AND had silently missed the 5/23 + 6/8 Novorossiysk crude-terminal re-strikes** — so the internal baseline I leaned on *under-stated the very channel the prediction tracked*. **Rule:** don't trust your own ledger as the baseline without checking its Last-updated + running a completeness pass; and when a first sweep says "nothing," escalate to a date-careful multi-angle sweep (per-facility June/July queries + a neutral dated timeline like Wikipedia + operational-impact queries) BEFORE banking a "no." A first-pass "no results" is a search-coverage statement, not a fact. HAW-15 resolved FAILED on the corrected evidence (KB-222/223).

## [2026-07-25] The unprocessed inbound lane contained the correction to my own canonical thesis
**What happened:** I went dark 13 days (7/12→7/25). In that window my STATUS **and** my NEXUS_BRIEF carried *"both theaters kinetic-high, Brent decoupled ~$76-79"* — the decoupling thesis being HAWK's own canonical property (FLOW-HAWK-19). Brent broke ~$85 on 7/16, printed its first-ever >$100 settle 7/23, and settled $96.78 on 7/24. On catch-up I found **17 unprocessed WALTER-lane signals**, and the *oldest* one — **SIG-W-20260716-002, dispatched 7/16** — said in its headline: *"oil decoupling BROKE on RISK-PREMIUM, not one lost barrel."* It even carried the exact premium-vs-supply-loss discriminator I spent this session re-deriving from scratch.
**Why it bites:** I had been treating the WALTER lane as **backlog hygiene** — a tidiness chore deferrable under my own CLAUDE.md rule ("do NOT process inbox on normal spawns unless boot step 7 finds pending signals"). It is not hygiene. For an agent whose value is a *thesis*, the inbound lanes are the **falsification channel** — the cheapest place a counterparty tells you your central claim just died. Deferring it is not deferring tidying; it is declining the correction. And because NEXUS boot-reads `NEXUS_BRIEF.md`, the wrong claim was not merely sitting inert, it was **propagating to a downstream consumer for nine days**. This is the HAW-03/Venezuela rot pattern (item 3) applied to the **primary** lane rather than a dormant one — strictly worse, because nobody expects the live surface to be the stale one.
**Rule:** **if a signal lane can carry a falsifier for a thesis you own, it is boot-mandatory, not spawn-optional.** Concretely: at boot, scan inbound *headlines* for anything naming your own canonical thesis or its key metric even when you are not spawned for inbox processing — that scan is seconds, and full processing can still be deferred. Corollary: **a dark window is not neutral for a thesis-owning surface.** Time does not preserve a claim; it only preserves the text. Any gap >1 week should trigger an explicit "is my canonical claim still true?" check against external primaries *before* the surface is cited or consumed. ## [2026-07-25] "Derived" is not "self-updating" — a regenerated surface rots at the cadence of its regeneration
**What happened:** `domain/energy-strikes/CROSS_WAR_SUMMARY.md` is explicitly designed as thin-and-derived, regenerated from the siblings' ledgers at closeout and never independently maintained. Its 7/12 version reported FALCON at **4 rows, swept-through 2026-03-19, "backfill not yet run."** That was accurate at the moment of spinout and **false within hours** — FALCON executed its founding-mandate backfill the same afternoon (5→27 rows, now 29). The table then carried that stale asymmetry for 13 days, and I would have kept quoting "FALCON is thin/unswept" if I hadn't regenerated it this session.
**Why it bites:** the "derived, don't hand-edit" convention is *correct* and it creates a false sense of safety — it reads as though the file tracks its sources automatically. It doesn't; it tracks them **as of the last regeneration**, and the staleness is invisible precisely because the file looks structural rather than observational. Worse, the stale content here was a **judgment about a sibling's maturity** ("thin/unbuilt"), which is the kind of claim that quietly biases how you weight their inputs.
**Rule (extended 2026-07-28, second instance):** every derived surface carries its **regeneration date in the same visual weight as its content**, and any claim it makes *about another agent's state* is re-verified at regeneration rather than carried. ⚠️ **It happened again on the very next pass, with the sign flipped:** the 7/25 regeneration reported FALCON at 29 rows / swept-through 7/12 and flagged *"worth a nudge if it hasn't advanced"* — FALCON had already advanced it to 7/27, so the table understated the same sibling twice running, and by 7/28 **OSPREY was the stale side** (swept 7/23, with Tyumen 7/25 and Golden Leo 7/26 un-rowed). **Corollary: never write a staleness judgement about a sibling into a derived file as a standing claim — write it as a dated observation with an explicit re-check, because the sibling is fixing it while you type.** When regenerating, **read the source artifact's own header/analysis, not just its row count** — FALCON's `ANALYSIS_2026-07-12.md` header stated the backfill was executed and re-worded the production-sparing claim; both were sitting there for 13 days. That same read is what surfaced the Feb–Apr production-class hits that forced this session's HAW-18 correction (60%→55%) — i.e. the sibling's artifact contained a partial falsifier for my own new prediction. `[[finding_read_the_artifacts_own_header_first]]`.

## [2026-07-28] I wrote one theater's prediction legs OPERATIONALLY and the other's as EXEMPLARS — and the asymmetry is invisible until the wrong theater moves
**What happened:** `HAW-18` (registered 7/25) claims *neither theater produces a production-class physical supply loss.* Its FALCON legs are written as an **operational test** — "(a) a production/export-terminal hit **with confirmed output offline OR force majeure declared**" — while its OSPREY legs are written as **named exemplars**: "(c) confirmed CPC SPM structural damage, (d) a Tengiz FM, (e) a named Russian crude-export berth destroyed." Leg (b), "a vessel confirmed SUNK by hostile action," is bracketed `[FALCON theater]`. Two events in three days walked straight into the gap. **Golden Leo** was confirmed sunk by hostile action on 7/26 — in the **Black Sea**, where my row offers no vessel leg at all. And the **CPC halt took ~440 kbpd of Kazakh output offline for a week** (2.07 → 1.63 M bpd, −21%; Tengiz −56%) — a production-class *quantity* my OSPREY legs cannot see, because I gave that theater only damage/FM proxies and never a direct output test.
**Why it bites:** the row read "no fire" on both, and **both no-fires were correct** — Golden Leo was a *grain* ship (zero hydrocarbon cargo), and the CPC barrels came back on a decision with zero capacity destroyed, which *confirms* the migration thesis rather than falsifying it. **That is exactly what makes it dangerous: the spec produced right answers for partly wrong reasons, so nothing forced me to notice.** What saved leg (b) was the cargo class, not the theater bracket — had a **crude tanker** been sunk in the Black Sea, my registered text would have said "no fire" while the thesis it exists to guard was being falsified. A defect that only shows up as a *silent* false negative never announces itself; it waits for the one case where the two grounds come apart. Fifth in the wording-wedge family: HAW-10 (locus) → HAW-14 (catalyst) → HAW-15 (mechanism) → FAL-01 (actor) → **HAW-18 (theater-scope)**.
**Rule:** **write every leg of a multi-theater prediction to the same standard — operational thresholds, not named exemplars — and state explicitly for each leg whether it is theater-scoped or cross-theater.** If a leg is genuinely cross-theater (a vessel sinking, a barrel count), do not let a `[theater]` bracket silently narrow it. **Corollary, and it is the non-obvious half:** the fix is *not* simply "add a direct output-offline test to the quiet theater." Had I done that, HAW-18 would have fired FAILED on the CPC episode — i.e. marked the thesis dead on evidence that confirms it. The correct successor leg is **output offline CONDITIONED ON DESTROYED CAPACITY (irreversibility)**, because `KB-HAWK-236` establishes that magnitude no longer discriminates a premium event from a physical one — only reversibility does. **Do not amend HAW-18** (evidence is already in-window; pre-registration governs); register the fix in the successor row at resolution, as FALCON did with FAL-01→FAL-03. Related: `[[finding_theater_check_before_gate_check]]`, `[[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]]`, `[[finding_threshold_spec_fails_before_world]]`.

## [2026-07-28] A promotion gate is only meaningful against the scale it is scored on — I attached a military test to a vector whose bands measure LNG inventory
**What happened:** On 7/25 I recommended holding `VX-HAWK-TWN-01` dormant and wrote a promotion gate into the row: *"promotion requires EITHER corroboration of the helicopter median-line crossing OR any median-line action touching Strait shipping."* Will ratified it. On 7/28 a news sweep **corroborated the helicopter** — three outlets where I'd had one — so the gate was met, and I took it to Will as a promote/don't decision. He asked the four-word question that broke it: **"what are we promoting?"** Pulling the row: `TWN-01` is named **"Taiwan LNG / TSMC Risk"** and **all four of its bands are Qatar-LNG / Taipower-reserve conditions** — Green *"reserves >14 days"*, Yellow *"reserves >12 days"*, Orange *"reserves <10 days, Taipower amber alert"*, Red *"Qatar >90% crash verified."* **There is no military content in any band.** Months of median-line, drill and sortie evidence had accreted in `Current_Value`, scored against nothing at all. And the row's 🔴 RED — which I had been reading and reporting as a Taiwan risk mark — had been **earned by the Qatar LNG crash**. The military content was riding a red flag it never earned; when I split the row and scored the military evidence against military bands for the first time, the honest mark was **YELLOW**.
**Why it bites:** a promotion gate **reads as belonging wherever it is written.** Nothing about the sentence looks wrong in isolation — it is a well-formed falsifiable test with a date and a source bar — and the row it sits in has a plausible name and a live status. So it survives review: **I wrote it, and Will ratified it, and neither of us caught it, three days apart.** It only fails at the moment the evidence arrives, which is exactly when you are least inclined to re-read the schema and most inclined to act on a met condition. Worse, the fusion was **inflating a mark**: a consumer reading "Taiwan 🔴 RED" alongside a paragraph of PLA sortie counts would reasonably infer the military situation was at red, and the file never said otherwise. This is the same wedge as LESSONS item 1 (HAW-10, prediction text vs promotion-threshold text) — but one layer up, between a **gate and the bands it is scored against** rather than between two prose descriptions. **Fifth instance of the family in a single day** (four in `HAW-18` that morning: theater-bracketed leg, exemplar-vs-operational legs, two-theater scope, oil-only molecule).
**Rule:** **when you add or amend a gate on an existing row, re-read that row's BANDS in the same edit, and state which band the gate resolves into.** If the gate cannot be written as "this moves the row from band X to band Y," it does not belong on that row — it belongs on a row that measures the thing the gate tests. **Corollary — the diagnostic that would have caught it in seconds: ask "what, concretely, changes if this fires?"** A gate whose answer is a vague "we promote it" rather than a named band transition is mis-attached. **Second corollary:** when a row's status is red, check *which band* fired before quoting the colour next to unrelated evidence — a fused row launders one leg's severity onto another's. Resolved by Will-approved split (option (a)) into `VX-HAWK-TWNMIL-01` (military/chokepoint, Red = any PLA action touching Strait shipping) and `TWN-01` (LNG/TSMC chain). `[[finding_threshold_spec_fails_before_world]]`, `[[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]]`.

## [2026-08-20] I fixed HAW-18's *wording* asymmetry and shipped the *unit-of-analysis* one four times in a single row
**What happened:** `HAW-19` was written as HAW-18's successor and explicitly built from its post-mortem — theater-agnostic, molecule-explicit, operational legs, irreversibility replacing magnitude. **It took four structural repairs on its first day, and three of them are the same defect wearing different clothes: NUMERATOR AND DENOMINATOR MEASURED AT DIFFERENT UNITS.** (1) The vessel leg compared a **cargo (stock)** to **productive capacity (flow)** — deleted at registration on my own argument. (2) LEG B paired a **blockade-wide** disabled/boarded counter with a **route-specific** alternate-route denominator (WALTER). (3) LEG A demanded damage at **component** level and magnitude at **terminal** level, so an irreversibly destroyed CPC mooring produced *zero* qualifying flow loss because the terminal kept loading through its other two (OSPREY). (4) Separately, the γ limb treated **absence as evidence of offline** using AIS-derived instruments — the one direction that class may not be used in (FALCON + OSPREY, with a known-positive control: a verified 2M-bbl Kharg loading printing as zero).
**Why it bites:** I used the stock-vs-flow argument as my *headline* that morning — it is what justified deleting the vessel leg over both siblings' signed concurrence — and then committed its perimeter twin in the adjacent leg **of the same row, in the same hour**. Invoking a principle creates the felt sense of having applied it. Worse, HAW-18's post-mortem taught me to look for *theater* asymmetry, so I removed the brackets, saw the legs read identically in both theaters, and stopped — but the legs only read the same; they **behave** differently, because the two theaters emit different evidence classes (the Gulf produces long halts, force majeure and restoration timelines; the Black Sea produces 2-7 day reversible halts and publishes nothing). **Theater-agnostic in TEXT, theater-asymmetric in REACHABILITY.** And the reachability claim I wrote to defend the row was itself false and sat published for ~2 hours.
**Rule:** **for every leg, name the unit of analysis on BOTH sides of the comparison before writing the threshold** — stock vs flow, component vs terminal, route-scoped vs theater-wide, instrument-perimeter vs claim-perimeter. If the two sides are not the same object, the leg is broken no matter how well-sourced the number is. **Corollary, and it is the non-obvious half: having just applied a principle is a RISK FACTOR for violating it nearby, not a protection.** Audit the adjacent leg *because* you fixed this one. **Third corollary — DECLINING TO ACT IS AN UNDERRATED DETECTOR, and it is the only part of this session's process that actually caught instance 4** (OSPREY's refinement, 2026-08-20, verified against my own KB text rather than accepted): the fourth instance — that the redundancy-exhaustion clause I *adopted* was itself an enumeration — surfaced **at the moment I articulated why I was DECLINING to patch gap five.** To justify leaving it open I had to state what the current fix **does not cover** (tankage vs moorings), and that sentence *is* the catch. ⚠️ **A patch that gets ADOPTED is never asked what it fails to cover; a patch you REFUSE TO EXTEND must be.** So the refusal did the work no check did — I had wrongly concluded it was pure luck and that no version of the process could have caught it. **Practice, and it costs one sentence: whenever you decline to patch, write down what the fix you already have does not reach.** That articulation is the detector. *(This does not displace the two-lanes conclusion — two desks looking at one object from different lanes is what put the observation in front of both of us — but the mechanism that converted it into a finding was the act of stating a boundary.)* **Second corollary:** an evidence class that is published in one theater and not the other makes an identically-worded leg unequally reachable — check what each theater actually EMITS, not just what the leg SAYS. `[[finding_cross_entity_comparison_needs_same_perimeter]]`, `[[finding_ais_port_export_darkfleet_blind]]`.

## [2026-08-20] A ruling does not travel to the instruments — three instances, three desks, one day
**What happened:** I ruled the Hormuz transit-count family **inadmissible as a falsifier basis** (`KB-HAWK-270`) in the morning, recorded it in the KB and on STATUS, and built `HAW-19` LEG B on a different instrument because of it. Four hours later, prompted by FALCON, I found **`SOURCES.md` — a live file — still instructing future sessions that "HAWK's use of the flow trackers below is narrow: chokepoint military-transit monitoring (Hormuz vessel counts, AIS dark-transit)"**, with TankerTrackers scoped to *"chokepoint transit counts only."* The ruling had never reached the table a session actually reads **at the point of use**. Two sibling instances the same day: OSPREY's published CPC ladder text carried an over-broad threshold (cargo qualifier missing) that **propagated onto three of my surfaces because I inherited it from OSPREY and neither end re-derived it**; and FALCON found a **retired** gate still issuing imperative instructions inside its own boot file, on an instrument it had just proven could not veto.
**Why it bites:** all three defects are invisible to the desk that made the ruling, because **the ruling feels like the work.** Recording a decision in a KB row, a STATUS line or a registry produces the complete sensation of having decided — while the imperative sentence that will actually be executed sits untouched in a sources table, a boot sequence, or a consumer's inherited copy. The propagation form is the worst of the three: a defect in a *published* instrument becomes the consumer's defect at the speed of the next packet, and **the consumer has no reason to re-derive what it was handed by the owner.**
**Rule:** **when you rule an instrument out, grep for its IMPERATIVE wording and fix it at the point of use in the same commit** — sources tables, boot sequences, script docstrings, consumer packets — not just at the point of decision. A ruling that lives only where decisions are recorded governs nobody's next action. **Corollary for publishers:** when you correct a threshold you have published, assume a consumer has copied it verbatim and route the correction; **corollary for consumers:** a threshold inherited from another desk is UNAUDITED until you have re-derived it, however authoritative the source.
