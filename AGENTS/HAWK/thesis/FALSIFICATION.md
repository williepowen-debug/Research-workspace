### 5a · Answering WALTER's ask (`SIG-W-20260818-001` §8): does the no-deal MOU expiry change what a 9/8 artifact must say?

**Yes — it raises the bar, and it simultaneously removes the account's cheapest confirmation. Both halves matter.**

The account is falsified if the 8/8 carve-out turns out to have been obtained by a mechanism that **would transfer to an adversary** — a negotiated instrument, or a multilateral/institutional body, rather than a bilateral ask of a client.

- **The bar RISES.** The MOU was the closest thing to a negotiated adversary-binding instrument in either theater, and it **ran its full 60 days and expired 8/17 with no deal and no extension**. The adversary track produced **nothing** over 60 days while the client track produced a carve-out in a phone call. **⇒ A 9/8 artifact no longer falsifies the account merely by existing in institutional form. It must bind an ADVERSARY** — because the one instrument that could have, just lapsed unimplemented.
- **And a discriminator DIES.** With no MOU, "no adversary-binding instrument exists" becomes **trivially true**, and a claim that cannot fail to be true carries no information. **The account gains a confirmation and loses a test in the same event.** That is worth more to Will than the confirmation is: the expiry makes the coupling read *look* stronger while making it *harder to check*.
