## Executive Summary

Taiwan is not currently rationing power and officials are projecting calm. However, the structural exposure is real: ~30% of LNG supply comes from Qatar (now offline), ~10 days of reserves under normal consumption, and 20-of-22 needed LNG cargoes for March–April secured — leaving 2 still under negotiation as of March 10. The critical date is **March 15**, after which shipments that already cleared Hormuz will have been consumed and the supply gap from Qatar/Gulf becomes live. No fab curtailments confirmed yet. Government messaging is reassuring but notably specific about the timeline problem.

---

## 1. LNG Reserve Levels

**Confirmed:** Taiwan holds approximately **10–11 days of LNG reserves** under normal consumption levels.

> "Taiwan's limited gas reserves — estimated at roughly 10 to 11 days under normal consumption levels — could quickly come under strain."  
> — BusinessToday.in, citing analyst commentary, March 8, 2026

**Normal buffer:** The 10–11 day figure is the standard operating level, not an emergency low — Taiwan structurally runs thin. Japan holds ~2–4 weeks; Korea ~2–4 weeks (Kpler data via CNBC, March 3, 2026). Taiwan is the most exposed of the three.

## 3. Qatar/Hormuz Exposure

**Confirmed figures:**
- **~30% of Taiwan's LNG from Qatar** (CPC Corp disclosure, March 2, 2026; confirmed March 10 by Minister Kung)
- **~10% from the US**, ~30% from Australia (CPC Corp)
- Qatar Energy halted LNG production after **Iranian drone attacks on Ras Laffan and Mesaieed Industrial City** (CNBC, March 3, 2026)
- ~20% of global LNG exports transit Hormuz, primarily from Qatar (Kpler via CNBC)

**Assessment:** Qatar is both the supply source AND the Hormuz-transit dependency. The double hit — production halt at Ras Laffan + closed strait — means Taiwan's ~30% Qatar exposure is effectively fully disrupted, not partially. The 60–70% from non-Gulf sources (Australia, US, others) is the buffer. The question is delivery timing vs. reserve drawdown rate.

**Analyst note (X / Sonia Gallego, geopolitical analyst):**
> "Qatar supplies ~33% of Taiwan's LNG - but this post suggests Taiwan has only 11 days of gas reserves. TSMC already consumes ~9% of Taiwan's entire electricity supply. This is a live-fire demo of Taiwan's real strategic weak point and the assumption is that Beijing is watching."
> — @SoniaRGallego (verified analyst), date unclear but recent

- BusinessToday.in (March 8, 2026): "Power shortages could affect semiconductor production if LNG shipments face disruption" — framed as risk, not confirmed event
- No reports found of TSMC, UMC, or other fabs receiving curtailment notices or altering production plans

**Assessment:** TSMC curtailment risk is **speculative but structurally credible**. No current confirmed signal. Watch for any reports from TSMC investor relations or Taiwan MOEA power reserve margin data.

## 5. Emergency Protocols / Alternative Supply Actions

**Confirmed active measures:**
1. **Spot market procurement underway** — Minister confirmed on March 10 that alternative LNG being sought through spot market (at higher prices). Deliveries brought forward from existing contracts where possible.
2. **Australia and US** explicitly named as alternative sources in case of prolonged Hormuz closure (Minister Kung, March 4, 2026).
3. **Non-gas backup generators on standby** — available for immediate activation (confirmed March 4).
4. **Coal-fired backup contingency** discussed but described as unnecessary as of March 10 given current LNG supply status.

**Not yet activated:**
- No formal emergency energy protocol declared
- No industrial rationing orders issued
- No reports of Taipower demand response programs for large industrial customers
