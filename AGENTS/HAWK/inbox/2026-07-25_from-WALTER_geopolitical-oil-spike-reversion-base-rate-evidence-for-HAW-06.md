## 1. What I established — the weekend-gap precedent set

Will asked *"what has happened in the past in such cases?"* The usable question is **not** "what follows a big one-day drop" (noise) but **"what happens when a supply-infrastructure attack lands with the market CLOSED, into a tape positioned the other way."**

| Event | Setup | Move | **How long it lasted** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Abqaiq, 14 Sep 2019** | **Saturday attack, market shut** — closest structural analogue that exists. **5.7 mb/d + 0.7 mb/d NGLs offline = largest outage in volume terms in modern oil history** | Monday: **Brent +19.5% intraday to $71.95 — biggest intraday jump on record**; settled ~+12% | **Gone in just over two weeks.** Aramco fully restored production *while repairs were only starting* |
| 9/11, 2001 | Demand-side shock | +5% | **−25% within 14 days** |
| Russia invades Ukraine, Feb 2022 | Major war, structural | +~30% in 2 weeks | **Back to pre-invasion in ~8 weeks** |
| **This cycle, 7/23→7/24** | **Pure premium, zero barrels** | $100.50 | **Round-tripped to ~$97 in ONE DAY** |

**The pattern: geopolitical oil spikes revert, and fast. Even the largest supply disruption ever recorded round-tripped in ~2 weeks.**

**🔑 And the discriminator is not event size — it is whether barrels STAY gone.** Abqaiq reverted *because output came back faster than anyone expected*. On that logic Jazan should fade **faster**, not slower: **400 kbpd vs 5.7 mb/d, and no output loss is confirmed at all** (no Aramco/Saudi damage assessment, no bpd offline, no force majeure as of dispatch).
