### [Process] — A Session That REVERSES a Regime Must Grep Its Own File for the OLD Regime Before Commit
**Pattern (2026-08-27, Will-directed audit — 21 defects, 7+ self-inflicted the same session):** I flipped the gamma call from negative to positive, refreshed the top of STATUS, and left **every downstream surface asserting the state I had just reversed** — the Verdict block, the ACTIVE THRESHOLDS SPX row, Axis 3's credit marks, the CREDIT paragraph (which then carried gap **875 AND 869** in one breath), the cross-cutting driver's DFII10, the leg-1 row, and the SKEW row. The peer-facing `NEXUS_BRIEF` was worse: a bullet labelled **"GAMMA — CURRENT"** carrying a 27-day-old NEGATIVE read **and publishing wall levels my own rule had withheld five times running.**
**Rule:** when a session reverses a regime, do not "update STATUS." **Grep the file — and the brief — for the superseded state BY NAME** (`NEGATIVE GAMMA`, the old gap number, the old level, the old adjective) and confirm every hit sits inside a strike record rather than a live assertion. **The detector is a grep; the gap is never detection, it is invocation.** Proven the same session: running it immediately surfaced two more defects the manual read had missed — a live `USD/JPY 158.33 [8/6]` and the stale brief.
**Corollary — a LEVEL update is a regime check too (2026-09-24, n=2 in one file).** The 2Y cell's level moved 4.56→4.67 and its state kept saying *"4bp under red"* for nine sessions over a >4.60 red line; the 10Y cell carried *"first close >5.0% on 9/18"* when 9/16 was already 5.01, and the claim went out in a packet. **Whenever you touch a level cell, re-derive the state word against the thresholds in the same row, and run a series query before any "first".**
**Corollary — supersession markers do not travel.** Labelling an old mark "superseded" in a parenthetical does nothing about the sentences beside it that restate the same numbers. Delete or strike the restatements, not just the header.

### [Verification] — Never Publish a Superlative About Your Own Instrument Without Querying the Full Series First
**Pattern (2026-08-27):** four false superlatives in one session, all about instruments I own — *"HY 7bp away, the closest it has ever been"* (**263bp [2026-06-17] was 4bp nearer**; the `<260` leg had been on the file since 2026-03-16); *"both legs nearer than at any point in the thesis's life"* (**refuted by my own table two lines below it** — 8/14 closed VIX 14.25 with the same HY 267); *"the >23 trigger is the furthest of the cycle"* (**8/14 was 8.75 away vs 8.36**); *"CCC peak 1,034 [7/31]"* (**1,039 [8/25]**). On 8/23 I had adopted WALTER's rule against exactly this after the third false-referent superlative in 24 hours.
**Rule:** "closest/furthest/highest/peak ever" is a claim about a **whole series**, not about the two prints you happen to be holding. **One `sort -n | head` on the series before the word ships.** ⚠️ **AND NAME THE WINDOW, YEAR-QUALIFIED — the correction pass failed this twice.** My fix pulled `cosd=2026-01-01` and presented a **2026-only** list as the series history, then wrote dates as `[1/22]` which collided with a peer's canonical **2025-01-22**. Two windows answer different questions and must be stated separately: **thesis's life** (since the leg was registered — min 263 [2026-06-17]) vs **full series** (n=787 — one sub-260 print, 259.0 [2025-01-22], i.e. the line HAS been broken once). **A peer found this, not me** — `finding_a_correction_pass_is_unreviewed_work`, and it is why a fix pass gets swept like any other work. A superlative costs one query to verify and is the single most quotable thing you write.
**Sharpest instance to remember:** the false claim sat **two lines above the table that refuted it**, inside a block whose stated purpose was honesty about risk cutting against me — and its direction of error was **alarmist, in my own risk disclosure, already escalated to Will and PROME.** Adjacency is not verification, and self-critical framing is not accuracy.

### [Registration] — Name the Instrument for the Mechanism in Your Hypothesis's OWN NAME, Before You Register It
**Pattern (2026-08-28, HEN-42 grade):** I registered a discriminator literally called **"policy-path vs TERM-PREMIUM"** and ran it for **five weeks with an instrument for only one half.** I measured the policy-path side every session (2s10s, front-end response to catalysts) and had **no instrument at all** for the term-premium side — I inferred it from curve shape. **FRED `THREEFYTP10` (Kim-Wright 10Y term premium) is free, daily, and was publishing the whole time.** On it, term premium rose **+7.01bp inside HEN-42's own 7/17→7/23 registration window = 44% of the 10Y's +16bp** — so the policy-path attribution was **overstated at registration**, measurably, on a series I could have pulled on day one. **BOND found it (8/18 sweep), not me**, and I only consumed it because PROME registered it as a DOCKET row after my desk went dark.
**Rule:** before registering a discriminator, **write down the instrument for each named alternative.** If the hypothesis's name contains a noun you cannot measure, you have a narrative, not a discriminator. Curve *shape* is a proxy for term premium; it is not a measurement of it, and the difference is exactly where the grade lives. `[[finding_hypothesis_needs_an_instrument_for_its_defining_mechanism]]`
**Corollary — grade the parenthetical too.** HEN-42's DENY clause read *"…on a term-premium impulse **(auction tail, fiscal, foreign demand)**."* At the grade, **none of the three named forms fired** — the impulse that did was duration/supply-channel, a fourth form I never listed. An illustrative parenthetical becomes a **binding clause** the moment someone grades the letter. Either enumerate exhaustively or write "e.g." and mean it.

### [Registration] — A Falsifier Keyed on a SPOT Instrument Cannot Grade a Claim About a QUARTER AVERAGE
**Pattern (2026-09-14, HEN-46):** the row CLAIMS that AAL and LUV miss their **Q3 fuel cost line** — a claim about a **quarter-average realised price**. Its registered falsifier `F1` keys on the **ULSD crack**, a **spot margin**. I registered it on 9/11 with the crack one session off an episode high and felt the evidence was overwhelming. It was not. Same series (`HO=F` close, $/gal), pure window delta: the carriers' assumption window (7/17–7/23) averaged **$4.1601**; Q3-to-date **as of that very 9/11 close** averaged **$4.1598 — −0.0%.** July and August ran at or below the assumption window and the squeeze is **9 sessions of a 63-session quarter**. Ceiling case with the remaining ~12 sessions held at the 9/11 *peak* close: **+3.8%**, against a `0.60` built on a **$0.25–0.45/gal** gap. **The magnitude leg was already dead on the day I registered it, and the instrument I chose could not say so.**
**Rule:** before registering, **write the claim's TIME BASIS next to the instrument's TIME BASIS and check they match.** Spot ≠ quarter average; a margin ≠ a cost line. Two extra failure modes travel with this: the instrument measured the **spread** (crack) while the P&L pays the **outright** — a crack can widen on falling crude while the buyer's bill is unchanged; and a spike late in an averaging window is **arithmetically incapable** of moving the average much, so "the instrument is at a record" and "the claim is in trouble" can both be true and unrelated. ⛔ **The tell is a falsifier that can fire on a day the thesis did not change, and fail to fire on a quarter in which it died.** `[[finding_instrument_measures_a_superset_of_the_thesis_subject]]`

### [Registration] — My Letters Keep Leaving a Region Unnamed. Enumerate the OUTCOME SPACE, Not Just the Interesting Outcomes
**Pattern (2026-08-28 — THREE instances in one day, all my own authorship, which makes it a pattern and not three accidents):**
1. **HEN-41** — CONFIRM was a disjunction whose second limb (`T10YIE >2.30`) was **already satisfied at registration**. A limb that is true when you write it never tests the world.
2. **HEN-42** — DENY's clause named its mechanism in a parenthetical *(auction tail, fiscal, foreign demand)*. At the grade **none of the three fired**; the impulse that did was duration/supply-channel, **a fourth form I never listed**. An "illustrative" parenthetical becomes **binding** the moment someone grades the letter.
3. **T3** — bands read *"<0.15 ⇒ shared factor is the dollar; ≥0.45 ⇒ ~1.5 near the ceiling."* **0.15 ≤ r < 0.45 names no verdict at all**, and the first observed value (**+0.399**) landed exactly there.
**Rule:** before registering, **write down the full outcome space and check every region has a named disposition** — including *"no verdict"*, which is a legitimate and often correct one. Three questions, all cheap: **(i)** is any limb already satisfied as I write it? **(ii)** is any list I wrote as examples going to be read as exhaustive by whoever grades it? **(iii)** what does the letter say for a value BETWEEN my bands?
**Corollary — an UNNAMED region is worse than a wide one.** A wide "no verdict" band is honest and forces the sample-size conversation. An unnamed gap looks like a complete spec and **lets the reader supply their own reading**, usually the one that flatters the author. All three defects above passed every audit I ran, because a row-counting check sees a populated cell and stops.
**And the sharpest tell:** in all three, the region I failed to name is **where the actual observation landed**. ⚠️ **n=4 by end of day — T3 also never named its ESTIMAND:** *"20-sess ΔHY"* reads either as daily differences over a 20-session window **or** as the 20-session change sampled daily (overlapping by 19/20). The two readings differ by **~20× in required sample** — 38 vs ~760 sessions for the same band — so the ambiguity silently decides whether the test is runnable at all. **Ambiguity is the same defect class as omission: both let the reader supply the spec.** That is not bad luck — the interesting middle is where reality usually sits, and it is exactly the region an author skips because neither headline claim lives there.

### [Verification] — AN ERROR THAT MAKES YOUR OWN FINDING MORE DRAMATIC HAS A CONSTITUENCY OF ONE
**Pattern (2026-09-14, the `F1` roll finding):** I published *"one roll step = **94%** of `F1`'s threshold separation."* Five pulls the same afternoon spanned **92.6–94.4%** and **my published figure sat at the TOP of the range.** The qualitative claim — *the step is comparable to the WHOLE separation* — was robust on every pull; the two-significant-figure number was not. **And the error ran in the direction that made my own finding more striking.** It had already propagated to another desk's card and to the coordinator's record before I re-checked it.
**Rule:** ⛔ **A figure that flatters your own finding has NO NATURAL AUDITOR.** A number that overstates someone else's position gets contested by them; a number that overstates YOUR OWN gets adopted, because nobody downstream is motivated to shave it and everybody upstream already agrees. ⇒ **Audit hardest the figures that make your case strongest, and re-check the claims you have already WON the argument on** — winning is exactly when checking stops. ⚠️ **Corollary on precision:** before quoting any figure to two significant figures, pull it twice. If the digits move, the qualitative claim is the finding and the number is decoration — say *"comparable to"*, not *"94%"*. `[[finding_a_charitable_reading_of_your_work_is_the_one_to_check]]`
**Corollary — the same blind spot wearing different clothes (TERRY, 2026-09-14): a STRENGTH CLAIM YOU MAKE ABOUT SOMEONE ELSE'S WORK IS STILL YOURS TO CORRECT, and almost nobody audits it because IT READS AS GENEROSITY RATHER THAN ASSERTION.** I called a peer's verification *"the strongest confirmation of anything today"*; it was independent in **method** but not in **sample** (same window, same rally) — **stronger than an echo, weaker than replication.** The peer corrected me and would otherwise have let it stand, *because praise is not contested.* ⇒ **Grade the evidentiary weight you assign to others' findings on the same scale you grade your own figures.**

### [Instrument] — A CONTINUOUS FUTURES TICKER CAN ROLL UNDER YOU MID-SERIES, AND A SPREAD'S TWO LEGS DO NOT ROLL TOGETHER
**Pattern (2026-09-14, HEN-46 F1 under a live WQ-213 decision):** I graded the ULSD crack as `HO=F`×42 − `CL=F` and reported it fell **−$9.59 (−8.86%)** in a session, "round-tripping the whole spike." **`HO=F` had rolled Oct→Nov THAT MORNING; `CL=F` had not.** Like-for-like, both legs Oct: **−$0.79 (−0.73%).** **~93% of the move was the roll.** The series had been calendar-MATCHED for its entire history — including the `$90.16` falsifier baseline and the `$109.93` peak — **and broke on precisely the session under decision.** The same artifact manufactured a "−3.80% heating oil / −4.90% gasoline on a day crude rose" print that was really **+0.48% / +0.81%**: a continuous series' %-change across a roll date computes against the PRIOR MONTH's close and **is not a price move.** I had already sent the wrong figure to another desk; it was caught only because a peer routed an instrument anomaly with an explicitly-uncertain diagnosis.
**Rule:** ⛔ **Before publishing any spread LEVEL, resolve every leg to its dated contract and confirm the months match.** The test is **`expireDate`**, not `shortName` (which truncates and may omit the month) and not price identity (which cannot separate "same contract" from "fallback") — **and it needs a NEGATIVE CONTROL: a different dated month must return a DIFFERENT expiry, or your resolver is collapsing everything onto one series and proves nothing.** ⚠️ **Every magnitude sanity check passes on a calendar-mismatched spread** — the number is real, reproducible, and about the wrong object. **A crack whose legs are not both named is NO-VERDICT, not a number.**
**Corollary — this NEVER CLOSES; it is structural and monthly.** Verified across three cycles: product legs expire **exactly 10 days after WTI every cycle** (Nov 10-30/10-20 · Dec 11-30/11-20 · Jan 12-31/12-21; Brent ~the 1st), and roll leads are **contract-specific** (HO/RB ~16 days, CL <8) ⇒ **a WTI-legged crack desynchronises in EVERY monthly cycle.** ⛔ **So never write a DATE as the end of the hazard** — I wrote "2026-09-22 is an upper bound on how long the mismatch can persist," which is true of the instance and false of the class, and a reader takes it as *safe after 9/22*. ⚠️ **That sentence survived INSIDE the very correction that re-keyed the hazard off dates — a correction pass is where this bites hardest, because the fix gets the attention and the sentences around it are assumed checked.**
**Corollary — the exposure follows the leg that DIDN'T roll.** Products and Brent rolled together; WTI did not, so the exposed set was "anything with a WTI leg," not "anything RB-based." Naming the wrong dimension sends the audit at the instruments that were already fine. ⛔ **And when a threshold sits inside such a window, DISCLOSE the artifact — do not re-spec the letter.** `F1` could have fired on a roll artifact; re-speccing it would have shaved in my own favour. `[[finding_instrument_reports_clean_against_the_wrong_reference]]`

### [Spec] — The One-Line SUMMARY of a Conditional Spec Is What Gets Transcribed, So It Cannot Be the Lossy Copy
**Pattern (2026-08-28, T3 v2 — and it happened to BOTH desks within hours, each catching the other's):** a spec was written correctly as a **conditional**, then compressed to a one-line ordering for the summary — and **the summary contradicted the spec**.
- **LIQUID's** linear token order returned `PENDING-PUBLICATION` for a read that was actually underpowered ⇒ told the reader *"check tomorrow"* when the true answer was *"check in three weeks."* **Wrong date.**
- **Mine** returned `UNDERPOWERED` for a read whose sessions had all occurred and merely hadn't published ⇒ *"wait for more sessions to occur"* when they already had. **Wrong cause.** ⛔ **Both are FALSE ACTION INSTRUCTIONS, not merely less informative — do not take the softer grade on your own.**
**Rule:** the summary line is the copy that gets pasted into the next document, so **either prove it equivalent to the conditional or don't ship it.** Equivalence is cheap to check — **enumerate the cases in a table** (here: 8 cases; `n_published` gave 1 mismatch, `n_projected` gave 0) — and that enumeration is the artifact, not the assertion.
**Corollary — the repair usually is ONE DEFINED TERM, and the defined term can open a new hole.** Evaluating "underpowered" on `n_projected` instead of `n_published` made the flat list exactly equivalent — **and created an IMMORTAL PENDING**: a session that occurs but never publishes keeps `n_projected` complete forever, so the read stays `PENDING` permanently while every individual token is "correct" at every step. **The termination condition has to be named explicitly** (here, the publication-lag check escalating to instrument-fault). **A defined term that fixes an ordering bug is not free — ask what it now counts forever.**

### [Verification] — An ABSENCE Claim Needs a POSITIVE CONTROL, and `2>/dev/null` Turns a Tool ERROR Into a False Negative
**Pattern (2026-08-28 — I published a wrong claim about ANOTHER DESK's record on this, and asked the coordinator to relay it):** I asserted four times that two figures were "not findable in HANS's STATUS on targeted greps." **They were there the whole time.** `grep` on this box is **ugrep**, and my context patterns of the form `grep -oniE ".{0,50}(alt|alt).{0,50}"` **fail with `error … exceeds complexity limits`** — and I had appended **`2>/dev/null`** to every one. **The error went to the void, stdout was empty, and an empty result is byte-identical to a true negative.**
🔑 **This is exactly the QUIET class I had audited my own recipes for THAT MORNING** (a command that exits non-zero, prints nothing, and looks like a clean negative). I found it in another desk's tool and in a third desk's remedy command — **then committed it four times myself in one session and shipped an outward-pointing correction on the strength of it.** Finding a failure class does not inoculate you against it.
**Rule:** ⛔ **Never suppress stderr on a scan whose ABSENCE is the finding.** And **every absence claim ships with a POSITIVE CONTROL** — a pattern over the same file that MUST return a hit. Here, `grep -c "1998"` returning **2** would have exposed it in one second, because my "no matches" pattern contained `1998` as a bare alternative.
**Corollary — prefer the dumb pattern for negatives.** The `.{0,N}(…).{0,N}` context wrapper exists to make output readable; it is the part that broke. For an absence check use plain fixed strings and read the count, then add context only once you have a hit. **Readability tooling on a negative result is where the negative gets manufactured.**
⚠️ **And the asymmetry that made it dangerous:** the same session's other scans were fine because they RETURNED SOMETHING — a hit proves the scan ran. **Only negative results can hide a dead instrument, which is precisely when nobody re-checks.**
