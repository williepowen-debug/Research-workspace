# LESSONS.md — HENRY Mistake Patterns & Rules

*Read at boot. Learn once, prevent forever.*

### [Data] — Always Pull Fresh Values
**Pattern:** STATUS.md values go stale between spawns (days/weeks). Citing stale VIX, HY OAS, or SPX levels produces wrong analysis.
**Rule:** If STATUS.md says "Known Data Issues" or values are >24h old, pull live data via web_search before any analysis. Never cite STATUS.md values as current without verifying.

### [Analysis] — VIX Spikes Can Be Artificial
**Lesson from Aug 2024:** VIX spike to 65 was 85% bid-ask widening, not real fear. 0DTE volume dropped 26%, recovery in 3 sessions. Don't treat VIX spikes as ground truth — check the mechanism (real selling vs liquidity withdrawal).

### [Analysis] — Credit Leads Equities, Not Vice Versa
**Rule:** Never call an equity bottom until HY OAS has peaked. Credit reprices before equity every time. If someone asks "has the selloff bottomed?" — check HY OAS first, not SPX technicals.

### [Process] — Distinguish Margin Trade vs Credit Trade
**Lesson from KRE:** Regional banks can sell off because the yield curve flattens (margin trade) or because credit quality deteriorates (credit trade). The distinction determines speed — margin trades grind, credit trades gap. Always identify which is driving the move before forecasting trajectory.

### [Verification] — Don't Cite Own STATUS.md for Live Prices
**Rule:** STATUS.md is for thresholds, frameworks, and thesis. It is NOT a live data feed. When asked about current VIX, SPX, or spreads, pull from web sources. Citing your own stale dashboard as "current" is circular.

### [Verification] — Cross-Agent Estimates Need Discount
**Lesson from LIQUID (Mar 3-4):** LIQUID's HY OAS model overestimated by ~30bps two sessions in a row (estimated 335-355bps; FRED confirmed 308bps). Pattern, not one-off. **Rule:** When using LIQUID's spread estimates, apply -30bps discount until the model is recalibrated. Always verify against FRED/ICE actuals before citing.

### [Analysis] — Consensus Revision Is a Framing Weapon
**Lesson from ADP (Mar 4):** ADP consensus was quietly revised from 130K → 50K before release, so +63K got framed as a "beat." The underlying data is unchanged — it's still a massive miss vs original expectations. **Rule:** When a data release drops, check what consensus was *before* any last-minute revision. The original consensus is the true sentiment anchor.

### [Analysis] — Geopolitical Headlines ≠ Fundamental Resolution
**Lesson from Iran peace talk leak (Mar 4):** NYT report of "indirect approach" → SPX +0.78%, VIX compressed. Same day IRGC declared "complete control" of Hormuz. Market rallied on hope, not change. **Rule:** Don't adjust thesis on geopolitical headlines alone. Require: (1) confirmed ceasefire/deal, (2) shipping lane reopened, or (3) oil price sustained below pre-event level. Until then, it's noise.

### [Analysis] — Unilateral Announcement ≠ Bilateral Resolution
**Lesson from Hormuz reopen (Apr 17):** Iran FM declared Hormuz "completely open" → Brent -11%, WTI -14%, SPX +1.2%, VIX 17.66. But US blockade remained in force — tankers still couldn't reach Iranian ports. Market priced full resolution; reality was asymmetric flow unwind. **Rule:** When a party unilaterally declares de-escalation, separate (a) the party's own actions (stopped blocking shipping) from (b) counterparty response (blockade persisting/lifting). Oil-flow normalization requires BOTH sides stepping back. Price the physical delta (tanker tracking) not the verbal one. Also: a clean oil unwind does not retroactively fix CPI/PPI/UMich prints already locked — stagflation survives even if the macro shock is subtracted.

### [Analysis] — Anchor the Yield Clause to the Right Tenor (hawkish-into-slowing flattens)
**Lesson from HEN-33 (FOMC 6/17 2026):** Predicted "FOMC dots hawkish-of-pricing → **10Y** +10bps within 2 sessions." The dots DID flip hawkish-of-pricing (cut→hike bias, median 3.8%, hawkish Warsh) — the directional call was right — but the 10Y FELL −3bps while the **2Y surged +15bps** (biggest Fed-day move since Mar 2008). The miss was the vehicle, not the direction. **Rule:** when a hawkish-of-pricing Fed ALSO cuts growth (SEP GDP 2.2%, PCE up = stagflationary), the curve FLATTENS — the front end (2Y) carries the hawkish repricing while the long end (10Y) is anchored or falls on the growth cut. Anchor the yield clause to the tenor the surprise actually hits: hawkish-rate-path surprise → 2Y; growth/term-premium surprise → 10Y. Don't default to the 10Y. (Cf. auto-mem `anchor-prediction-to-surprise-not-priced` — same family: get the *expression* right, not just the surprise.)

### [Verification] — Stunning Secondary Claims Need Primary Confirmation Before Integration
**Lesson from the "Fed flipped cut→HIKE" relay (6/23):** Multiple WALTER board signals carried "the Fed just flipped cut→HIKE 6/17." A cut→HIKE flip when hold was ~99.9% priced is a *stunning* claim — and read literally it's wrong: the Fed HELD 12-0; the DOT PLOT flipped to a hike bias (a "Dot Plot Flips to a Hike" shorthand that reads like an actual rate hike). The adversarial-verify stage pinned the fact against the primary FOMC statement. **Rule:** the more surprising a relayed/secondary claim, the more it needs primary confirmation before it goes load-bearing — especially policy actions (hike vs hold-with-hawkish-dots are very different). On catch-up boots after a gap, run a verify pass on the single most consequential gap event, not just a research pass. **Corollary (Will 6/23, the harder half of the lesson):** verify the FACT, but do NOT escalate an ambiguous relay phrasing into "the *fleet* believes the false thing" without checking the consumers' actual state. Here SAM/RED framed it correctly as the pricing/dots flip and REGINALD explicitly decoded the shorthand via IORB — there was no fleet error to correct. I asserted a fleet-wide misconception that didn't exist; that's its own overclaim.

### [Verification] — A Stale Point-Value Can INVERT an Axis Read — Pull the Direction, Not Just a Number
**Lesson from the HY-OAS inversion (6/23→7/1):** My 6/23 STATUS framed credit as "HY OAS 265, AT the 260 soft-kill, complacent-tightening even as equities de-rate" — and built a whole thesis leg on it (soft-kill's credit branch approaching). It was **directionally wrong**: HY was actually **278 [FRED 6/25], WIDENING toward the 280 X1**, not compressing to 260. A stale/mis-read point value (a ~13bp miss) didn't just age — it **inverted the sign of an entire axis** (soft-kill-compression vs structural-widening are opposite theses). PROME caught it (6/26); LIQUID (the credit owner) independently had it right (283→280→275, "<260 kill off the table"). **Rule:** for any load-bearing cross-domain number, verify the **direction/trend against the primary series or the owning agent** before you frame a thesis on it — not just the freshness of a single print. FRED gives the full daily series in one pull (6/9 finding) and the owning agent's STATUS carries the trend; a bare point value read as "current" can be both stale AND sign-flipped. When a number is doing thesis work, pull its neighbors. Cf. [[feedback_pull_live_primary_not_dashboard]], [[feedback_verify_counts_before_propagating]], and the 5/21 literal-triad-count discipline (count direction literally, don't narrate "approaching").

### [Process] — Split the Threshold From the Mechanism When You Re-Mark a Prediction
**Lesson from HEN-35 (6/23→7/1):** HEN-35 was written as a THRESHOLD prediction ("AI/semi unwind → vol-control cascade, VIX >23 sustained by 7/17"). Over the gap the threshold RECEDED (VIX crushed to 16.5, month-end absorbed, records) while the underlying MECHANISM (AI-capex correction) DEEPENED (DRAM/HBM relapse, hyperscaler-FCF-cliff, DEWEY circular-financing, ex-AI GDP contraction). The honest move was to mark the literal prediction DOWN (~15%) AND spin the strengthening mechanism into its own tracked thesis (HEN-36 ~55%), not to let a deepening thesis prop up a receding threshold or vice-versa. **Rule:** when a level-anchored prediction diverges from the thesis it was testing, grade the threshold on its literal terms and track the mechanism separately — don't average them into a muddy single number. Cf. [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]], and the data-right/positioning-early asymmetry (calling the thesis right ≠ the threshold firing).

### [Verification] — Don't Let a Figure You've Flagged Unconfirmed Go Load-Bearing in the SAME Edit
**Lesson from the NFP 10Y re-mark (7/2):** PROME relayed "10Y +3bp to 4.50 = hawkish-stagflation." My own feed showed 4.46 (flat), so I correctly **flagged** I couldn't confirm PROME's number — but in the same pass I **re-marked HEN-37 to "hawkish / front-end firmed"** *built on that very figure.* That's incoherent: I applied a skeptical evidentiary standard to the number and a credulous one to the conclusion I drew from it (the "same datum, two standards" trap, [[finding_same_datum_two_evidentiary_standards]]). The figure turned out to be a bad single pre-open tick (4.503) — PROME retracted it; three sources confirmed 10Y ~flat 4.47. **Rule:** when you disclaim a cross-agent number as unconfirmed, hold every downstream re-mark to the SAME standard — either wait to confirm before committing the conclusion, or mark the conclusion as provisional/"pending confirmation," never as settled. The healthy half: flagging (not silently adopting) PROME's figure is exactly what caught a live **circular-corroboration** case (his HEARTBEAT number had already propagated into another agent's re-marks within the hour); the correction round-tripped in ~45 min. Keep flagging; just don't build on the flagged thing before it clears. Cf. [[finding_circular_corroboration_via_state_file]], [[feedback_suspect_fresh_pull_over_curated_record]] (a single fast_info tick is the "fresh pull" to suspect).

### [Analysis] — +GEX Cushions a LEVEL Move, NOT a Correlation/Duration Shock (don't mis-apply "equity cushioned")
**Lesson from the mid-July node pre-load (7/6):** I wrote "equity is cushioned now (SPX +~1% over the gamma flip, +GEX dampening)" while the live risk path was the 7/9-30Y-reopen → CPI-7/14 sequence — a **rate/duration shock** (a soft 30Y gaps 10Y through 4.50 with no dovish-growth offset). The adversarial cross-domain pass flagged "equity cushioned" as *falsely reassuring* for that path, and it was my own logic (transmission = correlation-spike, not a level) turned load-bearing. **Rule:** +GEX (long dealer gamma) dampens moves ALONG the SPX price axis ONLY; it gives zero offset to off-axis shocks — rate/duration (discount-rate multiple compression), cross-asset correlation spikes (stocks+bonds down → risk-parity de-lever, cascade step 5), or credit-led selling. Worse, a fast off-axis gap that clears the flip converts +GEX → −GEX (dampener → amplifier). **Before citing "equity cushioned," classify the shock:** MOVE-led (rate vol, VIX flat) or stocks-and-bonds-down-together = off-axis = NOT cushioned. Full discriminator (shock-class table + 3 fast tells) → `domain/REFERENCE_TABLES.md` **GAMMA-CUSHION VALIDITY RULE (GCVR)**. Cf. cascade step 5 (correlation → risk parity), the LEADING INDICATOR SEQUENCE (MOVE leads VIX by 2–5 days), the margin-vs-credit KRE test, and [[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]].

### [Verification] — A Self-Built Proxy Does NOT Close a Paywall/Data Gap Until It's Validated Against the Thing It Replaces
**Lesson from the gamma-flip repull (7/17):** Will asked me to repull the paywalled SpotGamma flip. I computed one from the live ^SPX chain (BSM gamma + a naive long-call/short-put dealer assumption) and declared the "paywall GAP RESOLVED" + the pending Will spend-decision "MOOT." Both were overclaims, and Will caught it ("how is the paywall gap gone?"). Two errors: (1) **I substituted a cruder proxy for the paywalled product and called it equivalent** — but the paywall's actual value was the *dealer-positioning inference* my script fakes with an assumption; I'd rebuilt the *free* trackers, not SpotGamma. (2) **I treated self-agreement as validation** — my ~7,522 matching my own 7/16 ~7,530-7,545 estimate shares the same dealer assumption, so it's corroboration-by-shared-premise, not an independent check (the circular-validation family, [[finding_circular_corroboration_via_state_file]], [[finding_divergence_requires_fresh_likeforlike_baseline]]). Same "same datum, two evidentiary standards" trap as the 7/2 NFP re-mark ([[finding_same_datum_two_evidentiary_standards]]): skeptical of the paywalled number, credulous about my replacement. **Rule:** when you build a tool/proxy to route around a paywalled or missing data source, the gap is NOT closed until you validate the proxy against that source (or an independent one) at least once. Until then, state the tier honestly ("free-tier estimate, same assumptions as the free trackers, unvalidated"), and separate what IS robust (here: the SIGN — SPX 65pts below the flip exceeds the estimator's uncertainty) from what isn't (the precise level near a crossing). A reproducible self-hosted estimator is genuinely useful — it removes an external *dependency* — but removing a dependency ≠ closing a precision gap. Don't let "I can now generate a number" masquerade as "I have the right number."

### [Verification] — Validation Certifies a Tool Against GOOD Input; It Says Nothing About Its Behavior on BAD Input
**Lesson from the `gamma_flip.py` NaN failure (7/23):** On 7/17 I built the gamma estimator, validated it against two independent free trackers (flip within 20-34pts, walls exact-match), sensitivity-tested it, and wired it into `boot.py` as a standing read. Six days later it printed `Net GEX +nan · regime POSITIVE · put wall 8,100 · call wall 6,360` — with the put wall **above** the call wall, and the standalone CLI reporting **NEGATIVE** on the very same run where boot reported **POSITIVE**. Root cause was one line: `oi = r["openInterest"] or 0`. **`x or 0` does not catch NaN — NaN is truthy** — and `NaN <= 0` is False, so NaN-OI contracts sailed through the filter and poisoned the GEX sum. From there NaN silently took the *wrong branch of every comparison*: `"NEGATIVE" if g0 < 0 else "POSITIVE"` → POSITIVE, `"POSITIVE" if g0 > 0 else "NEGATIVE"` → NEGATIVE (same number, opposite labels), sign-change flip detection never fired, and `max()` over an all-NaN dict returned an arbitrary strike. **The validation on 7/17 was real and it protected nothing here**, because it exercised the tool on a healthy chain — it never asked what the tool does when yfinance returns NaN IV for most of the chain (98 usable contracts vs 6,206). **Rules:** (1) **A tool that can't get data must SAY so, not return a number** — fail loud, never fail silent (`[[finding_fail_loud_on_incomplete_data]]`). Add an explicit input-quality floor (here `MIN_CONTRACTS`), not just output plausibility. (2) **NaN is the specific poison in any numeric pipeline** — it is truthy, and every comparison against it is False, so it doesn't crash, it *quietly picks a branch*. Guard it explicitly; `or 0` / truthiness checks do not. (3) **Two surfaces reporting opposite labels off one number is a data bug until proven otherwise** — that contradiction was the cheapest possible tell and I should treat it as a first-class alarm, not a curiosity. (4) When a validated tool degrades, **do not fall back to its last good output** — the 7/17 flip (~7,522) was 6 days and −50 SPX points stale, and reusing it is precisely the stale-point-value trap that inverted the HY axis in June ([[finding_ledger_drift_behind_narrative]]). Report the gap instead. Cf. the 7/17 lesson directly above (a proxy isn't ground truth) — this is its sequel: *a validated proxy isn't a permanently validated proxy.*

### [Analysis] — The LEVEL Crossed a Round Number, but the Index Measures the MOVE (base-effect illusion)
**Lesson from the gas-pump/HEN-41 read (7/23):** My pre-registered interim proxy — "does the national average cross $4.00 in the 7/20-23 window?" — resolved **PASS** (AAA $4.003 on 7/20, EIA regular $4.001 same date, $4.091 by 7/23, +15c w/w, monotonic up). The obvious inference is "energy is re-inflating → HEN-41 CONFIRM." **That inference is wrong for the print it was built to forecast.** CPI measures the **monthly average**, and June *started* at $4.305 and fell all month (4.305/4.146/4.052/3.914/3.831 ⇒ **June avg ≈ $4.05**) while July runs 3.777/3.855/4.001 + a ~$4.13 final week (⇒ **July avg ≈ $3.94**). So **July gasoline CPI can print NEGATIVE MoM with the pump at $4.09 and climbing** — the "$4 gas is back" headline is a *base-effect illusion* for the 8/13 release, and the real passthrough test is **August CPI (~9/10)**, where both $100 Brent and a >$4 pump sit inside the full monthly average. **Rule: when a proxy resolves on a LEVEL but the prediction resolves on a RATE OF CHANGE, re-derive the rate before transferring the verdict — and always check what the comparison base was DOING, not just where it ended.** A base that was falling all through the prior month manufactures a soft print out of a rising series. Same family as `[[feedback_yoy_baseeffect_use_multiyear_stack]]` and the "LEVEL ≠ MOVE" clause in `[[finding_curve_shape_policypath_vs_termpremium]]`; the mirror image of the June-CPI-is-pre-spike sequencing catch — same discipline, opposite direction. Bonus trap avoided: my on-file EIA series ID `EMM_EPM0_PTE_NUS_DPG` is **all-grades** (~13c high); regular is `EMM_EPMR_PTE_NUS_DPG`.

### [Verification] — Count Convergence by EVIDENCE TYPE, Not by Agent Headcount
**Lesson from HEN-42 (7/23), corrected by BOND in the same session:** I registered a driver-rotation call (rates move is policy-path, not term-premium) and wrote in STATUS that LIQUID had reached it independently — *"two agents, two routes, same answer"* — while dutifully flagging shared-blind-spot risk. I routed it to BOND asking to be refuted. **BOND confirmed the call but refuted my framing, and his version is better:** LIQUID, BOND and I had all read **the same FRED curve** (2Y +13 > 10Y +12 > 30Y +9). That is **ONE datum, not three routes** — and both LIQUID's reversal and BOND's relabel happened *downstream* of it. Agreement reached off a shared antecedent is not independence no matter how many agents perform it, and my "flagging the risk" didn't fix it because I still counted the agreement as corroboration. **What actually earned trust was ORTHOGONALITY of evidence type:** my source-side route (Sept-hike odds 52→80%, Warsh's 7/20 testimony) vs BOND's composition-side route (10Y TIPS clearing the DFII10 series-high with indirect 65% / dealer 9.9%; 20Y-R indirect 69% ruling out a foreign strike). Those are genuinely different instruments answering the same question. **Rule: before banking convergence, ask "what did each agent actually LOOK AT?" — not "how many agents agree?" If the answer is the same series, it is one observation wearing several hats.** Prefer one orthogonal confirmation over three concordant re-readings. Corollary: when *you* are the one being converged with, name your antecedent explicitly so the other agent can test independence — I should have written "off the same curve you're reading" rather than "independently reached." Cf. `[[finding_shared_antecedent_independence_test]]`, `[[finding_circular_corroboration_via_state_file]]`, `[[finding_adversarial_verify_own_convergence]]`.

### [Verification] — Name a Root Cause by INSTRUMENTING It, Not by Picking the Plausible One
**Lesson from the gamma-feed misdiagnosis (7/23):** When `gamma_flip.py` returned only 98 usable contracts (vs 6,206 on 7/17), I wrote — and **committed and pushed** — "yfinance's ^SPX IV feed is degraded; most rows carry NaN IV." It was plausible: I'd just fixed a NaN bug, so NaN was salient, and an IV outage would explain thinness. **It was wrong.** When Will asked me to retry, I finally counted the rejections with a five-line `Counter()`: of **7,514** raw rows, **7,278 were dropped for `openInterest == 0`** — only 32 for out-of-band IV. **Volume and IV were fully populated; the `openInterest` field alone was empty** (max OI of *3* on the front expiry). Different cause, different remedy: an IV outage might clear on retry, whereas a dead OI field never will — it needs a **different source**. The misdiagnosis would have had me retrying a broken pull indefinitely; the correct diagnosis pointed straight at CBOE, which had real OI and produced the read in one attempt. **Rules:** (1) **A root cause is a measurement, not an inference.** Before naming one — especially in a commit — run the count that distinguishes the candidates. Here that was one `Counter()` over rejection reasons, cheaper than the sentence I wrote instead. (2) **Beware the salient cause:** having just debugged NaN, I saw NaN everywhere. The bug I most recently fixed is the least trustworthy hypothesis for the next symptom. (3) **Distinguish "the data is bad" from "this field is bad"** — the second is usually routable to another vendor, and field-level outages are common while whole-feed outages are rare. (4) When a diagnosis is already shipped, **correct it in the same surfaces that carried it**, and say plainly that the earlier claim was wrong. Cf. `[[finding_fail_loud_on_incomplete_data]]`, `[[finding_verify_runtime_context_before_tool_broken]]`.

### [Verification] — A Cross-Source Number Can Carry a SIGN but Not a DELTA; and Never Publish an Arg-Max Without Checking for a Near-Tie
**Lesson from the gamma validation (7/23):** After recovering the gamma read from a new source (CBOE, after yfinance's OI field died), I published two claims that a 6-tracker validation then killed. **(1) "Negative gamma DOUBLED since 7/17" (−$25.7B → −$45.2B).** I *had* flagged the flip *level* comparison as cross-source — then turned around and made a **magnitude** claim across the very same source change. A different vendor's OI (possibly already partly degraded on the old one) changes the level of every derived dollar figure; the sign survives a source change, a *delta* does not. The clean like-for-like came from a source that publishes a **daily series** (Modigin's flip ladder + ZeroGEX 7/17 vs 7/23), and it told a different story: **the flip had been pinned in a ~7,473-7,516 band for two weeks — spot fell away from a stationary flip, rather than the flip migrating toward spot.** Same directional conclusion, different mechanism, and the mechanism is what a reader would model off. **(2) "SPX is through BOTH walls."** My put wall printed 7,500 — the same strike as the call wall, which I *noticed was odd and did not chase*. It was an arg-max over a near-tie: gamma-weighted 7,500 (117.6) vs 7,300 (114.3), a 3% gap `max()` broke arbitrarily; by raw OI the ordering inverts entirely (7,000 ≫ 7,300 > 7,500). All six trackers read 7,300-7,400. So SPX was sitting *just above* put support, not through it — the opposite of the "bounded only by circuit breakers" framing I attached. **Rules:** (1) **A source change resets your baseline.** Sign and level survive it; *changes* measured across it do not. Before claiming a delta, find a source with a consistent history — a vendor publishing a daily series beats two of your own pulls from different vendors. (2) **`max()` over near-ties is a coin flip wearing a decimal point.** Any published arg-max (wall, peak strike, top contributor) needs a runners-up check; if #1 and #2 are within a few percent, report the band, not the winner. (3) **When a result looks structurally weird — a put wall equal to the call wall — that is the cheapest available tell. Chase it immediately.** I flagged it in passing and moved on; the validation had to find it for me. (4) **Carry the dissent with its date:** SpotGamma read *positive* gamma to 7,300 — reconcilable as a pre-selloff morning note, but "corroborated 5-of-6 with one dated dissent" is the honest phrasing, not "corroborated."

### [Data] — A Threshold Read Off CLOSES ONLY Hides the Range That Actually Tests It
**Lesson from the VIX reconciliation (7/23):** For a month I asserted — in STATUS, the threshold table, the thesis verdict and to Will — that *"VIX has never neared 23; cascade step 1 has never engaged,"* citing 19.49 (6/23), 18.77 (7/17), 18.70 (7/23). Every one of those is a **close**. Pulling the daily OHLC because VIOLET's midday figure disagreed with mine: **VIX 7/23 printed a high of 20.31 — the highest of the entire episode, above the 19.49 I'd been calling the ceiling.** My "never neared" was a close-only artifact; the tape had been reaching higher than my own threshold table showed, and I'd never checked because my boot pulls last/close. **The intraday data also carried the more interesting signal in the other direction:** VIX tagged 20.31 and **closed 18.70**, giving back 1.6pts into the bell on a −1.2% SPX day with dealers short gamma — vol *sold* into the spike, a **failed breakout**, not a boundary about to break. Both facts are load-bearing and neither is visible in a close. **Rules:** (1) **When a level is a trigger, track the range, not the settle** — "did it touch" and "did it hold" are different questions and you usually need both. Add High/Low to any boot pull that feeds a threshold. (2) **A rejected tag is information, not a non-event** — the ceiling moving up while the close stays calm is a distinct regime from a quiet range, and only OHLC shows it. (3) The prompt to check came from a **peer's number disagreeing with mine** (VIOLET's 19.86 vs my 18.70) — a cross-agent discrepancy on a shared metric is worth resolving to the underlying data rather than to whoever's timestamp is later; here neither of us was wrong and the reconciliation produced a fact *both* of us were missing. Cf. `[[feedback_verify_counts_before_propagating]]`, `[[finding_ohlc_verify_before_session_claims]]`.

### [Statistics] — A Detection Band and a Near-Null Band Are Not Two Outcomes of One Test
**Pattern (2026-08-28, LIQUID's T3 finding, verified independently here):** T3's two bands looked symmetric and cost **n=38** vs **n=348** — a **~9× asymmetry** at the same α and power. **You cannot demonstrate ABSENCE with the sample that detects PRESENCE.** At the registered n=20 the CI was **[−0.067, +0.722]**, which **contains both bands**, so the test could not separate its own two outcomes at *any* observed value.
**Rule:** whenever a spec has a "large ⇒ X / small ⇒ Y" pair, **price both legs separately before registering.** If the null-side leg needs a sample you will never have, it is a **dead band — dead by UNREACHABILITY**, and a row-counting audit cannot see it because the cell is populated and the threshold is sane.
**And the trap on the repair:** the obvious fix is an equivalence test (TOST) at a named margin — but **run its margin table before adopting it.** For T3, a margin that genuinely supports "the shared factor is the dollar" (≤0.20) costs **155+** sessions, while a margin reachable alongside the detection leg (0.40 → n=39) sits **0.05 from the detection threshold** — the equivalence and detection regions would nearly touch. **Computing the repair is what proved the repair wrong**; the honest answer was to go one-sided and stop claiming the null.

### [Statistics] — A Size-Dependent Penalty Must Be MEASURED ACROSS SIZES, Not at One Size and Not From Its Asymptote
**Pattern (2026-08-28, T3 overlap penalty — two desks, two errors, opposite directions, same class):** the question was how much overlapping k=20 windows sampled daily inflate the variance of a correlation estimator.
- **LIQUID** simulated at the test's actual size (n=20, k=20) and measured **VIF 3.3×, effective n≈8.8** — and nearly shipped *"HENRY's ~20× is really ~3.3×."* **Wrong: at n=20 with k=20 there are only ~40 days of span, so the windows physically cannot express their overlap. The estimator is SATURATED and the effect cannot appear at the size it was measured.**
- **I** used the textbook heuristic `effective n = n/k`, i.e. **VIF = 20**, and published **~760 sessions**. **Also wrong: that is an ASYMPTOTIC BOUND and it is never attained.** Measured VIF climbs 3.4 → 7.0 → 11.8 → 12.7 → **13.9 at n=540 and is still rising.** Truth ≈ **470 sessions**, ~1.6× below my figure and ~4× above LIQUID's.
**Rule:** when a penalty depends on sample size, **measure it at three or more sizes and look at the TREND.** A single measurement tells you the value at that size and nothing about the shape; an asymptotic formula tells you a bound you may never reach. **Both failure modes produce a confident number, and neither is detectable from inside its own calculation.**
**Corollary — the tell that saved both of us:** LIQUID caught its error by **extending the measurement** rather than extrapolating from the first read. I caught mine by **running the simulation on a correction that AGREED with me.** ⚠️ **I had just been told by the counterparty that my figure "stands unmodified." A counterparty endorsement is the weakest possible check on your own number, because it removes the only person likely to look.** `[[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]]` — the rigour must point INWARD hardest exactly when the news is good.

### [Spec] — A Pre-Commitment That Names a VALUE Instead of a RULE Becomes a Carry-Item
**Pattern (2026-08-28, caught by LIQUID):** I wrote *"+0.399 at n=20 is NO VERDICT and stays so unless n≥38 AND r≥0.45"* — intended as a guard against rationalising later. **As phrased it reads as though +0.399 is the number carried to the n=38 grade.
