### [Verification] — A NEGATIVE Existence Claim Needs a COMPLETE Scan; Check `processed/` and Your Own MEMORY Before Saying You Never Got It
**Lesson from the phantom routing failure (7/28), caught by VIOLET:** PROME directed me to answer VIOLET's gamma-flip ask, saying it was in my unprocessed inbox. I ran `ls` and `grep` over the **top level** of `inbox/`, checked **VIOLET's outbox**, found no VIOLET-authored packet, and wrote in bold — to Will, to PROME, and into a packet — *"there is no VIOLET packet in my inbox at all… if you sent one, it did not arrive."* I then **proposed a CLAUDE.md rule change partly on the strength of that "routing failure."** It was false. The ask was `inbox/processed/2026-07-27_from-PROME_gamma-refresh-ASK-live-position-rests-on-your-7-23-chain.md` — four numbered items, #1 the flip carried at ~7,496 — **and my own 7/27 session had already answered all four in that order.** Three compounding failures, each worse than the last: **(1)** I checked a *sender's outbox* and my *own top-level inbox* and concluded about **receipt** — a sender's outbox cannot establish what a recipient got (the mirror of `[[finding_delivery_check_is_not_a_knowledge_check]]`). **(2)** *"There is no X at all"* is a **negative existence claim**, which is precisely the class that requires an exhaustive scan — and I made it off two partial ones, in bold. Positive claims can survive sampling; negative ones cannot (`[[finding_comprehensive_grep_over_sampling]]`). **(3)** The worst: **a summary of that ask is in my own `MEMORY.md`, which I read at boot step 3 of that very session** — *"PRIOR SESSION (7/27 — PROME teams-spawn, time-boxed gamma refresh): delivered 4 numbers for a live VIOLET position."* I read the record of the ask and hours later asserted it never existed. **I also overstated in my own favour** in the same pass: I reported "two live PROME packets holding a stale copy" of my flip when **PROME's `DOCKET.tsv` and `SCRATCH.md` had already flagged it stale and computed the refreshed gap** — the coordinator was *ahead* of me on the point I presented as my finding. **Rules:** (1) **Before claiming you never received something, scan `inbox/processed/` and grep your own `MEMORY.md` — you have probably already handled it.** The default assumption on a mature agent should be *"I've seen this"*, not *"this never came."* (2) **Never infer a fleet-level failure (a lost packet, a broken route) from your own local absence** — a coordinator relay means the ask can reach you with the requester's name nowhere on it. Here VIOLET wrote the need on her STATUS, PROME read it and authored the packet: **the routing layer working exactly as designed looks identical to "no packet from VIOLET."** (3) **When a claim of yours would justify a process/rule change, raise the evidence bar, don't lower it** — a systemic conclusion is the most expensive kind to be wrong about, and I let one travel to Will inside an hour. (4) Watch for **error-of-the-same-family recurrence within a session**: I retracted the FedWatch-vintage claim at 02:30 for building on an unverified baseline, then at 04:00 made a fresh confident claim off an incomplete scan. Retracting one instance does not inoculate the pattern.

### [Process] — A Threshold Written as a DIRECTION With a Decorative Magnitude Band Never Tests the Magnitude
**Lesson from HEN-36's resolution (7/31):** My pre-registration read *"capex **held/raised** at ~$290-320B annualized AND FCF down YoY for ≥2 of 4."* It resolved CONFIRMED 4-of-4 and I was right about everything that mattered — **except that the actual number is ~$710-735B, roughly 2.3× my band, and nothing in the test could ever have told me so.** Because the operative verb was *"held/raised,"* **any** figure above the floor passes; the "~$290-320B" rode along for a month looking like a quantitative claim while doing zero work. I carried it into other reasoning too (the 7/6 note that layoff savings are "a rounding error vs ~$290B capex" — true, and even more true at $710B, which is exactly why the error never surfaced). **Rules:** (1) **If you write a magnitude into a threshold, give it a two-sided test or delete it** — a band with only a floor is a direction wearing a decimal point. (2) **At resolution, grade the magnitude separately from the direction**, even when the direction fires cleanly; a confirmed prediction is the *least* likely moment you'll go looking for a calibration error, which is precisely why it hides there. (3) **A number that is never load-bearing is never checked** — audit the decorative figures in your own registrations, not just the operative ones. Cf. `[[finding_threshold_level_is_a_measurement_not_a_constant]]`, `[[finding_threshold_spec_fails_before_world]]`, and the threshold-vs-mechanism split (HEN-35) — this is its third cousin: *threshold fired, mechanism confirmed, **scale** silently wrong.*

### [Verification] — When You Charge Someone Else's Falsifier With Being Mis-Specified, Expect the Same Charge to Come Back at You — and Don't Use It as an Escape Hatch
**Lesson from HEN-42's FOMC discriminator (7/28 → 7/31):** On 7/28 I criticized BOND's joint falsifier for not firing only because *"the 2Y did the OPPOSITE of tailing, which is not the DENY case being tested — the falsifier was mis-specified for the world that showed up."* Three days later **my own** HEN-42 criterion — *"front end leads on **hawkish** catalysts"* — met a **mixed** catalyst (dovish decision, hawkish dissents, guidance withdrawn) and came back DENY-side on both legs: 2Y −4bp against 30Y +11bp, 2s10s +35→+45bp in one session, the whole 10Y move in breakevens with real yields flat. **The identical defect, in my own spec, inside one week.** The temptation was obvious and I want it on the record as a temptation: *"my criterion was under-specified, therefore this doesn't count against me."* **That is an escape hatch, not a defense** — and it is *especially* unavailable to me having just called it a defect in someone else's work. **Rules:** (1) **A mis-specified criterion is a mark against the forecaster, not a get-out** — it means you didn't enumerate the states of the world, which is the job. Cut confidence *and* record the spec defect; don't trade one for the other. (2) **When you criticize a peer's falsifier, immediately re-read your own for the same failure** — the criticism proves you have the concept loaded, which is the cheapest moment to apply it inward. (3) **Ask of every criterion: what is the CATALYST's sign if it's mixed?** Hawkish/dovish is not binary for a decision that holds rates, dissents hawkish, and withdraws guidance all at once. (4) Even after granting the steelman, **check whether the surviving reading still supports your claim** — here it didn't: a dovish surprise that lifts breakevens and the 30Y to a 19-year high is an *inflation-credibility* term premium, a different object from the expected-real-policy-rate one HEN-42 claims. Cf. `[[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]]`, `[[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]]` (the inward-pointing half), `[[finding_compound_gate_jointly_unsatisfiable]]`.

### [Verification] — A Detector's HIT Is a Pointer, Not a Finding: Read the LINE, and Watch Which Hits You Bother to Read
**Lesson from the VIOLET false-positive (7/31), caught by PROME within minutes of delivery:** `consumer_check.py` returned two 🔴 stale-consumer hits. I opened the **SAM** one (`MOF_FLOWS.tsv:148`, matched on `7479`), saw immediately it was a 2007 MOF intervention-flow **data value** and not my gamma flip, and correctly wrote it up as a false positive — *in the same paragraph* where I took the **VIOLET** hit (`VIX_THESIS.md:32`, matched on `7496`) **on the grep's word**, called it *"~48pts of phantom headroom on a live position,"* escalated it to **🔴 route-first, before the close**, and put it at the top of my delivery to the coordinator. **The line I never opened reads: the kill *"read* ~7,496" — past tense — followed by the re-base to 7,455/7,491. It was the CHANGELOG ENTRY RECORDING THE CORRECTION.** Her file was not stale; it was the artifact *documenting* that the fix propagated. **And the position it governed had been closed for a day** — `TRY-VIOLET-VIXCS` exited 7/30 ~09:50 ET, TERMINAL, realized −$111.60, with `ACTIVE_DECISIONS.md:48` and HEARTBEAT §5 both saying so plainly. **So I called a closed box live, on a correct file, at maximum urgency, to the coordinator.** **Rules:** (1) **A detector reports a STRING MATCH; only reading the line reports a FACT.** Value-matching tools cannot see tense, negation, or "this is the entry announcing the change" — the three ways a number legitimately appears *after* being retired. Never route a check hit you haven't opened. (2) **🔑 The diagnostic that would have caught me is not "did I verify?" but "which hits did I bother to verify?"** I had the skill loaded and spent it on the hit that would have embarrassed a peer, then skipped it on the hit that made my own session look valuable. **Audit your verification effort for a gradient that tracks self-interest** — `[[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]]`, and note this was the *second* instance in one session (I caught the first, on the HEN-42 steelman, which proves the concept was live and I still missed it here). (3) **Before calling anything about a position "live," read the position's own record** — `ACTIVE_DECISIONS` / HEARTBEAT, not your own last-session memory of it. A threshold whose position is closed is not urgent, it is noise. `[[finding_guard_scope_expires_at_the_fill]]` is the same family: **guards, bands and kill-lines expire with the thing they guard, and the retirement is recorded somewhere you are not looking.** (4) **Urgency is an evidence multiplier and must raise the bar, not lower it** — this is the identical failure to the 7/28 phantom-routing-failure claim (bold assertion to Will and PROME off a partial scan), and *that* lesson's own rule ("when a claim would justify urgent action, raise the evidence bar") was three entries above this one in this very file when I did it again. **Retracting an instance does not inoculate the pattern; only changing the trigger does.** New trigger, adopted: **any 🔴 I am about to route about ANOTHER agent's file requires opening that file's line and that position's record — no exceptions, no time pressure defense.** (5) **When you're corrected, don't over-retract either:** PROME also gestured at my ">23 vol-control trigger" line as dead-position residue; I re-checked and it is my own cascade-mechanics row, predating VIXCS by months, so I kept it and said why. Accepting a correction wholesale to look appropriately chastened is its own failure of accuracy.

### [Process] — A Session That REVERSES a Regime Must Grep Its Own File for the OLD Regime Before Commit
**Pattern (2026-08-27, Will-directed audit — 21 defects, 7+ self-inflicted the same session):** I flipped the gamma call from negative to positive, refreshed the top of STATUS, and left **every downstream surface asserting the state I had just reversed** — the Verdict block, the ACTIVE THRESHOLDS SPX row, Axis 3's credit marks, the CREDIT paragraph (which then carried gap **875 AND 869** in one breath), the cross-cutting driver's DFII10, the leg-1 row, and the SKEW row. The peer-facing `NEXUS_BRIEF` was worse: a bullet labelled **"GAMMA — CURRENT"** carrying a 27-day-old NEGATIVE read **and publishing wall levels my own rule had withheld five times running.**
**Rule:** when a session reverses a regime, do not "update STATUS." **Grep the file — and the brief — for the superseded state BY NAME** (`NEGATIVE GAMMA`, the old gap number, the old level, the old adjective) and confirm every hit sits inside a strike record rather than a live assertion. **The detector is a grep; the gap is never detection, it is invocation.** Proven the same session: running it immediately surfaced two more defects the manual read had missed — a live `USD/JPY 158.33 [8/6]` and the stale brief.
**Corollary — supersession markers do not travel.** Labelling an old mark "superseded" in a parenthetical does nothing about the sentences beside it that restate the same numbers. Delete or strike the restatements, not just the header.

### [Verification] — Never Publish a Superlative About Your Own Instrument Without Querying the Full Series First
**Pattern (2026-08-27):** four false superlatives in one session, all about instruments I own — *"HY 7bp away, the closest it has ever been"* (**263bp [2026-06-17] was 4bp nearer**; the `<260` leg had been on the file since 2026-03-16); *"both legs nearer than at any point in the thesis's life"* (**refuted by my own table two lines below it** — 8/14 closed VIX 14.25 with the same HY 267); *"the >23 trigger is the furthest of the cycle"* (**8/14 was 8.75 away vs 8.36**); *"CCC peak 1,034 [7/31]"* (**1,039 [8/25]**). On 8/23 I had adopted WALTER's rule against exactly this after the third false-referent superlative in 24 hours.
**Rule:** "closest/furthest/highest/peak ever" is a claim about a **whole series**, not about the two prints you happen to be holding. **One `sort -n | head` on the series before the word ships.** ⚠️ **AND NAME THE WINDOW, YEAR-QUALIFIED — the correction pass failed this twice.** My fix pulled `cosd=2026-01-01` and presented a **2026-only** list as the series history, then wrote dates as `[1/22]` which collided with a peer's canonical **2025-01-22**. Two windows answer different questions and must be stated separately: **thesis's life** (since the leg was registered — min 263 [2026-06-17]) vs **full series** (n=787 — one sub-260 print, 259.0 [2025-01-22], i.e. the line HAS been broken once). **A peer found this, not me** — `finding_a_correction_pass_is_unreviewed_work`, and it is why a fix pass gets swept like any other work. A superlative costs one query to verify and is the single most quotable thing you write.
**Sharpest instance to remember:** the false claim sat **two lines above the table that refuted it**, inside a block whose stated purpose was honesty about risk cutting against me — and its direction of error was **alarmist, in my own risk disclosure, already escalated to Will and PROME.** Adjacency is not verification, and self-critical framing is not accuracy.

### [Registration] — Name the Instrument for the Mechanism in Your Hypothesis's OWN NAME, Before You Register It
**Pattern (2026-08-28, HEN-42 grade):** I registered a discriminator literally called **"policy-path vs TERM-PREMIUM"** and ran it for **five weeks with an instrument for only one half.** I measured the policy-path side every session (2s10s, front-end response to catalysts) and had **no instrument at all** for the term-premium side — I inferred it from curve shape. **FRED `THREEFYTP10` (Kim-Wright 10Y term premium) is free, daily, and was publishing the whole time.** On it, term premium rose **+7.01bp inside HEN-42's own 7/17→7/23 registration window = 44% of the 10Y's +16bp** — so the policy-path attribution was **overstated at registration**, measurably, on a series I could have pulled on day one. **BOND found it (8/18 sweep), not me**, and I only consumed it because PROME registered it as a DOCKET row after my desk went dark.
**Rule:** before registering a discriminator, **write down the instrument for each named alternative.** If the hypothesis's name contains a noun you cannot measure, you have a narrative, not a discriminator. Curve *shape* is a proxy for term premium; it is not a measurement of it, and the difference is exactly where the grade lives. `[[finding_hypothesis_needs_an_instrument_for_its_defining_mechanism]]`
**Corollary — grade the parenthetical too.** HEN-42's DENY clause read *"…on a term-premium impulse **(auction tail, fiscal, foreign demand)**."* At the grade, **none of the three named forms fired** — the impulse that did was duration/supply-channel, a fourth form I never listed. An illustrative parenthetical becomes a **binding clause** the moment someone grades the letter. Either enumerate exhaustively or write "e.g." and mean it.

### [Authority] — When Your Own Instrument Disagrees With a Publisher of Record, Deferring SILENTLY Propagates the Error
**Pattern (2026-08-28, WALTER `SIG-W-20260828-015`):** my `MARKET_DATA.tsv` 8/26 Brent cell took WALTER's published $86.36 over my own boot bar. **I did everything the process asks:** I pulled my own bar, found it did **not** reconcile, **wrote the disagreement into the cell**, marked my own reading **PROVISIONAL**, and cited the 8/20 precedent **by name**. WALTER's number was wrong ($87.84), and **it propagated through me anyway.**
**Rule:** ⛔ **A stronger disclaimer is NOT the fix, and reaching for one is the trap** — the disclaimer was present, the precedent was cited, the discrepancy was measured, and the deference was explicit and reasoned. There is no note strong enough to fix correctly deferring to a wrong authority. **The fix is a ROUTE, not a NOTE:** when your instrument disagrees with a publisher of record, ① carry **both** figures with **both bases named** in the cell, and ② **route the disagreement back to the publisher the SAME session.** A measured discrepancy that stays inside your own file is a caught error you chose not to spend. `[[finding_owner_of_record_means_authoritative_not_correct]]`

### [Registration] — My Letters Keep Leaving a Region Unnamed. Enumerate the OUTCOME SPACE, Not Just the Interesting Outcomes
**Pattern (2026-08-28 — THREE instances in one day, all my own authorship, which makes it a pattern and not three accidents):**
1. **HEN-41** — CONFIRM was a disjunction whose second limb (`T10YIE >2.30`) was **already satisfied at registration**. A limb that is true when you write it never tests the world.
2. **HEN-42** — DENY's clause named its mechanism in a parenthetical *(auction tail, fiscal, foreign demand)*. At the grade **none of the three fired**; the impulse that did was duration/supply-channel, **a fourth form I never listed**. An "illustrative" parenthetical becomes **binding** the moment someone grades the letter.
3. **T3** — bands read *"<0.15 ⇒ shared factor is the dollar; ≥0.45 ⇒ ~1.5 near the ceiling."* **0.15 ≤ r < 0.45 names no verdict at all**, and the first observed value (**+0.399**) landed exactly there.
**Rule:** before registering, **write down the full outcome space and check every region has a named disposition** — including *"no verdict"*, which is a legitimate and often correct one. Three questions, all cheap: **(i)** is any limb already satisfied as I write it? **(ii)** is any list I wrote as examples going to be read as exhaustive by whoever grades it? **(iii)** what does the letter say for a value BETWEEN my bands?
**Corollary — an UNNAMED region is worse than a wide one.** A wide "no verdict" band is honest and forces the sample-size conversation. An unnamed gap looks like a complete spec and **lets the reader supply their own reading**, usually the one that flatters the author. All three defects above passed every audit I ran, because a row-counting check sees a populated cell and stops.
**And the sharpest tell:** in all three, the region I failed to name is **where the actual observation landed**. ⚠️ **n=4 by end of day — T3 also never named its ESTIMAND:** *"20-sess ΔHY"* reads either as daily differences over a 20-session window **or** as the 20-session change sampled daily (overlapping by 19/20). The two readings differ by **~20× in required sample** — 38 vs ~760 sessions for the same band — so the ambiguity silently decides whether the test is runnable at all. **Ambiguity is the same defect class as omission: both let the reader supply the spec.** That is not bad luck — the interesting middle is where reality usually sits, and it is exactly the region an author skips because neither headline claim lives there.

### [Statistics] — A Detection Band and a Near-Null Band Are Not Two Outcomes of One Test
**Pattern (2026-08-28, LIQUID's T3 finding, verified independently here):** T3's two bands looked symmetric and cost **n=38** vs **n=348** — a **~9× asymmetry** at the same α and power. **You cannot demonstrate ABSENCE with the sample that detects PRESENCE.** At the registered n=20 the CI was **[−0.067, +0.722]**, which **contains both bands**, so the test could not separate its own two outcomes at *any* observed value.
**Rule:** whenever a spec has a "large ⇒ X / small ⇒ Y" pair, **price both legs separately before registering.** If the null-side leg needs a sample you will never have, it is a **dead band — dead by UNREACHABILITY**, and a row-counting audit cannot see it because the cell is populated and the threshold is sane.
**And the trap on the repair:** the obvious fix is an equivalence test (TOST) at a named margin — but **run its margin table before adopting it.** For T3, a margin that genuinely supports "the shared factor is the dollar" (≤0.20) costs **155+** sessions, while a margin reachable alongside the detection leg (0.40 → n=39) sits **0.05 from the detection threshold** — the equivalence and detection regions would nearly touch. **Computing the repair is what proved the repair wrong**; the honest answer was to go one-sided and stop claiming the null.

### [Statistics] — A Size-Dependent Penalty Must Be MEASURED ACROSS SIZES, Not at One Size and Not From Its Asymptote
**Pattern (2026-08-28, T3 overlap penalty — two desks, two errors, opposite directions, same class):** the question was how much overlapping k=20 windows sampled daily inflate the variance of a correlation estimator.
- **LIQUID** simulated at the test's actual size (n=20, k=20) and measured **VIF 3.3×, effective n≈8.8** — and nearly shipped *"HENRY's ~20× is really ~3.3×."* **Wrong: at n=20 with k=20 there are only ~40 days of span, so the windows physically cannot express their overlap. The estimator is SATURATED and the effect cannot appear at the size it was measured.**
- **I** used the textbook heuristic `effective n = n/k`, i.e. **VIF = 20**, and published **~760 sessions**. **Also wrong: that is an ASYMPTOTIC BOUND and it is never attained.** Measured VIF climbs 3.4 → 7.0 → 11.8 → 12.7 → **13.9 at n=540 and is still rising.** Truth ≈ **470 sessions**, ~1.6× below my figure and ~4× above LIQUID's.
**Rule:** when a penalty depends on sample size, **measure it at three or more sizes and look at the TREND — with MONTE-CARLO ERROR BARS on every point.** ⚠️ **n=3 by end of day, and the third instance is the subtlest:** LIQUID then refined *"still rising"* to *"plateau"* from a **700-trial** sample whose MC SE (±0.72) could not resolve the effect. At **12,000** trials the rise is real — **+1.33 ± 0.23, 5.7σ** — but small and saturating (13–14, never 20). **So the family is not "wrong sample size", it is `the measurement was taken where the effect could not appear` — too few observations, too few reps, or past the point of saturation.** ⛔ **And I do not get to bank being right: my "still rising" was asserted from a monotone-LOOKING series with no error bars. LIQUID's METHOD (never claim a direction the noise cannot carry) was better than mine even though its CONCLUSION was wrong. Getting the right answer without the entitlement to it is not a win.** A single measurement tells you the value at that size and nothing about the shape; an asymptotic formula tells you a bound you may never reach. **Both failure modes produce a confident number, and neither is detectable from inside its own calculation.**
**Corollary — the tell that saved both of us:** LIQUID caught its error by **extending the measurement** rather than extrapolating from the first read. I caught mine by **running the simulation on a correction that AGREED with me.** ⚠️ **I had just been told by the counterparty that my figure "stands unmodified." A counterparty endorsement is the weakest possible check on your own number, because it removes the only person likely to look.** `[[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]]` — the rigour must point INWARD hardest exactly when the news is good.

### [Spec] — A Pre-Commitment That Names a VALUE Instead of a RULE Becomes a Carry-Item
**Pattern (2026-08-28, caught by LIQUID):** I wrote *"+0.399 at n=20 is NO VERDICT and stays so unless n≥38 AND r≥0.45"* — intended as a guard against rationalising later. **As phrased it reads as though +0.399 is the number carried to the n=38 grade. It is not: r will have moved, and the graded number is the r on the then-current window.**
**Rule:** a pre-commitment must name **the rule and the window that will be graded**, never the currently-observed value. *"At n≥38 the grade is against the r computed on the THEN-CURRENT 38-session window; today's value is superseded, not carried forward."* **The irony is the point: a device written specifically to stop a future self from drifting had drifted into a carried assertion that never self-evaluates.** `[[finding_dated_carry_item_has_no_expiry_check]]`
