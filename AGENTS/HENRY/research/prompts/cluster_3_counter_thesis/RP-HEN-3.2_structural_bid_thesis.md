**Priority:** 8

---

## Context for Research Assistant

HENRY operates on the assumption that valuations mean-revert over time. However, a counter-thesis exists: structural demand sources (passive flows, buybacks, retirement contributions, foreign demand) may create a persistent "bid" under the market that fundamentally changes mean reversion dynamics. This research investigates whether structural flows might sustain elevated valuations indefinitely.

---

## Research Questions

### 1. PASSIVE INVESTING FLOWS

**Quantify the Passive Bid:**
- Monthly net inflows to passive equity funds (ICI data)
- Is this flow price-insensitive? (Flows regardless of valuation)
- 10-year trend in passive flows
- Projected flows based on demographic trends

**401(k) and Retirement Contributions:**
- Total annual 401(k) contributions
- What % goes to equities?
- Auto-enrollment impact on flows
- Target-date fund equity allocations by age

**Math:**
- Annual passive inflows as % of market cap
- Does this create meaningful upward pressure?

### 2. CORPORATE BUYBACKS

**Current Buyback Activity:**
- S&P 500 annual buyback volume (most recent year)
- Trend over past 10 years
- Buyback yield (buybacks / market cap)

**Net Issuance:**
- Buybacks minus new issuance (IPOs, secondaries, stock compensation)
- Is the net effect positive or negative for share supply?
- Historical comparison

**Countercyclical or Procyclical:**
- Do buybacks accelerate when prices fall (value buying)?
- Or do they pause (cash conservation)?
- 2020 example: What happened to buybacks during COVID crash?
- 2022 example: Buyback behavior during decline?

### 3. PENSION REBALANCING

**Mechanical Rebalancing Bid:**
- When equities fall, pensions rebalance by buying
- Quantify pension equity allocations (public + corporate)
- Total AUM subject to rebalancing
- Does this create a floor on declines?

**Historical Analysis:**
- Did pension rebalancing slow declines in 2008? 2020? 2022?
- Is rebalancing automatic or discretionary?

### 4. FOREIGN DEMAND

**Foreign Ownership of US Equities:**
- Current foreign holdings of US stocks (%)
- Trend over past 20 years
- Which countries are biggest holders?

**"US Exceptionalism" Premium:**
- Arguments for persistent foreign demand
- Rule of law, liquidity, growth differential
- Is foreign demand price-sensitive?

**Currency Considerations:**
- Does dollar strength attract or repel foreign buyers?
- Hedged vs unhedged foreign ownership

### 5. SUPPLY/DEMAND DYNAMICS

**Equity Supply:**
- Net equity issuance (negative due to buybacks)
- M&A taking companies private
- Is equity becoming "scarce"?

**Demand vs Supply Math:**
- Model: Annual passive demand + buybacks + foreign demand
- Model: Annual supply (IPOs, secondaries, spin-offs)
- Net supply/demand balance

### 6. HISTORICAL MEAN REVERSION ANALYSIS

**Has Mean Reversion Slowed?**
- Compare CAPE mean reversion speed:
  - 1970-2000 episodes
  - 2000-2025 episodes
- Is CAPE staying elevated longer?

**Alternative Explanation:**
- Fed intervention preventing mean reversion?
- Or structural flows changing equilibrium?

**Counter-Evidence:**
- 2000-2002: Mean reversion happened despite passive flows
- 2022: Significant decline happened despite structural bid
- Does the structural bid just delay, not prevent, corrections?

### 7. STRESS TEST THE THESIS

**What Could Break the Structural Bid?**
- Demographic shift (Boomers selling, not buying)
- Recession forcing retirement withdrawals
- Loss of confidence in US institutions
- Passive-to-active rotation

**Redemption Scenario:**
- Model: 2008-level redemption rates on current passive AUM
- Can the structural bid become structural selling?
- Liquidity under redemption stress
