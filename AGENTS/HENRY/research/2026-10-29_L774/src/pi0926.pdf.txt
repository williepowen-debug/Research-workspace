Personal Income and Outlays, September 2026 

Personal income increased $54.5 billion (0.2 percent at a monthly rate) in September, according to 
estimates released today by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). Disposable personal income 
(DPI)—personal income less personal current taxes—increased $52.7 billion (0.2 percent), and personal 
consumption expenditures (PCE) increased $113.1 billion (0.5 percent).

Personal outlays—the sum of PCE, personal interest payments, and personal current transfer 
payments—increased $113.0 billion in September. Personal saving was $911.1 billion, and the personal 
saving rate—personal saving as a percentage of DPI—was 3.8 percent. 

The increase in current-dollar personal income in September primarily reflected increases in 
compensation and government social benefits.

Changes in personal income and outlays for September 

Technical Notes 

The September increase of $54.5 billion in personal income primarily reflected increases in 
compensation and government social benefits. 

•  Within compensation, the increase was led by private wages and salaries based on U.S. Bureau 

of Labor Statistics’ (BLS) Current Employment Statistics (CES). 

•  The increase in government social benefits was led by Medicare, based on data from the 
Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services and the Budget of the U.S. Government. 

Revisions to personal income 

Estimates have been updated for July and August reflecting revised BLS CES data. The revision to 
personal interest income was led by monetary interest, based on newly available data from the U.S. 
Bureau of the Fiscal Service.
