## #1 — THE HAMILTON FRAMEWORK: WE KNOW THE EXACT TIMELINE
**🔴 SMOKING GUN**

**The finding:** A 47-log-point oil price shock triggers a mathematically predictable GDP collapse of -3.0 to -4.9 percentage points, with peak damage arriving in Q1 2027 — exactly four quarters after the Hormuz closure began in March 2026.

**Why it matters:**
James Hamilton (University of California San Diego), in peer-reviewed research published in 2003 and 2009, showed that oil price shocks destroy economic output on a reliable lag structure — not immediately, but over 3-5 quarters. His model uses "Net Oil Price Increases" (NOPI): the amount by which today's oil price exceeds the highest price of the prior three years. NOPI at 47 log points is a historically severe shock — comparable to the 1973 Arab Oil Embargo and the 1979 Iranian Revolution. The coefficient structure means current GDP drag is less than one-third of eventual damage. The market is pricing the first quarter of a four-quarter destruction sequence.

Apply this to what we know: Q4 2025 GDP is already 0.7%. Hamilton's estimated drag: -3.0pp to -4.9pp. That puts the GDP trajectory at **-2.3% to -4.2%** by peak impact. That is not a slowdown. That is a recession. The 0.7% number is the pre-shock starting line, not the floor.

**The source:** Hamilton, James D. (2003), "What Is an Oil Shock?" *Journal of Econometrics* 113(2); Hamilton (2009), "Causes and Consequences of the Oil Shock of 2007-08," *Brookings Papers on Economic Activity*. Applied via HENRY/mail/inbox/processed/SIG-HENRY-20260312-hamilton-demand-destruction-framework.md.

**Quote-ready slide sentence:**
> *"Hamilton's model says the worst GDP damage from the Hormuz shock hasn't happened yet — it arrives in Q1 2027, and the math puts us on track for -2.3% to -4.2% GDP at peak."*

## #2 — STAGFLATION CONFIRMED: BOTH MANDATES FAILING SIMULTANEOUSLY
**🔴 SMOKING GUN**

**The finding:** Q4 2025 GDP came in at +0.7% (revised down from +1.4%) while Core PCE is running at 3.0-3.1% — the first time both the Fed's growth mandate and inflation mandate have simultaneously failed this cycle.

**Why it matters:**
The Federal Reserve has two jobs: keep inflation near 2% and keep employment/growth near potential. When both fail in opposite directions — growth too low, inflation too high — the Fed is paralyzed. It cannot cut rates (that would re-ignite 3%+ inflation) and cannot hike rates (that would accelerate a 0.7% economy into contraction). This is the textbook definition of a stagflation trap. For markets, this is critical: equity P/E multiples assume the Fed will cut rates when the economy softens, providing a floor under valuations. That floor doesn't exist when inflation is simultaneously running hot. The market is pricing a scenario the Fed cannot deliver.

**The source:** BEA GDP Second Estimate Q4 2025 (released March 12, 2026); BEA Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index (Core PCE, March 13, 2026); Lighthouse Macro Prices Framework (Feb 2026) — "PCI > +0.5 = Fed constrained."

**Quote-ready slide sentence:**
> *"GDP at 0.7%, inflation at 3.1%: the Fed cannot cut to save growth without reigniting inflation it hasn't tamed — the market is pricing rate relief that structurally cannot come."*

## GLOSSARY
*For the finance-literate reader who may not track these terms daily.*

---

**NOPI (Net Oil Price Increase)**
A measure developed by economist James Hamilton that captures how much an oil price exceeds its highest level over the prior three years. The key insight is that the *direction* of an oil shock matters less than whether the price breaks new ground — consumers and businesses respond most severely to prices that exceed their prior experience. A NOPI of 47 log points means oil has breached prior highs by 47% in log terms, placing the current shock in the same category as the 1973 embargo and the 1979 Iranian Revolution.

**Stagflation**
An economic condition where inflation is rising at the same time that economic growth is stalling or contracting. The word combines "stagnation" and "inflation." It is dangerous because the standard policy tools point in opposite directions: fighting inflation requires higher interest rates (slowing growth further), while fighting stagnation requires lower rates (which rekindles inflation). The 1970s was the defining stagflation era; the U.S. is currently showing GDP at 0.7% with Core PCE at 3.1% — both mandates failing simultaneously.

**Bear Market**
A decline of 20% or more from a recent market peak, typically associated with deteriorating economic fundamentals. Bear markets during recessions are historically deeper and longer than bear markets that occur without recessions. The 1973-74 bear market, accompanied by an oil shock and stagflation, produced a 48% peak-to-trough decline over approximately 21 months.

**VIX (Volatility Index)**
Often called the "fear gauge," the VIX measures how much investors are paying to buy options that protect against S&P 500 moves. A VIX of 15 means the market expects roughly ±15% annual moves — calm conditions. A VIX above 30 signals elevated fear; above 40 signals crisis conditions. The VIX is suppressed when investors believe the Fed will cut rates to cushion any downturn — removing that belief (via the March dot plot) is the mechanical trigger for a rapid VIX repricing.

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)**
The portion of a company's profits attributed to each share of stock. When the stock market sets a price, it is implicitly making a bet about future EPS — typically expressed as a "P/E ratio" (price divided by earnings). If earnings disappoint, the stock price falls to re-establish a rational ratio. Wall Street's current 2026 consensus assumes 12-14% EPS growth for the S&P 500 — a forecast built on ~2.5-3% GDP. If GDP comes in at 0.7% and falling, EPS growth will miss significantly, forcing prices lower even without any change in investor sentiment.
