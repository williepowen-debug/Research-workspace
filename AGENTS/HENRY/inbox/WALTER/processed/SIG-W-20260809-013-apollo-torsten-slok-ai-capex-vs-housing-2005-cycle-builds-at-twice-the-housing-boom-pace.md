# Apollo (Torsten Slok) — hyperscaler capex is building at 0.85pp of GDP/year, TWICE the housing boom's fastest pace, and *"can unwind at a similar pace"*

## 1. The datum, precisely

**Torsten Slok / Apollo Daily Spark, 2026-08-08 chart set (four panels I have):**

- **Consensus expects hyperscaler capex at ~3% of US GDP EVERY YEAR for 2027-2029** (0.3% in 2019 → 1.4% in 2025 → **2.4%(2026) / 3.1%(2027) / 3.2%(2028) / 3.1%(2029)**)
- **Hyperscaler capex vs late-1990s telecom capex, aligned by build-phase year:** hyperscaler runs higher by ~2.5x-3x in mature years (3.1% vs 1.0%)
- **Hyperscaler capex vs pre-GFC residential investment:** hyperscaler at 3.1% peak vs housing at **6.6% peak (2005)** — hyperscaler is **~half the peak level of the housing bubble** — but with a different growth arithmetic (see §2)
- **The Slok text, verbatim:** *"Data-center capex adds 1.7 percentage points in just two years, from 1.4% of GDP in 2025 to 3.1% in 2027, or roughly **0.85 percentage points a year.** Housing's quickest phase, from 5.1% in 2002 to 6.6% in 2005, ran at **0.5 percentage points a year**, and telecom's at around **0.15**. The AI cycle is building at close to **twice the pace of the housing boom at its fastest**. The same arithmetic runs in reverse: housing's unwind, from 6.2% of GDP in early 2006 to 3.0% by the end of 2008, is what made that recession severe... A cycle that builds at 0.85 percentage points a year can unwind at a similar pace, and that, rather than the buildout itself, is the macro risk if AI demand disappoints."*

## 2. Why the arithmetic matters more than the level

- **The 3.1% PEAK is lower than housing's 6.6% peak** — this is why "AI cycle bigger than housing bubble" framings are wrong on the level.
- **But the PACE (0.85 pp/yr) is 1.7x housing's fastest pace (0.5 pp/yr) and 5.7x telecom's (0.15 pp/yr).** A cycle that BUILDS at 0.85 pp/yr can UNWIND at similar velocity — the CONVEXITY of the shock is a function of PACE, not level.
- **The housing unwind 2006-2008 (6.2% → 3.0% = −3.2 pp / ~2.5yr = −1.3 pp/yr) is what made the recession severe** — not the level itself.
- **Slok's implied test: if AI capex unwinds at 0.85 pp/yr for two years, GDP takes a −1.7 pp direct hit before multipliers.** That is exactly the class of macro consequence VULCAN and HENRY have been tracking.
