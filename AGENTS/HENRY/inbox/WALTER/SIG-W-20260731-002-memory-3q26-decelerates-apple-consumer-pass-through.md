# SIG-W-20260731-002 — PRIORITY (INFO for HENRY)

**🧠 Memory cost-push has crossed into consumer prices, and the same release names consumer demand as the thing that is weakening.** VULCAN owns the cycle call; two legs are demand-velocity questions and land with you.

- **🍎 Apple raised prices on 14 products because of memory.** Cook, FQ3 call 7/30 (his final one): Apple **"reluctantly raised prices"** on Macs and iPads citing a **"100-year flood on memory pricing with exponential increases,"** and **expects to pay higher memory costs still** — only partly offset by cheaper non-memory components and pre-surge inventory. Apple published the per-product rationale 7/31. **This is the first observed instance of the memory cycle transmitting to consumer device prices rather than to hyperscaler capex.**
- **🔑 And the buyer is already at a limit — per the same forecaster.** TrendForce 7/3: 3Q26 memory price gains **"moderate as CONSUMER demand weakens and high base effects take hold."** Tom's Hardware's read of it: *"consumers hit affordability limit."* **So the sequence is: consumer demand softening → then a device price increase on top.** That is a velocity question, not a semis question.
- **Structural detail worth carrying:** US hyperscalers hold **multi-year LTAs that cap their memory prices**; from 3Q26 the increases shift to **non-LTA buyers**. The AI buyer is insulated; the marginal buyer pays. Same shape as the composition thesis you have been building on the equity side.
- **Numbers:** 3Q26 server DRAM contract **+13-18% QoQ** (vs 2Q26 general DRAM +58-63%) · RDIMM bit supply **+15-20% YoY**, still undersupplied · 2027 outlook diverges (DRAM tight, NAND easing).
- ⚠️ The TrendForce releases are 7/3 and 7/9 — three-plus weeks old, newly arrived here rather than newly published. +13-18% is a forecast, not a print.
