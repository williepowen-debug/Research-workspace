### [Verification] — Validation Certifies a Tool Against GOOD Input; It Says Nothing About Its Behavior on BAD Input
**Lesson from the `gamma_flip.py` NaN failure (7/23):** On 7/17 I built the gamma estimator, validated it against two independent free trackers (flip within 20-34pts, walls exact-match), sensitivity-tested it, and wired it into `boot.py` as a standing read. Six days later it printed `Net GEX +nan · regime POSITIVE · put wall 8,100 · call wall 6,360` — with the put wall **above** the call wall, and the standalone CLI reporting **NEGATIVE** on the very same run where boot reported **POSITIVE**. Root cause was one line: `oi = r["openInterest"] or 0`. **`x or 0` does not catch NaN — NaN is truthy** — and `NaN <= 0` is False, so NaN-OI contracts sailed through the filter and poisoned the GEX sum. From there NaN silently took the *wrong branch of every comparison*: `"NEGATIVE" if g0 < 0 else "POSITIVE"` → POSITIVE, `"POSITIVE" if g0 > 0 else "NEGATIVE"` → NEGATIVE (same number, opposite labels), sign-change flip detection never fired, and `max()` over an all-NaN dict returned an arbitrary strike. **The validation on 7/17 was real and it protected nothing here**, because it exercised the tool on a healthy chain — it never asked what the tool does when yfinance returns NaN IV for most of the chain (98 usable contracts vs 6,206). **Rules:** (1) **A tool that can't get data must SAY so, not return a number** — fail loud, never fail silent (`[[finding_fail_loud_on_incomplete_data]]`). Add an explicit input-quality floor (here `MIN_CONTRACTS`), not just output plausibility. (2) **NaN is the specific poison in any numeric pipeline** — it is truthy, and every comparison against it is False, so it doesn't crash, it *quietly picks a branch*. Guard it explicitly; `or 0` / truthiness checks do not. (3) **Two surfaces reporting opposite labels off one number is a data bug until proven otherwise** — that contradiction was the cheapest possible tell and I should treat it as a first-class alarm, not a curiosity. (4) When a validated tool degrades, **do not fall back to its last good output** — the 7/17 flip (~7,522) was 6 days and −50 SPX points stale, and reusing it is precisely the stale-point-value trap that inverted the HY axis in June ([[finding_ledger_drift_behind_narrative]]). Report the gap instead. Cf. the 7/17 lesson directly above (a proxy isn't ground truth) — this is its sequel: *a validated proxy isn't a permanently validated proxy.*

So **July gasoline CPI can print NEGATIVE MoM with the pump at $4.09 and climbing** — the "$4 gas is back" headline is a *base-effect illusion* for the 8/13 release, and the real passthrough test is **August CPI (~9/10)**, where both $100 Brent and a >$4 pump sit inside the full monthly average. **Rule: when a proxy resolves on a LEVEL but the prediction resolves on a RATE OF CHANGE, re-derive the rate before transferring the verdict — and always check what the comparison base was DOING, not just where it ended.** A base that was falling all through the prior month manufactures a soft print out of a rising series. ### [Verification] — Name a Root Cause by INSTRUMENTING It, Not by Picking the Plausible One
**Lesson from the gamma-feed misdiagnosis (7/23):** When `gamma_flip.py` returned only 98 usable contracts (vs 6,206 on 7/17), I wrote — and **committed and pushed** — "yfinance's ^SPX IV feed is degraded; most rows carry NaN IV." It was plausible: I'd just fixed a NaN bug, so NaN was salient, and an IV outage would explain thinness. **It was wrong.** When Will asked me to retry, I finally counted the rejections with a five-line `Counter()`: of **7,514** raw rows, **7,278 were dropped for `openInterest == 0`** — only 32 for out-of-band IV. **Volume and IV were fully populated; the `openInterest` field alone was empty** (max OI of *3* on the front expiry). The misdiagnosis would have had me retrying a broken pull indefinitely; the correct diagnosis pointed straight at CBOE, which had real OI and produced the read in one attempt. **Rules:** (1) **A root cause is a measurement, not an inference.** Before naming one — especially in a commit — run the count that distinguishes the candidates. Here that was one `Counter()` over rejection reasons, cheaper than the sentence I wrote instead. (2) **Beware the salient cause:** having just debugged NaN, I saw NaN everywhere. The bug I most recently fixed is the least trustworthy hypothesis for the next symptom. (3) **Distinguish "the data is bad" from "this field is bad"** — the second is usually routable to another vendor, and field-level outages are common while whole-feed outages are rare. (4) When a diagnosis is already shipped, **correct it in the same surfaces that carried it**, and say plainly that the earlier claim was wrong. Cf. `[[finding_fail_loud_on_incomplete_data]]`, `[[finding_verify_runtime_context_before_tool_broken]]`.

### [Verification] — A Cross-Source Number Can Carry a SIGN but Not a DELTA; and Never Publish an Arg-Max Without Checking for a Near-Tie
**Lesson from the gamma validation (7/23):** After recovering the gamma read from a new source (CBOE, after yfinance's OI field died), I published two claims that a 6-tracker validation then killed. **(1) "Negative gamma DOUBLED since 7/17" (−$25.7B → −$45.2B).** I *had* flagged the flip *level* comparison as cross-source — then turned around and made a **magnitude** claim across the very same source change. A different vendor's OI (possibly already partly degraded on the old one) changes the level of every derived dollar figure; the sign survives a source change, a *delta* does not. The clean like-for-like came from a source that publishes a **daily series** (Modigin's flip ladder + ZeroGEX 7/17 vs 7/23), and it told a different story: **the flip had been pinned in a ~7,473-7,516 band for two weeks — spot fell away from a stationary flip, rather than the flip migrating toward spot.** Same directional conclusion, different mechanism, and the mechanism is what a reader would model off. **(2) "SPX is through BOTH walls."** My put wall printed 7,500 — the same strike as the call wall, which I *noticed was odd and did not chase*. It was an arg-max over a near-tie: gamma-weighted 7,500 (117.6) vs 7,300 (114.3), a 3% gap `max()` broke arbitrarily; by raw OI the ordering inverts entirely (7,000 ≫ 7,300 > 7,500). All six trackers read 7,300-7,400. So SPX was sitting *just above* put support, not through it — the opposite of the "bounded only by circuit breakers" framing I attached. **Rules:** (1) **A source change resets your baseline.** Sign and level survive it; *changes* measured across it do not. Before claiming a delta, find a source with a consistent history — a vendor publishing a daily series beats two of your own pulls from different vendors. (2) **`max()` over near-ties is a coin flip wearing a decimal point.** Any published arg-max (wall, peak strike, top contributor) needs a runners-up check; if #1 and #2 are within a few percent, report the band, not the winner. (3) **When a result looks structurally weird — a put wall equal to the call wall — that is the cheapest available tell. Chase it immediately.** I flagged it in passing and moved on; the validation had to find it for me. (4) **Carry the dissent with its date:** SpotGamma read *positive* gamma to 7,300 — reconcilable as a pre-selloff morning note, but "corroborated 5-of-6 with one dated dissent" is the honest phrasing, not "corroborated."

### [Data] — A Threshold Read Off CLOSES ONLY Hides the Range That Actually Tests It
**Lesson from the VIX reconciliation (7/23):** For a month I asserted — in STATUS, the threshold table, the thesis verdict and to Will — that *"VIX has never neared 23; cascade step 1 has never engaged,"* citing 19.49 (6/23), 18.77 (7/17), 18.70 (7/23). Every one of those is a **close**. Pulling the daily OHLC because VIOLET's midday figure disagreed with mine: **VIX 7/23 printed a high of 20.31 — the highest of the entire episode, above the 19.49 I'd been calling the ceiling.** My "never neared" was a close-only artifact; the tape had been reaching higher than my own threshold table showed, and I'd never checked because my boot pulls last/close. **The intraday data also carried the more interesting signal in the other direction:** VIX tagged 20.31 and **closed 18.70**, giving back 1.6pts into the bell on a −1.2% SPX day with dealers short gamma — vol *sold* into the spike, a **failed breakout**, not a boundary about to break. Both facts are load-bearing and neither is visible in a close. **Rules:** (1) **When a level is a trigger, track the range, not the settle** — "did it touch" and "did it hold" are different questions and you usually need both.
