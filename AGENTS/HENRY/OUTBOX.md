## Synthesis Gaps / Things I'd Add

1. **Immigration enforcement as labor supply shock is UNDERWEIGHTED.** Five districts flagged it. Minneapolis: 43% English class enrollment decline, "employees, vendors, customers afraid to travel." This is a dual supply+demand destruction that standard models don't capture. It's deflationary for demand but inflationary for labor costs in affected sectors (construction, ag, hospitality). Net effect: stagflationary.

2. **Private credit expanding to fill bank gaps (San Francisco)** — this is explicitly the bank→shadow transmission channel we've been tracking. The synthesis flags it but doesn't connect it hard enough to APO/shadow credit thesis. When banks tighten and shadow lenders backfill at worse terms, you get the exact credit quality deterioration that blows up later.

3. **Healthcare deferral is a leading indicator the synthesis correctly flags but undersells.** Richmond (dental patients not following through), St. Louis (elective treatment delays), Dallas (ACA subsidy uncertainty freezing spending). When consumers defer healthcare, they're past "trading down" — they're rationing essentials. This is the stage before delinquency acceleration.

4. **The "fear-based retention" finding (Dallas) is novel.** Workers staying in jobs they'd leave because the next job would be worse. This is labor market *freeze*, not *health*. It means when it does crack, it cracks fast — pent-up separations release simultaneously.
