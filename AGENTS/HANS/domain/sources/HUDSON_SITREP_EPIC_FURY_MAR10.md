# Hudson Institute — Operation Epic Fury Situation Report
# Battlefield Effects and Early Strategic Signals
**Author:** Can Kasapoğlu, Senior Fellow, Hudson Institute
**Date:** March 10, 2026 (Day 10 of operations)

## Battlefield Assessment

The United States began Operation Epic Fury alongside Israel's Operation Roaring Lion on February 28. By the end of the first week, the two allies had systematically dismantled the Islamic Republic's military infrastructure and put serious pressure on Iran's long-range strike salvos. Nonetheless, the IRGC is tightening its grip on the country's political system. Gulf Arab states—and global energy markets—remain under growing stress. A systematic Iranian campaign targeting the region's desalination plants looms as the most dangerous wildcard.

Over those first seven days alone, the US military reportedly struck more than 3,000 targets across Iran. As of the tenth day of the campaign, American forces have engaged some 5,000 targets, marking a hike in operational tempo. The US military has also hit more than 50 warships overall.

Washington's target set prioritized the elimination of the country's missile launch capabilities and military facilities, as well as the command nodes tied to the Revolutionary Guards' strategic weapons.

In parallel, the Israeli Air Force concentrated on degrading the Islamic Republic's air deterrent. Israeli strikes targeted 10 of Iran's 18 air bases, and cratered runways, destroyed hardened aircraft shelters, and reportedly damaged or eliminated several heavy-lift and combat aircraft.

The US military has reportedly shifted from a reliance on long-range, standoff weapons to stand-in precision-strike methods against Iranian targets. Rather than employing expensive cruise missiles and other standoff munitions with high price tags, American aircraft are now increasingly using JDAMs. This transition reflects two dynamics: (1) earlier phases heavily degraded Iran's air defense network, allowing closer operations, and (2) cost considerations — first 100 hours cost $3.7 billion.

Israel's campaign increasingly targeted the regime's security apparatus. In Isfahan, IDF strikes hit Basij HQ, a regional corps command, an IRGC base, and a police command facility. Israeli forces also targeted missile launch sites and rocket-engine production facilities in Parchin and Shahrud.

The joint US-Israeli campaign began striking elements of Iran's oil infrastructure, including several fuel storage facilities and depots — widening target sets to impose economic pressure.

Mehrabad Airport struck — satellite imagery shows 17+ aircraft damaged, several destroyed. IRGC Quds Force allegedly used these aircraft to transport weapons to Hezbollah and proxy militias.

## Iranian Campaign

Iran continued missile and drone salvos at declining tempo. As of March 9: 174 waves of strikes against Israel since Feb 28. More than half targeted Tel Aviv metropolitan area. Strike tempo dropped roughly 4x between Feb 28 and Mar 9.

IRGC employing "shotgun-type" warhead configurations — Khorramshahr family missiles (North Korean Musudan lineage) with multiple sub-munitions to stress missile defenses.

### UAE Targeting
- 1,440 drones, 8 cruise missiles, 253 ballistic missiles as of Mar 9
- UAE claims 90% interception rate
- AH-64E Apache gunships engaging Shahed drones (Ukraine-style counter-drone ops)

### THAAD Radar Damage
AN/TPY-2 radar units in UAE show visible structural damage and scorched equipment. Whether destroyed or damaged remains unclear. These are X-band high-resolution tracking sensors critical for distinguishing warheads from decoys.

Russia reportedly providing "critical data transfers regarding American force deployments" to Iran. Iran's own C2 monitoring capability degraded by US/Israeli strikes — Moscow filling the gap.

### Regional Strikes

- UAE and Bahrain high-value sites
- General Jabari: regional energy sites now explicit targets, within operational range

## Mojtaba Khamenei Succession

Election was "a hasty decision that bypassed deliberation among many Iranian elites." IRGC, MOD, Defense Council, IRGC Quds Force all pledged allegiance. Pezeshkian also pledged loyalty.

Mojtaba closely aligned with IRGC and radical factions. Several immediate family members killed in strikes. "In political systems shaped by patronage circles and personal loyalties, vendettas can reinforce extremist instincts rather than political moderation."

Assessment: Iran may end up a military dictatorship when current operations cease.

## Energy Markets

Lloyd's List Intelligence: NO tankers >10,000 DWT entered Gulf westbound since March 3. Oil briefly $119/bbl on Mar 9 (historical peak ~$147 in 2008).

Pipeline bypass capacity: Saudi east-west 5M bpd + UAE Gulf of Oman 1.5-2M bpd = ~7M bpd max vs 20M bpd normal Hormuz flow.

## Water Security — The Hydro-Strategic Dimension

- 90% of Kuwait's drinking water from desalination
- 86% of Oman's
- ~70% of Saudi Arabia's
- GCC has ~50% of world's desalination capacity
- 90%+ of output from a few dozen major coastal facilities
- Facilities exposed, hard to defend, concentrated

Mar 8: Iranian drone struck Bahrain desalination plant. Bahrain Interior Ministry accused Tehran of attacking civilian facilities.

"In a prolonged conflict, water infrastructure, not oil, may emerge as the region's most strategically sensitive resource."

Disruption could halt production entirely. Restoration: months. Full reconstruction: years.

## NATO Dimension

Second Iranian ballistic missile launched at Turkey — intercepted by NATO-integrated air/missile defense. Tests alliance southeast flank. Risk of conflict expanding beyond CENTCOM to Euro-Atlantic security space.
