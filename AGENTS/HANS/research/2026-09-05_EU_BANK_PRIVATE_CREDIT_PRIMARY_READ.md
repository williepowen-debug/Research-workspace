## 1. THE HEADLINE FINDING IS NOT THE NUMBER — IT IS THAT THERE ARE THREE NUMBERS

The number I would have carried from secondary sources is **not** the ECB's number, and neither is the number the banks themselves disclose. All three are live in circulation right now on nominally the same subject:

| # | Figure | Perimeter | Denominator | Publisher · vintage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **€62.5bn drawn** | **12 euro-area banks**, exposures **worldwide** | **0.2% of total assets · 2.5% of total equity** | **ECB supervisory data**, FSR May-2026 (2024 data) — ✅ **PRIMARY** |
| **B** | **€108bn drawn** + **€11bn undrawn at 2 banks** | **7 largest _European_ banks** (names DB, BNPP, Crédit Agricole) | **~2.0% of total customer loans** | **S&P Global Ratings**, May 2026 |
| **C** | **€122.1bn disclosed** | **33 European banks**, as of **2026-03-31** | none given | **Bank regulatory disclosures**, via Risk.net |

**A → C is a 1.95× spread on the same subject.** ⚠️ **None of the three is wrong. They are not the same measurement**, and every structural check on each one passes `[[finding_cross_entity_comparison_needs_same_perimeter]]`:

1. **Perimeter:** "euro area" (A) ≠ "European" (B, C). B and C include UK/Swiss G-SIBs — Barclays, HSBC, UBS — which are outside the ECB's SSM. This alone plausibly carries most of the A→B gap.
2. **Denominator:** **0.2% of total assets** (A) and **2.0% of total customer loans** (B) are a **10× difference in the ratio with no disagreement about the numerator** — customer loans are a fraction of total assets. **Quoting either percentage without its denominator produces a wrong impression of scale in opposite directions** `[[finding_output_shape_implies_more_than_the_measurement]]`.
3. **Vintage:** A rests on **2024** supervisory data; C is **Q1-2026**. An 18-month gap in the fastest-growing credit segment in Europe.
4. **Construction:** A is **supervisory** (mandatory, consistent, confidential-source); C is **voluntary disclosure** — and Risk.net says so explicitly: *"inconsistent disclosures mean the figure offers only a partial view."*

**⇒ OPERATING RULE FOR THIS DESK: `HANS-T-14` and any onward routing cite figure A with its perimeter and denominator attached — "€62.5bn across 12 euro-area banks, 0.2% of total assets [ECB FSR May-2026, 2024 data]" — never a bare euro number.** A bare "€108bn of European bank private-credit exposure" is the sentence that travels and it is unreadable without its perimeter.

## 3. ⚠️ ONE PART OF MY OWN 8/28 POINTER DOES NOT SURVIVE THE PRIMARY

On 8/28 I passed BROCK an **"arranger/warehouse structural finding"** under a pointer label. The primary names **three** bank→private-credit lending channels, and they are not the arranger/warehouse framing I relayed:

> *"Banks interact with the private credit ecosystem by lending to three different sets of players: (i) private credit **investors**, (ii) private credit **funds**, and (iii) **portfolio companies** (directly)."*

**Channel (i) — lending to private-credit *investors* — is the leverage-on-leverage channel and it is the one I under-weighted.** It is not warehouse financing; it is banks financing the LPs. That is the layered-leverage structure the ECB says its monitoring exercise **struggled to capture**, and it is the channel most likely to transmit a private-credit drawdown into a bank balance sheet through a route no exposure table shows.

**⇒ To BROCK: my 8/28 pointer was directionally fine and structurally incomplete. Use the ECB's three-channel taxonomy, not my two-channel relay** `[[finding_rederived_signal_loses_the_senders_caveats]]`.

## 4. THE ECB'S OWN CONCLUSION, AND WHAT IT DOES *NOT* SAY

> *"Private credit in isolation is unlikely to threaten financial stability in the euro area at present."*

**Read it precisely — three qualifiers are doing work:** *in isolation* (not jointly with an equity/HY drawdown), *at present* (2024 data), *euro area* (not the UK/Swiss G-SIBs where REGINALD located the cross-border counterparty risk). The article still flags **spillovers to broader markets through valuation losses in equities and high-yield bonds**, and an **exploratory scenario analysis revealed significant variation in banks' risk profiles** — i.e. the sector aggregate is benign and the dispersion is not `[[finding_cohort_too_small_to_move_the_index]]`.

**This is consistent with, and does not upgrade, my standing 🟡 QUIET call on the European bank-contagion channel** (DB record Q2 €1.9B, sector doubled in 2yr, no CDS stress). **`HANS-T-14` does not fire**: no named-entity distress event, and the ECB/ESRB warning leg requires a systemic warning **naming specific institutions** — this article names none.
