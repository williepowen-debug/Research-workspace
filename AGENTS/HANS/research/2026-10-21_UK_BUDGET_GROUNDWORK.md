# UK Budget, Wed 2026-10-28 — groundwork for the gilt / LDI read

**HANS · hans-1021 · written one week before.** Feeds `HANS-T-06` (10Y >5.50), `HANS-T-13` (30Y >6.00), `HANS-T-17` (UK core CPI), `FLOW-HANS-5`, KB-HANS-135–139.
**Chancellor: John Healey** (Rachel Reeves's successor; several sources: Grant Thornton, Britbrief, EY "the Chancellor will inherit"). Fiscal rules are still Reeves's: the **stability rule** (current budget balance in 2029-30) and the **investment rule** (public sector net financial liabilities falling as a share of GDP). The OBR now tests them **only at Budgets**.

## Answer first

1. **The arithmetic: headroom has roughly halved since March, and the Budget's job is to rebuild it.**
   - The OBR's March margin was **£23.6bn**. Private estimates now put it at **~£11–12bn** (EY, Grant Thornton). The IFS says up to ~£20bn on plausible inflation assumptions, and that earnings judgements alone swing it ±£10bn.
   - The drivers are higher gilt yields, a quarter of gilts being index-linked into 3.5% CPI, and the energy shock.
   - The consensus expects **~£5–14bn of targeted tax rises** (bank surcharge, capital gains tax, National Insurance on landlords and partnerships), with headline income tax, NI and VAT rates untouched.
2. **Supply into the event is light, and the Bank of England is out of the market.**
   - APF gilt auctions are **paused until ~April 2027**, and the longest-dated £120bn is held to maturity.
   - The DMO's 2026-27 long conventional issuance is only **~£22.4bn of £246.2bn (~9%)**.
   - Borrowing is running **£6.0bn above the OBR's path year-to-date**, so expect an **upward NFR revision** on Budget day. **The maturity mix of any extra issuance is the line that matters for the 30Y.**
3. **The tail is credibility, not mechanics.**
   - LDI funds are now sized for **≥250bp** yield shocks (FPC 2023), against **+140bp in three days** in 2022.
   - `T-13`'s 6.00% is a **watch level**, ~20bp above today. **It is not an LDI break point.**
   - What would move the long end is a Budget that **loosens the rules or leaves headroom thin**, not the size of the tax take.

## 3. Gilt supply and the Bank of England

**DMO 2026-27 remit, revised 2026-04-23 (PRIMARY PDF, read via local extraction):**
- **NFR £251.2bn** (−£5.9bn vs the March Spring Forecast). **Gross gilt sales £246.2bn** (incl. £12.0bn green). T-bills contribute a net £5.0bn.
- **Auctions £174.4bn (70.8%)** in 53 auctions: short £95.0bn (20) · medium £56.0bn (15) · **long conventional £7.4bn (5)** · index-linked £16.0bn (13).
- **Syndications ~£42.0bn (17.1%)**: medium £20.0bn (2) · **long conventional £15.0bn (3)** · index-linked £7.0bn (2).
- **Unallocated £29.8bn (12.1%).**
- ⇒ **Long conventional ≈ £22.4bn ≈ 9.1% of sales.** The press's "~9%" agrees. ⚠️ A search summary misread the PDF table as "£15.0bn long auctions". **The primary governs.**
- **Budget-day risk:** with borrowing £6.0bn over the OBR path, the remit is likely revised **up** on 10/28. Whether the extra goes to bills, shorts or the unallocated pot (benign) or to longs (adverse for `T-13`) is **the line to read first.**

**Bank of England APF (market notice 2026-09-17 — PRIMARY):**
- **Multi-year unwind:** sales at an "annualised pace of £20 billion a year" alongside maturities, "concluding around … 2034".
- **Held to maturity:** "£222 billion of gilts maturing before 2035", plus "£120 billion of the longest-dated gilts … held to maturity" to back banknotes.
- **"Bank APF auctions will pause in the meantime"** while the Bank and HMT/DMO consider selling gilts **to the Government**. Details "by April 2027".
- ⇒ **No BoE selling into the Budget, and none at the long end at all.** Ramsden reportedly put the announcement-day fall in the 30Y at ~10bp (secondary).
- **Bank Rate 3.75%** (held 9/17). **Next MPC 11/05, a 25bp hike priced** (secondary) after the Sept CPI print of 3.5%, core 2.8%, services 3.7% (`KB-HANS-110`).

| Today | UK 30Y **5.7970 [TE 10/21]**; 10Y **5.3882 [TE 10/21]** | own pull |

**Read:**
- On the FPC's own sizing, a 2022-style shock (+140bp) is now **inside** the buffer. Exhausting it would take a further **~250bp beyond the level at which buffers were last rebuilt**.
- **`T-13` (>6.00) therefore measures a long end under pressure, not an LDI accident.** That is the right job for an orange line, but it must never be relayed as "LDI is about to break".
- **The 2026 stress channel has moved:** the FSR names **hedge-fund leverage in gilt basis and repo trades** (deleveraging that "amplified" moves). LDI is no longer the named one. Neither has a free instrument on this desk.

## 6. Transmission to the US

- **Gilt–Treasury co-movement:** UK 10Y minus US 10Y is **24.2bp** (5.3882 − 5.146, 10/21), re-widened from 9.9 [10/02]. A Budget credibility shock in the UK has, in past episodes, spilled into global term premium (Sept 2022). With US 10Y at 5.15% and US fiscal supply heavy, **a scenario C would be a US long-end catalyst, not just a UK one.** → BOND (time-critical owner).
- **UK banks:** a bank-surcharge rise is an equity and earnings item for Lloyds/NatWest/Barclays. **It is not a funding or credit event.**
- **TIC:** UK US-Treasury holdings reached **$1,009.5bn** (August, TIC Table 5 — PRIMARY), the largest European holder. A gilt shock that forces UK sellers would show up in September/October TIC, released ~mid-Nov and ~mid-Dec.

## 7. Gaps

1. **The OBR ready-reckoner sensitivity** (debt interest per 100bp on gilts) was **not read at the OBR**. Every "gilts ate £Xbn" figure here is a private estimate.
2. **The FPC's 250bp is the 2023 recommendation.** Current buffer levels across LDI funds are not published. The July 2026 FSR was only partly read: 100k of 850k characters.
3. **Hedge-fund gilt basis/repo exposure** (the FSR's named channel) is **uninstrumented.**
4. **UK 30Y is still a manual row** (no IADB 30Y). On Budget day, grade on the TE close, with a CNBC cross-check (registered basis).
