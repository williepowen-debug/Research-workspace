## 2) TTF / European Gas Price — Seasonal Relief vs Structural Punitive

**Current:** Trading Economics reports EU Gas/TTF CFD **€42.49/MWh on Jun22**, +0.93% on the day, **-6.81% MoM**, **+4.58% YoY**, with the all-time high **€345/MWh** in 2022. Earlier Jun data cited TTF one-month futures peaking around **€49.9/MWh on Jun10**.

**Threshold read:**
- Below HANS alert threshold **>€50/MWh**.
- Far below old March crisis levels / 2022 panic.
- Still high enough to keep German chemical/metals/energy-intensive industry under pressure.

**Classification:** **manageable seasonal relief, structurally punitive**. Price has not fired the outbox threshold, but it is not cheap energy normalization.

## 3) EU-China / EU-U.S. Energy-Cost Wedge

IEA Electricity 2026: EU electricity prices for energy-intensive industries in 2025 stayed elevated, **averaging over twice U.S. levels and nearly 50% above China**, similar to 2024. Average EU wholesale electricity price in 2025 was about **USD 95/MWh**, roughly **twice U.S.** levels and higher than India/Australia/Japan comparisons in the IEA set.

**Read:** The competitiveness/deindustrialization concern remains valid. The energy channel is less about immediate household winter panic and more about persistent cost disadvantage: Germany/EU can muddle through physically while still losing industrial margin/capex share to the U.S. and China.

## 4) Hormuz Risk — Does It Re-Arm March Energy-Stagflation?

**Current HANS baseline:** signed-but-fraying MOU; Jun20 Hormuz re-closure declaration is official/declaratory/contested, not physically confirmed; broad cascade unconfirmed.

**What current sources support:**
- Trading Economics explicitly ties Jun22 gas price action to signs of US-Iran talks / communication channels to keep Hormuz open, while noting market concern about storage refill.
- Search/news snippets are contradictory: some claim continued vessel transits; others claim closure. This is exactly the “contested, not confirmed” regime.
- EIA’s Apr28 note is valuable for mechanism: prior Hormuz closure affected >10 Bcf/d / ~20% of global LNG, mostly Qatari volumes, and drove TTF/JKM higher while U.S. Henry Hub was constrained by LNG export capacity. But it is **April mechanism evidence**, not proof of a live June physical closure.

**Read:** Hormuz risk re-arms HANS only as **tail risk** unless/until verified physical disruption appears in vessel traffic, Qatar LNG force majeure, TTF/JKM spike, Brent/war-risk insurance, or LNG cargo cancellation data.
