## Executive Summary

European diesel markets have undergone a structural transformation since 2022 — but the story is not over. While the EU's February 2023 ban on direct Russian diesel imports successfully eliminated ~96% of that flow, **three new pressure vectors are converging in 2026** to create the next shock: (1) sustained Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries, (2) EU 18th sanctions package closing the India/Turkey loophole effective Jan 21 2026, and (3) cumulative deindustrialization in German/European manufacturing that makes each additional cost shock more dangerous than the last.

**Bottom line for HANS thesis:** A 20-30% further decline in Russian diesel exports would add €5-8/bbl to European diesel crack spreads, raise German truck/logistics costs ~6-8%, and act as a **material accelerant to the deindustrialization feedback loop** already underway. This is not a new crisis — it is a ratchet tightening further on an already-broken industrial base.

**US Market Implication:** Medium-term bearish for German/European equities, German IP → US ISM correlation means further deterioration in US industrial sector. Longer-term bullish for US energy (XLE), logistics (diesel crack), and potentially inflation (PPI input costs).

## 1. European Diesel Supply: Pre-Sanctions vs. Now

### Historical Russian Dominance

| Period | Russian Diesel to EU | EU Import Share | Context |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Pre-2022 (2021)** | ~1.0 million bpd (peak Apr 2021) | **~40% of all EU diesel imports** | Cheap, reliable, geographically convenient |
| **2022 (post-invasion, pre-ban)** | ~630,000-770,000 bpd | **~50% of EU diesel imports** | Did NOT drop after invasion; EU kept buying |
| **Feb 5, 2023 (EU ban)** | -- | -- | EU embargo on Russian petroleum products began |
| **Post-ban (Feb-May 2023)** | **~24,000 bpd** | **<1%** | **96% collapse** |
| **2023 redirected** | ~1.0 million bpd (globally) | 5% to EU (excl. Turkey) | Redirected to Turkey (315k bpd), Brazil (136k bpd), Saudi Arabia (61k bpd), Africa |
| **2024-2025** | Variable; ~900k-1.0M bpd (total global) | ~5% to EU (via loopholes) | India/Turkey refineries process Russian crude, re-export to EU |
| **Jan 21, 2026** | India/Turkey loophole closing | Up to 160,000 bpd at risk | EU 18th sanctions package takes effect |

**Key insight:** Europe successfully weaned itself off *direct* Russian diesel, but created indirect exposure via India and Turkey re-processing Russian crude. That loophole is now closing.

### How Europe Replaced Russian Diesel (Post-Ban)

Europe now imports ~850,000 bpd from outside the region. Supplier breakdown (2024):

| Supplier | Volume (bpd) | Share | Vulnerability |
|----------|-------------|-------|--------------|
| **Middle East** (Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, UAE) | ~340,000 | 40% | Geopolitical risk, Al Zour outage (Kuwait) |
| **United States** (Gulf Coast) | ~150,000 | 18% | Weather, domestic demand competition |
| **India** (Reliance Jamnagar) | ~120,000 | 14% | **Russian crude feedstock** — sanctions risk |
| **Turkey** | ~40,000 | 5% | **Russian crude feedstock** — sanctions risk |
| **Other** | ~200,000 | 23% | Various |

**The 2026 problem:** The India + Turkey volumes (~160,000 bpd) are at risk from EU 18th sanctions package (import ban on products derived from Russian crude, effective Jan 21, 2026). Jamnagar sources **~1/3 of its crude from Russia**. Three Turkish refineries source ~**50% of crude from Russia**.

## 2. Russian Refinery Strikes: Current Impact

### Drone Campaign Timeline

| Period | Event | Diesel Export Impact |
|--------|-------|---------------------|
| **Jan 2024** | First major refinery strikes (Ryazan — 13.1 Mtpa, ~5% national capacity) | Refinery runs begin declining |
| **Q1 2024** | IEA estimates 600,000 bpd Russian refining capacity offline (10-14% of total) | Russian domestic fuel prices spike |
| **H1 2025** | Sustained campaign; >20 refineries hit | Russian diesel exports: ~4.0 million mt/month avg |
| **Aug-Sep 2025** | Intensified: Volgograd hit 4x, Novokuibyshev 3x, Ryazan/Saratov/Salavat 2x each | Russian diesel exports collapsed to **<3.0 million mt** (Sep-Oct) |
| **September 2025** | Russian diesel exports fell to **5-year low of ~586,000 bpd** | European ARA diesel crack: $46/bbl (2-year high) |
| **November 2025** | Record 14 drone strikes in one month; 16 refineries attacked Aug-Sep | ~20% national capacity offline at various points |
| **Dec 2025** | Refineries repair faster than expected | Exports recover to ~900,000 bpd |
| **Jan 2026** | Russia's diesel output at 1.8 million b/d (highest since Jan 2025) | ARA crack eases to ~$21/bbl |

**Carnegie Endowment Assessment (Oct 2025):** Ukraine has attacked 16 refineries with 123 million t/year capacity (38% of Russia's 327 Mt/year total capacity). But actual production only fell ~10% (gasoline) due to: spare capacity, partial damage, rapid repairs. The real headline is not the peak damage but the **cumulative attrition** — "a man being repeatedly punched."

**OPIS Assessment (Nov 2025):** Russian diesel exports down **30-50%** at peak from H1 2025 levels. Buyers shunning available supply due to sanctions contamination concerns, even beyond the physical shortage.

### Russia's Resilience (The Complicating Factor)

Russia has demonstrated capacity to repair damaged refineries faster than expected:
- Volgograd repaired within 11 days of August 2025 attacks
- Refinery runs recovered from 5.0 million b/d (Sep) to 5.5 million b/d (Dec)
- Primorsk loading program: 2.2 million mt in January 2026 (+27% MoM), highest loading level ever recorded
- Russia imposed export ban on diesel (Sep 2025, extended through Mar 2026) for non-producing companies — managing domestic supply priority

**Net assessment:** Drone strikes create acute shortages (spikes) but not permanent reduction. However, they have fundamentally altered the risk premium embedded in Russian diesel — **buyers are sanctioning-away available Russian supply even when physical supply exists.**

### Scenario Analysis: 20-30% Further Decline in Russian Diesel Exports

**Starting point:** Russian global diesel exports ~900,000 bpd (Jan 2026 recovered level)  
**20% decline scenario:** ~720,000 bpd (−180,000 bpd)  
**30% decline scenario:** ~630,000 bpd (−270,000 bpd)

This volume must be replaced from the Middle East and US — already primary suppliers to Europe. The impact:

| Scenario | ARA Crack Impact | European Pump Price Impact | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------------|-----------|
| **Base (status quo)** | $21-22/bbl | €1.536/L EU avg | Current: India/Turkey loophole closing offsetting refinery recovery |
| **−20% Russian exports** | $30-35/bbl | **€1.70-1.80/L** (+10-15%) | Global pool tightens; Middle East/US must fill gap, commanding premium |
| **−30% Russian exports** | $38-46/bbl | **€1.90-2.05/L** (+25-35%) | Return to Nov 2025 crisis conditions; European industrial squeeze |

**Note:** The EU Jan 21, 2026 sanctions alone (~160,000 bpd from India/Turkey) are already implementing the equivalent of a ~20% further tightening in import availability. If combined with renewed Ukrainian strike success or Middle East disruption (Al Zour already offline), +30% crack spike is well within range.

## 4. Which European Industries Are Most Diesel-Dependent?

### Diesel Consumption Breakdown (Europe)

| Sector | Diesel Share of Energy | Diesel Dependence | Substitutability |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Road Freight/Transport** | ~95% of fuel use | **EXTREME** — €1.536/L × huge volumes | EV trucks 2-3 years from scale; biofuels limited |
| **Agriculture** | ~80% of machinery fuel | **HIGH** — tractors, combines, pumps | Very low; no practical alternative for heavy equipment |
| **Construction** | ~90% of machinery fuel | **HIGH** — cranes, excavators, generators | Very low |
| **Marine (coastal/inland)** | 100% (gasoil/HFO) | **HIGH** — Rhine/Elbe shipping | None at scale |
| **Manufacturing logistics** | ~80% of internal transport | **HIGH** — forklifts, delivery, plant vehicles | Partial (electric forklifts growing) |
| **Power generation (backup)** | 100% diesel generators | **MODERATE** — emergency use | Grid provides primary; diesel = backup |

**Road freight context:** Fuel represents **25-30% of operating costs** for European truck operators. A 25% diesel price spike → ~7% total operating cost increase → typically passed through with 2-4 month lag to manufacturers.

**German road freight:** 3 billion tonnes transported annually; Germany is 25.4% of EU road freight market. Polish carriers (closely linked to German economy) are also severely impacted — Polish trucks are the largest European carrier segment.

## 6. Does This Accelerate the Deindustrialization Thesis?

### Germany's Industrial Position: Already Critical

| Metric | Data | Status |
|--------|------|--------|
| German Manufacturing PMI (Dec 2025) | **47.0** (below 50 = contraction) | 🔴 Deepening downturn |
| German GDP growth (2025) | Modest, uneven; zero growth Q3 2025 | 🟠 No recovery |
| Manufacturing jobs lost (2024 alone) | **~120,000** | 🔴 Accelerating |
| German firms considering relocation | **51% of large companies** (DIHK 2024 survey, 500+ employees) | 🔴 Crisis level |
| Manufacturing output since 2017 | **Declining trend** | 🔴 Structural, not cyclical |
| Energy costs (German vs. global) | **3x pre-crisis levels** for gas; diesel 15.7% above EU avg | 🔴 Severe competitiveness gap |

**The DIHK 2024 survey finding is alarming:** 51% of companies with 500+ employees either considering or already moving production abroad — up 8% from 2023. Energy costs cited as primary driver. This is **self-reinforcing**: as large companies leave, the ecosystem (suppliers, workers, infrastructure) degrades, making it harder for remaining companies to survive.

### Diesel as Accelerant: The Mechanism

**Why diesel matters more than gas to the deindustrialization thesis:**

1. **Ubiquity:** Unlike gas (which large manufacturers can substitute with alternatives), diesel permeates *every* logistics node in the German supply chain. You cannot escape it.

2. **Sticky costs:** Diesel costs cannot be hedged long-term at scale for most SMEs. The German "Mittelstand" (small/medium enterprises = 99% of German businesses, 60% of employment) has no hedging infrastructure. They absorb price shocks directly.

3. **Compounding:** Diesel costs compound on top of already-elevated energy costs. For manufacturers already operating at break-even, a 25% diesel spike can turn a thin profit to a loss.

4. **Agriculture → food costs:** Higher diesel → higher farming costs → higher food inflation → pressured German consumer → weak domestic demand → further demand destruction for German manufacturers (feedback loop).

5. **Trigger for relocation decisions:** A diesel shock does not cause single-day relocations, but it moves companies from "considering" to "deciding." The DIHK survey shows 51% are already at "considering." A shock of this magnitude would push 10-15% of that cohort into "deciding."

### What the Data Says About Deindustrialization Pace

- German IP has been declining since 2017 (structural)
- Energy cost shock 2022-2023 turned "structural" into "accelerating"
- 2024-2025: Recession deepened despite energy price normalization — the damage is semi-permanent
- A new diesel shock in 2026 (from India/Turkey loophole closure + continued strikes) would hit **before** any recovery has occurred
- Unlike 2022 (when companies hoped it was temporary), in 2026 they would view it as confirmation of permanent European uncompetitiveness

**Assessment: YES — this meaningfully accelerates the deindustrialization thesis.** The risk is not a cliff-edge collapse but rather a ratchet: each energy shock permanently destroys another layer of marginal capacity. The companies that survive are those that have already relocated or hedged.

### The Core Thesis Update

**Russian diesel is no longer a direct European supply issue — it's a global market tightness issue.** The EU ban worked to remove direct dependency, but:

1. Europe now relies on longer-haul, more expensive Middle East/US diesel supply
2. Any shock to Russia's global diesel exports tightens the global pool from which Europe draws
3. The new India/Turkey loophole closure (Jan 2026) is *already* the equivalent of the scenario asked about
4. Ukraine's drone campaign provides an ongoing, recurring pressure that prevents Russian exports from normalizing
5. Russia's resilience (faster-than-expected repairs) means this is a sustained attrition war, not a decisive knockout

**For the HANS deindustrialization thesis:** Diesel is the *linchpin* that connects energy prices to the *entirety* of German manufacturing, not just energy-intensive sectors. Unlike natural gas (which primarily affects energy-intensive industrial processes), diesel affects logistics for *all* manufacturers. Germany's world-class supply chain efficiency (just-in-time, tight supplier networks) becomes a **liability** when the input that powers it becomes expensive and unreliable.

The 51% of large German companies already considering relocation are one diesel shock away from committing. **This is the threshold.**
