## 🔴🔴 RUSSIA SHOCK — NEW MATERIAL DEVELOPMENT

### Primorsk Terminal Shutdown (>1M bpd Baltic exports offline)

**This is a major second-order shock for European energy that has nothing to do with Hormuz.**

**Context:** Primorsk is Russia's primary Baltic crude export terminal (Ust-Luga is secondary). Together they handle >1.5M bpd of Russian crude exports, primarily to Asia and "friendly" nations. Primorsk shutdown alone = ~1-1.2M bpd offline from the global market.

**HANS European assessment:**
- Europe itself is largely sanctioned-out of Russian crude, BUT:
  1. **Global supply tightness:** Every barrel offline anywhere tightens the global balance. With 5.5M+ bpd already gone from Gulf + Iraq + Saudi, adding 1M+ bpd Baltic = global market now ~6.5M bpd short
  2. **India/Turkey re-export disruption:** European refiners have been partially accessing Russian crude via Indian/Turkish re-export channels (refined products from Russian feedstock). Primorsk shutdown disrupts this pathway
  3. **Baltic freight market:** Primorsk handles significant crude volumes on vessels that also serve European ports. Freight market tightening = costs up
  4. **Diesel supply:** Russia was a key diesel exporter (even post-sanctions, Turkey/India/UAE re-export). Primorsk offline = refined products tighter in European markets
- **ARA diesel crack expected to widen sharply.** Was ~$25/bbl as of Mar 11. Estimate 35-45+ now.

### Ufa Refinery Strike

**Ufa is Russia's 6th-largest refinery (capacity ~330,000 bpd), located in Bashkortostan.**

- Strike source unclear — Ukrainian long-range drone, likely

- Chinese manufacturers pay ~$65-75/bbl crude equivalent (discounted Gulf + Russian supply via existing deals)

- **Effective energy cost premium for European industry: 25-40% above Chinese competitors**
- This is not a temporary tariff — it's a structural cost wedge that persists for the duration of the war AND the several-year normalization period

**2. LNG: China captures Qatar supply Europe can't access**
- Qatar LNG is "permanently lost" for Europe (production + shipping + insurance all broken)
- China had pre-war LNG supply agreements with QatarEnergy that it is attempting to renegotiate/maintain
- China is also drawing on Russian LNG (Arctic LNG 2, Yamal) which is sanctioned from Europe
- Result: China's gas prices remain subdued relative to Europe's. Chinese fertilizer, chemical, aluminum producers maintain output; European equivalents are curtailing or shutting

**3. Industrial production: the chemical and aluminum sectors are the leading edge**
- European ammonia production (nitrogen fertilizer precursor): ~60% offline at current TTF levels (estimated). This affects 2026 crop yields → European food inflation secondary wave
- European aluminum smelters: many already curtailed (pre-war €40+ TTF threshold). At current levels, structural closures (not just curtailments) are likely
- Chinese equivalents: running at capacity, gaining global market share
- **This is the 2022 energy crisis but 3x worse and with no Russian supply as a fallback**

**4. Trade flow rerouting disadvantage**
- Cape of Good Hope rerouting adds 7-10 days transit for EU-Asia trade
- Chinese goods from factory to European port: same Cape rerouting adds cost, but Chinese factories run on cheaper feedstock
- Net: Chinese exports to Europe get *more competitive* (price advantage more than offsets shipping cost)
- European exports to Asia: more expensive AND slower

**5. Capital flows**
- Chinese state capital (sovereign wealth, state banks) is actively buying discounted assets from distressed Western sellers
- European private credit distress (Apollo/Ares gating) creates secondary market opportunities that Chinese institutional capital can access
- EU screening mechanisms (FDI screening) will slow but not stop this

**6. The strategic implication for EU-China relations**
- EU cannot simultaneously sanction Russia, avoid Iran, maintain US alliance, AND compete with China on energy-asymmetric industrial terms
- Something gives: either EU seeks its own "arrangement" with China for energy access (would fracture transatlantic relations), or EU industrial base continues structural contraction
- Watch for: EU-China back-channel energy discussions, particularly around LNG re-export deals. Any Scholz/Macron visit to Beijing in this environment = signal that EU is exploring the arrangement option.
