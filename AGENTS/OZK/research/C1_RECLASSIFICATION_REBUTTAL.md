# C1: Reclassification "Innocent Explanation" Rebuttal
**Created:** 2026-03-24

---

## The Bull Argument
OZK launched CIB to diversify away from CRE construction. C&I growth (+153% over 8 quarters) reflects genuine corporate lending to non-CRE borrowers. The 37.6% Memo Item 3 ratio means only 37.6% of C&I is RE-linked — the other 62.4% is real diversification. The C&I/construction divergence is strategy, not reclassification.

## The Structural Rebuttal

### Step 1: C&I and NDFI Are Separate Line Items

On RC-C, the loan composition is:
- **Item 4: C&I = $3,432M**
- **Item 9a: Loans to NDFIs = $2,743M** (separate from C&I)
- **Memo Item 3: CRE-linked loans in items 4 AND 9 = $1,289M**

These are NOT overlapping. Total "non-RE" loans (items 4 + 9a) = **$6,174M**.

### Step 2: The NDFI Book Is CRE

CEO Gleason, Q3 2025: "A chunk of our NDFI loans that show up on our call report are actually RESG loans... debt funds that do commercial real estate lending."

Deep research confirmed: 19 of 19 named CRE counterparties are CRE debt funds, bridge lenders, or CRE-focused PE. Zero non-CRE entities found in the RESG-managed NDFI book.

Conservative estimate: **70-90% of $2,743M NDFI = $1,920-2,469M is CRE-linked.**

### Step 3: MI3 Captures Only a Subset

Memo Item 3 definition: *"Loans to finance commercial real estate, construction, and land development activities (not secured by real estate)."*

This catches:
- ✅ C&I loans used to finance CRE but not collateralized by RE
- ✅ NDFI loans used to finance CRE but not collateralized by RE

This MISSES:
- ❌ NDFI subscription lines secured by LP commitments (not "financing CRE" directly, even though the fund invests in CRE)
- ❌ NDFI warehouse lines where collateral is the fund's loan portfolio (technically secured by financial assets, not "financing CRE construction")
- ❌ C&I loans to CRE-adjacent businesses (property managers, contractors, etc.) that aren't directly "financing" CRE

**MI3 ($1.289B) is a floor, not a ceiling, for CRE exposure hidden in items 4+9.**

### Step 4: The Math

| Component | Amount | CRE Exposure | How We Know |
|-----------|--------|-------------|-------------|
| NDFI — confirmed CRE (70-90%) | $2,743M | **$1,920-2,469M** | CEO quote + 19 named counterparties |
| MI3 (items 4+9 combined) | $1,289M | **$1,289M** | Call Report definition |
| **Less: MI3/NDFI overlap** | | **-($500-900M)** | Estimated: portion of MI3 attributable to NDFI loans |
| **Total CRE in "non-RE" categories** | | **$2,300-2,860M** | |

**Of $6,174M in "non-RE" loans (C&I + NDFI), $2,300-2,860M (37-46%) is CRE-linked.**

The "genuine diversification" portion is **$3,300-3,900M** — real, but smaller than the $6.2B headline suggests.

### Step 5: The Trend Is What Matters

The bull says: "OZK is diversifying via CIB." Let's check CIB growth by category:

From management comments:
- CIB total: $5.25B (16.3% of loans), up from 9.0% YoY
- CIB grew ~$2.4B in one year

From Call Report:
- C&I grew $1,703M YoY (Q4 2024 → Q4 2025)
- NDFI grew approximately alongside C&I

The question: **is CIB growth coming from genuine corporate lending or from NDFI/CRE-adjacent lending?**

CIB sub-groups (from Q3 earnings call):
- ABLG (Asset-Based Lending) — led Q3 growth
- CBSF (Corporate Banking, Sponsor Finance) — strong Q3
- Fund Finance — deliberately shrinking (shedding low-utilization)
- Natural Resources — new, growing
- Lender Finance — provides credit to NDFIs

**ABLG and CBSF are the genuinely diversifying businesses.** Fund Finance is shrinking. Lender Finance IS the NDFI/CRE channel. Natural Resources is new and small.

Without sub-group dollar breakdowns (not disclosed), we can't prove what % of CIB growth is genuine vs CRE-adjacent. But we know:
1. The NDFI book ($2.74B) is a large portion of the non-RESG loan growth
2. 70-90% of that NDFI book is CRE-linked
3. MI3 captures an additional $1.29B of CRE in C&I/NDFI
4. The CEO himself says the NDFI/CRE overlap exists

### Step 6: Conclusion

**The "innocent explanation" is partially correct but materially overstated.**

Yes, OZK has a real CIB strategy. Yes, ABLG and CBSF probably represent genuine diversification. But the headline CIB growth ($5.25B, +77% YoY) is inflated by $2.0-2.5B of CRE-linked NDFI lending that shows up in "non-CRE" categories on the Call Report.

The bull argument that "62.4% of C&I is real diversification" is wrong because:
1. It only looks at C&I ($3.4B), not C&I + NDFI ($6.2B)
2. It ignores that NDFI is almost entirely CRE
3. MI3 captures a floor, not the full CRE linkage

**Our counter:** "OZK IS diversifying — but about $2.3-2.9B of what they call 'diversification' is CRE exposure through a different pipe. The true non-CRE, non-RE-linked corporate book is ~$3.3-3.9B — about 10-12% of total loans. That's real but modest, and it's being used to market a narrative that obscures the concentration."

## Publication-Ready Framing

Don't say: "OZK is faking diversification through reclassification."
Do say: "OZK's CIB strategy includes genuine corporate lending growth, but a substantial portion of the NDFI book — which the CEO acknowledges includes RESG loans to CRE debt funds — represents CRE exposure classified outside traditional CRE categories. Reported CRE concentration ratios understate true exposure by an estimated 50-60 percentage points of Tier 1 capital."
