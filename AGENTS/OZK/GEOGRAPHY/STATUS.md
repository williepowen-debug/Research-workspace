## Assessment: 🟠 Risk is still surgical — life science + office in six metros — but those metros are running off SLOWER than the book

The core finding from March still holds: **geography is not the risk; the intersection of geography and asset type is.** Every Q3-26 RESG problem credit is office, life science or land, in six metros plus Baltimore and Lake Tahoe. Florida has none.

**New at Q3 (DERIVED from two primary figures six quarters apart):** RESG commitments fell **−22.3%** ($32.7B Q1-25 → $25.4B Q3-26), but the six problem metros fell only **−16.5%** ($7.72B → $6.45B), so their share of the book **rose 23.6% → 25.4%**. San Diego (RaDD-dominated, now extended to Dec-2029) barely shrank (−5.5%) and Los Angeles grew (+6.0%, new multifamily construction such as Culver City, KB-265). Chicago (−42%) and Seattle (−36%) ran off fastest, through resolutions and foreclosures. ⚠️ **Inference limit:** commitments by MSA do not show property type within a metro, so this says the problem *metros* are a growing share, not that problem *loans* are.
