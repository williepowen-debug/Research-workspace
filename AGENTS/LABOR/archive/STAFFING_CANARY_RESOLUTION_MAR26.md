## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**RHI and KFRC are showing sequential quarterly growth for the first time in ~12 quarters. But this is NOT a broad labor market recovery.** The growth is:
1. **Sequential, not year-over-year** — both companies had their 3rd consecutive year of annual revenue decline in 2025
2. **Driven by private sector flex demand** — companies substituting permanent hires with temp/contract workers, which is a DISTRESS indicator, not health indicator
3. **Not government/defense-driven** — zero evidence of federal contracts masking private sector weakness
4. **A cyclical temp swing, not structural improvement** — American Staffing Association index turning positive confirms sector-level but not economy-level

**Resolution: Downgrade counter-signal from "uncertainty" to 🟢 EXPLAINED AWAY.** The sequential green shoot *confirms* rather than challenges the thesis.

## 1. RHI (ROBERT HALF) — Q4 2025 RESULTS

### Revenue Performance
- **Q4 2025 revenue:** $1.30B — beats estimates by 0.9%, but **down 5.8% year-over-year**
- **Full year 2025:** $5.38B — full year declined
- **Key signal:** Returned to sequential growth on same-day constant currency basis **for the first time since Q2 2022** (per Q3 2025 earnings call, confirmed in Q4)
- **Sequential growth definition:** Q4 2025 was higher than Q3 2025, adjusted for billing days. This is NOT YoY growth.

### Segment Breakdown
| Segment | Q4 2025 Revenue Share | Trend |
|---------|----------------------|-------|
| Contract Talent Solutions | ~55% | Sequential improvement (contract/temp placement) |
| Permanent Placement | ~8% | Still weak — NO recovery in perm hiring |
| Protiviti (consulting) | ~37% | +5% YoY; has been only growth engine for 2 years |

**Critical read:** Permanent placement (8%) is NOT recovering. Companies are NOT committing to full-time hires. They are using Contract Talent Solutions (temp) as a flex buffer. This is the classic recession-adjacent behavior pattern: "We'll temp first, hire later" — except "later" keeps getting pushed out.

### Protiviti — What Is It?
Protiviti is RHI's consulting arm. It handles internal audit, risk, compliance, technology consulting. It serves financial services, healthcare, and government clients.
- **Government/federal exposure:** Protiviti does have some federal consulting work, but it is NOT the majority. Core clients are Fortune 500 private sector.
- **Q4 2024:** +5% YoY globally
- **Q4 2025:** Still growing but sequentially stabilizing

### What Sectors Are Driving RHI Growth?
Per Q3/Q4 2025 earnings call commentary:
- CEO Keith Waddell: "Overall hiring and quit rates remain low, job openings continue to trend well above historical averages" — NOT a strong labor market signal
- Sequential improvement came from **finance/accounting and technology contractors** in September–October 2025
- **No mention of government or defense contracts** driving demand
- Management narrative: "Clients prefer flexible talent models given uncertainty" — explicitly framing as uncertainty-driven, not confidence-driven

### RHI Government/Defense Exposure
- RHI places primarily finance, accounting, legal, and technology professionals
- Federal government is a small fraction of placements
- **No material defense contract exposure** in Contract Talent Solutions segment
- Protiviti may have 5-10% federal revenue — immaterial to overall sequential growth signal

- **Data and digital practices led recovery** — classic private sector tech modernization spending
- COO David Kelly: "Growth is not just about AI. Traditional staffing also picked up." — indicating broad-based client demand, not niche

### Government/Defense Exposure in KFRC?

- Client base: primarily private sector technology companies and financial institutions
- **No significant federal/defense contract revenue** — Kforce does not prominently operate in government IT staffing unlike SAIC, Booz Allen, Leidos
- Revenue drivers explicitly cited: **AI projects, cloud, ERP, data analytics** — these are commercial private sector clients
- **Zero earnings call mention of government contract wins** driving sequential improvement

### KFRC — The "AI Reality Check" as Demand Driver
CEO framed Q4 2025 growth around a shift in how clients think about AI:
> "70-75% of AI success comes from change management, not technology alone"

This is companies realizing their AI rollouts require data infrastructure, system integration, and tech talent support. That is entirely private sector demand. It does NOT suggest government contract masking.

## 3. RESOLUTION ANALYSIS

### Is the Sequential Growth Real?
**Yes, it is real.** Both companies delivered genuine sequential revenue improvements:
- KFRC: 3rd consecutive quarter of sequential gains
- RHI: First sequential improvement in 12 quarters (since Q2 2022)
- American Staffing Association industry index turned positive August 2025

**But "real sequential growth" ≠ "labor market improvement."**

### The Critical Distinction: What Does Temp Staffing Growth Mean?

| Scenario | What It Would Look Like | What We Actually See |
|----------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **True recovery** | Perm placement growth, wage growth, YoY staffing gains | Perm flat/down; YoY still negative |
| **Cyclical temp bounce** | Flex demand up, perm flat, uncertainty narrative | ✅ EXACTLY THIS |
| **Defense/Gov masking** | Contract wins from federal IT, mention in earnings | ❌ Zero evidence |
| **Distress indicator** | Companies avoiding FTEs, using contractors as buffer | ✅ ALSO THIS |

### The "Temp Up, Perm Down" Pattern
- **Historically:** temp employment leading indicator — both on the way UP and on the way DOWN
- **Recession-adjacent pattern:** Companies substitute perm hires with temps when uncertain. Temp demand rises briefly before final perm hiring freeze.
- **Current evidence:** RHI permanent placement (8% of revenue) NOT recovering. KFRC's F&A growth is in "flexible talent" explicitly.
- This pattern appeared in **Q3-Q4 2007 before the Great Financial Crisis** — temp agencies saw brief sequential uptick before 2008 collapse.

### Government/Defense Masking?
**No evidence. Clear verdict:**
- Neither RHI nor KFRC cited government or defense contracts as growth drivers
- Neither company has meaningful federal/defense revenue exposure in their temp staffing segments
- Growth sectors: commercial tech (AI/cloud/data) and commercial finance — both private sector
- Protiviti (RHI's consulting arm) has some federal exposure but is NOT driving the sequential growth signal; it was already the one growing segment in 2024

### What % of RHI/KFRC Revenue Is Federal/Defense?
| Company | Estimated Federal/Defense Revenue % | Confidence |
|---------|--------------------------------------|------------|
| RHI (Protiviti only) | ~5-10% of Protiviti (~37% of total) = ~2-4% of total | Medium |
| KFRC | <5% of total revenue | Medium-High |
| Both combined | **<5% of combined revenue** | Medium |

This is immaterial. Defense/federal sector cannot explain a 3-5% sequential swing in either company.

## 4. VERDICT — UPGRADE OR DOWNGRADE?

### **VERDICT: 🟢 DOWNGRADE — Counter-signal explained away. Thesis intact.**

**Rationale:**

1. **Sequential ≠ Recovery.** Both companies still declining YoY. 3 years of consecutive annual decline. The sequential green shoot means Q4 > Q3, not that the labor market is healing.

2. **Flex demand = uncertainty signal.** Companies preferring temp/contract workers over perm hires is BEARISH for permanent employment. It is a leading indicator that perm hiring is NOT about to recover. Exactly what you'd see in a late-cycle or recession-onset environment.

3. **No government masking.** The thesis that RHI/KFRC growth = fiscal masking via defense contracts is NOT supported. Growth is driven by commercial AI/cloud spending and F&A flex demand — entirely consistent with a "companies avoiding FTE commitments" narrative.

4. **The pattern historically confirms thesis, not challenges it.** Temp uptick before permanent hiring freeze is the classic late-cycle signal. 2007 analog: staffing agencies saw Q3–Q4 temp uptick before 2008 collapse of permanent hiring.

5. **American Staffing Association index +** = industry-level confirmation that temp market is real, but industry-level recovery is NOT the same as economy-level recovery. ASA has turned positive before while BLS continued softening.
