## 6. NATIONAL EXHAUSTION PEAK — WHICH MONTH?

### Wave Architecture
The exhaustion cascade builds on multiple layers:

| Layer | Event | Firing Period | Exhaustion Peak |
|-------|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| A | DOGE Phase I (2025 cuts) | Feb–Apr 2025 | Jun–Jul 2025 ✅ (passed) |
| B | Private sector (WARN, tech, retail) | Q1–Q2 2025 | Jul–Sep 2025 ✅ (passed) |
| C | DOGE Phase II (2026 cuts) | Mar–Jun 2026 | Jun–Aug 2026 ← CURRENT |
| D | Duration elongation effect | Ongoing | Aug–Oct 2026 ← NEXT |
| E | Long-term unemployed exhausting | Oct 2025–present | Dec 2026–Jan 2027 |

### National Peak Estimate: **June–August 2026**

**Why June–August 2026:**
1. DOGE Phase II waves (Mar–Jun 2026 firings) begin exhausting in June
2. Duration 25.7wk at 4-yr high = people who filed in Jan–Feb 2026 exhaust in June–July
3. Private sector WARN pipeline: 766 notices/91,190 workers YTD (5th-highest since 2009) — filed Jan–Apr 2026 → exhaust Jun–Sep 2026
4. FL's 12-week structure concentrates exhaustions faster than national average
5. No extended benefits programs active (UR not high enough to trigger federal triggers)

**Single worst month: July 2026**
- FL Wave 1 + Wave 2 both exhausted
- DOGE Phase II cuts at scale exhausted (26-state average)
- Summer hiring seasonal weakness in service sector
- No EUC (Emergency Unemployment Compensation) program — Congress has not passed extension

### National Monthly Exhaustion at Peak (July 2026 estimate):
- Insured unemployed projection: ~2.1–2.3M (rising with DOGE Phase II)
- At avg 22wk effective duration (mix of 12 and 26-week states)
- **Peak monthly exhaustion: ~410,000–480,000 people/month**

**FL is a 12-week canary.** While CA/NY/TX workers get 26 weeks to find new employment and maintain consumption, FL workers hit the cliff in 12 weeks. The June–July 2026 exhaustion window is a concentrated consumption shock:

- FL federal waves: ~7,400 workers losing $230/wk → **$7.4M/month** spending hole

- The cliff is NOT a gradual decline — it is a step-function cut when benefits terminate
