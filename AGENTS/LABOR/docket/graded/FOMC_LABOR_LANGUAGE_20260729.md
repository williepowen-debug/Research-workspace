## 1. The divergence being tested

The Fed will be looking at U-3 **4.2%**, initial claims **187K** (lowest since 1969), AHE **+3.5% YoY**. Read naively, that is an unambiguously tight labor market — and a reason to hike.

**LABOR's position is that all three are one-sided or artifactual:**

| Gauge | Fed-facing read | LABOR's read |
|---|---|---|
| U-3 4.2% (↓) | labor market tight | **artifact** — labor force −720K, NILF +832K, LFPR 61.5% (−0.3pp). Supply shrank faster than demand cooled (**L-06**) |
| Claims 187K | firing is minimal → strength | true, but measures **only** firing. Says nothing about hiring |
| AHE +3.5% (↑) | wage pressure → inflation risk | **scarcity pricing on a shrunken supply base**, not demand-pull. Possibly composition (low-wage exits mechanically lift the average) — **ECI 7/31 is the clean test** |
| *(not cited)* | — | hires rate **3.3%** frozen; openings **7.594M** 2-yr high going unfilled; quits **1.9%** (no worker confidence); LT-unemployed **1.9M / 27.3% share / +286K YoY** |

**The frozen labor market and the tight labor market produce the same U-3 and the same claims number.** They imply opposite policy. That is the entire content of this read.

## 2. Verbatim baseline — June 16-17, 2026 (what the language must be diffed against)

Grading a "shift in tone" without a baseline is how post-hoc reads happen. The June minutes said:

1. *"The unemployment rate was 4.3 percent in May and had changed little, on balance, since the middle of the previous year."*
2. *"Nonfarm payroll employment rose at a solid pace in the three months through May."*
3. *"Participants generally remarked that payroll employment gains had **strengthened this year** and appeared roughly consistent with **underlying labor force growth**."*
4. *"Several participants remarked that **the labor market was not currently a source of inflationary pressures**, or that nominal wage growth remained consistent with inflation moving toward 2 percent."*
5. *"A few participants commented that... there was a case for **raising** the target range... but those participants indicated that they supported maintaining the current target range at this meeting."*
6. ECI private-sector **3.4%** through March; AHE **3.4%** through May.

**Critical, and the sharpest thing on this card: line 3 is already factually superseded.** Those minutes reflect the June 16-17 meeting — **before** the July 2 NFP print that came in at **+57K** and revised April/May **down a net 74K**. "Payroll employment gains had strengthened this year" was true on the vintage the Fed held then (Apr 179K / May 172K); on the revised series it is **false** (148K / 129K / 57K). The Fed's stated labor premise has been invalidated by data it has now seen but has not yet spoken to.

**Line 3's second clause is also the closest the Fed has come to my supply story** — "consistent with underlying labor force growth" — but framed as *benign equilibrium* rather than *supply contraction*. Whether that clause hardens into an explicit supply read is the single highest-information thing to watch.

**Note:** the June minutes contain **no discussion at all** of whether the unemployment decline reflected labor supply/immigration rather than labor demand. As of the last full record, **the Fed is not reading the artifact.**

## 5. The thing the Fed will not have on 7/29

**ECI Q2 releases Friday 7/31, 8:30 ET — two days after the decision.** The June minutes leaned on ECI 3.4% for line 4 ("labor market not currently a source of inflationary pressures"). The Committee therefore decides **without** the current reading of its own composition-controlled wage measure, while AHE has accelerated 3.4→3.5%.

**Why this is LABOR's call and not a curiosity:** AHE is composition-contaminated — a labor force shrinking by 720K, concentrated in lower-wage cohorts, mechanically raises the average wage **with no worker getting a raise**. ECI controls for composition. So:

- **ECI accelerates ≥3.6%** → wage pressure is real; the demand-pull read gains support; my supply-artifact framing weakens and I should say so.
- **ECI flat/decelerating ~3.4% while AHE accelerated** → the AHE move was **composition** — strong corroboration of the supply-shrink read, and it undercuts the "labor is inflationary" premise the hike path leans on.
