### The Timiraos exchange is the single most informative moment

Nick Timiraos (WSJ) offered the labor-transmission framing directly: *"Rates bring inflation down by cooling demand, it's generally thought that can show up in the labor market. **If that's not the channel you're relying on, what is?**"*

Warsh **declined the frame entirely** — no tradeoff, no labor channel: *"I don't believe that either part of our mandate is generally at war with the other part... if you want to do the most harm to the labor markets, you would run a period of high inflation."* He then routed transmission through **interest rates → lending channels** and **financial-market prices**, not labor.

**A direct, expert-posed invitation to make labor the policy channel was refused on the record.** That is stronger evidence than any absence-count.

## 5. 🔴 THE CORRECTION — my 7/24 regime fold was wrong on mechanism

**What I wrote (STATUS § FED TRAP, 7/24):**
> *"Benign claims prints now FEED the hike path rather than arguing against cuts. The 187K 57-year low was hawkish fuel... the hike case rests on a labor-tightness premise built from exactly the three gauges I've flagged as artifacts (U-3, claims, AHE)."*

**What the primary text shows:** there is **no labor-tightness premise underneath this hike case.** Warsh's stated reaction function makes labor a *precondition already satisfied and set aside* — *"where the labor markets are more or less at equilibrium... when he or she sees underlying inflation moving higher, he or she is more inclined to tighten."* Labor's role is to be **quiet**, not to be **tight**. The hawkish energy comes from 63+ months of above-target inflation; the three dissenters said so; the Chair spends his discussion on prices because *"we're doing pretty well on the full employment side."*

**So the sign flip I folded in on 7/24 is wrong.** A benign claims print is not hawkish fuel. It is *nothing* — it confirms the constraint is satisfied and the Committee moves on to prices.

**The correct asymmetry, and it cuts against my book:** if labor is a satisfied side-constraint rather than a policy input, **the bar for labor data to matter to policy just went UP.** A low claims print doesn't feed the hike (I was wrong); a mildly soft one doesn't restrain it either. **Only a labor deterioration large enough to break "more or less at equilibrium" re-enters the reaction function at all.** Marginal wage and claims wiggles — the stuff I produce most of — are currently policy-irrelevant by the Chair's own construction.
