## 2. CONFIRMED RAID IMPACTS + FEAR MULTIPLIER

### Ground Truth Data Points

**57 Concrete (Rio Grande Valley, TX):**
- Filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy, December 2025
- CEO Eliud Cavazos: "60% reduction in residential concrete volume"
- Triggered by ICE raids at Monte Cielo and surrounding developments
- Tile supplier Materiales El Valle: lost **$5.3M in sales**, forced layoffs
- Hidalgo County residential construction: estimated **-30% decline**

**UR Homes (South TX):**
- 10-15 ICE raids on active jobsites
- Management reporting **~60% construction volume drop**
- Half-built homes abandoned mid-construction, crews not returning

**AGC National Survey (Aug 2025, 92% hiring difficulty):**
- **28% of firms** report being directly affected by immigration enforcement actions
- **5%** had ICE agents visit a jobsite
- **10%** lost workers due to actual or rumored raids
- **20%** reported subcontractors lost staff due to enforcement fear

**Tallahassee, FL:** Single raid at FSU-area construction site = **100+ arrests** (ICE, June 2025)
**St. Paul, MN:** 4 workers arrested, fear ripple to surrounding sites
**Home Depot parking lots (LA):** Day laborer raids, direct removal of construction labor pool

### Fear Multiplier (the key dynamic BLS won't capture)

The AGC data reveals the structural asymmetry: **10% report actual workers lost** vs. **20% report subcontractor staff lost via fear** — 2:1 fear-to-direct ratio at minimum.

South TX builders association: "Immigrants — both with and without legal status — make up more than **half** of Texas's construction workforce." Legal workers are also staying home (documented by AGC: "both undocumented and documented workers" affected).

**Estimated fear multiplier: 3-5x direct raid impact.** Basis: 
- 10% of firms lost workers to actual raids
- 20% lost via fear/rumor
- Anecdotal reports of crews not showing 1-2 days per week when rumors spike
- Credibly documented 1-in-4 workers has legal status but still avoids sites after nearby raids (NPR reporting)

## 3. CLAIMS/NFP IMPACT MODEL: APR–MAY 2026

### The Claims/Shadow Split

**Who files unemployment insurance claims:**
- Workers with W-2 employment history
- Must have worked legally (E-Verify compliant)
- Cannot be undocumented

**Who does NOT file:**
- Undocumented workers (ineligible)
- Many day laborers (paid cash, no payroll record)
- Subcontractor workers misclassified as 1099
- Workers who self-deport or flee before layoff

### Construction Employment Structure in TX/AZ/FL

Total construction workers (~1.65M mid-estimate):
- **~38% immigrant overall → ~627,000 immigrant construction workers**
- **~18-20% undocumented → ~297,000–330,000 undocumented construction workers**
- **~20% documented immigrant → ~330,000 legal immigrant construction workers**
- **~62% US-born → ~1.02M US-born construction workers**

Key additional factor: **misclassification**. Construction has the highest rate of 1099 misclassification of any sector. Estimates range 20-30% of construction workers are misclassified as independent contractors. These workers cannot file claims regardless of immigration status.

### Claims-Filing Eligible Workers (TX/AZ/FL)
- US-born on payroll: ~62% × 1.65M = ~1.02M
- Legal immigrants on payroll (documented): ~20% × 1.65M = ~330,000
- But subtract misclassified (~25% of total): –412,500
- **Adjusted claims-eligible pool: ~940,000–970,000 workers**

### Job Loss Estimate

**Direct raid arrests:** ICE construction-sector arrests are not broken out separately. DHS reported 400K total deportations through Sept 2025 (250 days). Estimated construction sector share: ~8-12% of total deportees based on sector's share of undocumented workforce → **~32,000–48,000 construction workers arrested/deported nationally through Sept 2025.**

TX/AZ/FL pro-rated share (these 3 states = ~35-40% of undocumented construction nationally):
- **Direct arrests in TX/AZ/FL through Q1 2026: ~15,000–25,000 workers**

**Fear-effect worker absences (ongoing, not one-time):**
- AGC: 20% of firms = subcontractor staff losses from fear
- If 20% of TX/AZ/FL construction firms have lost subcontractor staff, and average subcontractor crew = 8-15 workers...
- Firms in TX/AZ/FL: ~60,000–80,000 construction firms (see Section 4)
- 20% affected = 12,000–16,000 firms × avg 5 workers absent per affected firm
- **Fear-effect absences: 60,000–80,000 workers regularly not showing up**

**Subcontractor cascade (firms shutting down or scaling back):**
- 57 Concrete model: supplier shutdown = 50-200 direct employees each
- Tile companies, lumber suppliers, concrete firms all filing bankruptcy or downsizing
- Hidalgo County alone: 30% construction drop = ~5,000–8,000 fewer workers needed
- TX/FL/AZ extrapolated from South TX epicenter: **30,000–60,000 additional workers idled**

### Total Job Loss Estimate (TX/AZ/FL):
| Category | Low | High |
|----------|-----|------|
| Direct arrests/deportations | 15,000 | 25,000 |
| Fear-effect (regular absences) | 60,000 | 80,000 |
| Subcontractor cascade / firm closures | 30,000 | 60,000 |
| **TOTAL** | **105,000** | **165,000** |

**Mid-point estimate: ~135,000 construction workers effectively removed from TX/AZ/FL construction labor pool.**

### NFP/Claims Translation (Apr–May 2026)

Of 135,000 estimated job losses:
- **Undocumented (cannot file): ~55,000–70,000** → shadow unemployment, invisible to BLS/DOL
- **Misclassified 1099 (cannot file): ~15,000–25,000** → also invisible
- **Claims-eligible workers who lose payroll jobs: ~40,000–60,000** in TX/AZ/FL

**Expected UI claims attributable to construction enforcement (TX/AZ/FL, Apr-May):**
- Not all will file immediately; fear keeps workers from DOL offices too
- Effective claims filing rate for undocumented/cash-paid workers = ~0%
- For W-2 documented workers who lose jobs: ~60-70% filing rate (normal construction worker claim behavior)
- **Expected new UI claims attributable: ~24,000–42,000 across TX/AZ/FL over Apr-May**
- **Weekly initial claims signal: +2,400–4,200/week spread over 10 weeks**

**NFP Impact (payroll survey):**
- BLS payroll survey captures W-2 employees; misses self-employed and some subcontractors
- Shadow undocumented job losses (~55-70K) will NOT appear in NFP
- Documented worker job losses that hit payroll: **~30,000–45,000 jobs lost in NFP construction count**
- Spread over April-May: approximately **-15,000 to -22,500/month NFP construction drag** from TX/AZ/FL alone
- National construction employment: 8.31M; this represents **0.18-0.27% of sector** per month — visible signal if concentrated

**Key distortion**: NFP will UNDERCOUNT real construction job losses by 2-3x because undocumented and misclassified workers are the hardest hit and least visible to surveys.

## 4. THE "57 CONCRETE ZONE" — SMALL/MID CONSTRUCTION FIRMS

### Firm Size Profile (NAICS 23, Census County Business Patterns)

Nationally, construction has ~950,000 establishments. In TX/AZ/FL:
- **Texas:** ~125,000–135,000 construction firms
- **Florida:** ~90,000–100,000 construction firms  
- **Arizona:** ~35,000–40,000 construction firms
- **Total: ~250,000–275,000 construction firms in TX/AZ/FL**

**Size distribution (consistent across states):**
- <10 employees: ~75% of firms (~190,000 firms)
- 10-49 employees: ~19% (~50,000 firms)
- 50-249 employees: ~5% (~13,000 firms)
- 250+: <1%

### The "57 Concrete Zone" (highest risk)

**Definition:** Small-to-mid firms (10-99 employees), residential-heavy, immigrant workforce >50%, operating in TX/AZ/FL metro and border areas.

Criteria overlap:
- Residential construction subcontractors (concrete, tile, framing, roofing, drywall)
- TX/AZ/FL only
- Workforce >50% immigrant (higher than state averages)
- Revenue concentration in single geography/client

**Estimated count: 15,000–25,000 firms** fitting this exact profile in TX/AZ/FL.

These firms are the transmission mechanism to KRE: they carry construction loans, have drawn on credit lines, and have receivables from homebuilders. A 30-60% volume drop means:
- Debt service coverage collapses
- Draws on existing credit facilities spike
- New project financing dries up (lenders quoted in CRE Daily as tightening)

**Materiales El Valle ($5.3M lost)** and **57 Concrete** are early examples of what happens when demand drops 60% at firms with fixed costs and short credit lines.

## 6. TOTAL CONSTRUCTION EMPLOYMENT HIT — INTEGRATED ESTIMATE

### Three-Scenario Model (TX/AZ/FL focus, but national implications)

**Scenario A — Contained (low estimate):**
- Raids localized to border areas (South TX, AZ border counties)
- Fear effect limited to undocumented workers
- Firms adapt with documented/legal workforce
- **Total hit: 75,000–100,000 workers; NFP signal: -8,000 to -12,000/month construction**

**Scenario B — Base Case (most likely given DHS Day 40 + 60+ day guarantee):**
- Raids spreading to major metros (Houston, Miami, Phoenix, Tampa)
- Fear effect reaching documented workers (AGC already documenting this)
- Subcontractor cascade accelerating as projects stall
- **Total hit: 125,000–165,000 workers in TX/AZ/FL; NFP signal: -15,000 to -22,000/month**

**Scenario C — Cascade (if enforcement escalates through Q2):**
- Major metro construction paralysis
- Multiple additional bankruptcies in "57 Concrete zone"
- Legal workers quit sector to avoid association
- **Total hit: 200,000–275,000 workers in TX/AZ/FL; NFP signal: -30,000 to -45,000/month**

### National Scaling
TX/AZ/FL = ~35-40% of undocumented construction workers nationally, but ~55-60% of the enforcement intensity (border proximity + DHS focus states).

If national construction hit = 1.5-2x the TX/AZ/FL hit:
- **National total: 190,000–330,000 construction workers affected (base to cascade)**
- **Shadow unemployment (invisible to BLS): 120,000–210,000 workers**
- **Visible NFP construction drag nationally: -20,000 to -60,000/month April-June 2026**

### The Claims Gap (the most important number for trading)
At base case national estimate of 250,000 total construction job losses:
- **Claims-invisible shadow unemployment: ~170,000–180,000 workers** (undocumented + misclassified)
- **UI claims filed: ~20,000–35,000** (only 10-15% of actual losses show in claims data)
- **NFP undercounting: construction sector NFP shows perhaps -30K/month while true losses are -80K/month**

This means: **when construction NFP starts showing weakness, actual losses are already 2-3x worse.**
