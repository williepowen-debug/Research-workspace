## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Thesis:** Federal contractor workforce faces structural displacement risk from DOGE-driven agency cuts, with concentrated exposure in high-cost metros creating cascading employment-to-housing transmission.

**Key Findings:**
- **Top 10 contractors**: $130B+ annual federal revenue, ~850K direct employees
- **Geographic concentration**: 40%+ of workforce in DC/NoVA/MD corridor (390K+ jobs)
- **Agency dependency**: USAID, EPA, IRS cuts = $25-30B contractor revenue at risk
- **Downstream multiplier**: 2.5-3.0x indirect jobs (facilities, subs, local services) = 2M+ total jobs exposed

**Risk Rating:** 🔴 **CRITICAL** — Q1-Q2 2026 danger window as DOGE cuts materialize

### 1. **Direct Employment Loss**
- **Timing:** Q1-Q2 2026 (WARN pipeline + contract non-renewals)
- **Evidence:** KFRC Q4: "federal contractor hiring -23% YoY" (Jan-Jul 2025)

### 3. **Local Services Collapse**
- **High-wage contractor spend:** $80-100K/year local consumption
- **135K job loss = $10.8-13.5B demand destruction** in DC metro
- **Hit:** Restaurants, retail, childcare, home services

### 4. **Commercial Real Estate**
- **Contractor office demand:** 18-22 sq ft/employee average
- **135K jobs = 2.4-3.0M sq ft vacated**
- **Already weak:** DC CBD office vacancy 20%+, Tysons 15%+

## WORKFORCE COMPOSITION & VULNERABILITY

### Skillset Profile (High-Wage, Specialized)

**Typical GovCon Roles:**
- **Cleared IT professionals:** $110-160K (security clearances = wage premium)
- **Management consultants:** $130-200K (strategy, transformation)
- **Data scientists/analysts:** $120-180K (modernization, AI projects)
- **Program managers:** $140-220K (contract oversight)

**Vulnerability Factors:**
1. **High fixed costs:** Mortgages, private schools, dual-income dependence
2. **Narrow skillset:** Government-specific (clearances, compliance, legacy systems)
3. **Limited mobility:** Private sector demand for gov-specific skills is weak
4. **Geographic lock-in:** Clearances + family → can't easily relocate

### Re-Employment Difficulty

**Unlike manufacturing/retail layoffs:**
- **Cannot pivot to gig economy** (Uber/DoorDash won't cover $4K mortgage)
- **Private sector demand weak** for gov-specific skills (FISMA, FedRAMP, etc.)
- **Clearance decay:** Inactive clearances expire (lose wage premium)
- **Age/experience:** Many 45-60 years old, high salary expectations

**Expected duration of unemployment:** 30-40 weeks (vs. 24-week average)  
**Long-term unemployment risk:** HIGH (mortgage distress within 6 months)

## HISTORICAL PRECEDENTS

### 2013 Sequestration (Mild Analog)
- **Federal contractor cuts:** ~5-8% workforce reductions
- **DC unemployment:** Rose from 5.4% → 6.2% (2013-2014)
- **Housing impact:** NoVA sales -12%, price appreciation stalled
- **Recovery:** 18-24 months (defense spending resumed)

**2026 Difference:** DOGE cuts are **structural, not cyclical**. No spending resumption expected.

### 1990s Base Realignment and Closure (BRAC)
- **Defense contractor cuts:** 30-40% in affected regions
- **Metro impacts:** San Diego, Hampton Roads, Charleston SC
- **Transmission time:** 12-24 months from announcement to peak distress
- **Housing:** 15-25% price declines in high-concentration zip codes

**2026 Parallel:** Similar magnitude, but hitting **civil (DC) instead of defense (distributed)**.
