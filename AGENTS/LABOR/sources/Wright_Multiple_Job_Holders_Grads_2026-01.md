# Wright: Multiple Job Holders & Graduate Unemployment

*Source: Melody Wright Substack (Jan 9 & Jan 20, 2026)*

---

## Multiple Job Holders Exceed 2008

> "We had more workers working a full-time and part-time job in November to make ends meet than we did in **November 2008**."

| Metric | Change Since 2008 |
|--------|-------------------|
| Population | +12.32% |
| Multiple job holders | **+30.00%** |

**Implication:** 2.4x faster growth in multiple-job holders than population. Hidden stress not in U-3.

---

## Recent Graduate Unemployment

| Period | Unemployment Rate (22-27) |
|--------|---------------------------|
| December 2007 | 3.4% |
| December 2025 | **5.8%** |

**Context:** Young workers entering job market in worse position than pre-GFC. This cohort:
- Has student debt
- Faces AI displacement narrative
- Can't afford housing

---

## Challenger Layoffs 2025

> "Employers announced **1,206,374 job cuts in 2025**, an increase of **58%** from the 761,358 announced in 2024."

> "Those layoffs are **still flowing through the system**."

---

## AI Layoff Narrative is Cover

> "The primary motivation for this rebranding of job cuts appears to be investor relations... attributing staff reductions to AI adoption 'conveys a more positive message to investors' than admitting to traditional business failures, such as weak consumer demand."

> "We are **not yet at a stage where massive layoffs would occur** due to the implementation of these technologies."

**Aligns with:**
- Burry: AI productivity = 1 bp in 2025
- Real driver = demand weakness + offshoring

---

## Implications for LABOR

1. **Multiple job holders** — Add to hidden stress tracking (like U-6, part-time for economic reasons)
2. **Graduate unemployment 5.8%** — Worse than Dec 2007; entry-level market frozen
3. **AI = narrative cover** — Real causes are cyclical/structural, not technological
4. **Layoffs still flowing** — Q4 2025 announcements = Q1-Q2 2026 actuals

**Cross-reference:**
- "Hotel California" framework (hard to find new job)
- Challenger data (confirms +58% YoY)
- Young worker stress → housing demand destruction
