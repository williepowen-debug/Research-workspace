## L-11 — Propagating a correction is a per-surface READ, not a pattern-match: sweeps fail in BOTH directions, and the over-correction deletes true statements
**Pattern:** On 7/31 I found that STATUS's 7/16 German-PMI note was sign-confused — it said German mfg expansion *"tempers the AT-RISK case,"* when expansion abroad with a ~2-mo US lead argues US ISM employment goes **UP**, i.e. it *raises* breach risk (**L-05** class). Correct catch, correct strike. I then propagated that strike to `NEXUS_BRIEF.md` "for consistency" — **without re-reading what the brief actually said.** The brief said *"tempers the **sub-49-break** case,"* which is **sign-CORRECT** (expansion arguing UP does argue against a break *down* through 49). The two surfaces said **different things**; only one was wrong. So I deleted a **true counter-signal** from the surface NEXUS reads, and wrote a self-critical commit note (*"a sweep that fixes one surface and misses another is the recurring failure"*) that was **itself factually wrong** — the brief had never carried the error. Caught by PROME's round-2 audit; verified against the pre-commit blob (`d95f3f34^`) before reversing. **The night produced both failure directions four hours apart:** *under-swept-but-claimed-done* (three surfaces still carrying the retired "0.4" inside a file whose own header announced that sweep complete) and *over-swept-and-claimed-behind* (this). A sweep's self-report is unreliable in **both** directions, so neither "done" nor "I missed one" should be believed without the per-surface read.
**Fix:** (1) When propagating a correction, **open each target surface and read its actual wording before editing it.** Surfaces that *look* like they carry the same error frequently carry a differently-scoped — and sometimes correct — version. A correction is N reads, not one find-and-replace (`[[finding_verification_correction_downstream_propagation]]` covers finding all instances; this is the complementary failure — *mis-identifying* an instance). (2) **Deleting a true statement is a real cost, not a safe default.** Over-correction feels conservative because it looks like extra rigour, but it silently removes evidence from consumers — here, a genuine counter against the deep-pullback bands of a card grading the next trading morning. (3) **Verify the blob, not the memory** — the reversal was only provable because the pre-commit text was recoverable in git (`[[finding_loadbearing_number_must_be_reproducible]]`). (4) **Distinguish what a restored counter acts on:** the German/S&P leads move **P(band)**, not **V(band)** — conditional on landing in the pullback bands the leads are *contradicted*. Restoring a counter is not a licence to re-weight the values it doesn't touch.
**First seen:** German-PMI strike, made and reversed **2026-07-31**, both within the same evening and both before the Aug-3 print it bears on.

## L-10 — A "holds through DATE" prediction is N draws, not one call — decompose to per-draw survival; and a risk LABEL is not a REPRICE
**Pattern:** LAB-06 (*"ISM Mfg Employment <50 through 2026"*) sat at **80%** for a month while its own ledger note read *"0.4pt from breach — AT RISK."* Both halves of that were wrong. **(a) The structure was never priced.** This is a *survive-N-draws* claim — any single print ≥50.0 kills it permanently — with **5 prints remaining**. So 80% overall was implicitly asserting **80^(1/5) = 95.6% survival per print**, from **49.7** while the series climbed **+1.1 in June and +2.2 over two months**. The move that kills it (**+0.3**) was *smaller than its recent average monthly move*. Nobody ever computed the per-draw number, so an indefensible claim looked like a confident one. **(b) The "AT RISK" label was doing the work the number should have done.** The marker had been on the row since 7/1 and the confidence field was untouched — the label discharged the *feeling* of having updated while the value that actually scores stayed put. **(c) Two spec defects rode along undetected underneath the label:** every surface said *"0.4pt from breach"* when `<50` is falsified by `≥50.0`, so from 49.7 the true distance was **0.3** — one tick nearer than I'd been telling myself and everyone downstream; and *"through 2026"* vs a 2026-12-31 due date was never resolved (December data publishes ~Jan 2027, i.e. **after** the due date — N=5, not 6). **(d) A sign-confused counter-signal survived inside the same row:** the 7/16 German-PMI note claimed expansion abroad *"tempers the AT-RISK case,"* but German mfg expansion with a ~2-mo US lead argues US ISM employment goes **UP** — it *raises* breach risk. Both upstream leads pointed the same way (toward breach) and the row read one of them backwards (**L-05** class).
**Fix:** (1) For any *"X stays above/below L through DATE"* claim, **count N and compute `conf^(1/N)` at registration** — if the per-draw survival you're implicitly asserting isn't defensible against the series' own monthly volatility and current distance-to-line, the headline confidence is wrong. Heuristic: **if the move that kills it is smaller than the series' recent average monthly move, no multi-draw call belongs above ~50%.** Now **§C gate #12**. (2) **Freeze which prints qualify at registration** — publication lag decides membership, not the data month. (3) **Never let a qualitative risk marker stand on a row whose confidence hasn't moved**: either move the number or delete the label. Now **§C gate #13**, and it runs as a **sweep over the whole open book at C2**, not only the row in hand. (4) State thresholds at the tick that actually falsifies them (`<50` → `≥50.0`), and recompute distance-to-line every time the level moves. (5) **The general lesson: an annotation is not an update.** LABOR's signature failure is high-confidence threshold calls (§A: 0/3), and this is how they survive scrutiny — the row *looks* examined because it carries a warning.
**First seen:** LAB-06, repriced **80% → 20% on 2026-07-31, pre-print and unforced** (the July print lands ~Aug 3). The reprice was prompted by asking "what would I be embarrassed by on Monday?", not by any new data — every input had been on the row since July 1.
**⚠️ RECURRED THE SAME DAY, ONE LAYER DOWN — appended 2026-07-31 eve.** Having decomposed LAB-06 into per-draw survival to reprice it, I then wrote the Aug-3 grading card's outcome bands **ignoring that same decomposition**: Band B said a survival at 49.5–49.9 meant *"no confidence change — the hazard that produced the 20% is unchanged."* That conflates **per-draw hazard** with **survival probability**. Surviving July *removes a draw*, so LAB-06 must rise mechanically even if the hazard is literally unchanged; the floor is the remaining-path product (0.68×0.75×0.78×0.80 = **31.8%**), not zero change. Worse, the bands were **non-monotone** — Band C (a survival *further* from the line) awarded +10pp while Band B awarded 0 — which is the tell that should have caught it without any arithmetic at all. Caught by PROME's Will-directed audit, **fixed pre-print**. **Three things this adds:** (1) **The gate you just built is not automatically applied to the artifact you build next.** #12 was written that morning and violated that afternoon, in the first card written after it shipped — a new gate covers *registration*, and nobody had asked whether it also governs *grading bands*. When you install a gate, sweep the adjacent artifact class the same session. (2) **Grading-card bands must reproduce the headline confidence** under the law of total probability, and must be **monotone** in the evidence direction — a 30-second check that catches this class without re-deriving the model (now **§C gate #14a**). (3) **A fix supplied by an auditor still needs verifying, and needs carrying through.** PROME's diagnosis was right, but its implied July survival (63%) was an artifact of dividing the *posted* 20% by the path rather than the *modeled* 16%, and its prescribed fix — "raise Band B" — would have **inverted** the card against Band C had I applied it as given. Fixing B forced C and D up too (`[[finding_verify_recommended_fix_not_just_finding]]`). **A fourth, separable point (→ §C gate #14b):** the same card asserted its Brier treatment was *"per §A convention"* when the convention is **silent** on a pre-print unforced reprice — its whole rationale is about conceding *after* the evidence turned. Asserting a control that does not reach the case is the **documented-but-absent** pattern from L-08's own delay, recurring within a week. The resolution keeps the as-made 0.64 as the headline and records the pre-print value as a labelled diagnostic only — legitimate solely because it is **symmetric** (a pre-print reprice that is wrong the other way is penalised by the same amount) and because it must be **declared before the print**, never claimed retroactively.

## L-09 — An ABSENCE-tell is only evidence if the instrument had room to speak; and diff like-for-like (statement↔statement, minutes↔minutes)
**Pattern:** The frozen FOMC labor-language card (7/24, graded 7/31) leaned much of its ~70% branch (a) on **absence** tells — *"no mention of participation, the hiring rate, job-finding rate, or long-term unemployment."* Those tells all fired. But they fired **trivially**: the 7/29 statement body is ~140 words, and Warsh has explicitly *"pulled back from forward guidance"* and is *"somewhat spare on our words"* by design. In a deliberately spare instrument, "the Fed didn't mention X" is nearly costless to observe and carries almost no information — the same tell would have fired on a Fed that fully agreed with me. **The tells that actually carried information were (i) the PRESENCE tells** (*"labor markets, solid, steady"*; the *"more or less at equilibrium"* reaction function) **and (ii) the response to a direct expert question** (Timiraos offered the labor-transmission channel by name and Warsh declined it). A refusal under direct questioning is strong evidence; silence in a document that says little about anything is weak. **Second, separate defect in the same card:** §2's baseline was the June **minutes**, and a tell was written against minutes language (*"payroll gains had strengthened this year"*) — then graded against a **statement**. That phrase was never in a statement. Different instrument, different authorship, different cadence: the diff was **not like-for-like** and the tell had to be scored ungradable and deferred to the July minutes (~Aug 19).
**Fix:** (1) **Before pre-registering a language read, base-rate the instrument** — how many words does this issuer typically spend on this topic in *this* document type? An absence-tell against a source that habitually says nothing is not a test (`[[finding_base_rate_the_instrument_before_its_event_table]]`). (2) **Weight tells explicitly at registration:** presence-tells and direct-question responses > absence-tells. Where an absence-tell is used, condition it — *"no mention of participation **in a document that discusses labor for ≥N words**."* (3) **One baseline per instrument.** A statement leg needs a statement baseline; a minutes leg needs a minutes baseline. Write both if the card grades both. (4) **A correct branch call can still carry a wrong implication** — the card resolved to its 70% branch and the branch's *stated consequence* ("benign claims become hawkish fuel") was refuted by the same text. **Grade the branch and the implication separately**; the implication is where the thesis damage lives (`[[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]]`).
**First seen:** FOMC labor-language card, registered 2026-07-24, graded 2026-07-31 — same session as its own defect discovery.

## L-08 — A WARN cohort must be ≥~10% of the weekly claims base to be visible in the national SA series; below that, the threshold is untestable and the *specification* is the defect
**Pattern:** LAB-17 pre-registered "initial-claims 4-wk MA ≥235K by Aug 6" as the test of the WARN→claims framework (r=0.78 at ~6wk lag), driven by the big-tech cluster. Doing the arithmetic *before* resolution: the entire in-window cohort (LinkedIn 606 eff 7/13 + Meta 4,665 eff 7/22 + Intuit 910 eff 7/31) is **~6,181 workers, spread across three separate claims weeks, against a ~200K weekly base** — roughly **3% of a single week**. Moving a 4-wk MA from 187.5K to 235K requires **+28K on the MA**, i.e. a **~327K single print**. The cohort was **never large enough to confirm the threshold**, no matter how well the mechanism worked. LinkedIn's 606 was pre-flagged invisible and *was* invisible; Meta's 4,665 is ~2.3% of a print, below the ±1-8K back-week revision noise floor.
**Fix:** (1) Before registering a cohort-driven threshold on an aggregate series, compute the **cohort-to-base ratio**. Detectability floor: **≥~10% of the weekly base (~20K+ separations landing in one week)**. (2) Below that floor the hypothesis is only testable in **state-level** data (CA weekly claims here), not the national number — register it there or don't register a threshold at all. (3) **A ❌ on such a threshold does not falsify the mechanism** — it falsifies the sizing. Say that explicitly at resolution so the framework isn't discarded for a specification error (`[[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]`, gate #3 of the PREDICTIONS_SCOREBOARD §C pre-write checklist). (4) This is LABOR's signature failure mode — raw as-made Brier **0.256** (was 0.277 pre-LAB-17), dragged entirely by high-confidence *threshold* misses (LAB-01 85%, LAB-02 65%) while every *mechanism* call landed. LAB-17 is the same shape caught earlier: walked 35→5% on evidence rather than defended. Generalizes to `[[finding_threshold_spec_fails_before_world]]` — the threshold usually fails on its **spec**, not on the world.
**First seen:** LAB-17, diagnosed 2026-07-24 in the frozen 7/30 grading card — **before** the Aug-6 resolution, not after.
**✅ CONFIRMED AT RESOLUTION 2026-07-31 — the diagnosis was right and the arithmetic held.** Claims w/e Jul 25 printed **197K** against the card's required **≥327K**; the 4-wk MA *fell* to **202,750**, moving 32,250 further from the 235K bar. Meta's ~4,665 (the cohort's anchor, effective in exactly this claims week) was pre-committed invisible and **was** invisible: the 9K rise is inside back-week revision noise and is fully explained by a seasonal-factor miss (NSA fell 17,803 where the factors expected 25,633), so nothing is attributed to it in either direction. The mechanism was never tested — only the sizing was, and the sizing was knowably wrong at registration. **Two things this lesson earned that it did not have on 7/24:** (a) the confidence cap it produced *worked* — LAB-17 scored Brier **0.09**, the book's best row and its first correctly-hedged threshold call (§A finding 3); (b) but capping confidence is the **consolation prize**, not the fix — the test should have been registered against **CA state-level weekly claims**, where a 4,665-worker cohort is a detectable fraction, or not registered as a threshold at all. **Standing rule: the cohort-to-base ratio is `PREDICTIONS_SCOREBOARD.md` §C **gate #4** — computed and written into the prediction's Notes before any cohort→aggregate threshold is registered.** ⚠️ **Installed 2026-07-31, and the delay is part of the lesson:** L-08 was written here on 7/24, but §D step 4 (*"write the lesson **and** add a §C gate pointing to it"*) was only half-executed — for a week this file and §A both **asserted** the gate existed while §C had ten gates and none of them was this one. A control that is documented-but-absent is worse than a known gap, because the next reader stops looking. Caught in the 7/31 boot-doc audit as items B1/B2 and fixed atomically with the §C install and the `CLAUDE.md` B4 list.

## L-07 — Book the announcement TYPE (voluntary offer vs involuntary RIF); verify WARN filings exist before attributing a WARN surge to a company
**Pattern:** LABOR carried "MSFT 8,750 (Jun 6)" in the priced-cuts list and attributed part of the mid-June WARN surge to "MSFT WARNs landing." The Jul-2 sweep found: (a) the 8,750 was a **voluntary-retirement OFFER program** (Rule of 70; offers Apr 23-26, window closed ~Jun 6; acceptances never public) — VR acceptances mostly do NOT file UI claims, so booking it as layoffs overstates the claims pipeline; (b) **Microsoft has zero 2026 WARN filings** — the surge attribution had a phantom leg, which propagated into a pre-registered prediction's mechanism text (LAB-17) within hours of writing it.
**Fix:** (1) Every entry in an announcement ledger carries its TYPE: VR-offer / involuntary RIF / closure / contract-churn — they have different claims-flow signatures (offers ≈ 0 claims; contractor-swap WARNs can convert to ~0, e.g. Fortrex TX 85→0; remote-heavy cuts skip WARN entirely, e.g. Rackspace 750). (2) Before naming a company as a WARN-surge driver, confirm its filings exist in a tracker/state primary. (3) When a correction lands, grep ALL derivative surfaces same-day (auto-memory `[[finding_verification_correction_downstream_propagation]]`). **Promoted to auto-memory `[[finding_announcement_type_and_filing_precheck]]` 2026-07-24** (transferable to any agent aggregating announcements — BROCK, CARL, REGINALD, CORAL).
**First seen:** Jul 2 2026 PM sweep (MSFT VR correction; LAB-17 mechanism re-worded same-day).

## L-06 — Ratio-gauge thresholds (U-3) can be silently defeated by the DENOMINATOR; playbook grids need a supply-artifact branch
**Pattern:** The Jul-2 NFP playbook grid conditioned every branch on (headline NFP × U-3-up-or-flat). The tape printed the un-written cell: NFP +57K (<100K) **with U-3 FALLING to 4.2%** — because the labor force shrank 720K (participation −0.3pp) and mechanically suppressed the rate. T-06 ("NFP <100K + U-3 jump") could not fire on a genuinely weak print; LAB-02 (U-3 ≥4.7%) died not because labor demand held but because the *gauge* was structurally suppressed. Same trap forward: LAB-12 (U-3 ≥5.0%) and T-03/T-04 are all denominated in a rate whose denominator is now the fastest-moving part.
**Fix:** (1) Every U-3-conditioned threshold gets graded **JOINTLY with LFPR** — a participation-driven U-3 move counts as NO-SIGNAL, not as strength/weakness. (2) Print-reaction grids must carry an explicit third-axis branch for the denominator artifact (here: "NFP <100K + U-3 ↓ + LFPR ↓ = supply-shrink; grade on claims/hires instead"). (3) The pre-registered cross-domain signature (U-3↓ + wages↑ + LFPR↓ = supply shock, from the Jun-16 ICE cross-domain table) is what made the print gradable same-day — keep pre-registering discriminating signatures BEFORE the release. Promoted to auto-memory `[[finding_ratio_gauge_denominator_branch]]`.
**First seen:** NFP June 2026 (Jul 2 print).

## L-05 — Cross-domain signals can be scored on the WRONG SIDE of the convergence matrix
**Pattern:** The convergence matrix carried ICE enforcement (5🔴🔴) and H-2A bottleneck (4🔴) as bearish-employment convergence — +9 points of a 57/85. But those are MARCO labor-*supply* signals, and a migration-driven supply cut keeps unemployment *low* / wages *up* — the **opposite** of LABOR's demand-weakness (rising-claims/U-3) thesis. The instinct to "re-verify the stale carries" would have *confirmed* them (ICE had actually strengthened — funding signed into law Jun 10) and left the 9 mis-signed points in place. The defect was sign, not staleness.
**Fix:** For every cross-domain vector, write down which way it moves LABOR's headline metric (claims/U-3). If it moves it the wrong way or sideways, it's CONTEXT, not convergence — pull it into a separate cross-domain table, keep only the same-direction slice (here: ICE worksite-disruption → on-site layoffs, scored 2). Watch supply-vs-demand inversions specifically (a supply shock and a demand shock move quantity the same way but slack/price the opposite way). LABOR domain explicitly EXCLUDES migration-driven displacement (= MARCO) — so importing ICE/H-2A at full bearish weight was also scope creep. Promoted to auto-memory `[[finding_convergence_sign_check]]` (transferable to any agent with a convergence matrix).
**First seen:** Jun 16 2026 (Orc-prompted thesis-honesty pass; matrix 57/85 → 48/80).

## L-04 — Structured ledgers (VX/KB/FLOW) drift months behind the narrative STATUS
**Pattern:** STATUS.md gets refreshed every session, but the C3 workbook sync keeps getting deferred — so on Jun 14 the VX claims rows were still dated **Mar 9** (213K initial / 1.868M CC with dead DHS-shutdown caveats), and FLOW still read "Claims 209K / NFP +50K (Dec)." A query against the ledger returns confidently-wrong stale values with **no staleness signal** — the row just shows an old "Last Updated" date a reader may not check. Orc's framing: "this is how a ledger gap silently distorts a trajectory read later."
**Fix:** (1) When deferring full C3, still refresh the **load-bearing rows** (claims/NFP/U-3) — they're cheap and most-queried. (2) Do **not** blanket-stamp a too-recent `[STALE date]` — the rows carry *their own* (often much older) dates; a generous stamp overstates freshness. (3) Treat a 2+-cycle C3 deferral as a real debt, not a footnote. Promoted to auto-memory `[[finding_ledger_drift_behind_narrative]]` (transferable to any agent with a STATUS+workbook split — CARL/REGINALD/BROCK/HENRY).
**First seen:** Jun 14 2026 (Orc-flagged; partial fix = 2 claims rows refreshed, rest still owed).

## L-03 — DOGE/government YoY comps are base-effect-poisoned
**Pattern:** Challenger YTD job-cuts showed -43% YoY (and DOGE-specific -94% YoY) in mid-2026 — which reads as "layoffs improving" but is an artifact of the inflated 2025 DOGE base (284,827 federal cuts in 2025 vs ~16K in 2026).
**Fix:** For any DOGE/federal-workforce YoY series, anchor to a pre-DOGE baseline (or use level + MoM), not the 2025-comp YoY. The comp laps a structural one-off. See auto-memory `[[feedback_yoy_baseeffect_use_multiyear_stack]]`.
**First seen:** Challenger May 2026 (Jun 5 print).

## L-02 — Track the *revised* NFP series, not just the first print
**Pattern:** The May NFP print (Jun 5) revised March/April UP by a net +93K (April 115K→179K). The original prints understated; the revision flipped the thesis read and pushed March across the Kill-A 200K line. Reasoning off first-print NFP would have kept the bearish "realization-weak" read alive longer than the data justified.
**Fix:** At every NFP, re-read the revised back-months, not only the headline. Exit/Kill rules (Kill A: NFP ≥200K ×3) must be evaluated on the revised series. The first print is the noisiest data point in the release.
**First seen:** NFP May 2026 (Jun 5 print).

## L-01 — Company-tier revenue ≠ industry employment
**Pattern:** LAB-01 ("Temp YoY <-6%") was FALSIFIED because the call conflated staffing-firm revenue weakness (KELYA/RHI down double digits) with BLS CES temp-help *employment* — which was actually expanding (+7.9K MoM Apr; ASA +4.9% YoY). Company-level reads lag and don't equal the industry-level series.
**Fix:** Pull the canonical industry series (BLS CES `TEMPHELPS`, ASA index) for any employment prediction — not the company proxy. Match the prediction's measure to its canonical data source. See auto-memory `[[feedback_prediction_canonical_measure]]`.
**First seen:** LAB-01 resolution (Jun 2 2026).
