## L-16 — A finished judgement filed under a NON-TERMINAL disposition token re-enters the queue forever: the six parks were one wrong word, not six failures to assess
**Pattern:** `SIG-W-20260727-006` (WALTER, grocery unit sales) parked **six consecutive sessions** and was escalated to PROME as a routing problem. **It was not a routing problem, and it was not neglect.** The 7/31 `board_log.tsv` row already contained a *correct and complete* merits assessment — *"consumer-demand datum, CARL's domain not mine; the LABOR-side read is second-order; NOT threshold-relevant: no LABOR threshold is denominated in consumer volumes, and no vector moves on it."* **That is a finished verdict.** It was filed with `disposition=deferred`. Because `deferred` is a **non-terminal** state, every subsequent boot's un-dispositioned scan re-surfaced it, and each session re-read it, re-agreed, and re-parked it — **five more times, at full attention cost each time, with the escalation note growing angrier at a queue that was working exactly as specified.**
**Why it is invisible from the inside:** the park notes each *described* the assessment, so every session had evidence the merits were known — and still could not close it, because the closing action is choosing a different **token**, not doing more analysis. **Re-reading the item cannot fix it: the defect is one field wide.** My own escalation (*"please route or kill it — I will not re-park it a seventh time"*) asked PROME to solve a problem entirely inside my own ledger.
**The second half, and it is the reason not to just auto-close stale items:** the merits **changed while it sat**. On 7/31 the "second-order, not my lane" call was right. On **8/7** the payroll print landed retail trade −19K with **warehouse clubs/supercenters −21K** — the payroll counterpart of precisely the grocery-volume channel the signal measures — turning it into a dated demand-side antecedent for a sector loss my own header had written off as *"off my axis."* **So the correct terminal disposition on 8/12 was `acted`, not the `info-only` that would have been correct on 7/31.** A stale item is not automatically a dead item (`[[finding_dated_carry_item_has_no_expiry_check]]`).
**Fix:** (1) **Every inbox/board item exits a session in a TERMINAL state — `acted`, `info-only`, or `skipped`-with-reason. `deferred` is legitimate only with a written fold-by DATE and a named blocker**, and a blocker of *"needs a decision I have already made"* is not one. (2) **When you find yourself agreeing with your own prior park note, that is the signal to CLOSE it, not to re-park it** — repeated agreement is a completed assessment, not an ongoing one (sibling of `[[finding_audit_resolution_path_before_reattempt]]`: repeated deferral of a supposedly easy item means the PATH is blocked, and here the path was the vocabulary). (3) **"No action needed from me" ≠ "not yet actioned"** — the first is terminal and the second is not, and the fleet's `STATE_VOCABULARY.md` exists precisely so these do not collapse into one token. (4) **Before escalating a stuck item, check whether the stuckness is inside your own ledger.** (5) **On closing an aged item, re-read its merits against TODAY's data before choosing the token** — this one had changed value while parked.
**First seen:** SIG-W-20260727-006, parked 7/31 → 8/5 → 8/7 (×3 logged, six sessions surfaced), closed `acted` 2026-08-12. Partners with `[[finding_registered_gate_captures_attention]]` — the escalation absorbed the attention the one-field fix needed.

## L-15 — Base-rate a threshold BEFORE building it: a LEVEL bar is usually a descriptor, and "don't build it" is a legitimate answer
**Pattern:** A single BD pass on 2026-08-07 base-rated six candidate thresholds against 1990-2026 monthly data. **Three results, and two of them cancelled the work the register had asked for.**
**(a) The level-vs-delta failure.** T-03 (*U-3 ≥4.7%*) and T-04 (*U-3 ≥5.0%*) had been live escalation triggers for months. Measured: the 4.7% bar was true in **65.4% of all months since 1990** and separated recession from non-recession by only **+12.9pp**; the 5.0% bar, **58.1%** and **+13.8pp**. **A condition that holds in two-thirds of all history is a description of the world, not a trigger** — it tells a downstream agent nothing when it fires. The *delta* form of the same series is fine (U-3 rose ≥0.2pp m/m separates **+50.7pp**), which is the whole lesson in one comparison: **the series was never the problem; the LEVEL construction was.** Replacements (EPOP drawdowns) separate **+53.6 to +63.3pp** at base rates of 8-12%.
**(b) "Register a threshold" is a hypothesis, not a task — and it can be REFUTED.** BD-16 asked for an up-side continuing-claims trigger. Testing first: CC **lags** initial claims (peak YoY correlation at **+1 month, r=0.907**; CC-leads shifts only r=0.32-0.52), and a **CC-only** stress signal fired in 5.2% of months and was in or near a recession **9%** of the time. **A CC-only trigger would be wrong nine times in ten, so it was killed rather than built.** BD-17 asked for a government-payroll trigger after a −53K print at the 5.7th percentile: government declines separate recession from non-recession by **+5.0pp** at ≤−25K, **+0.0pp** at ≤−50K, and **−4.0pp** on a 3-month sum — *it fires more often outside recessions*. Also killed. **Two of three build items closed by measurement showing they should not exist.**
**(c) The same measurement stopped a matrix error.** July's −50K local-government-education loss was the largest single sector decline in the print and the obvious candidate for scoring a vector up. **Because government payrolls carry no cycle information, scoring it would have imported noise into the convergence total** — so the vector was renamed (scope widened) and **held at 1**. An unusual *level* is not a signal; only a level that discriminates is.
**Fix:** (1) **Before registering ANY threshold, compute two numbers: its unconditional base rate, and its separation between the states it is meant to distinguish.** A trigger with <~20pp separation is a descriptor — do not ship it. (2) **Prefer deltas and drawdowns to levels** unless the level is itself the decision boundary (a stop, a covenant). Levels drift with regime; changes do not (`[[finding_escalation_line_needs_delta_not_level]]`). (3) **Treat "build a threshold" as a question with a real NO branch**, and record the NO with its numbers — a killed threshold is a permanent saving, and the register entry stops the same idea being re-proposed. (4) **Base-rate CONJUNCTIONS jointly, never by multiplying the legs** — the T-04 compound measured 8.2%, which is not the product of its parts (BD-11's root cause, recurring). (5) **Sanity check every new spec against the live print: if it fires the day you write it, suspect yourself.** None of the three adopted here fires today, which is weak evidence they were not reverse-engineered. (6) Where a discredited gauge is still what the market watches (U-3), **keep publishing it and move the trigger** — demote, don't delete.
**First seen:** BD-15/16/17 pass, 2026-08-07 — the session immediately after BD-11 established joint base-rating as the standard.

## L-14 — A GROSS FLOW cannot falsify a claim about a NET count; and check whether the decoupling is rare *in general* or rare *except right now*
**Pattern:** LABOR's bull-side kill (the freeze-thaw gate) had a leg denominated in **JOLTS gross hires >5.5M**, and the Aug 3–7 grading card scored vector 4 off **gross-hires bands**. On 8/4 hires printed **5,348K, +96K** — I graded card §2b's "thawing at the margin" band, cut vector 4, and wrote that *"the post-don't-hire gap is closing from the hiring side."* **Three days later, revising the gate, I computed the other side of the identity: separations rose +91K to 5,351K, so JOLTS NET (hires − separations) was −3K.** May was −8K. **Hires and separations rose together — that is churn, not net hiring**, and the "closing from the hiring side" claim does not survive it. **The freeze thesis is a claim about NET employment; I was testing it with a GROSS flow, which is arithmetically incapable of answering it** — gross hires can rise without limit while net is flat or negative, provided separations keep pace.
**The second half of the lesson is the part I nearly missed.** My first instinct was "gross and net decouple often, so gross is just a bad measure." **Measured, that is false: gross hires rose in 66 months since 2015 and net was ≤0 in only 2 of them — 3%.** Direction of gross agrees with direction of net **73%** of the time. **So gross hires is *usually* a serviceable proxy — and the two exceptions in eleven years are May-2026 and June-2026, i.e. exactly the two prints I graded.** That is a much sharper and more useful finding than "the measure is bad": **we are currently in the rare regime where the proxy inverts, and the base rate is what proves it is a regime rather than noise.** Had I stopped at "the proxy is fine 97% of the time" I would have kept the grade; had I stopped at "the proxy is bad" I would have thrown away a measure that works.
**Fix:** (1) **Before denominating a threshold in a flow, write down whether the claim is about a GROSS flow or a NET stock/count, and match them.** If the thesis says "employment is not growing," the test is a net measure. (2) **When both sides of an identity are published, compute both** — JOLTS publishes hires *and* separations; using one without the other is a choice, and it should be a stated one. (3) **When a proxy and its target disagree, base-rate the disagreement before deciding what it means.** Rare-in-general + happening-now is a *regime* finding and belongs in the thesis; common-in-general is a *measure* finding and belongs in the spec. **They call for opposite actions.** (4) **Do NOT quietly restore a score the corrected measure would justify, on the day the correction favours your book.** Here the corrected measure argued for restoring vector 4 to a more bearish value on a bearish print day — I left the score unchanged, recorded the defect, and **pre-committed the corrected re-grade to the next release before the data existed** (`[[feedback_dont_bank_unpassed_forecast]]`). (5) Keep the counter visible: the separations rise was **quits-driven** (quits rate back to 2.0%), and rising quits are worker confidence, not distress — so "net ≤0" does not automatically mean "still frozen." **A high-churn/zero-net market is a third state**, distinct from both freeze and thaw, and neither my vectors nor my gate had a name for it before today.
**First seen:** JOLTS June 2026, graded 2026-08-05 on gross hires, corrected 2026-08-07 while base-rating the freeze-thaw gate for BD-11 — i.e. **found by a spec revision, not by any grading step.**

## L-16 — A frozen card's branch set must PARTITION on ONE surface, or your post-hoc judgment picks the winner
**Pattern:** The 8/19 FOMC card's §3a branches were not mutually exclusive. **W-2** was written as a claim about the *participants'* language (*"`strengthened` persists unchanged"*); **W-3** as a claim about *any* softening *anywhere* in the document (*"softening appears only in staff review"*). The minutes satisfied **both** on the letter — "strengthened" survived verbatim (W-2) *and* the Staff Review said *"Nonfarm payroll employment growth slowed in June"* (W-3). I graded **W-2** on substance, and the substance argument is sound — a one-month data note with an explicit offset is not a *characterisation* change. **But the call was made AFTER seeing the text, by me, which is the exact thing a frozen card exists to prevent.** A card that lets the grader choose between two live branches has, at that point, the epistemic status of no card at all.
**Why it happened:** the branches were written at different *altitudes* without noticing — one scoped to a section, one scoped to the document. They read as a clean ladder (walked back → survived → hedged → absent) precisely because the scope shift is invisible when you write them in sequence.
**Fix (cheap, pre-freeze, mechanical):** before freezing any card, **for each pair of branches, try to write a single outcome that satisfies both.** If you can, the partition is broken — re-scope every branch to the *same named surface* ("the Participants' Views section," not "the minutes"). **Do this on the Aug-28 QCEW card before its print, not on print morning.**
**Sibling to L-09** (a card grading two instrument types needs a separate baseline for each): L-09 is about *baselines*, L-16 is about *branch scopes*. Both are failures to name the surface a claim is about.
**First seen:** FOMC minutes grade, 2026-08-20 (card §6 D-i). Cost: no score moved, but the grade's authority is weaker than it looks and I have said so on the card.

## L-17 — A frozen card cannot help if nobody boots; and the branch that "cannot happen" is the one you forgot to enumerate
**Pattern, two halves from one session.** **(a)** The 8/13 claims card and the 8/19 FOMC card both sat **unconsumed** — 7 days and 1 day — because no session ran between 8/12 and 8/20. Both graded cleanly the moment someone looked. **The cards worked; the summons did not.** B5b catches a stale card *at the next boot*, which is a real backstop and also a bounded one — it cannot summon a session. **(b)** The 8/19 card's §3b attribution branches (S-1 supply-as-driver / S-2 demand-only / S-3 absent) were **jointly unsatisfiable by the actual outcome**: supply language was present but invoked as a *balance* argument ("labor demand and supply in balance," "consistent with recent labor force growth"), which is the **opposite polarity** to S-1's assumption that supply would be raised to *excuse weakness*. Plus a third mechanism with no slot at all (AI/productivity "keeping both hiring and firing low").
**Fix:** **(a)** stop treating B5b as cover for the whole problem — it is the *catch*, and **BD-02 + an external CATALYSTS-driven alert (PROME's) is the prevention.** Say which one you are relying on. **(b)** when enumerating branches over a *qualitative* text, add an explicit **"present but in an unanticipated polarity/role"** branch, and pre-commit it to **move no score**. My cards already have the "moves no score" convention for unenumerated outcomes; the improvement is to *anticipate the class* rather than discover it.
**First seen:** 2026-08-20 (both cards).

## L-13 — An N-of-M implication test must be BALANCED between intentions and realized counts, or it fires on the intentions half alone
**Pattern:** The Aug 3–7 grading card pre-registered implication **I-1** (*"the freeze is intact and deepening"*) as failing if **≥2 of four** conditions landed: hires >5.3M · NFP ≥150K · ISM employment ≥50.0 · Challenger AI collapsing. On 8/5 two landed — **JOLTS hires 5,348K and ISM Mfg employment 52.8** — so I-1 failed on its own terms and, per the card's §7 pre-commitment, I **called the thaw that week**. **Within 48 hours both hard counts went the other way: NFP printed −23,000 with −103K of revisions, and ISM *Services* employment fell back to 47.4.** The rule fired correctly; the *call it produced* was contradicted almost immediately. **The defect is in the composition of the condition set, not in the logic or the discipline.** 🔧 **CLASSIFICATION CORRECTED 2026-08-07, two days after this lesson was written — and the correction makes it sharper, not weaker.** This paragraph originally counted **JOLTS hires as an intentions measure**, which is **wrong**: JOLTS hires is establishments reporting hires that actually occurred, i.e. a **realized** measure. The honest split of I-1's four conditions is: **ISM employment (diffusion index — intentions) · Challenger AI share (announcements — intentions) · NFP (realized NET count) · JOLTS hires (realized GROSS flow).** So the defect is **two-dimensional, and the second dimension is the one that actually bit**: of the two conditions that landed, one was an intentions measure and the other was a **gross flow standing in for a net claim** — and JOLTS *net* was **−3K** that month (**L-14**). **Neither landing condition was a realized net count, which is the only kind that can confirm "the freeze is ending."** *(Recording the misclassification rather than silently fixing it: I wrote this lesson at speed on the day of the failure and got a taxonomy detail wrong inside a lesson about taxonomy. The fix below is amended accordingly.)* So a 2-of-4 bar could be cleared *entirely* by the intentions half, which is exactly what happened — and intentions are the layer that moves first and reverses most. The card's own §7 correctly identified that I was most exposed to a hot week; it did not notice that the instrument built to detect one could be satisfied without a single hard count confirming.
**Fix:** (1) **When pre-registering an N-of-M test, tag every condition on BOTH axes — `INTENTIONS`/`REALIZED` and `GROSS`/`NET` — and require that at least one condition which is *both* REALIZED *and* NET be among those that clear the bar.** *(Amended 8/7 from a one-axis rule; the one-axis version would still have passed I-1, because JOLTS hires is realized.)* **Enforced structurally where possible rather than by counting** — the revised freeze-thaw gate makes its realized-net leg **mandatory** (`LEG A AND (B OR C)`), so it cannot fire on the other legs alone no matter how many of them land. A 2-of-4 that can be satisfied by two intentions readings is really a 2-of-3 intentions test with a decorative fourth condition. (2) **Weight by reversal rate, not by availability.** Survey and openings data are abundant and print early, which is why they dominate condition sets — and they are precisely the series most likely to be revised or reversed (JOLTS May hires revised **up** 82K; ISM Services employment 51.2 → 47.4 in one month). (3) **State the failure mode on the card**: *"this test can be cleared without any realized count confirming"* is a one-line pre-mortem that would have flagged it at freeze time. (4) **This does NOT license un-saying the call.** The rule was pre-committed, it fired, and I said what it told me to say — retro-actively rescuing the conclusion because the next print disagreed is exactly the improvisation frozen cards exist to prevent. **Fix the instrument for next time; leave the graded call standing.** (5) Related but distinct from **L-09** (grade branch and implication separately) — that lesson is about *scoring* an implication, this one is about *constructing* one. Cross-ref `[[finding_compound_gate_jointly_unsatisfiable]]` for the mirror failure (a gate that cannot fire); this is a gate that fires **too easily on one layer of evidence**.
**First seen:** I-1, registered 2026-07-31, fired 2026-08-05, contradicted by the realized count 2026-08-07 — **all three inside one week**, which is what made it visible at all.

## L-12 — A cross-check with a FREE PARAMETER validates nothing; and `[CONF]` must name a primary, not a consensus of secondaries
**Pattern:** From 2026-07-06 to 2026-08-05 STATUS carried **ISM Services June employment = 47.4**, described as *"sub-50, ~4th straight month contracting,"* and used it as the survey-layer confirmation of the freeze thesis. **The actual figure is 51.2 — an expansion, up 3.3pp from May's 47.9** (*"The Employment Index expanded for the first time in four months..."*, ISM release, verbatim). The same row also carried **Business Activity 56.1** against an actual **55.4**. Two independent errors, one month, one row, and the employment one was **wrong in sign** — it inverted a signal rather than degrading it. **The source tag is the whole lesson: `[CONF] 2 independent web reads + arithmetic cross-check (4 sub-indexes avg 54.0)`.** Both halves failed, in different ways. **(a) "2 independent web reads" is not independence** — secondary aggregators copy each other, so agreement between two of them is one observation reported twice, and neither is the ISM release. **No primary was ever opened**, yet the row was tagged `[CONF]`, which in my own output rules means *confirmed data with source + date*. **(b) The arithmetic cross-check was STRUCTURALLY INCAPABLE of catching the error, which is worse than not running one.** The ISM headline is the mean of four sub-indexes (Business Activity · New Orders · Employment · Supplier Deliveries). 🔧 **KNOWNS-COUNT MADE PRECISE 2026-08-07** (Will-review R6; verified against the pre-correction blob `2afc819b5`, not from memory): at check time the row held **headline 54.0 (correct)**, **Business Activity 56.1 — itself WRONG (actual 55.4)**, **Employment 47.4 — the value under test, wrong**, and **New Orders + Supplier Deliveries ABSENT ENTIRELY.** So of the three sub-indexes other than the one being tested, **I held zero correctly**: the check had **two free parameters AND one wrong input**, and could not produce a single implied value at all. ⚠️ **The demonstration this lesson used to give — *"with the true 51.2 it implies Supplier Deliveries 54.3; with the false 47.4 it implies 58.1, both pass"* — is an ANACHRONISM and is struck as a reconstruction.** It was computed on 8/5 with the *corrected* BA 55.4 and NO 55.1, values the 7/6 checker never had. **It remains a valid illustration of the ONE-unknown case** (which is why it is kept here, labelled) — the actual 7/6 failure was worse than it depicts. **The methodological lesson survives both versions and is strengthened: a cross-check with ANY free parameter validates nothing, and a wrong known makes it actively misleading.** A check that cannot fail is not evidence — it is a ritual that manufactures confidence, and here it converted a bad secondary read into a `[CONF]` tag that then survived a month of boots, a spine-freshness gate, two doc audits and a `consumer_check` sweep, because **every one of those mechanisms tests freshness, propagation or format — none tests whether the number was ever right** (`[[finding_freshness_check_cannot_catch_a_fresh_lie]]`). **The cost was not the number, it was a month of the wrong story:** the survey-employment thaw actually began in **June services**, so when ISM Mfg printed 52.8 on 8/3 I read it as an isolated first-ever break when it was the **second** expansion signal across two sectors — and my 7/6 claim that the freeze was *"corroborated on the survey AND hard-filing layers"* had been half-false from the day it was written. It was published, and real consumers took it (HENRY's CPI-node scenario table, CARL's squeeze backdrop, and **two PROME surfaces including a fleet-distributed digest**).
**Fix:** (1) **Count the unknowns before trusting an identity check.** An arithmetic cross-check is only a test when the number of unknowns is **zero** — i.e. you have every other input independently. With `n` unknowns it has `n` degrees of freedom and confirms nothing. **If you cannot state what the check would have looked like had it FAILED, you did not run a check** (`[[finding_test_the_guard_not_just_the_guarded]]`). (2) **`[CONF]` requires a NAMED PRIMARY.** Two secondary reads that agree are one source, not two. Where only secondaries exist, the honest tag is `[EST]` or a new `[2ND]` — never `[CONF]`. Raised to PROME as a possible fleet-wide tag rule, since `[CONF]` is load-bearing across every agent's dashboard. (3) **For any recurring release I score a vector on, pull the issuer's own release — it is one fetch.** ISM, BLS and DOL releases are free and public; there was never a reason to be reading aggregators for a number that anchors a convergence vector. (4) **A sign error hides better than a magnitude error.** 47.4 was *plausible* — it sat right next to May's 47.9 and continued an existing narrative, which is exactly why nothing flagged it. **Numbers that confirm the current story deserve the primary check most**, not least (`[[finding_plausible_stale_value_evades_review]]`). (5) **When correcting, separate the legs.** The 7/6 claim had a survey leg (false) and a hard-filing WARN leg (independently sourced, true). Retract the leg that failed and say explicitly that the other survives — over-retraction is its own error class (**L-11**).
**First seen:** ISM Services June 2026 employment, published 2026-07-06, **caught 2026-08-05** — and caught only by accident: a search run to find *July's* Services print surfaced June's real figure in the results. **No mechanism in my boot or closeout would have found it.** That is the honest provenance and it is why fix (3) is stated as a standing rule rather than a reminder.

## L-11 — Propagating a correction is a per-surface READ, not a pattern-match: sweeps fail in BOTH directions, and the over-correction deletes true statements
**Pattern:** On 7/31 I found that STATUS's 7/16 German-PMI note was sign-confused — it said German mfg expansion *"tempers the AT-RISK case,"* when expansion abroad with a ~2-mo US lead argues US ISM employment goes **UP**, i.e. it *raises* breach risk (**L-05** class). Correct catch, correct strike. I then propagated that strike to `NEXUS_BRIEF.md` "for consistency" — **without re-reading what the brief actually said.** The brief said *"tempers the **sub-49-break** case,"* which is **sign-CORRECT** (expansion arguing UP does argue against a break *down* through 49). The two surfaces said **different things**; only one was wrong. So I deleted a **true counter-signal** from the surface NEXUS reads, and wrote a self-critical commit note (*"a sweep that fixes one surface and misses another is the recurring failure"*) that was **itself factually wrong** — the brief had never carried the error. Caught by PROME's round-2 audit; verified against the pre-commit blob (`d95f3f34^`) before reversing. **The night produced both failure directions four hours apart:** *under-swept-but-claimed-done* (three surfaces still carrying the retired "0.4" inside a file whose own header announced that sweep complete) and *over-swept-and-claimed-behind* (this). A sweep's self-report is unreliable in **both** directions, so neither "done" nor "I missed one" should be believed without the per-surface read.
**Fix:** (1) When propagating a correction, **open each target surface and read its actual wording before editing it.** Surfaces that *look* like they carry the same error frequently carry a differently-scoped — and sometimes correct — version. A correction is N reads, not one find-and-replace (`[[finding_verification_correction_downstream_propagation]]` covers finding all instances; this is the complementary failure — *mis-identifying* an instance). (2) **Deleting a true statement is a real cost, not a safe default.** Over-correction feels conservative because it looks like extra rigour, but it silently removes evidence from consumers — here, a genuine counter against the deep-pullback bands of a card grading the next trading morning. (3) **Verify the blob, not the memory** — the reversal was only provable because the pre-commit text was recoverable in git (`[[finding_loadbearing_number_must_be_reproducible]]`). (4) **Distinguish what a restored counter acts on:** the German/S&P leads move **P(band)**, not **V(band)** — conditional on landing in the pullback bands the leads are *contradicted*. Restoring a counter is not a licence to re-weight the values it doesn't touch.
**First seen:** German-PMI strike, made and reversed **2026-07-31**, both within the same evening and both before the Aug-3 print it bears on.

## L-10 — A "holds through DATE" prediction is N draws, not one call — decompose to per-draw survival; and a risk LABEL is not a REPRICE
**Pattern:** LAB-06 (*"ISM Mfg Employment <50 through 2026"*) sat at **80%** for a month while its own ledger note read *"0.4pt from breach — AT RISK."* Both halves of that were wrong. **(a) The structure was never priced.** This is a *survive-N-draws* claim — any single print ≥50.0 kills it permanently — with **5 prints remaining**. So 80% overall was implicitly asserting **80^(1/5) = 95.6% survival per print**, from **49.7** while the series climbed **+1.1 in June and +2.2 over two months**. The move that kills it (**+0.3**) was *smaller than its recent average monthly move*. Nobody ever computed the per-draw number, so an indefensible claim looked like a confident one. **(b) The "AT RISK" label was doing the work the number should have done.** The marker had been on the row since 7/1 and the confidence field was untouched — the label discharged the *feeling* of having updated while the value that actually scores stayed put. **(c) Two spec defects rode along undetected underneath the label:** every surface said *"0.4pt from breach"* when `<50` is falsified by `≥50.0`, so from 49.7 the true distance was **0.3** — one tick nearer than I'd been telling myself and everyone downstream; and *"through 2026"* vs a 2026-12-31 due date was never resolved (December data publishes ~Jan 2027, i.e. **after** the due date — N=5, not 6). **(d) A sign-confused counter-signal survived inside the same row:** the 7/16 German-PMI note claimed expansion abroad *"tempers the AT-RISK case,"* but German mfg expansion with a ~2-mo US lead argues US ISM employment goes **UP** — it *raises* breach risk. Both upstream leads pointed the same way (toward breach) and the row read one of them backwards (**L-05** class).
**Fix:** (1) For any *"X stays above/below L through DATE"* claim, **count N and compute `conf^(1/N)` at registration** — if the per-draw survival you're implicitly asserting isn't defensible against the series' own monthly volatility and current distance-to-line, the headline confidence is wrong. Heuristic: **if the move that kills it is smaller than the series' recent average monthly move, no multi-draw call belongs above ~50%.** Now **§C gate #12**. (2) **Freeze which prints qualify at registration** — publication lag decides membership, not the data month. (3) **Never let a qualitative risk marker stand on a row whose confidence hasn't moved**: either move the number or delete the label. Now **§C gate #13**, and it runs as a **sweep over the whole open book at C2**, not only the row in hand. (4) State thresholds at the tick that actually falsifies them (`<50` → `≥50.0`), and recompute distance-to-line every time the level moves. (5) **The general lesson: an annotation is not an update.** LABOR's signature failure is high-confidence threshold calls (§A: 0/3), and this is how they survive scrutiny — the row *looks* examined because it carries a warning.
**First seen:** LAB-06, repriced **80% → 20% on 2026-07-31, pre-print and unforced** (the July print lands ~Aug 3). The reprice was prompted by asking "what would I be embarrassed by on Monday?", not by any new data — every input had been on the row since July 1.
**⚠️ RECURRED THE SAME DAY, ONE LAYER DOWN — appended 2026-07-31 eve.** Having decomposed LAB-06 into per-draw survival to reprice it, I then wrote the Aug-3 grading card's outcome bands **ignoring that same decomposition**: Band B said a survival at 49.5–49.9 meant *"no confidence change — the hazard that produced the 20% is unchanged."* That conflates **per-draw hazard** with **survival probability**. Surviving July *removes a draw*, so LAB-06 must rise mechanically even if the hazard is literally unchanged; the floor is the remaining-path product (0.68×0.75×0.78×0.80 = **31.8%**), not zero change. Worse, the bands were **non-monotone** — Band C (a survival *further* from the line) awarded +10pp while Band B awarded 0 — which is the tell that should have caught it without any arithmetic at all. Caught by PROME's Will-directed audit, **fixed pre-print**. **Three things this adds:** (1) **The gate you just built is not automatically applied to the artifact you build next.** #12 was written that morning and violated that afternoon, in the first card written after it shipped — a new gate covers *registration*, and nobody had asked whether it also governs *grading bands*. When you install a gate, sweep the adjacent artifact class the same session. (2) **Grading-card bands must reproduce the headline confidence** under the law of total probability, and must be **monotone** in the evidence direction — a 30-second check that catches this class without re-deriving the model (now **§C gate #14a**). (3) **A fix supplied by an auditor still needs verifying, and needs carrying through.** PROME's diagnosis was right, but its implied July survival (63%) was an artifact of dividing the *posted* 20% by the path rather than the *modeled* 16%, and its prescribed fix — "raise Band B" — would have **inverted** the card against Band C had I applied it as given. Fixing B forced C and D up too (`[[finding_verify_recommended_fix_not_just_finding]]`). **A fourth, separable point (→ §C gate #14b):** the same card asserted its Brier treatment was *"per §A convention"* when the convention is **silent** on a pre-print unforced reprice — its whole rationale is about conceding *after* the evidence turned. Asserting a control that does not reach the case is the **documented-but-absent** pattern from L-08's own delay, recurring within a week. The resolution keeps the as-made 0.64 as the headline and records the pre-print value as a labelled diagnostic only — legitimate solely because it is **symmetric** (a pre-print reprice that is wrong the other way is penalised by the same amount) and because it must be **declared before the print**, never claimed retroactively.

## L-09 — An ABSENCE-tell is only evidence if the instrument had room to speak; and diff like-for-like (statement↔statement, minutes↔minutes)
**Pattern:** The frozen FOMC labor-language card (7/24, graded 7/31) leaned much of its ~70% branch (a) on **absence** tells — *"no mention of participation, the hiring rate, job-finding rate, or long-term unemployment."* Those tells all fired. But they fired **trivially**: the 7/29 statement body is ~140 words, and Warsh has explicitly *"pulled back from forward guidance"* and is *"somewhat spare on our words"* by design. In a deliberately spare instrument, "the Fed didn't mention X" is nearly costless to observe and carries almost no information — the same tell would have fired on a Fed that fully agreed with me. **The tells that actually carried information were (i) the PRESENCE tells** (*"labor markets, solid, steady"*; the *"more or less at equilibrium"* reaction function) **and (ii) the response to a direct expert question** (Timiraos offered the labor-transmission channel by name and Warsh declined it). A refusal under direct questioning is strong evidence; silence in a document that says little about anything is weak. **Second, separate defect in the same card:** §2's baseline was the June **minutes**, and a tell was written against minutes language (*"payroll gains had strengthened this year"*) — then graded against a **statement**. That phrase was never in a statement. Different instrument, different authorship, different cadence: the diff was **not like-for-like** and the tell had to be scored ungradable and deferred to the July minutes (~Aug 19).
**Fix:** (1) **Before pre-registering a language read, base-rate the instrument** — how many words does this issuer typically spend on this topic in *this* document type? An absence-tell against a source that habitually says nothing is not a test (`[[finding_base_rate_the_instrument_before_its_event_table]]`). (2) **Weight tells explicitly at registration:** presence-tells and direct-question responses > absence-tells. Where an absence-tell is used, condition it — *"no mention of participation **in a document that discusses labor for ≥N words**."* (3) **One baseline per instrument.** A statement leg needs a statement baseline; a minutes leg needs a minutes baseline. Write both if the card grades both. (4) **A correct branch call can still carry a wrong implication** — the card resolved to its 70% branch and the branch's *stated consequence* ("benign claims become hawkish fuel") was refuted by the same text. **Grade the branch and the implication separately**; the implication is where the thesis damage lives (`[[finding_confidence_priced_against_thesis_not_letter]]`).
**First seen:** FOMC labor-language card, registered 2026-07-24, graded 2026-07-31 — same session as its own defect discovery.

## L-08 — A WARN cohort must be ≥~10% of the weekly claims base to be visible in the national SA series; below that, the threshold is untestable and the *specification* is the defect
**Pattern:** LAB-17 pre-registered "initial-claims 4-wk MA ≥235K by Aug 6" as the test of the WARN→claims framework (r=0.78 at ~6wk lag), driven by the big-tech cluster. Doing the arithmetic *before* resolution: the entire in-window cohort (LinkedIn 606 eff 7/13 + Meta 4,665 eff 7/22 + Intuit 910 eff 7/31) is **~6,181 workers, spread across three separate claims weeks, against a ~200K weekly base** — roughly **3% of a single week**. Moving a 4-wk MA from 187.5K to 235K requires **+28K on the MA**, i.e. a **~327K single print**. The cohort was **never large enough to confirm the threshold**, no matter how well the mechanism worked. LinkedIn's 606 was pre-flagged invisible and *was* invisible; Meta's 4,665 is ~2.3% of a print, below the ±1-8K back-week revision noise floor.
**Fix:** (1) Before registering a cohort-driven threshold on an aggregate series, compute the **cohort-to-base ratio**. Detectability floor: **≥~10% of the weekly base (~20K+ separations landing in one week)**. (2) Below that floor the hypothesis is only testable in **state-level** data (CA weekly claims here), not the national number — register it there or don't register a threshold at all. (3) **A ❌ on such a threshold does not falsify the mechanism** — it falsifies the sizing. Say that explicitly at resolution so the framework isn't discarded for a specification error (`[[finding_threshold_vs_mechanism]]`, gate #3 of the PREDICTIONS_SCOREBOARD §C pre-write checklist). (4) This is LABOR's signature failure mode — raw as-made Brier **0.256** (was 0.277 pre-LAB-17), dragged entirely by high-confidence *threshold* misses (LAB-01 85%, LAB-02 65%) while every *mechanism* call landed. LAB-17 is the same shape caught earlier: walked 35→5% on evidence rather than defended. Generalizes to `[[finding_threshold_spec_fails_before_world]]` — the threshold usually fails on its **spec**, not on the world.
**First seen:** LAB-17, diagnosed 2026-07-24 in the frozen 7/30 grading card — **before** the Aug-6 resolution, not after.
**✅ CONFIRMED AT RESOLUTION 2026-07-31 — the diagnosis was right and the arithmetic held.** Claims w/e Jul 25 printed **197K** against the card's required **≥327K**; the 4-wk MA *fell* to **202,750**, moving 32,250 further from the 235K bar. Meta's ~4,665 (the cohort's anchor, effective in exactly this claims week) was pre-committed invisible and **was** invisible: the 9K rise is inside back-week revision noise and is fully explained by a seasonal-factor miss (NSA fell 17,803 where the factors expected 25,633), so nothing is attributed to it in either direction. The mechanism was never tested — only the sizing was, and the sizing was knowably wrong at registration. **Two things this lesson earned that it did not have on 7/24:** (a) the confidence cap it produced *worked* — LAB-17 scored Brier **0.09**, the book's best row and its first correctly-hedged threshold call (§A finding 3); (b) but capping confidence is the **consolation prize**, not the fix — the test should have been registered against **CA state-level weekly claims**, where a 4,665-worker cohort is a detectable fraction, or not registered as a threshold at all. **Standing rule: the cohort-to-base ratio is `PREDICTIONS_SCOREBOARD.md` §C **gate #4** — computed and written into the prediction's Notes before any cohort→aggregate threshold is registered.** ⚠️ **Installed 2026-07-31, and the delay is part of the lesson:** L-08 was written here on 7/24, but §D step 4 (*"write the lesson **and** add a §C gate pointing to it"*) was only half-executed — for a week this file and §A both **asserted** the gate existed while §C had ten gates and none of them was this one. A control that is documented-but-absent is worse than a known gap, because the next reader stops looking. Caught in the 7/31 boot-doc audit as items B1/B2 and fixed atomically with the §C install and the `CLAUDE.md` B4 list.

## L-07 — Book the announcement TYPE (voluntary offer vs involuntary RIF); verify WARN filings exist before attributing a WARN surge to a company
**Pattern:** LABOR carried "MSFT 8,750 (Jun 6)" in the priced-cuts list and attributed part of the mid-June WARN surge to "MSFT WARNs landing." The Jul-2 sweep found: (a) the 8,750 was a **voluntary-retirement OFFER program** (Rule of 70; offers Apr 23-26, window closed ~Jun 6; acceptances never public) — VR acceptances mostly do NOT file UI claims, so booking it as layoffs overstates the claims pipeline; (b) **Microsoft has zero 2026 WARN filings** — the surge attribution had a phantom leg, which propagated into a pre-registered prediction's mechanism text (LAB-17) within hours of writing it.
**Fix:** (1) Every entry in an announcement ledger carries its TYPE: VR-offer / involuntary RIF / closure / contract-churn — they have different claims-flow signatures (offers ≈ 0 claims; contractor-swap WARNs can convert to ~0, e.g. Fortrex TX 85→0; remote-heavy cuts skip WARN entirely, e.g. Rackspace 750). (2) Before naming a company as a WARN-surge driver, confirm its filings exist in a tracker/state primary. (3) When a correction lands, grep ALL derivative surfaces same-day (auto-memory `[[finding_verification_correction_downstream_propagation]]`). **Promoted to auto-memory `[[finding_announcement_type_and_filing_precheck]]` 2026-07-24** (transferable to any agent aggregating announcements — BROCK, CARL, REGINALD, CORAL).
**First seen:** Jul 2 2026 PM sweep (MSFT VR correction; LAB-17 mechanism re-worded same-day).

## L-06 — Ratio-gauge thresholds (U-3) can be silently defeated by the DENOMINATOR; playbook grids need a supply-artifact branch
**Pattern:** The Jul-2 NFP playbook grid conditioned every branch on (headline NFP × U-3-up-or-flat). ## L-05 — Cross-domain signals can be scored on the WRONG SIDE of the convergence matrix
