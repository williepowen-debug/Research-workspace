## 2. The pre-registered grade — DENY branch, by more than it needed

Registered 2026-07-24 in the STATUS monitoring calendar, **before the print**: *"≥3.6% = wage pressure real (my supply-artifact framing weakens); ~3.4% flat while AHE went 3.4→3.5% = the AHE move was COMPOSITION → supply-shrink corroborated, 'labor is inflationary' premise undercut."*

**Print: 3.4% flat on total comp, with wages *decelerating* to 3.2% civilian / 3.1% private.** The DENY branch fires on the letter, and the wage component came in *below* what the branch required.

**The load-bearing number is the wedge, and both levels are stated so you can see it isn't a common-mode move:**

| Gauge | Composition control | Jun-2026 12-mo | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AHE** (CES average hourly earnings) | **none** — a raw average | **3.5%** | ⬆️ accelerating (from 3.4%) |
| **ECI private wages & salaries** | **fixed job + industry mix** | **3.1%** | ⬇️ decelerating (from 3.4%) |
| **Wedge** | | **0.4pp** | **widening** — they agreed at ~3.4-3.5% in March |

That is what a composition shift looks like: if the labor force shrinks by 720K concentrated in low-wage cohorts, the *average* wage rises with nobody getting a raise, while a fixed-weight index does not move. **On 7/24 I routed this to CARL and HENRY with the explicit caveat that I had the mechanism and direction but NOT the decomposition. ECI is the decomposition, and it validated the call.**

**Three honest limits, stated because they bound how far you can push this:**

1. **⚠️ The clearest counter-evidence, up front: on the *quarterly* basis private wages ACCELERATED, 0.7% → 0.9%** (~3.6% annualized), and the q/q series has sat in a 0.8–1.0% band for seven straight quarters. Part of the 12-month deceleration is a Q2-2025 base effect (Jun-25 was 3.5%). **The defensible claim is "composition-controlled wage growth is not accelerating, and AHE is a contaminated gauge" — NOT "wages are rolling over."** Anyone using this downstream should carry that sentence.
2. **ECI confirms the wedge is a mix effect; it does not identify *which* workers left.** The "exiting cohort was low-wage / immigration-driven" step is still inference — now consistent with a validated mix effect, but not directly measured.
3. **Total comp is held at 3.4% by benefits (+3.8%, health +6.0%) — medical cost inflation on employers, not scarcity pricing on workers,** and it doesn't reach take-home pay.
