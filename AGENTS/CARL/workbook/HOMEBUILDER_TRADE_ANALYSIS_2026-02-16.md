### 2. MORTGAGE RATE SENSITIVITY VS CURRENT ENVIRONMENT

**The "Warsh Fed" Divergence:**
- Fed cutting short-term rates: 3.50-3.75% currently, targeting 3.1% by end 2026
- BUT selling MBS holdings → **mortgage spreads widening**
- Result: 30-year mortgages STUCK at 6.0-6.5% despite Fed cuts
- Forecast: May drift to 5.5% by end 2026 (modest improvement)

**Builder Advantage:**
- DHI and LEN use captive mortgage subsidiaries
- Offer aggressive rate buydowns (4.99-5.25%) to "manufacture" affordability
- This gives builders competitive edge vs resale market

**The Problem:**
- Rate buydowns compress margins significantly
- Builders already reporting margin pressure (18% in 2024, targeting 22-24% recovery)
- If demand stays soft, buydowns become margin destroyers

**Interpretation:** Builders are LESS sensitive to mortgage rates than resale market due to buydown capability. This WEAKENS our thesis linkage.

### 5. DIRECT LINKAGE TO OUR THESIS

**Our Core Thesis (CARL STATUS):**
- CC 90+ DQ: 12.70% (92% of GFC peak) 🔴
- Wright Q4: CC DQ +92% YoY, HELOC +121% 🔴
- 609K current→delinquent 🔴
- 37% can't cover $400 🔴
- Employment stress: 580K layoffs, severance exhausts Q2-Q3 🔴
- Fannie MF DQ: 0.75% (5bps from GFC breach) 🔴

**Transmission Path to Homebuilders:**
```
Employment loss (LABOR) →
Consumer mortgage stress (CARL) →
Tighter underwriting + fewer qualified buyers →
Homebuilder demand falls →
Cancellations rise, inventory backs up →
Margin compression, earnings miss
```

**The Complexity:**

**Path A — BEARISH (Our Thesis):**
1. Mortgage stress → Fewer buyers qualify
2. Rising delinquencies → Banks tighten underwriting
3. Employment losses Q2-Q3 → Fewer move-up buyers
4. Demand craters → Cancellations spike → Margins implode

**Path B — NEUTRAL/BULLISH (Market View):**
1. Rate lock-in → Existing supply stays tight
2. Builders offer buydowns → Capture market share from resale
3. Structural undersupply → New builds fill gap
4. Fed cuts + lower inventory financing costs → Margins hold

**Which Path Prevails?**

The answer depends on **magnitude of employment stress**:
- **Mild stress:** Path B wins. Builders muddle through with buydowns.
- **Severe stress:** Path A wins. No amount of buydowns can offset mass unemployment.

**Critical Variables:**
1. **Underwriting Tightening:** If consumer stress causes banks to raise mortgage standards, builder buydowns don't matter — buyers can't qualify at ANY rate
2. **Move-Up Buyer Cohort:** Higher-income buyers are builder bread-and-butter. If white-collar layoffs hit (LABOR predicts), this cohort disappears
3. **Inventory Financing Costs:** Fed cuts HELP builders. This is a tailwind offsetting demand headwinds

## FINAL ASSESSMENT

**Is this a clean expression of our thesis?**

**NO. Cross-currents muddy it significantly.**

**The Problem:**
1. **Fed policy HELPS builders** (inventory financing rate cuts) even as consumer stress rises
2. **Captive mortgage subs** allow builders to offset some demand weakness
3. **Rate lock-in effect** (though fading) still provides relative advantage vs resale
4. **Market has already rallied** — stocks at 2+ year highs, pricing in recovery
5. **Timing mismatch** — Our thesis plays out Q2-Q3, but builders report quarterly. Could miss the window.

**Why Consumer Finance Names Are Better (SYF, ALLY):**
- **Direct exposure:** Consumer stress immediately shows up in delinquencies and NCOs
- **No offsetting factors:** No equivalent of "rate buydowns" or "captive mortgage subs"
- **Monthly data:** ABS trustee reports give real-time signal (30-45 day lag vs 90-day earnings)
- **Valuation:** Less extreme than homebuilders' recent rally

**The Exception — IF:**
If LABOR stress is SEVERE (unemployment spikes, white-collar layoffs accelerate), homebuilders become vulnerable. But it requires:
1. Mortgage underwriting tightening (not yet visible)
2. Move-up buyer cohort disappearing (higher-income layoffs)
3. Cancellation spike overwhelming rate buydown capability

**Confidence Level:** Only 40% that this trade would outperform consumer finance names. Too many moving parts.
