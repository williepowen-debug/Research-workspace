## #1 — Subprime Auto Repossessions: 3 Million and Counting

**The Finding:** America repossessed 3 million cars in 2025 — 76% more than at the peak of the 2008 financial crisis, the worst economic event most people alive today have ever experienced.

**Why It Matters:** Repossession is the most visceral consumer credit failure — someone physically takes your car while you're still paying your mortgage and feeding your family. The fact that we have *already surpassed GFC levels* while unemployment is still relatively low tells you that when layoffs actually hit, the number will go parabolic. This is the thesis in one number: the consumer is already broken, and the detonator hasn't fired yet.

**Source:** Industry/ATTOM data; CARL cross-referenced against NY Fed Q4 2025 household debt report.

**Strength:** 🔴 **Smoking gun**

---

## #2 — Subprime Auto Delinquency: All-Time Record, Never Been Worse

**The Finding:** 7.1% of subprime auto borrowers are more than 60 days late on their car payments — an all-time record that has never been higher in the history of the data series.

**Why It Matters:** Subprime auto is the canary in the coal mine for consumer credit broadly. It's the first to crack in a downturn because these borrowers have the thinnest margins and the least flexibility. We are not "approaching" the historical danger zone — we have broken through it. Auto delinquency leads mortgage delinquency by 1-2 quarters, meaning what we see in auto today is what we will see in housing in 6 months.

**Source:** Fitch ABS February 2026 report. This is lender-reported data from actual securitization pools — not a survey, not an estimate.

**Strength:** 🔴 **Smoking gun**

## #3 — The Savings Rate Has Matched 2008

**The Finding:** The U.S. personal savings rate fell to 3.6% in January 2026 — the same level it reached in 2008, right before the financial crisis, when the last of consumer buffers evaporated.

**Why It Matters:** The savings rate is the shock absorber. When it's high (10%+, like 2021), consumers can handle a job loss, a car repair, an unexpected bill. At 3.6%, the buffer is essentially gone. Academic research (Baker, *JPE* 2018) shows that highly-leveraged households amplify consumption changes by 25% — meaning the spending cut when these households face stress is not gradual, it's a cliff. The 2008 comparison isn't rhetoric: it's a direct match on a quantitative metric that predicted the last crash.

**Source:** BEA personal saving rate series; PROME DD-3 research; Baker (2018, *Journal of Political Economy*) on leverage and consumption elasticity.

**Strength:** 🔴 **Smoking gun**

## #4 — Goeasy: Canada's Canary Just Collapsed -57% in One Day

**The Finding:** Goeasy, Canada's largest subprime consumer lender, crashed 57% in a single trading day in March 2026 — dividend suspended, outlook withdrawn, $233 million in writeoffs, and their charge-off rate is heading into the mid-teens.

**Why It Matters:** Canada's consumer credit cycle runs 1-2 quarters ahead of the United States. Goeasy is the closest comparable to companies like OneMain Financial and World Acceptance in the U.S. A mid-teens charge-off rate would be *double* the worst U.S. bank's charge-off in 2008. Management suspended the dividend — which means they have no confidence in their own near-term cash flows. This is the preview. The same companies in the U.S. haven't reported this yet, but the data is coming.

**Source:** Financial Post (Bloomberg), @kshaughnessy2 (X/Twitter), March 10 2026. Goeasy ticker GSY.TO.

**Strength:** 🔴 **Smoking gun**

## #5 — Credit Card Delinquency: 92% of the Way to the 2008 Peak

**The Finding:** 12.70% of credit card balances are more than 90 days late — the highest level since 2017, and already 92% of the way to the 2008 financial crisis peak of 13.74%.

**Why It Matters:** Credit cards are the *last* thing people stop paying. In the consumer payment hierarchy, people pay their car first, then their mortgage, then their utilities — credit cards are last because losing a car or a house is immediate and irreversible. The fact that credit card delinquency is this high *before* the employment shock, *before* gas hits $4, and *before* student loan delinquency peaks means we are not at the beginning of this stress cycle. We are in the middle of it.

**Source:** NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, Q4 2025. This is Federal Reserve data.

**Strength:** 🔴 **Smoking gun**

## #6 — SoFi Triggered Its First-Ever Cumulative Net Loss Clause

**The Finding:** SoFi's 2025 personal loan securitization triggered its cumulative net loss protection clause for the first time in company history — meaning losses are bad enough that investors in the lower tranches have been legally cut off from receiving payments.

**Why It Matters:** This is not subprime. SoFi lends to prime and near-prime borrowers — people with jobs and college degrees. When CNL triggers fire on personal loans at this credit quality, it means stress is climbing *up* the quality stack, not just hammering the bottom. ABS triggers are firing while high-yield bond spreads haven't moved — structured credit is the early warning system, and right now it's flashing red while the broader bond market hasn't noticed yet.

**Source:** Eisman Playbook Podcast Episode 49, Dwight Collins (Autonomous Research), March 9 2026.

**Strength:** 🟠 **Strong signal**

## #7 — K-Shape Is Now Converging Downward (Both Ends Breaking Simultaneously)

**The Finding:** For two years, the wealthy spent and the poor struggled — but as of early 2026, even high-income households are cutting spending due to wealth-effect erosion from falling markets, and both cohorts are deteriorating simultaneously for the first time this cycle.

**Why It Matters:** The K-shape economy had a built-in offset: upper-income spending kept aggregate GDP numbers respectable while lower-income stress mounted. That offset is now gone. Upper-income consumers are the *primary* driver of aggregate spending — the bottom 60% don't have enough disposable income to move the needle on their own. When the top also pulls back, GDP falls fast and hard. The K-shape data is stark: FHA mortgage delinquency at 11.52% vs. conventional at 2.89% — a nearly 9-percentage-point gap that illustrates two entirely different Americas. Now both Americas are contracting.

**Source:** MBA Q4 2025 National Delinquency Survey (FHA vs. conventional); Reuters K-shape convergence reporting March 2026; BEA GDP Q4 second estimate (consumer spending revised 2.4%→2.0%).

**Strength:** 🟠 **Strong signal**

## #8 — The Gas Price Behavioral Breakpoint Is 2-4 Weeks Away

**The Finding:** At $4/gallon — which Americans historically hit and change their behavior by cutting spending elsewhere — the national average is currently $3.60-3.75 and rising, with the Strait of Hormuz actively mined and WTI crude above $107.

**Why It Matters:** $4/gallon is not just a round number — it's a documented behavioral threshold (Kilian & Zhou, 2024) where low-income consumers begin making explicit fuel-vs-food tradeoffs, reducing discretionary spending, and increasing credit card usage to cover necessities. The structural cause (Hormuz mined) means this isn't a spike — there is no relief valve until there is a military resolution. Gas stays high through Q2. Diesel is already at $4.86, which means trucking surcharges hit grocery shelves in 6-8 weeks. This is the ignition switch for the food inflation story.

**Source:** AAA national gas price data; HAWK energy signals March 13 2026; Kilian & Zhou (2024) on fuel price elasticity differentials by income.

**Strength:** 🟠 **Strong signal**

## #9 — Student Loan Delinquency at 16.3% — Worst Ever Recorded

**The Finding:** 16.3% of student loan borrowers are 30+ days late on their payments — the worst delinquency rate ever recorded for student loans, and this comes *after* the Biden-era grace periods officially ended.

**Why It Matters:** Student loans represent $1.6 trillion in household debt owed overwhelmingly by working-age adults who are simultaneously trying to rent apartments, buy cars, and eventually buy homes. The 16.3% figure represents roughly 7 million borrowers who are already behind — not in forbearance, not in deferment, but actually late. These same borrowers are the ones showing up in the credit card and auto delinquency data. This is not a separate phenomenon — it's the same stressed cohort, piling up failures across every obligation simultaneously.

**Source:** NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, Q4 2025. Federal Reserve data.

**Strength:** 🟠 **Strong signal**

## #10 — Fertilizer Prices Up 34% in 10 Days: Food Inflation Is Coming in Q3-Q4

**The Finding:** With Gulf fertilizer exports stranded behind the Strait of Hormuz, urea prices spiked 27-34% in just 10 days to $700/ton — and because spring planting deadlines are fixed, farmers will pay any price, locking in a food inflation shock for Q3-Q4 2026.

**Why It Matters:** This is the "it gets worse" slide. The consumer is already stressed by gas prices, credit card debt, and wage stagnation. In 3-6 months, grocery bills will also be rising, compounding the squeeze. The historical analog: 1973 fertilizer prices spiked 45-90% within weeks, food prices followed 6-12 months later. This is a second inflation vector that is independent of energy — even if oil somehow retreated, the food price shock is already locked in because fertilizer has been purchased or committed for this year's crop. The Fed cannot cut into this. Stagflation in the actual data, with more pressure in the pipeline.

**Source:** HAWK energy/commodity signals; urea NOLA physical market price data ($683/mt, +$167/wk); Maersk shipping surcharges ($1,800/TEU) confirming transmission pipeline.

**Strength:** 🟡 **Supporting evidence** (high-confidence directionally, but timing is 1-2 quarter lag)
