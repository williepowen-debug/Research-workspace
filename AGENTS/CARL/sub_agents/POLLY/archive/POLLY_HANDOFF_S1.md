## S — SYNTHESIS

### Key Mental Model

**The Insurance Stress Thesis in Three Parts:**

1. **INDICATOR Function (Validated):**
   - 15.4% uninsured motorist rate = consumers can't afford mandatory coverage
   - 33.4% uninsured OR underinsured = one-third of drivers at risk
   - Premium growth (11.1% auto, 6% health) consistently exceeds wages
   - These signals precede credit stress — consumers cut coverage before defaulting

2. **AMPLIFIER Function (Validated):**
   - $88B medical debt in collections = health events become credit events
   - High deductibles ($1,886 avg, $2,631 small firms) = effective underinsurance
   - CA wildfire victims without coverage = property loss + no recovery
   - Coverage gaps convert manageable incidents into catastrophes

3. **Geographic Bifurcation (New Finding):**
   - FLORIDA: Market RECOVERING after 2022 tort reforms; private insurers returning
   - CALIFORNIA: Market FAILING despite reforms; FAIR Plan now largest residual market
   - This divergence is diagnostic: reforms CAN work (FL) but not when risk exceeds viable pricing (CA)

**What Matters Most:**
The insurance stress thesis is VALIDATED but requires geographic nuance. National averages mask regional extremes. Florida's recovery is real but untested against another major hurricane. California's crisis is acute and worsening. The transmission mechanism (insurance stress → consumer financial stress) remains intact.

**Biggest Uncertainty:**
1. Is FL recovery durable? Next hurricane season will test
2. Will CA regulatory reforms attract private insurers back?
3. Will ACA subsidy expiration materialize (+10M uninsured)?

**Transmission Channels:**
- **DIRECT (immediate):** Premium burden (auto 3.39% of income) reduces discretionary spending
- **LATENT (event-triggered):** Coverage gaps (33.4% inadequate auto; CA uninsured HH) create catastrophic exposure activated by incident
