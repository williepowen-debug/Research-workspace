## Executive Summary

- **Pipeline is real and loading:** SBA 7(a) default rate hit 3.7% in 2024 (highest since 2012), tripling from 2021 lows. Commercial Ch.11 filings +37% YoY Q1 2026; Subchapter V (SB restructuring) +67% YoY Q1 2026. The stress is not theoretical — it is already visible in SBA loss data and bankruptcy courts.
- **Tariff multiplier is accelerating the cycle:** Monthly tariff costs for SB importers tripled from ~$3,800 in early 2024 to $11,400 average in Feb 2026 (NSBA). 61% of SBs report negative impact; 38% report cash flow problems. The tariff shock lands on top of already-elevated interest rates — a dual squeeze that collapses debt service capacity.
- **Regional banks hold the bag:** Community and regional banks (<$250B assets) hold ~$600B in SB loans ≤$1M (Fed Vice Chair Bowman, Mar 31, 2026). CRE-heavy regionals account for 70% of the ~$1.6T in CRE debt maturing 2025–2026. Small business tenant stress (retail, restaurant, light industrial) is a direct CRE transmission mechanism.
- **Timeline: stress visible in bank earnings Q2–Q3 2026.** Historical lag from economic stress → 30DPD delinquency is 2–3 months; delinquency → charge-off is 4–6 months for C&I/SB loans. Tariff-driven cash flow pressure from Q4 2025–Q1 2026 should surface in bank special mention and classified loan disclosures starting Q2 2026 earnings (July–August).
- **OZK is a concentrated CRE/construction risk; WAL has diversified somewhat.** OZK's 2022 construction vintage ($13.8B originated) hits hard maturity Q1–Q3 2026 — not primarily SB exposure, but signals the broader regional bank stress environment. WAL's C&I is 48% of portfolio and includes warehouse/homebuilder; direct SB exposure is less transparent but not negligible.

### Mapping to Bank Loan Categories
- **Retail CRE tenanted by SBs:** Strip malls, main street retail — when SB tenants go dark, CRE collateral value drops immediately. This is a CARL → REGINALD transmission vector.
- **Restaurant C&I and CRE:** Restaurant industry hit by tariffs on food inputs + labor + energy. Delinquency in this cohort should be tracking near the 69–61% "impacted" headline.
- **Manufacturing SB loans:** Regional banks in Midwest/South hold manufacturing C&I concentration; tariff exposure here is direct input cost pressure.
- **High-cost alternative financing as a leading indicator:** SBs turning to MCA (merchant cash advance) before bank default — MCA market $19.65B in 2025 [CapitalExpressLLC, 2026]. Industry default rate 7–12% [Emerge Law, 2025]. MCA defaults typically precede bank delinquency by 2–4 months (borrower exhausts alternatives first).
- CNBC/NPR reporting (Dec 2025 / Feb 2026): SBs explicitly taking high-rate loans (20%+) to cover tariff costs — this debt stacks on top of existing bank obligations, worsening debt service ratios. [CNBC Dec 2025; NPR Feb 2026]

## 4. Default Timeline Model

### Lag Structure (Stress → Bank Charge-Off)
| Stage | Typical Lag from Initial Stress | Data Signal |
|-------|--------------------------------|-------------|
| Cash flow squeeze | 0 months (immediate) | MCA drawdown, revolving credit utilization |
| MCA default / alternative lender default | 2–4 months | MCA servicer data (not publicly reported) |
| Bank 30-60 DPD delinquency | 3–5 months from cash flow stress | Equifax SBDI, PayNet, bank call report data |
| Bank 90+ DPD / non-accrual | 6–9 months from cash flow stress | FDIC call reports, bank earnings disclosures |
| Special mention / classified designation | Often simultaneous with 60-90 DPD | Bank earnings "criticized loans" disclosures |
| Bank charge-off (regulatory trigger: 120–180 days) | 9–12 months from initial stress | Net charge-off rate in quarterly earnings |

Regulatory standard: installment loans must be charged-off after 120 days; revolving after 180 days [Credit industry standard; FDIC guidance]

### Applying the Timeline to Tariff Shock
- Tariff escalation began in earnest Q1 2025; cash flow pressure clearly mounting Q3–Q4 2025
- First visible bank delinquency stress: Q1–Q2 2026 (already entering this window)
- First visible bank charge-off impact: Q2–Q3 2026 earnings
- Peak bank loss recognition: Q3–Q4 2026 (if stress does not abate)

**Predicted earnings cadence:**
- Q1 2026 (reports Apr–May): Banks cite "uncertainty" but no material deterioration yet; special mention increases possible
- Q2 2026 (reports Jul–Aug): First hard delinquency data; classified loan increases visible; provisions begin rising
- Q3 2026 (reports Oct–Nov): Charge-off increases; if tariff regime unchanged, peak stress period

### Historical Calibration
- 2008 GFC: SBA 7(a) failure rate peaked at 11.9% in 2008, SBA charge-offs peaked at $2.04B in 2010 — approximately 8 quarters from stress onset to charge-off peak [CNN Money, Feb 2009; SBA historical data]
- C&I loan noncurrent rate peaked at 3.6% in 2009; net charge-off rate 1.8% in 2010 [FDIC/Fed historical data]
- Key GFC calibration: Recession declared Dec 2007; bank C&I charge-offs peaked Q2–Q3 2010 — roughly 10–12 quarters lag from initial stress to peak losses
- Current cycle is slower-burn (not acute liquidity crisis), so peak lag may be shorter (6–8 quarters from Q4 2024 stress start = peak around Q2–Q4 2026)

## 5. Historical Precedent

### 2008–2009 GFC: Small Business Credit Cycle at Banks
- SBA loan failure rate: 2.4% (2004) → 11.9% (2008) — 5x expansion over 4 years [CNN Money, 2009]
- SBA charge-off rate: 0.4% (2004) → 1.9% (2008) → $2.04B peak (2010) [SBA historical; Jamestown Capital]
- Bank C&I delinquency peak: Q3 2009 at ~3.6% noncurrent rate [Federal Reserve H.8 / FDIC data]
- Bank C&I net charge-off peak: Q2–Q3 2010 at ~1.8% [Federal Reserve charge-off data]
- Small banks showed earlier delinquency spikes than large banks during GFC, but lower ultimate charge-off rates (more workout/modification activity) [American Finance Association study]
- NBER research: Big-bank small business lending declined sharply during GFC; community banks filled some gap but also took higher losses [NBER Working Paper #23843]
- CFPB Data Point (2022): "Small Business Lending and the Great Recession" — SB credit contracted severely 2008–2012; recovery uneven

### COVID Cycle (2020–2023): PPP Masked the Cycle
- PPP loans ($800B+ issued): 92% forgiven by Jan 2023, effectively removing the default from the credit system [NPR, Jan 2023]
- EIDL direct loans ($300B+): NOT bank-held, but burden on SBs servicing EIDL simultaneously with bank loans
- Cumulative EIDL charge-offs: $70B+ through FY2024 — represents the real credit loss the program masked at the bank level [deBanked, Nov 2024]
- Post-PPP reality: SBs that survived COVID on subsidized credit now face: (1) EIDL repayment obligations, (2) normalized interest rates, (3) tariff cost increases — a triple squeeze
- Community bank PPP performance: Top performers were regional banks <$30B with concentrated market knowledge (Glacier Bank, United Community Bank) [Enigma, 2022]; but the forgiveness guarantee insulated banks from losses regardless

### S&L Crisis (1980s–early 1990s): Limited SB Relevance
- S&L crisis was primarily CRE/construction lending driven; commercial lending less central
- However: S&L failure wave (1986–1995) removed SB credit supply in affected markets — analogous to current small bank stress reducing SB lending capacity

## 8. CARL/REGINALD Implications

### For CARL (Consumer Stress → Transmission Chain)
The SB stress → regional bank pipeline operates **in parallel** with consumer credit delinquency, not instead of it. The mechanism is:

```
Tariff cost shock → SB cash flow squeeze → SB owner/employee income stress
                                         → SB delinquency on bank loans
                  → SB layoffs/closures → Employee consumer credit delinquency
                                        → CARL's direct consumer metrics
```

- SB employees (particularly in retail, restaurant, manufacturing) are disproportionately represented in the bottom 60% of CARL's K-shape framework — they are the same cohort already showing CC 90+ DQ stress.
- Payment hierarchy relevance: SB owners prioritize keeping the business open (paying suppliers, rent) before personal consumer debt — but when the business fails, ALL obligations fail simultaneously.
- The gig economy overlap: GIG sub-agent tracks Dave 28DPD (~2.0%); many gig workers are also micro-SBs (sole proprietors, 1099). SB stress = gig stress in this overlap.

### For REGINALD (Bank-Level Impact)
**Direct transmission vectors:**
1. **SB C&I loan delinquency:** Community/regional bank C&I portfolios begin showing deterioration Q2–Q3 2026. Key signal: FDIC call report SB loan noncurrent rates.
2. **CRE → SB tenant cascade:** Strip mall, restaurant CRE, and light industrial CRE are valued on SB tenant cash flows. If 61–69% of retail/restaurant SBs are negatively impacted, CRE NOI collapses → collateral values drop → banks with >50% CRE (most KRE constituents) face mark-to-market and refinancing stress.
3. **OZK maturity wall Q1–Q3 2026:** OZK's $13.8B in 2022 construction vintage is the sharpest near-term catalyst for REGINALD's bank-level tracking.
4. **KRE P/B at 1.1x:** Currently not crisis-priced. If Q2/Q3 earnings show provision escalation, next rerating could push P/B toward 0.8–0.9x (implying 20–30% downside from current levels).
