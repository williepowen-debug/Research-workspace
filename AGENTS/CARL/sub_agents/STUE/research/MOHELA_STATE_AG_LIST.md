## SECTION 1: COMPLETED / SETTLED ENFORCEMENT ACTIONS

### 1A. California DFPI Consent Order (SETTLED)

- **Authority:** California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI)
- **Official Quoted:** DFPI Commissioner Clothilde Hewlett
- **Date:** April 24, 2024
- **Action Type:** Consent Order / Administrative Penalty
- **Status:** SETTLED
- **Specific Violation:** MOHELA failed to timely provide contact information for California borrowers regarding the April 30, 2024 deadline for the Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) Account Adjustment program. MOHELA's delay was approximately three weeks behind other servicers, impeding DFPI's outreach efforts.
- **Law Cited:** California student loan servicer consumer protection statutes (Student Loan Servicing Act / DFPI licensing requirements)
- **Penalty:** $27,500 in administrative penalties
- **Corrective Requirements:** MOHELA required to provide borrower contact information on schedule; specific compliance measures detailed in consent order
- **Significance:** DFPI was the **first state regulator to take public enforcement action against MOHELA** for violating state consumer protection laws. Described by SBPC Legal Director Winston Berkman-Breen as "a model for state role in protecting student loan borrowers." Over 265,000 California borrowers were ultimately alerted to the debt relief opportunity.
- **Sources:**
  - https://dfpi.ca.gov/press_release/dfpi-takes-action-against-mohela-to-protect-california-student-loan-borrowers/
  - https://protectborrowers.org/california-dfpi-consent-order-with-mohela-is-model-for-state-role-in-protecting-student-loan-borrowers-from-failing-servicing-companies/
  - https://www.consumerfinanceinsights.com/2024/04/24/dfpi-enters-into-consent-order-with-higher-education-loan-authority/

### 1B. U.S. Department of Education Administrative Penalty (FEDERAL — for reference)

- **Authority:** U.S. Department of Education (Biden admin)
- **Date:** October 30, 2023
- **Action Type:** Contract penalty / administrative withholding
- **Status:** RESOLVED (payment withheld; forbearance ordered)
- **Specific Violation:** MOHELA failed to send monthly billing statements to 2.5 million borrowers when loan repayments resumed after COVID pause. ~800,000 borrowers became delinquent.
- **Penalty:** $7.2 million withheld from MOHELA's October 2023 contract payment. All affected borrowers placed in forbearance (interest frozen, payments suspended).
- **Note:** This is a federal administrative action, not a state AG action. Included for completeness as it triggered the wave of state investigations.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.cnbc.com/2023/10/30/education-dept-penalizes-student-loan-servicer-mohela-for-errors.html
  - https://www.cnn.com/2023/10/30/politics/student-loan-mohela-late-bills/index.html

## SECTION 2: ACTIVE STATE/DC LITIGATION

### 2A. Maldonado v. MOHELA — California (ACTIVE, KEY CASE)

- **Case Name:** Maldonado v. Missouri Higher Education Loan Authority (MOHELA)
- **Court:** U.S. District Court, Northern District of California (removed from Alameda County Superior Court)
- **Judge:** Judge Chhabria
- **Date Filed:** September 4, 2024 (state court); removed to federal court November 8, 2024
- **Status:** ACTIVE — post-summary-judgment phase as of March 2026
- **Plaintiffs:** California student borrowers (class action)
- **Key Allegations / Laws Violated:**
  - **California Student Borrower Bill of Rights** — primary violation
  - **California Unfair Competition Law**
  - **Rosenthal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act** (CA equivalent of federal FDCPA)
  - Specific conduct: MOHELA continued sending billing statements misrepresenting borrowers' loan obligations AFTER receiving discharge lists from the Department of Education for discharges ordered for borrowers from six predatory for-profit schools (Marinello Schools of Beauty, Corinthian Colleges, ITT Technical Institute, Westwood College, Art Institute, Colorado-based College America campuses)
- **Key Rulings:**
  - April 9, 2025: Motion to dismiss denied (first attempt)
  - July 8, 2025: Motion for judgment on the pleadings denied (second attempt)
  - **March 10, 2026:** Order on cross-motions for partial summary judgment — GRANTED IN PART, DENIED IN PART. Court confirmed MOHELA violated California state law by sending billing statements misrepresenting borrowers' obligations after receiving discharge lists.
- **Relief Sought:** Immediate implementation of loan discharges; cessation of inaccurate billing/collection communications; class certification for affected borrowers
- **Monetary Penalties:** Not yet determined (case proceeding)
- **Significance:** This is the ruling referenced in the task brief. The March 2026 ruling on summary judgment is a major PRECEDENT confirming state law enforcement authority over MOHELA's discharge failures.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.ppsl.org/cases/maldonado-v-mohela
  - https://www.ppsl.org/news/court-denies-mohelas-second-attempt-to-dismiss-borrowers-lawsuit

### 2B. AFT v. MOHELA — District of Columbia (ACTIVE)

- **Case Name:** American Federation of Teachers v. Higher Education Loan Authority of the State of Missouri (MOHELA)
- **Court:** Superior Court of the District of Columbia (DC Superior Court)
- **Date Filed:** July 22, 2024
- **Status:** ACTIVE — in discovery following January 2026 amended complaint. No settlement as of February 2026.
- **Plaintiffs:** American Federation of Teachers (AFT), on behalf of ~8 million borrowers
- **Represented by:** Student Borrower Protection Center (SBPC), National Consumer Law Center (NCLC), Selendy Gay PLLC
- **Defendant:** MOHELA
- **Laws Cited:**
  - **District of Columbia Consumer Protection Procedures Act (CPPA)** — core violation
  - Consumer Financial Protection Act (federal, referenced in context)
- **Key Allegations:**
  - Illegal overcharging on monthly student loan bills
  - Failure to timely process IDR and PSLF paperwork
  - Systematic "call deflection" scheme directing ~8 million borrowers away from live customer service to ineffective self-help options (DOE paid MOHELA over $1.1B since 2011 for this service)
  - Actively misleading and misinforming borrowers about repayment options
  - Worst customer service of 5 major servicers: MOHELA borrowers wait ~7x longer than ED Financial borrowers; ~50x longer than Aidvantage/NelNet borrowers to reach a representative
- **Amended Complaint:** Filed January 15, 2026 — expanded allegations detailing continued misconduct throughout 2025 post-filing
- **Relief Sought:** Cessation of unlawful servicing practices; damages potentially in billions of dollars
- **Missouri AG Interference:** Missouri AG Andrew Bailey filed amicus curiae brief in September 2024 DEFENDING MOHELA, arguing MOHELA has Eleventh Amendment sovereign immunity and can "never be held accountable in federal court for cheating borrowers"

## SECTION 3: STATE AG CIVIL INVESTIGATIVE DEMAND (CID) WORKING GROUP

### Overview

State AGs and financial regulators formed a coordinated multistate working group beginning in late 2023. As of April/May 2025, nine states/jurisdictions confirmed with CIDs or parallel investigations. The working group is formally collaborative.

**Reported in:** Yahoo Finance / multiple outlets, April 28, 2025. Senators Warren, Schumer, and Sanders issued supporting statement same date.

**Risk:** Trump administration interference reported — Acting Under Secretary James Bergeron (former student loan industry lobbyist) allegedly working to impede law enforcement scrutiny. CFPB told to "deprioritize" student loan cases. May 7, 2025: AFT and SBPC sent letter to Inspectors General for DOE and CFPB warning of Trump scheme to protect MOHELA.

## SECTION 6: STATES WITH STUDENT BORROWER BILL OF RIGHTS — MALDONADO PRECEDENT APPLICABILITY

The March 10, 2026 Maldonado ruling confirms that California's Student Borrower Bill of Rights can be enforced against MOHELA in court. States with similar laws are positioned to bring equivalent private or AG enforcement actions.

**Key Maldonado Precedent:** The March 2026 ruling establishes that:
1. State student borrower protection laws apply to federal student loan servicers
2. MOHELA cannot use its status as a Missouri state entity to evade California law
3. Sending billing statements AFTER receiving discharge lists = actionable state law violation
4. The California Student Borrower Bill of Rights creates private rights of action for these failures

**States best positioned for Maldonado-style follow-on actions:** Colorado, Illinois, New York, Massachusetts, Oregon, DC — all have both servicer oversight laws AND active AG investigations already underway.

## SECTION 7: GAPS / INTELLIGENCE NOTES

1. **AG names not publicly confirmed:** The states in the CID working group (WA, NY, PA, CO, IL, MA, DC) have not had their individual AG names confirmed in available sources. The investigations were first reported in April 2025 by Yahoo Finance/AOL Finance citing sources. Some AG offices declined to confirm or denied on the record.

2. **No monetary penalties from AG CIDs yet:** The CIDs are investigative tools (information-gathering), not final enforcement actions. No state AG has yet announced a formal complaint or consent order beyond California's $27,500 DFPI action as of April 2026.

3. **Trump administration interference risk:** The CFPB has deprioritized student loan enforcement. Acting Under Secretary James Bergeron (former industry lobbyist) may be impeding state-federal coordination. This could slow AG actions that depend on federal data-sharing.

4. **Pennsylvania notable absence from known state laws:** PA does not appear in the 19-state list with a Student Borrower Bill of Rights, yet is part of the CID working group. Would rely on general consumer protection laws (UTPCPL) for enforcement action.

5. **Sovereign immunity defense unresolved:** Missouri AG Bailey's Eleventh Amendment argument (that MOHELA = arm of state, immune from suit) is still pending in appellate courts. If upheld, it could complicate all state-law enforcement actions. Watch for circuit court rulings.
