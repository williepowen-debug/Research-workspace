## DATA POINTS — VERIFIED FROM PDF (PRIMARY SOURCE)

All quotes below are verbatim from the white paper PDF (https://www.fico.com/en/resource-access/download/56986).

### National Average FICO Score
- **Current:** 714 (as of October 2025)
- **Prior period:** 715 (April 2025)
- **Change:** -1 point H1→H2 2025; -2 points year-over-year (October 2024 → October 2025)
- **Trend since 2024:** Prior average was 716 (2024); gradual downward trend since 2023
- **Source quote:** "Average FICO® Score decreases to 714 from 715 in April 2025"

### Student Loan Delinquency as Primary Driver
- **Source quote (verbatim from PDF):** "Nearly one-third of student loan borrowers with a payment due (7.1 million consumers) have a new delinquency reported on their credit file, causing a 62-point drop in their score on average since January 2025."
- **Note on "7M+" claim:** The FICO report gives 7.1 million precisely. The "7M+" formulation in circulation is accurate.
- **Note on "-62 points" claim:** CONFIRMED verbatim in PDF.
- **Additional context from PDF:**
  - 1.1 million borrowers still in "in-between" status (payment due, no delinquency yet reported, likely awaiting IBR approval)
  - 13.8 million borrowers have no delinquency and made at least one payment in the last year
  - After April 2025 spike (delinquency reporting resumption), only a 0.1% increase in student loan delinquency between April and October 2025 — FICO characterizes this as "leveling"

### High-Score Borrower Impact
- **From Apollo Academy (citing FICO data):** "For households with a FICO score higher than 760, the average credit score change associated with a new student loan delinquency is a decline of 171 points."
- **Status:** This figure appears in third-party analysis of FICO data, NOT verbatim in the Spring 2026 PDF. Treat as plausible but verify independently before citing as FICO-sourced.

### Gen Z (18-29) Score Drop Statistics
- **Source quote (verbatim from PDF):** "the proportion of 18–29 year olds that have seen a 50-point decrease in their score rose to 14.4%. This is likely due to ongoing issues with student loan repayment."
- **Comparison:** ~10% of all Americans saw 50+ point drops in same period (Oct 2024 → Oct 2025); 14.4% for 18-29 is the elevated share
- **Age group definition note (from PDF):** FICO used 18-29 as a proxy for Gen Z because credit files cannot exist before age 18; this is slightly wider than the strict Gen Z birth-year definition
- **CONFIRMED:** 14.4% figure is verbatim in PDF

### K-Shape / Score Distribution
- **48.1%** of U.S. consumers have FICO Scores of 750 or higher (record high, up from 43.3% in 2019)
- **Score distribution reflects "K-shaped economy"** — FICO's own language in the report
- **Delinquency by product:** Auto, CC, personal loan DQ rates "leveled off or improved"; mortgage DQ "continued rising toward pre-pandemic levels"

### Additional Data from PDF
- **Severe delinquency (90+ day, all products):** 11.0% Oct 2025 (from chart)
- **Non-student-loan severe DQ:** 6.6% Oct 2025, down from 7.1% in April 2025
- **Student loan severe DQ:** ~8.5% Oct 2025 (held elevated from April jump)
- **Payment hierarchy confirmed:** Auto > Mortgage > other products (auto 90+DQ 2.9% vs mortgage 3.4% for consumers with both)
- **CC utilization:** Stabilized at $7,622 avg balance / 35.7% utilization
- **State with lowest avg FICO:** Mississippi 676, Alabama 691, Georgia 692
- **State with highest avg FICO:** Minnesota 742, Wisconsin 739, Massachusetts 733

## DATA POINTS — NOT IN FICO REPORT (SEPARATE SOURCE)

### Near-Prime 680→580 Drop / 2 Million Borrowers

**This figure does NOT appear in the FICO Spring 2026 white paper.**

**Actual source:** The Century Foundation + Protect Borrowers joint report
- **Title:** "Trump's Student Loan Delinquency Crisis, Unmasked"
- **Publication date:** February 20, 2026 (predates FICO Spring 2026 by ~5 weeks)
- **URL:** https://protectborrowers.org/release-student-loan-delinquency-spikes-to-record-25-percent-under-trump-destroying-credit-scores-and-locking-millions-out-of-economy/
- **TCF URL:** https://tcf.org/content/report/trumps-student-loan-delinquency-crisis-unmasked/ (403 at time of research)
- **Verbatim from Protect Borrowers press release:** "For 2 million borrowers with near-prime or better credit in 2024, their scores fell 100 points on average in 2025—from 680 to 580."
- **Data source used by TCF/PB:** "nationally representative credit panel data" (NOT FICO proprietary data)
- **Average score drop in TCF/PB study:** -57 points (vs FICO's -62 points — different methodology/time window)

**CRITICAL NOTE FOR STUE:** The "near-prime, -100 points, 2 million borrowers" figure has been widely cited alongside FICO Spring 2026 data, but it originates from a separate February 2026 advocacy organization study (TCF/Protect Borrowers), not from FICO. The two studies overlap in subject matter but are distinct. Do not attribute the 2M/100-point figure to FICO.

### Fortune Article Cross-Reference
- **URL:** https://fortune.com/2026/03/26/gen-z-credit-scores-declining-under-trump-student-loan-crackdown/
- Fortune cited FICO + TCF/PB data together, which is likely the origin of the conflation
- Fortune confirmed: "Around 2 million borrowers saw scores plummet from 680 to 580 (a 100-point decline)" — citing TCF/PB
- Fortune confirmed: "more than 7 million student loan borrowers had a new credit delinquency reported last year, causing an average 62-point drop" — citing FICO
