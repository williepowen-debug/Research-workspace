## CASE BACKGROUND

Sweet v. McMahon (originally Sweet v. DeVos, then Sweet v. Cardona) is a class action filed 2019 in N.D. Cal., accusing DOE of sitting on hundreds of thousands of Borrower Defense applications for years without decisions. Settlement finalized November 2022, establishing binding deadlines.

**Post-class applicants** = borrowers who filed a Borrower Defense application between June 23 and November 15, 2022 (after case filing, before final approval).

Two sub-groups:
- **Exhibit C applicants** (~80% of post-class, ~170,000 borrowers): Schools where DOE identified "strong indicia of substantial misconduct." Deadline: January 28, 2026.
- **Non-Exhibit C applicants** (~20% of post-class): All other schools. Deadline: April 15, 2026.

If no decision is issued on a non-Exhibit C post-class application by April 15: automatic Full Settlement Relief applies. Components:

This is the same mechanism that triggered for Exhibit C applicants when DOE missed January 28.

## STUE ASSESSMENT

**Probability DOE misses April 15 non-Exhibit C deadline: HIGH.**

Rationale:
1. DOE already missed the Jan 28 Exhibit C deadline (same mechanism, same administration).
2. As of Apr 6, DOE is still seeking an extension, not reporting compliance.
3. No public record of non-Exhibit C decisions issued.
4. The Trump administration has been consistently seeking delays and appealing at every stage.
5. The Ninth Circuit's stay denial removes the only mechanism that would legally excuse a miss.

**If missed:** Automatic relief for this cohort is self-executing. DOE would need a court order to block it — which the Ninth Circuit already indicated is unlikely on the merits.

**Watch for:** Court ruling on DOE's pending motion (could come any day before Apr 15). Any Apr 15 compliance report filed by DOE or PPSL.

**CARL linkage:** If automatic relief triggers, this is a PARTIAL INVALIDATION signal for the thesis — government intervention (student loan forgiveness for this cohort) is one of CARL's listed partial invalidation conditions. However, this is a court-compelled discharge for a defined post-class cohort, not broad policy-driven forgiveness. Assess thesis impact accordingly.
