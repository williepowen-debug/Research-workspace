## SOURCE 1: CFPB — "Analysis of the First Seven Months of Federal Student Loan Return to Repayment"

**URL:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/analysis-of-first-seven-months-of-federal-student-loan-return-to-repayment/
**Publication date:** May 2024 (covers Oct 2023 – Apr 2024)
**Methodology:** Credit record/administrative data (NOT survey)
**Data cutoff:** April 2024

### Key Statistics
- **~40% successfully made payments** as of April 2024
- **~30% missed payments** (on-ramp protected, not reported to bureaus)
- **~20% had $0 scheduled payments** (IDR/SAVE administrative forbearance)
- **28 million borrowers returned to repayment all at once** (total universe)
- **8.9 million borrowers benefited at least once from the on-ramp policy**
- **47% of first-time repayers not actively repaying** — this is the most load-bearing figure for CRL-13

### Geographic/Income Proxy Breakdown
- Borrowers with missed payments: **27% more likely** to live in high-poverty areas
- Borrowers with $0 scheduled payments: **33% more likely** to live in high-poverty areas
- Low-income borrowers show approximately **60% higher delinquency rates** on non-student loan debt (spillover indicator)

### Methodology Note
Administrative/credit record data — NOT self-reported. This is the gold standard source. The on-ramp period means missed payments were not reported to credit bureaus through Sept 30, 2024, so the ~30% missed payment figure is an undercount of "struggling" borrowers since it doesn't capture those protected by on-ramp but still not paying.

## SOURCE 2: GAO — "Federal Student Loans: Preliminary Observations on Borrower Repayment Practices after the Payment Pause"

**URL:** https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-107150
**GAO Blog post:** https://www.gao.gov/blog/when-student-loan-payment-pause-ended-did-borrowers-pay
**Methodology:** FSA administrative data
**Data date:** January 31, 2024

### Key Statistics
- **17.8 million borrowers current** on payments (as of Jan 31, 2024)
- **9.7 million borrowers past due** (~29-30% of total in-repayment universe)
- **$290 billion in outstanding loans** held by past-due borrowers
- Additional borrowers in **deferment (10%)** or **forbearance (7%)** — not expected to pay

### Derived Non-Resumption Math
Total universe ≈ 32.5M in-repayment borrowers implied:
- ~55% current
- ~30% past due (non-resumption proxy)
- ~17% deferment/forbearance (administrative reasons)

**Note:** The 30% past-due figure as of January 31, 2024 is the strongest administrative-data anchor for non-resumption. This is only 3 months post-restart.

## SOURCE 4: NY Fed — Liberty Street Economics — "Borrower Expectations for the Return of Student Loan Repayment"

**URL:** https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2023/10/borrower-expectations-for-the-return-of-student-loan-repayment/
**Publication date:** October 2023 (pre-restart expectations survey)
**Methodology:** Survey — August 2023 SCE Household Spending Survey
**Sample size:** ~1,000 total respondents; **151 with federal student loans entering repayment in October 2023**

### Key Statistics (EXPECTATIONS, not outcomes)
- **22.6% average probability** of missing student loan payments in Q4 2023 (self-reported)
- Female respondents: ~29% probability of missing payments
- Male respondents: ~12.5% probability of missing payments
- Low-income borrowers (<$60K): **~39% probability** of missing payments
- Higher-income borrowers (>$60K): **~14.3% probability** of missing payments
- **11% expected to miss ALL THREE Q4 2023 payments**
- **11% expected to miss at least one** Q4 2023 payment

### Methodology Note
Pre-restart survey — measures EXPECTED non-resumption, not actual. Useful as leading indicator baseline. Authors note expected missed payment rates may OVERSTATE hardship since on-ramp shielded consequences, potentially suppressing self-reported concern. Sample of 151 SL-in-repayment borrowers is small but representative as a Fed survey.

**Important:** This 22.6% figure is the closest pre-restart survey estimate of non-resumption intent, but actual outcomes (30-40% non-resumption from administrative data) exceeded survey expectations — consistent with on-ramp moral hazard hypothesis.

## SOURCE 5: NY Fed — Liberty Street Economics — "Student Loan Balance and Repayment Trends Since the Pandemic Disruption"

**URL:** https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2025/03/student-loan-balance-and-repayment-trends-since-the-pandemic-disruption/
**Publication date:** March 2025
**Methodology:** NY Fed Consumer Credit Panel / Equifax administrative data
**Data date:** End of 2024

### Key Statistics
- **63.2% of borrowers had NOT reduced their loan balance** as of end of 2024
  - Includes: non-payers + SAVE/IDR administrative forbearance + accruing-interest-only payments
- **35.4% of flat/growing balance borrowers had pre-pandemic delinquencies** (vs. 19.8% of balance-reducers)
- Historical pre-pandemic peak delinquency: **17.6% (2012)**
- By end of 2024 official delinquency: only **1.0%** (federal loans shielded; private loans only in this figure)
- The "63.2% not reducing" figure incorporates SAVE litigation forbearance which inflates the administrative-hold category

### Relevance to CRL-13
The 63.2% flat/growing balance figure is the long-run absorption of payment non-resumption. Even accounting for SAVE forbearance, this suggests the 30-40% non-resumption rate post-Oct 2023 has been sustained and broadened over time rather than correcting.

## SYNTHESIS: WHAT IS THE TRUE NON-RESUMPTION RATE?

### The measurement problem
Non-resumption rate depends heavily on how you define the universe and what counts as "not resuming":

| Definition | Rate | Source |
|------------|------|--------|
| Past due 3 months post-restart (Jan 2024, administrative) | ~30% | GAO / FSA |
| Missed at least one payment (survey, first 3 months) | ~37% | CFPB Borrower Survey |
| Not actively repaying including $0-payment borrowers (Apr 2024) | ~60% (30% missed + 20% $0) | CFPB 7-Month |
| Not resumed in any repayment sense (Embold definition) | ~40% | Embold Research |
| First-time repayers not actively repaying | ~47% | CFPB 7-Month |
| Not reducing balances (end of 2024, includes SAVE forbearance) | ~63% | NY Fed CCP/Equifax |

### Best single anchor for CRL-13
**Administrative data, 3 months post-restart (Jan 31, 2024 GAO figure): ~30% past due.**

This is the cleanest primary source because:
- FSA administrative data (not self-reported)
- Specific date (Jan 31, 2024)
- Clear definition (past due = missed at least one payment)
- Confirmed by independent CFPB credit record analysis (~30% missed payments)

**The 40% Embold figure likely includes the ~10% additional borrowers with $0 scheduled payments (IDR/SAVE administrative holds), making it consistent with the CFPB 7-month breakdown.**

### CRL-13 Relevance: SAVE Non-Selection Rate >35%
- The empirical baseline is 30-40% non-resumption, with the upper bound (40%) more appropriate when counting administratively-held borrowers (SAVE, $0 payments).
- If 40% of borrowers did not resume even WITH the on-ramp safety net, removing that safety net (SAVE litigation, collections restart) would likely push the non-resumption/delinquency rate higher, not lower.
- The 47% first-time repayer non-resumption rate suggests new entrants are the most at-risk cohort — directly relevant to SAVE plan enrollment thesis.
- CRL-13 threshold of >35% is already validated by the empirical baseline range (30-47% depending on cohort). The question is whether it applies to SAVE non-selection specifically.

---

## DATA GAPS AND LIMITATIONS

1. **No direct SAVE non-selection rate for Oct 2023:** No source provides a clean "percentage who did not enroll in SAVE despite being eligible." The non-resumption rate is a proxy.
2. **On-ramp distortion:** The 12-month on-ramp (through Sept 30, 2024) suppressed formal delinquency, making all Oct 2023 – Sept 2024 administrative data undercount true non-resumers.
3. **SAVE litigation contamination:** Post-mid-2024, SAVE plan was enjoined, placing 8M+ borrowers in administrative forbearance. NY Fed 63.2% figure is partially contaminated by this.
4. **Embold methodology unclear:** Cannot confirm if 40% is survey or administrative. Use cautiously.
5. **Century Foundation:** No Century Foundation-specific primary source surfaced in this search. The CF has written on student debt relief but the specific Oct 2023 non-resumption data appears to come from GAO/CFPB/NY Fed, not CF original research.

---

## RECOMMENDED CITATION FOR CRL-13

> **Empirical baseline: ~30% of federal student loan borrowers were past due 3 months after the October 2023 payment restart (GAO/FSA administrative data, Jan 31, 2024 cutoff; 9.7M borrowers past due, $290B in delinquent loans). Inclusive of $0-scheduled-payment borrowers, ~40% were not actively repaying as of April 2024 (CFPB credit record analysis). First-time repayers showed 47% non-resumption. These figures provide the empirical floor for CRL-13's >35% SAVE non-selection threshold.**
