## THESIS

**"The Invisible $400 Billion" — Phantom debt masks true consumer leverage.**

Official consumer debt: $18.78T (NY Fed Q4 2025). But $400B+ in BNPL, cash advances, earned wage access, and payday lending is **invisible** to credit bureaus. True consumer debt load is 20-30% higher than reported for the bottom 60%.

This matters because:
- Lenders underwrite mortgages/auto/CC against incomplete DTI (35% apparent → 47% real)
- Credit scores don't reflect actual payment burden
- When employment shock hits, the "hidden" payments compete with "visible" payments
- Default cascades start in phantom debt and migrate to visible credit
- 63% of BNPL users are stacking — invisible leverage building

**PHAN sits BETWEEN consumer spending and CARL's credit vectors:**
```
Consumer spending pressure
    ↓
Turn to BNPL/cash advance (invisible borrowing)
    ↓
Stack multiple plans (63% stacking, 50%+ multi-app in NYC)
    ↓
Phantom obligations consume cash flow (invisible to lenders)
    ↓
Cannot service visible debt (CC, auto, mortgage)
    ↓
CARL vectors fire — but AFTER phantom debt already defaulted
    ↓
True losses higher than models predicted (the $40-60B surprise)
```

## CFPB 1033: WHAT HAPPENED

**Original plan:** CFPB Rule 1033 would force largest banks to share consumer financial data by Apr 1, 2026. BNPL providers (as card issuers per CFPB interpretive rule) would be covered. This would have created a "visibility shock" — suddenly phantom debt becomes visible.

**What actually happened:**
1. Banking groups sued to block the rule
2. Federal judge enjoined enforcement
3. Trump administration's CFPB questioned its own funding mechanism
4. Aug 2025: CFPB issued Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on "reconsideration"
5. Apr 1, 2026: Deadline passed with rule unenforced

**CARL implication:** The visibility shock is DELAYED INDEFINITELY. This is actually WORSE for the thesis:
- Phantom debt keeps growing without constraint
- Lenders can't see true DTI
- When the repricing finally comes (via defaults, not regulation), it will be larger and more sudden
- The $40-60B in distressed invisible BNPL debt continues to accumulate
