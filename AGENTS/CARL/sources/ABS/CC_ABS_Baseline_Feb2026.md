# Credit Card ABS Baseline — February 2026

**Compiled:** 2026-02-14
**Sources:** Fed, NY Fed, Discover Q4 2025 Earnings, Capital One Q4 2025 Earnings, WalletHub

---

## Executive Summary

**Key Finding:** Major issuer credit metrics are **STABILIZING**, not accelerating. Both Discover and Capital One reported declining charge-offs and flat-to-improving delinquencies in Q4 2025. However, state-level and age-cohort data reveal **severe bifurcation** — younger consumers and Sun Belt states remain under acute stress.

**Implication for CARL thesis:** Employment shock required to break the stabilization. Without labor deterioration, credit metrics may plateau rather than spike.

## Payment Rate (Leading Indicator)

**From Discover/Capital One commentary:**
- Payment rates have "stabilized" (Discover)
- Minimum payment proportion "running somewhat above pre-pandemic levels" (Capital One)

**Interpretation:** Payment rate is NOT declining — meaning delinquency formation is slowing, not accelerating. This is a LEADING indicator that suggests near-term credit improvement, not deterioration.

**Critical Watch:** If payment rates start declining again, that would be an early warning of renewed stress.

## CARL Thesis Calibration

### What This Data Says

1. **Major issuers stabilizing** — Discover/CapOne both improving
2. **State-level divergence extreme** — FL/NV/TX 12-14% vs MN/WI 7-8%
3. **Age cohort stress real but improving** — 30-49 still elevated
4. **Charge-off dollars declining** — $13B Q3 vs $15B Q1
5. **Payment rates stable** — not declining (bullish for near-term)

### What This Means for CARL

**Without employment shock:** Credit metrics likely plateau at elevated-but-not-crisis levels. Major issuers can absorb this.

**With employment shock (LABOR thesis):** The ~2.5pp elevation in 30-49 cohort + already-stressed Sun Belt states would convert rapidly to spike. The system has **no buffer** but is currently **not accelerating**.

**Key insight:** CARL is a **coiled spring**, not an **active fire**. Employment deterioration is the release mechanism.
