# Wright: Hidden Stress Indicators

*Source: Melody Wright "Politicians Be Politicking" (Jan 9, 2026)*

---

## Multiple Job Holders Exceed 2008 Levels

> "We had more workers working a full-time and part-time job in November to make ends meet than we did in **November 2008**."

| Metric | Since 2008 |
|--------|------------|
| Population growth | +12.32% |
| Multiple job holders growth | **+30.00%** |

**Implication:** Hidden stress not captured in unemployment rate. People working multiple jobs just to survive = no financial cushion.

---

## Challenger Layoff Data

> "Employers announced **1,206,374 job cuts in 2025**, an increase of **58%** from the 761,358 announced in 2024."

> "Those layoffs are still flowing through the system."

**Context:** Announcements precede actual separations by 60+ days (WARN Act). Q4 2025 announcements = Q1-Q2 2026 actuals.

---

## AI Not Causing Mass Layoffs (Yet)

> "We are **not yet at a stage where massive layoffs would occur due to the implementation of these technologies**."

Wright cites analysis showing AI layoff claims are often rebranding:
> "The primary motivation for this rebranding of job cuts appears to be investor relations... attributing staff reductions to AI adoption 'conveys a more positive message to investors' than admitting to traditional business failures."

**Aligns with:** Burry's finding that AI productivity = 1 basis point in 2025.

---

## MBS Intervention = Diminishing Returns

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Agency MBS purchased | $47B |
| Rate reduction achieved | 76 bps |
| Cost per basis point | **$618M** |

> "From January of 2021 to April of 2022, the Fed bought $700B in MBS, $500B more than the agencies will be allowed to buy. **Mortgage rates actually rose during that time** from 2.65% to 3.69%."

**Wright's conclusion:** Additional $200B MBS buying won't restore affordability. "These operations tend to have a **diminishing return**."

---

## Institutional Investor Bailout Prediction

Wright predicts "Phase 2" following the institutional buyer ban:

> "What I foresee is some sort of bailout where the government offers to buy or facilitate the purchase of some of these single-family homes at a **price floor** that would ultimately be less painful than actual price discovery."

> "I see this outcome as the **highest probability** at the moment."

**Implication:** Policy may attempt to prevent true price discovery. But: "Even if the administration tries this... those efforts **will not arrest home price declines**."
