## 7. FEDERAL CONTRACTOR DEBT PROFILE — SUPPORTING THE 0-2 MONTH HYPOTHESIS

### Why Contractors Are More Vulnerable Than Direct Federal Employees

| Factor | Federal Contractor | Federal Employee |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Stop-work notice type | Immediate, no warning required | 60-day RIF notice |
| Back-pay guarantee | **NONE** | Guaranteed during notice |
| Severance pay | 0 (stop-work order) | 0 (RIF) but back-pay during notice |
| UI eligibility | Varies — often lag in filing | Can file immediately on separation |
| Financial buffer from employer | Nothing | ~2-3 months effective income from notice |
| Typical financial profile | High income, high housing cost | Similar income, similar costs |
| **Effective time to first missed payment** | **0-2 months** | **1-3 months** |

### National Survey of Consumer Finances — High-Income Household Vulnerability

From Fed Financial Stability Report (November 2025):
- National household debt-to-GDP: 20+ year lows (AGGREGATE is fine)
- **BUT:** Mortgage debt = 75% of all household debt ($13.5T of $18.78T total)
- High-income households in high-cost areas (like DC metro Maryland) have HIGH ABSOLUTE mortgage debt
- The SCF shows that 60th-80th income percentile households (typical contractors) have HIGH housing cost burden in expensive metros despite high nominal incomes

**Key concept from academic literature:**
- "Asset-rich, cash-flow poor" is the defining vulnerability of DC metro federal workers
- High home values create paper wealth → LOW incentive to default if equity exists
- BUT: 2022-2023 vintages have LESS equity (bought near peak) → double trigger
- High cost of living + moderate equity + zero back-pay = structurally fragile
