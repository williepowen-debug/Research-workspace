## Prompt

I'm building a monitoring system to track "latent vulnerability" — US populations who are NOT currently in financial distress but are ONE INCIDENT away from crisis (medical event, accident, job loss, disaster).

I need to understand: (1) how other industries track pre-crisis populations, and (2) what data sources I can use for ongoing monitoring.

## PART C: EXISTING METHODOLOGIES

How do these domains identify and track at-risk-but-not-yet-distressed populations?

1. INSURANCE INDUSTRY:
   - How do actuaries model "at-risk but not yet claimed" populations?
   - What metrics indicate elevated future claims probability?
   - How is "adverse selection" population identified?

2. CREDIT RISK / BANKING:
   - How do lenders identify "thin file" or "credit invisible" high-risk borrowers?
   - What behavioral signals indicate approaching distress BEFORE missed payments?
   - Are there "early warning" scores beyond FICO?

3. PUBLIC HEALTH SURVEILLANCE:
   - How does CDC/HHS track health-vulnerable populations before they become patients?
   - What indices measure community health vulnerability?
   - How is "deferred care" population sized and monitored?

4. DISASTER PREPAREDNESS:
   - How does FEMA model populations vulnerable to disaster-triggered financial loss?
   - What is the "Social Vulnerability Index" — how constructed?
   - Are there financial vulnerability overlays on disaster risk maps?

5. POVERTY/ECONOMIC RESEARCH:
   - How is "asset poverty" or "liquid asset poverty" defined and measured?
   - What longitudinal studies track transition from "struggling" to "distressed"?
   - Are there composite vulnerability indices combining multiple risk factors?

For each: methodology, key data sources, any public indices/scores, update frequency.
