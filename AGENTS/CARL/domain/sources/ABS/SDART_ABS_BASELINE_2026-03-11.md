**Date:** March 11, 2026
**Source:** SEC EDGAR 10-D filings (Servicer Certificates, EX-99.1)
**All data as of:** January 31, 2026 collection period (filed February 2026)

---

## Executive Summary

Santander's auto ABS pools show **dramatically elevated delinquency and loss rates** compared to prime originators like Honda. At 25 months seasoned, SDART 2024-1 has a cumulative net loss (CNL) of **8.48%** — roughly **34x Honda's 0.25%** at identical seasoning. Total 30+ day delinquency for SDART trusts runs **17-25%** vs Honda's **1.21%**. These are expected given Santander's subprime/non-prime origination profile, but the absolute levels and vintage trajectory deserve close monitoring given Santander's documented $550M fraud settlement history.

## 4. Fraud Signal Assessment

### Background: $550M Multistate Settlement (May 2020)
- 34 state attorneys general settled with Santander Consumer USA
- Allegations: Santander "knew or should have known" that certain dealers were **falsifying income and other information** on auto loan applications
- Santander continued funding loans through these dealers despite internal models showing **high probability of default**
- Settlement required Santander to implement ability-to-pay underwriting standards

### Current Performance — Red Flags:

1. **Extremely high early delinquency**: SDART 2025-1 has 17.15% total delinquency at only 13 months. This means borrowers are going delinquent almost immediately after origination — a classic fraud signature.

2. **Low recovery rates**: SDART recovery rates (~43% on liquidation proceeds vs. defaults) are significantly below Honda (~63%). This suggests either inflated vehicle values at origination or poor collateral quality.

3. **Volume-driven origination continues**: SDART 2025-1 was the largest pool at $2.25B (99,060 receivables). Pool sizes have been growing, not shrinking, despite persistent performance issues.

4. **WAC of 18%+**: Weighted average coupon above 18% confirms deep subprime origination. At these rates, the borrower profile is inherently high-risk.

5. **Servicing transfer (April 2025)**: SEC 8-K filing (March 2025) disclosed that Santander Consumer reorganized its servicing operations, with "a majority of the motor vehicle receivable servicing performed by SC" transferred to Santander Bank, N.A. as of April 1, 2025. Servicing transfers during active deterioration are a potential red flag — or an attempt at remediation.

### What's NOT a red flag (yet):
- No delinquency triggers breached (24% threshold)
- OC levels appear adequate (SDART 2024-1 at 20.26% OC)
- No reported demands for repurchase on any trust
- Monthly loss rates are NOT accelerating — they're roughly flat across recent months

## 5. Key Risk Vectors to Monitor

1. **Tariff impact on used car values**: If tariffs push new car prices up, used car values may rise (supporting recoveries) — but if tariffs slow the economy and hit subprime borrowers' employment, delinquency could spike further.

2. **Seasonal patterns**: January is typically a weak month for auto loan performance (post-holiday stress). The data above reflects January collections.

3. **Extension rates**: Not directly reported in servicer certificates. Would need supplemental data to assess whether Santander is using extensions to mask delinquency (a known historical practice per the $550M settlement).

4. **Compare to DRIVE trusts**: DRIVE Auto Receivables Trust (deeper subprime) was Santander's other shelf. Need to check if still active or wound down. Historical DRIVE deals had CNL expectations of 15-17%.

5. **2022 vintage tail risk**: Per industry reports, 2022 vintage subprime auto loans are the worst-performing cohort since the GFC. SDART 2023 vintages may be inheriting some of this stress as they season.

## 7. Bottom Line

**Santander SDART performance is poor but roughly in line with historical subprime expectations.** The pools are losing ~0.65-0.80% per month and running 17-25% total delinquency. Terminal CNL will likely reach 12-14%.

**The fraud signal is ambiguous.** The $550M settlement was real, the origination practices were documented, and the performance is objectively terrible compared to prime originators. But for subprime auto ABS, these numbers aren't wildly outside historical norms for SDART specifically. The question isn't whether Santander's pools perform badly — they always have — it's whether the **current vintages are performing worse than historical SDART vintages at the same seasoning point.**

**To answer that definitively**, we would need historical monthly data from older vintages (2019, 2020, 2021, 2022) at equivalent months seasoned. S&P's Auto Loan Securitization Index (ALSI) tracks this but is paywalled. Notably, S&P reported that SDART was among the issuers showing **lower CNLs** on 2023 vintages compared to 2022 — suggesting some improvement, not worsening.

**The real watch point is whether economic stress (tariffs, unemployment) causes these already-stretched pools to breach their structural protections.** With 20%+ OC and reserve accounts fully funded, there's still meaningful subordination. But if monthly losses accelerate from 0.8% to 1.2%+, the math starts to change.
