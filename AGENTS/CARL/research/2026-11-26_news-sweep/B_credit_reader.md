**What changed (Part 1)**
- **Year over year the pools improved:** charge-offs fell at every trust from October 2025 to October 2026. Net rates: COMET 2.34%→1.91%, Synchrony 4.35%→3.83%, AmEx 1.34%→0.95%, Citi 1.95%→1.79%, BA 2.10%→1.96%. Chase held at 1.44%. 30+ delinquency was also lower or flat at every trust (COMET −12bp, AmEx −7bp, Chase −4bp, Synchrony −2bp, Citi −2bp; BA +2bp).
- **Month over month, early-stage delinquency rose at all six trusts from September to October 2026.** 30+: COMET +3bp, Synchrony +13bp, AmEx +2bp, Citi +5bp, Chase +3bp, BA +3bp. 60+: Synchrony +10bp and BA +6bp. Some of this is the normal Q4 seasonal pattern; CARL holds no seasonal factor, so the size of the seasonal part is unknown.
- **COMET's monthly charge-offs rose:** gross 2.85%→3.06% and net 1.69%→1.91% from September to October. They are still 43bp below October 2025 on a net basis.
- **Payment rates stayed healthy (counterevidence to the stress thesis):** Chase 51.42% (equal to October 2025), AmEx 49.63% (above 49.07% in October 2025), COMET 46.55% (above 46.41%), Synchrony 24.02% (above 23.13%). No trust is near a pay-out or early-amortization trigger. Every trust's excess spread is between roughly 16% and 25%.
- **Synchrony, the private-label and most subprime-tilted pool here:** 30+ is 3.01% against 3.03% a year earlier. That is flat on the year, but Synchrony had the largest monthly rise in early-stage delinquency (+13bp).
- **DCENT no longer reports.** The trust deregistered (Form 15-15D, 2026-09-15), so Discover's card performance cannot be seen in public 10-Ds.

**Gaps/blocked (Part 1):** none blocked; EDGAR served everything. The COMET excess-spread percentage was not pulled, only the dollar amount. Trust-level gross and net charge-off rates for BA are not printed in the exhibit.

**What changed (Part 2)**
- **Card delinquency is still split between borrowers and balances.** TransUnion's borrower-level 90+ rose to 2.50% (+13bp YoY) while the balance-level figure fell to 1.99% (−6bp). Personal-loan borrower-level 60+ reached 4.12% (+60bp), the highest Q3 since 2010. More borrowers are delinquent on smaller balances, which points to a lower-tier concentration.
- **Lenders are lending further down the credit scale.** Below-prime share of card originations rose to 42.3% (from 40.6%) and of personal loans to 69.0% (from 66.1%). Subprime card originations grew 16.5% YoY. That seeds 2027 vintage risk. Cox and KBRA in Part 3 show the same in auto.
- **Household expectations got worse in the NY Fed survey:** the expected chance of missing a debt payment jumped from 12.2% to 13.8% in one month, and job-loss and unemployment expectations also rose. The Credit Access Survey shows applications down to 42.1%, voluntary closures at a series-high 18.6%, and rejections up to 18.3% from 16.1% (but below October 2025's 24.8%).
- **Counterevidence, spending:** BofA shows lower-income spending growth of 5.9%, leading all cohorts, and lower-income after-tax wages up 4.7%, the strongest cohort. Velera debit spending is up 9.5%. PNC total spending is up 5.6%. This is consistent with October12 scope review's finding of no broad downward convergence.
- **Counterevidence, bureau delinquency:** Equifax bankcard severe DQ is 2.74% (−9bp YoY), private-label 3.85% (−40bp) and auto 1.50% (−7bp). Bankcard write-offs are 52.4bp vs 55.5bp.
- **The two banks disagree on revolvers:** BofA says the share paying in full is rising in every cohort. PNC says the share of balances paid off has declined through 2026 and balances per account are up more than 9%. The bases differ (households vs balances, two different bank books), so this is not reconciled. Also: BofA minimum-payment-only share and 401(k) hardship withdrawals are both rising "at the margins."

**What changed (Part 3)**
- **Car-Mart filed for Chapter 11 on 2026-11-24.** The November standstill ran out (relief through 11/22) and became a prepackaged sale. The purchaser assumes the securitization platform and liabilities, so the ACM ABS trusts are **not** obviously impaired by the filing itself. It is the second subprime auto dealer-lender failure after Tricolor in 2025.
- **Subprime ABS delinquencies are flat to slightly lower on the year.** KBRA non-prime 60+ is 6.70%, −3bp YoY. Fitch (secondary) is 6.27%, −19bp YoY, its third straight YoY decline. **Losses have not followed:** KBRA non-prime annualized net loss is 10.28%, +46bp YoY. The losses are coming from severity and recoveries, not new delinquency.
- **Credit availability is the loosest since April 2015.** Cox's index is 107.1, subprime share 17.9% (+180bp YoY), approvals 73.9%, and loans over 72 months 31.1% (+340bp YoY). With the TransUnion subprime auto originations at +9.8%, this is late-cycle loosening. It is a timing risk for the 2026–27 vintages, not a current delinquency signal.
- **Used-car values are flat, which caps recovery support.** Manheim is unchanged at 206.8 and only +0.7% YoY, down from +2.0% in October. Not-seasonally-adjusted prices are −0.1% YoY. Combined with negative-equity trades at 58.7%, this is consistent with KBRA's loss rate rising year over year.
- **Counterevidence:** prime auto is benign (KBRA 0.58% 60+, net loss 0.64%, −11bp YoY). Bureau-level auto delinquency is down (TransUnion 1.64% vs 1.65%, Equifax 1.50%, −7bp).
