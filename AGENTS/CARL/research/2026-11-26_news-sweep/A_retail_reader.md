# Sweep A: U.S. retail fiscal Q3 2026 reports, read by income cohort

Reader: CARL read-only sweep. Compiled 2026-11-26. Window: reports released 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-26, with O'Reilly (2026-10-28) added for context.

**Source rules**
- **PRIMARY** means one of two things: a company 8-K exhibit pulled from SEC EDGAR (User-Agent "CARL Research carl@research.local"), or a page or transcript hosted by the company itself (Walmart's own transcript PDF, Target's corporate page).
- **SECONDARY** means a third-party transcript or a press repost.
  - The fool.com and singjupost transcripts are marked "AI-assisted" by those sites.
  - Their quotes came to me through a WebFetch summarizer and were **not checked against the call audio**. Treat the exact wording as provisional.
- **Year check:** every period below was confirmed as 2026 against the filing's own quarter-end date. Search results often mixed in the **fiscal Q3 2025** calls held Nov–Dec 2025. One example is Dollar Tree's "60% of 3M new households earn >$100K" line, which comes from the **2025-12-03** call and is **not** used here as 2026 data.

## Cross-company pattern (fiscal Q3 2026)

- **Traffic held up at value formats.** Dollar Tree traffic +3.8%, Target +5.0%, Ross comp +8% traffic-led, Sam's Club transactions +5.1%, BJ's promo traffic +6%, Walmart U.S. transactions +1.5%. *Counterevidence:* this is not a picture of lower-income shoppers pulling back across the board in Aug–Oct 2026.
- **Income-cohort claims split.**
  - Upper-led: Walmart's share gains were "led by upper-income households"; more than half of Dollar Tree's growth is "trade-in" middle- and higher-income shoppers.
  - Broad-based: Target ("spanning income demographics"), Ross ("extremely consistent across incomes"), TJX ("not ... a dramatic shift ... by income group"), BJ's ("every income level").
  - *Counterevidence:* four of six retailers who spoke to it say the strength is broad-based.
- **Spending is shifting toward necessities.**
  - Dollar Tree consumables +8.9% vs discretionary +0.4%, "to stretch their budgets".
  - Burlington's low-to-moderate income core is "buying closer to need" and hurt by "the rise in gas prices".
  - Home improvement transactions fell (Home Depot -0.5%, Lowe's -1.7%) while ticket rose; customers are doing small repair and maintenance work; Lowe's says the DIY customer is "under pressure" on discretionary projects like kitchens and flooring.
- **Price, not volume, carried several comps.**
  - Best Buy +6.1% = average selling price ~+6% with units flat (computing ASP +25%, units down mid-teens).
  - Home Depot's +3% ticket is about half price.
  - Costco U.S. +12.6% reported vs +8.1% ex gas and FX; BJ's +13.5% vs +4.1%.
  - Gasoline inflation is inflating club headline numbers.
- **The fuel shock is everywhere.**
  - Diesel at about $6 (Lowe's, BJ's); Walmart has absorbed over $2B of diesel and transport cost; Burlington cut Q4 EPS $0.20.
  - Gap, TJX, Ross and Dollar Tree all cite fuel and freight.
  - For the household side, only Burlington ties gas prices directly to shopper spending. The others discuss fuel as a cost to the company.
- **Tariff-refund money (IEEPA) was a one-time boost and partly went into prices.**
  - Refunds lifted Q3 EPS at Target ($0.50), Lowe's ($0.34), Kohl's ($0.28) and ANF ($0.40), among others.
  - Walmart (~7,700 rollbacks), Dollar Tree ($103M), Lowe's (~1/3) and Burlington (Q3 and Q4) put part of it back into prices.
  - Price relief at the low end is therefore partly one-off. BJ's flags "considerable pressure ... price increase" for 2027.
- **October was weak and November is mixed.**
  - Warm weather hit October at Target, Gap, Burlington (-300 bps), Ross and TJX.
  - November improving: Target ("accelerating"), Ross ("very solid start"), Gap ("recent improvement"), Home Depot ("consistent with guidance").
  - November still soft: Burlington ("continued into November").
- **Most guidance went up.**
  - Raised: Walmart, Target, Best Buy, Dollar Tree, Ross (EPS), BJ's, Kohl's, ANF, Dick's; Lowe's raised sales but cut margin.
  - Cut: Gap (sales and EPS), Burlington (Q4 EPS for fuel); Home Depot narrowed EPS to ~+2%.
  - Burlington and Gap have the most value-heavy or lower-income customer mixes in this set, and they are the ones that cut.
