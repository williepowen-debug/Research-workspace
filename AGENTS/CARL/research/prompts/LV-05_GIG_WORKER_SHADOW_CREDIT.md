## Context

Previous research established:
- 58% of gig workers seek emergency loans quarterly (RadCred 2026)
- Gig workers have 73% traditional credit denial rate
- Cash advance apps, earned wage access, and BNPL are primary credit sources for this population
- We estimated $50B in gig-specific phantom debt (10M workers × $5K average) but this is unvalidated

Gig workers may be the primary "phantom debt factory" — generating invisible consumer debt that doesn't appear in credit bureau data until crisis.

## Prompt

I'm researching the shadow credit usage patterns of gig economy workers in the United States. I need to validate estimates of gig-worker-specific phantom debt and understand their credit behavior in detail.

## PART A: GIG WORKER POPULATION SIZING

1. **Current gig worker count:**
   - How many Americans currently work in the gig economy?
   - Break down by type:
     - Rideshare (Uber, Lyft)

     - Freelance/contract (Upwork, Fiverr)

2. **Demographic profile:**
   - Income distribution of gig workers

2. **Cash advance apps:**

3. **Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL):**

   - Delinquency rates for gig workers on BNPL
   - "BNPL stacking" prevalence (using multiple BNPL for same purchase or concurrent purchases)

4. **Informal/alternative credit:**
   - Payday loan usage among gig workers
   - Pawn shop usage
   - Title loan usage
   - Family/friend borrowing patterns
   - Any platform-specific financing (e.g., vehicle financing through Uber)
