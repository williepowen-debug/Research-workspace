## Context

We know 28-38% of Americans currently defer medical care due to cost. But this is a snapshot. If care deferral is INCREASING over time, then:
- The latent vulnerable population is growing
- The "deferred liability" reservoir is filling faster
- Future conversion waves will be larger

Similarly, other behavioral adaptations (skipping insurance, avoiding maintenance, reducing coverage) indicate growing latent vulnerability. Understanding the TRAJECTORY of these behaviors is crucial for forecasting.

---

## Prompt

I'm researching how American households have changed their risk-avoidance and cost-deferral behaviors over time, specifically behaviors that create future financial vulnerability.

## PART A: MEDICAL CARE DEFERRAL TRENDS

1. **Historical time series:**
   - What percentage of Americans reported delaying/skipping medical care due to cost in:
     - 2010
     - 2015
     - 2019 (pre-pandemic)
     - 2021 (pandemic)
     - 2023
     - 2025 (most recent)
   - Source this from consistent surveys (NHIS, Gallup, Commonwealth Fund) for valid comparison

2. **Deferral by type of care:**
   - Trend over time for:
     - Preventive care (screenings, checkups)
     - Dental care
     - Mental health care
     - Prescription medications
     - Specialist visits
     - Elective procedures
   - Which types of deferral are increasing fastest?

3. **Deferral by population segment:**
   - Trends by income level (low, middle, high)
   - Trends by insurance status (uninsured, underinsured, adequately insured)
   - Trends by age group
   - Are any segments showing acceleration?

4. **The "new normal" question:**
   - Did pandemic-era care deferral become permanent for some populations?
   - Has care-seeking behavior recovered to pre-pandemic levels?
   - What percentage report new lasting habits of avoiding healthcare?

2. **Auto insurance behavior:**

3. **Homeowner/renter insurance:**

## PART D: DRIVERS OF BEHAVIORAL CHANGE

1. **Cost pressure drivers:**
   - Healthcare cost inflation vs. wage growth over time
   - Insurance premium growth vs. wage growth
   - How much of deferral increase is explained by relative cost increases?

2. **Structural drivers:**
   - High-deductible plan proliferation → care deferral
   - Gig work growth → insurance gap growth
   - Inflation → general deferral increase

3. **Awareness and attitudes:**
   - Have attitudes toward healthcare seeking changed?
   - Is there a generational difference in deferral behavior?
   - Has COVID changed risk perception (either direction)?

## PART E: FORECASTING

1. **Trend extrapolation:**
   - If current trends continue, what will care deferral rate be in 2028? 2030?
   - At what rate is the "deferred liability reservoir" filling?

2. **Inflection points:**
   - Are there thresholds where deferral behavior changes discontinuously?
   - E.g., if healthcare costs exceed X% of income, does deferral spike?
   - Have we crossed any such thresholds recently?

3. **The conversion lag:**
   - If someone defers care in 2025, when does the deferred liability typically come due?
   - What's the average "incubation period" for deferred care to become acute care?
   - This helps predict when today's deferral becomes tomorrow's crisis
