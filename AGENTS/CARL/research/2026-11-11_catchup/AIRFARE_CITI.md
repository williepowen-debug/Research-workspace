**What it does to the household travel and discretionary read: very little, and it leans against squeeze, not toward it.**

1. **It is a price, not a quantity.** Fares are high and the monthly increases are shrinking (Aug +2.68%, Oct +0.41% SA). Nothing here shows fewer trips.
2. **The people paying it are mostly the top cohort.** Air travel spending is concentrated in higher-income households (CARL's framework premise; the BLS Consumer Expenditure income split was not pulled today). A fare rise is a cost on the upper cohort's discretionary budget. It does not reach the bottom-60 credit rows that CARL measures.
3. **Demand held through the rise.** JetBlue Q3 revenue +21.1%, Fort Lauderdale unit revenue up about 15% on capacity up over 65% (KB-567, 10/28); BofA September spending grows in every income cohort (STATUS trade-down row). High fares plus held demand is top-cohort resilience, consistent with the 10/12 scope review (broad downward convergence NOT established).
4. **Spirit is the one bottom-cohort channel, and it is unmeasured.** Spirit's May liquidation (MARCO) removed the cheapest seats. For price-sensitive travellers that is losing the option, not paying more, and the index partly records it as a price rise. No income-split trip-count series exists to measure who was shed (MEMORY M47).
5. **Inflation read unchanged.** Airline fares sit inside October core (+0.296%); the +0.41% October fare move is a small part. V12 is already at 5.
