## Reading order and consequences

1. Save BOTH release documents and tables with retrieval time/hash. Record source period and release vintage. Compare any revised July/August figures to the saved old vintage BEFORE deriving the September change. Retain old and new cells separately; no circular revision comparison.
2. Read September real PCE, goods/durables/nondurables/services breadth, real DPI, saving rate and essential-cost composition. Nominal spending alone cannot distinguish more purchases from higher fuel prices. Higher saving with falling spending can be retrenchment; falling saving with spending/income weakness indicates buffer drawdown. Neither alone identifies the bottom 60%.
3. Read Q3 real PCE growth and its GDP contribution separately from headline GDP, inventories, net exports and government. A positive GDP print driven by inventories or imports does not establish consumer strength. Do not add chained-dollar category levels as though they were additive current-dollar totals.
4. Positive real monthly spending is evidence against a current aggregate collapse, even if affordability is worse. One negative month is a contraction observation, not proof of a broad or sustained collapse. A negative quarter plus weakness across goods/services would strengthen the aggregate retrenchment read; income/wealth-cohort convergence still needs independent cohort evidence. Survey intentions are not realized outlays.
5. Keep scope assessment distinct from score: the 10/12 judgment was concentrated stress supported, broad spending collapse unestablished, 60/40 assumption unmeasured. Update the judgment in both directions. Apply only the existing canonical THESIS downgrade/kill letters; PCE is not an automatic score switch. DOC's healthcare-services contribution is passed as source evidence, not a CARL edit/grade of its child ledger.

It shows why a positive Q3 consumption number can coexist with a weak September. Recompute after revisions; never freeze these illustration levels into a new gate.
