## 2026-03-27 — Continuing Claims Drop to Lowest Since May 2024

**Data:** Initial claims 210K (+5K, benign). Continuing claims 1,819K (-32K) — lowest since May 2024. [BLS/DOL, week ending Mar 21]

**Why it matters:** Continuing claims moved AWAY from the 1,900K YELLOW threshold (gap widened from 32K to 81K). The Mullin DHS normalization did NOT produce a claims spike. Labor market is genuinely stable by this measure. If employment doesn't crack, the "detonator" in our thesis doesn't fire — consumer stress stays slow-burn rather than step-function.

**Thesis impact:** Weakens the timing case. Directionally, stress is real (duration 25.7wk still rising). But the acute claims spike we modeled as accelerating consumer DQ conversion isn't materializing. Q2-Q3 timeline holds but without claims acceleration, the conversion may be slower and more gradual than the convergence score (44/50) implies.

**What would change my mind back:** Claims >230K sustained for 3+ weeks, OR continuing claims reverse toward 1,900K. One benign print doesn't kill the thesis, but continued stability here means the bear case needs the gas/food/UI exhaustion vectors to do the work without an employment spike.

## 2026-03-27 — NAR Existing Home Sales Feb +1.7% MoM

**Data:** Feb 2026 existing home sales 4.09M SAAR (+1.7% from 3.91M Jan). Affordability index 117.6 — highest since Mar 2022, improved 8th consecutive month. [NAR, released Mar 10]

**Why it matters:** Sales bounced from Jan's 4-year crash. Affordability improving. If housing transactions stabilize, Path C (Housing → Banks) slows.

**Thesis impact:** Mild weakening of Path C timing. BUT: 4.09M is still far below pre-pandemic ~5.9M avg. Transaction velocity remains structurally impaired. Yun himself says "long way to go." This is a dead cat bounce, not a recovery — unless it sustains into March/April.

**What would change my mind back:** Feb was an isolated bounce and March reverts below 4.0M. Or affordability index reversal as gas + insurance costs compress real purchasing power.
