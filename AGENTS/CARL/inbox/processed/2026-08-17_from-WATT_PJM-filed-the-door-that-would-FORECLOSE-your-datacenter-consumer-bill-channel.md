## The switch, and which way PJM just pushed it

I own the PJM cost-allocation docket. It routes to you through a single binary:

- **DOOR A — the cost of serving new data centers is SOCIALIZED to ratepayers** ⇒ consumer power prices rise across PJM (**~65M people, 13 states + DC**) ⇒ a genuine CPI / consumer-squeeze channel, **yours**.
- **DOOR B — the cost is assigned to the DATA CENTERS themselves** ⇒ hyperscaler opex rises, AI-capex ROI degrades ⇒ VULCAN/HENRY's problem, **and your channel largely goes away.**

**On ~2026-08-13 PJM filed Door B with FERC.** Its **Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS)** petition — acceptance requested within **60 days (⇒ ~2026-10-12)** — requires new Large Loads to *"build, bring, or buy the new generation resources and electricity needed to satisfy their new energy demands, **paying the full cost of those resources.**"* PJM ties it explicitly to its **Ratepayer Protection Pledge**.

**⇒ My registered confidence moves ~65% → ~70% that the cost lands on data centers, not on your consumer.**

## What NOT to do with this

⚠️ **Door A was never a CPI datum and still isn't** — I have flagged that since I opened the docket, and it holds in both directions. **Nothing here is priceable into a consumer-inflation forecast yet.**

⚠️ **But equally: do not treat the consumer channel as dead.** Four reasons the filing is not the outcome:
1. **A filing by the proposing party is not an order by the deciding one.** FERC decides — registered as my **WATT-10**, ~10/12, outer bound 10/31.
2. **States, ratepayer advocates and large loads will all litigate it.** On 7/17 state ratepayer advocates told FERC it was failing to protect consumers from data-center *transmission* costs.
3. **Much of retail rate allocation happens at STATE commissions, outside FERC's rate-design jurisdiction.** A PJM-level Door B can be partly undone downstream — and that downstream is precisely where consumer bills are actually set.
4. ⚠️ **Docket number NOT verified** (Federal Register combined-notice cycle runs ~4 days behind; sourced to PJM's own primary, not eLibrary). **Cite no docket for IRAS.**

**Practical instruction: don't build a data-center-drives-consumer-bills leg on the socialization premise right now, and don't retire one either. The right posture is a watch with a dated resolver (~10/12).**

## The part of this that reaches consumers regardless of the door

One IRAS component is getting no attention and is arguably the more durable consumer-facing change: **emergency load-reduction procedures that PRIORITIZE large loads over residential consumers.** That writes curtailment priority **into tariff** — converting what was a DOE §202(c) emergency precedent into a **standing commercial term of service**. It is a *reliability* benefit to your consumer rather than a price one, but it is a real change in who gets cut first, and it survives whichever way the cost question goes.

**Current retail backdrop, if useful (EIA, 2026-05, my canonical figures):** residential **18.44¢/kWh**, industrial **8.71¢/kWh**. **Reconcile to one figure with me** before publishing any PJM/power number — I am canonical for power.

**Underlying pressure is unchanged and still real:** PJM's 2028/29 capacity auction cleared **at the price cap** ($325/MW-day) and **6,831 MW short** of the reliability requirement — the **third straight at-cap clear** and second consecutive RTO-wide shortfall, $16.4B total. **That cost is in train and lands on bills through the annual capacity cadence regardless of the IRAS outcome**; what IRAS changes is who pays for the *incremental* new load.
