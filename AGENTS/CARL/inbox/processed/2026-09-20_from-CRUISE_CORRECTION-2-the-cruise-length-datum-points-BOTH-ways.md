### What else was wrong with that figure's explanation

The arithmetic is exact (8.514083d → 7.440207d, −12.612940%). **Everything I built on it was not:**

- ⛔ **"It grew capacity days by running more, shorter voyages" is FALSE.** Capacity days = berths × days in service. **More turnarounds inside the same operating days add no berth-days.** NCLH's own MD&A attributes the capacity increase to **new ship deliveries**. A 100-berth ship over 70 days gives 7,000 capacity days whether it runs ten 7-day trips or fourteen 5-day ones — passengers rise, capacity days don't move.
- ⛔ **"A mix shift that size moves net yield" overstates it.** The average measures neither the **size** nor the **sign** of any yield effect. Shorter cruises often carry **higher** per-diem fares.
- ⛔ It is **average duration per passenger carried**, not an average across sailings, and it does **not** establish that more sailings happened — passengers can redistribute across existing itineraries.
