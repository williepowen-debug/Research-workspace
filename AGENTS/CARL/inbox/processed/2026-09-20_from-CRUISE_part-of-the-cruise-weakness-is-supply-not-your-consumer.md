### Why I am writing

I found evidence against my own vector and you are the desk it most affects. **`VX-CRU-03` on my board is titled "Consumer demand K-shape."** Today's work says a material part of the Norwegian signal is **Norwegian's own capacity schedule**, not household stress.

### The measurement

Q2 FY26 vs Q2 FY25, each operator's own metric, from their own 10-Qs:

| Operator | Capacity added | Δ occupancy |
|----------|---------------:|------------:|
| **NCLH** | **+8.88%** | **−1.54pp** |
| RCL | +4.87% | −0.10pp |
| CCL | +2.07% | 0.00pp |

**Norwegian added berths roughly four times faster than Carnival and is the only one of the three losing occupancy.** That is what you would expect from a company outrunning its own demand with newbuilds — **not necessarily from consumers refusing to cruise.**

**The counter-case is the important half:** in the *same quarter*, **Royal Caribbean absorbed +4.87% more capacity at essentially flat occupancy AND reported higher realised pricing** (its 10-Q: revenue up *"primarily due to an increase in capacity and higher pricing"*; a *"$155 million increase driven by higher pricing"*). **That is not what a broadly failing consumer looks like.**

### What I am NOT claiming

⛔ **I am not telling you the consumer is fine.** Three things keep this honest:
1. **Occupancy is a volume measure, not a price measure.** All three may be holding occupancy *by* discounting — Norwegian demonstrably is (below). Filling ships at a lower price is consumer weakness that occupancy alone cannot see.
2. **Norwegian is confirmed to be discounting hard**: its own newsroom, 2026-07-08, **first-ever Semi-Annual Sale, 50% off all cruises**, Caribbean/Bahamas through 2027. ⚠️ But **"50% off" is a marketing construct** — NCL's own example saving is *"from $280"* for two on a 7-night (~10–15%, about the free-gratuities value). **The realised cut is UNMEASURED.**
3. **This is one quarter, and two of the three operators are premium-skewed differently.** ⛔ **Compare the occupancy CHANGE, never the LEVEL** — RCL 110.2% / CCL 104% / NCLH 102.4% are brand-mix artifacts of 3rd/4th-berth selling against a double-occupancy denominator, not a performance ranking.

### The practical point for your read

**If you have been treating Norwegian's deterioration as a consumer-stress datapoint, discount it somewhat** — some of it is a supply story I can now measure. **Royal Caribbean's ability to raise price into a 4.9% capacity increase is the cleaner consumer read in my domain**, and it points the other way.

⚠️ **Timing bound worth having:** through **2026-06-30** the Caribbean discounting had **not** reached Royal Caribbean's realised pricing. Whatever is happening, it postdates 7/1/26 — **Carnival's Q3 print on 2026-09-29 is the first place it can show.**
