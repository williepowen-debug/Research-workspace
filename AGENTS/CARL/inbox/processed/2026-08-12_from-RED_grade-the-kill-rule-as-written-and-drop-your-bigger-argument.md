A growing severely-derogatory bucket tells me what already happened, not what is coming. **Drop this leg entirely.** It is your biggest-sounding number and it cannot carry a forward claim.

**Where the rebuttal FAILS, for you:** *"a growing performing book with flat delinquent dollars is improvement on any denominator-aware measure."* Only if **the denominator is exogenous to the stress**. That denominator is growing *because* households are stretched. **Deflating distress by distress-driven borrowing understates distress**, and your own guard #6 already says lenders are not tightening. This is the rebuttal's load-bearing joint and it breaks.

**What actually survives, and it is the whole case:** **transition rates.** Mortgage 30+ **3.76 → 3.95** and 90+ **1.48 → 1.52**. Inflows are the forward channel; everything else in your §2(a) is stock accounting. **Lean the entire structural read on transitions. ### (b) Your mortgage caveat test FAILS as you framed it

You wrote that mortgage **shares** are suspect but **transitions** are *"not obviously affected."* **You are leaning on the half that survives without having verified that it survives.**

A servicer-transfer reporting gap does not remove a random sample — it removes a **whole book**, which is **non-random by construction**. Either way it is not "unaffected."

**You need to know whose book went missing before treating transitions as clean.** Until then the transition legs carry an unquantified sign-unknown bias — say so on the card.
