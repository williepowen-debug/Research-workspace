### Unicus Research: Auto Lending Fraud → CRE Fraud — Same Architecture

**When:** 2026-03-10 (processed 23:00 UTC)
**Source:** Unicus Research (institutional short research), "Curiouser and Curiouser"
**Via:** Will (paid newsletter)

**Raw signal:**

**Floor Plan Fraud (Auto):**
- Dealers borrow against vehicle inventory, sell vehicle, retain proceeds, don't notify lender
- Same inventory pledged to two floor plan lenders simultaneously ("out of trust" = double pledging)
- Persists for years due to: infrequent physical verification, reliance on paper records, originate-to-distribute structure
- "Double-pledging fraud is silently persistent in the auto sector. We refuse to believe otherwise unless lenders' loan pools are audited in their entirety."

**Santander Consumer USA Case:**
- May 2020: $550M settlement with 34 attorneys general (investigation since March 2015)
- Core finding: Santander KNEW dealers were falsifying borrower income/expense figures
- Had internally categorized specific dealers as high-risk — continued funding because volume was profitable
- Massachusetts AG found Santander identified a group called its "fraud dealers" and kept doing business with them
- Loans packaged into ABS and sold to investors
- Separate $22M Massachusetts settlement in 2017

**Thesis (Unicus):**
- Same fraud architecture across auto, CRE, floor plan lending
- "Fraud does not announce itself. It accumulates quietly across asset classes, across origination desks, across automated underwriting systems that bless whatever numbers they are fed."
- "Each incident gets filed away as isolated. Idiosyncratic. A one-off... Nobody connects the dots until the credit cycle turns and all the problems surface at once."
- "If you want to understand where CRE fraud is heading, look at what auto lending has already been through"
