## Domain boundary — SHADE vs BROCK

- **BROCK owns asset/fund stress:** private-credit funds, BDCs, interval/non-traded BDCs, redemption gates, PIK, dividend cuts, NAV marks, CLO/BDC mechanics, alt-manager equity tape.
- **SHADE owns insurance-wrapper transmission:** PE-owned insurers, captive/offshore reinsurance, statutory reserve credit, affiliated assets, Schedule BA/private-credit holdings inside insurers, FABN/FHLB/funding-agreement liabilities, NAIC/SVO/AG 55/PBR regulatory pressure.
- Trigger SHADE when the question becomes: **did the insurer wrapper absorb, hide, finance, or amplify private-credit stress?**
- Do not duplicate BROCK's fund dashboard. Reference BROCK facts with `[CONF BROCK date]`, then analyze the insurer balance-sheet/funding consequence.

## Core transmission question

Private-credit impairment becomes more dangerous when it enters an insurance wrapper that is:
1. liability-matched on paper but exposed to illiquid/Level-3/affiliated assets;
2. dependent on optimistic statutory treatment, permitted practices, or offshore/captive reserve credit;
3. funded partly through runnable or refinance-sensitive liabilities (FABN, FHLB advances, funding agreements, deposit-type contracts);
4. vulnerable to NAIC/SVO/AG 55/rating-agency changes that turn opacity into forced capital pressure.

## Source-quality caveats

- Statutory insurance filings and primary regulatory documents beat media summaries. Use annual statements / NAIC / state insurance filings / SEC filings when trade-relevant.
- Old SHADE `STATUS.md` contains March position rows and market prices. Treat them as historical until refreshed.
- Bank NDFI exposure numbers should be routed through REGINALD/BROCK/LIQUID owner docs before SHADE uses them as system-wide facts.

## SHADE forensic-discipline lessons

- **FHLB "borrowing capacity" ≠ the drawn book.** An FHLB figure on an insurer's IR liquidity slide is often *undrawn available capacity*, not advances outstanding. (6/21: baseline carried "$2.4B FHLB capacity"; the FI deck showed **$28B advances out / $38B pledged collateral** — the $2.4B was the undrawn line.) Always pull advances-outstanding + pledged collateral from the FI deck / statutory, and net encumbered assets out of the "highly liquid" cushion. Concrete instance of auto-memory `finding_number_carries_threshold_unit_source`.
- **A funding-spread threshold needs a peer-relative sub-row.** An absolute spread can sit in the green band while the issuer is the *widest* of its cohort. (6/21: Athene 5Y FABN T+123 = green absolutely, but +43–48bp vs IG peers = the load-bearing kill-path-1 canary.) The peer-relative penalty, not the absolute level, is the signal. Single-issuer-vs-peers variant of auto-memory `finding_blended_index_masks_bifurcation`.
- **Surveillance ≠ a rating action.** Authoritative quantification (Moody's/FSB/FSOC/Treasury/Proskauer) can rise in chorus while zero downgrades/negative-outlooks land on watchlist PE-insurers. Keep "rising attention" on the surveillance side of the green/yellow line until a rating-agency *action* or enforcement *escalation* actually fires. Catalyst ≠ consequence (`finding_catalyst_vs_consequence_conflation`).
- **APO price is not a SHADE stress gauge.** Athene is ~60% of Apollo equity value but the wrapper risk is a balance-sheet/funding/ratings mechanism that can crack while the equity holds. The March APO threshold band is a stale tape level; confirm the canonical APO mark with BROCK rather than carrying a fixed band as "green = no stress."
- **The Athene FABN maturity wall is private-by-construction — kill-path-1's quantum is 144A-invisible.** (6/22) Athene Global Funding is **not an SEC filer**; **100% of the $34.5B FABN is Rule 144A / Reg S**; Athene publishes **no FABN-only maturity ladder** anywhere. The only year-bucketed primary figure (MD&A "interest-sensitive contract liabilities") blends all annuities+FA+GICs, undiscounted — NOT the FABN wall. So the "$16.5B 2026-2027" was carried as fact but is **structurally unverifiable** from free public data → re-marked third-party/unverified. The *mechanism* is still sourceable from primary (issuance collapse $2.0B vs $13.4B, ~9-10mo syndication gap, FHLB/FABR substitution, active 2027 tenders); only the *quantum* is hidden. **Free recovery path — EXECUTED & WORKED (6/22):** scripted EDGAR crawl of **1,602 NPORT-P fund filings** (Bash+Python, User-Agent header required; 0 fails) recovered a **$3.29B par floor** of 2026-2027 AGF maturities held by registered funds, who hold **18.1% of all AGF** → grossed up to a **~$13-18B** wall, bracketing/corroborating the $16.5B. So the 144A wall IS sizeable from the holder side, just not the issuer side. Reusable crawler: `research/AGF_NPORT_crawl_2026-06-22.py`. Key gotchas: EDGAR FTS needs a UA header (WebFetch can't; Bash/curl can); restrict to ONE snapshot date undercounts (funds have staggered fiscal-Q-ends) — use latest-public-filing-per-fund; AGF CUSIP family = `04685A`/`04686E`. Transferable kernel: [[finding_private_by_construction_unverifiable]].

- **The related-party disclosure line is a self-graded exam — treat it as self-reported, not measured.** (7/27) SSAP No. 25 identifies related-party investments where return is *"predominantly contingent on the performance of related parties"* — and **the filer defines the threshold.** Delaware Life defined "predominantly contingent" as **>50%**, and **$16.4B of affiliate-linked private credit sat in the general-account BOND line, outside the related-party disclosure, until a federal grand jury forced a re-look** (restatement: FY2024 **$2.26B → $11.56B = 5.1×**; FY2025 total **$17.24B = 37.6% of GA assets**). It was not hidden offshore and not in a captive — **classification alone did it.** **Consequence to SHADE's own instruments: Key Ratio #1 (Affiliated Reinsurance) and #3 (Capital Leakage) are built on this self-reported line and inherit its error bar.** Athene's related-party figures get re-labelled **self-reported/unverified-by-construction**, the same treatment as the 144A FABN wall — *not* a claim they're wrong. Kin to [[finding_private_by_construction_unverifiable]] but distinct: the FABN wall is invisible because it's **144A**; this is invisible because the **filer sets the test**.
- **Statutory-basis audits ALWAYS carry an "Adverse Opinion on U.S. GAAP" — it is boilerplate, never a finding.** (7/27) SAP ≠ GAAP, so every statutory-basis audit pairs an **unmodified statutory opinion** with an **adverse GAAP opinion**, and routinely calls the variances "material and pervasive." Reading it as a red flag would have manufactured a scandal out of a formatting convention. **The real audit signals are: whether the STATUTORY opinion is modified (Delaware Life's was NOT), and whether an Emphasis of Matter was added (it was).** Concrete instance of auto-memory `finding_read_the_artifacts_own_header_first`.
- **Distinguish a DISCLOSURE correction from a CREDIT event — the loudest number may carry no loss.** (7/27) Delaware Life's 5.1× restatement moved **no** write-down and **did not restate surplus**. "Impaired vs mis-labelled" stayed open. A 12× headline multiple with a grand jury attached reads like a solvency event and is, so far, an **accounting-classification** event. Ask "what was written down?" before letting a restatement into any default/mark/loss series. Kin to `finding_catalyst_vs_consequence_conflation`.
- **N-VPFS is a real but ISSUER-SPECIFIC window into statutory financials — check per issuer, never assume.** (7/27) Delaware Life bundles the **depositor insurer's full statutory financial statements** (with the related-party notes) into its separate-account **Form N-VPFS** filings, making a normally-off-EDGAR statutory note free-text-searchable. **Athene does NOT** — Athene Variable Annuity Account A and ALAC Separate Account 1 (both FY2025, filed 2026-04-30) carry **separate-account statements only**. So: worth checking for any insurer with variable-product separate accounts (Global Atlantic/Commonwealth, Corebridge/American General, Brighthouse, F&G all file N-VPFS) — **but the Athene Schedule-BA wall STANDS.** ⚠️ **And an EDGAR full-text screen for a statutory-note phrase is a FALSE ZERO as a cohort test** — it measures *which insurers bundle statutory financials onto EDGAR*, not which insurers use the practice; most statutory financials aren't on EDGAR at all (`finding_discovery_tool_wrong_slice_false_zero`).

- **Statutory-disclosure visibility tracks PRODUCT MIX, not materiality — and state examination is not the forcing function SHADE assumed.** (7/27) Two lessons from the same event. **(a)** Delaware Life's related-party restatement is public **only because it sells variable products**, which forced its statutory financials into an SEC N-VPFS filing. **Clear Spring Life — same parent, same subpoena, same restatement — writes fixed/FIA only, has no separate accounts, no EDGAR registration, and is therefore invisible.** Before concluding an insurer "didn't disclose," check whether it has any SEC-filing obligation at all. **(b)** The Delaware DOI's **multi-state statutory examination of Clear Spring (as of 12/31/2023, adopted 6/23/2025) reported "no significant findings" and "no recommendations"**, and **AM Best revised the outlook to POSITIVE on 10/10/2025** — then a **Feb-2026 federal grand jury subpoena** produced a 5.1× related-party restatement. **Neither the examiner nor the rating agency found it.** This directly weakens kill-path #2 (AG 55 forced disclosure), which assumes supervision eventually forces disclosure: the realistic discovery channels here were **prosecutorial or product-mix-accidental**. Do not treat a clean exam or an affirmed/upgraded rating as evidence an affiliated-asset book is sound.
