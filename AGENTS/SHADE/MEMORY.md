## Domain boundary — SHADE vs BROCK

- **BROCK owns asset/fund stress:** private-credit funds, BDCs, interval/non-traded BDCs, redemption gates, PIK, dividend cuts, NAV marks, CLO/BDC mechanics, alt-manager equity tape.
- **SHADE owns insurance-wrapper transmission:** PE-owned insurers, captive/offshore reinsurance, statutory reserve credit, affiliated assets, Schedule BA/private-credit holdings inside insurers, FABN/FHLB/funding-agreement liabilities, NAIC/SVO/AG 55/PBR regulatory pressure.
- Trigger SHADE when the question becomes: **did the insurer wrapper absorb, hide, finance, or amplify private-credit stress?**
- Do not duplicate BROCK's fund dashboard. Reference BROCK facts with `[CONF BROCK date]`, then analyze the insurer balance-sheet/funding consequence.

## Core transmission question

Private-credit impairment becomes more dangerous when it enters an insurance wrapper that is:
1. liability-matched on paper but exposed to illiquid/Level-3/affiliated assets;
2. dependent on optimistic statutory treatment, permitted practices, or offshore/captive reserve credit;
3. funded partly through runnable or refinance-sensitive liabilities (FABN, FHLB advances, funding agreements, deposit-type contracts);
4. vulnerable to NAIC/SVO/AG 55/rating-agency changes that turn opacity into forced capital pressure.

## Source-quality caveats

- Statutory insurance filings and primary regulatory documents beat media summaries. Use annual statements / NAIC / state insurance filings / SEC filings when trade-relevant.
- Old SHADE `STATUS.md` contains March position rows and market prices. Treat them as historical until refreshed.
- Bank NDFI exposure numbers should be routed through REGINALD/BROCK/LIQUID owner docs before SHADE uses them as system-wide facts.

## SHADE forensic-discipline lessons

- **FHLB "borrowing capacity" ≠ the drawn book.** An FHLB figure on an insurer's IR liquidity slide is often *undrawn available capacity*, not advances outstanding. (6/21: baseline carried "$2.4B FHLB capacity"; the FI deck showed **$28B advances out / $38B pledged collateral** — the $2.4B was the undrawn line.) Always pull advances-outstanding + pledged collateral from the FI deck / statutory, and net encumbered assets out of the "highly liquid" cushion. Concrete instance of auto-memory `finding_number_carries_threshold_unit_source`.
- **A funding-spread threshold needs a peer-relative sub-row.** An absolute spread can sit in the green band while the issuer is the *widest* of its cohort. (6/21: Athene 5Y FABN T+123 = green absolutely, but +43–48bp vs IG peers = the load-bearing kill-path-1 canary.) The peer-relative penalty, not the absolute level, is the signal. Single-issuer-vs-peers variant of auto-memory `finding_blended_index_masks_bifurcation`.
- **Surveillance ≠ a rating action.** Authoritative quantification (Moody's/FSB/FSOC/Treasury/Proskauer) can rise in chorus while zero downgrades/negative-outlooks land on watchlist PE-insurers. Keep "rising attention" on the surveillance side of the green/yellow line until a rating-agency *action* or enforcement *escalation* actually fires. Catalyst ≠ consequence (`finding_catalyst_vs_consequence_conflation`).

- **The related-party disclosure line is a self-graded exam — treat it as self-reported, not measured.** (7/27) SSAP No. 25 identifies related-party investments where return is *"predominantly contingent on the performance of related parties"* — and **the filer defines the threshold.** Delaware Life defined "predominantly contingent" as **>50%**, and **$16.4B of affiliate-linked private credit sat in the general-account BOND line, outside the related-party disclosure, until a federal grand jury forced a re-look** (restatement: FY2024 **$2.26B → $11.56B = 5.1×**; FY2025 total **$17.24B = 37.6% of GA assets**). Kin to [[finding_private_by_construction_unverifiable]] but distinct: the FABN wall is invisible because it's **144A**; this is invisible because the **filer sets the test**.
- **Statutory-basis audits ALWAYS carry an "Adverse Opinion on U.S. GAAP" — it is boilerplate, never a finding.** (7/27) SAP ≠ GAAP, so every statutory-basis audit pairs an **unmodified statutory opinion** with an **adverse GAAP opinion**, and routinely calls the variances "material and pervasive." Reading it as a red flag would have manufactured a scandal out of a formatting convention. **The real audit signals are: whether the STATUTORY opinion is modified (Delaware Life's was NOT), and whether an Emphasis of Matter was added (it was).** Concrete instance of auto-memory `finding_read_the_artifacts_own_header_first`.
- **Distinguish a DISCLOSURE correction from a CREDIT event — the loudest number may carry no loss.** (7/27) Delaware Life's 5.1× restatement moved **no** write-down and **did not restate surplus**. "Impaired vs mis-labelled" stayed open. A 12× headline multiple with a grand jury attached reads like a solvency event and is, so far, an **accounting-classification** event. Ask "what was written down?" before letting a restatement into any default/mark/loss series. Kin to `finding_catalyst_vs_consequence_conflation`.
- **N-VPFS is a real but ISSUER-SPECIFIC window into statutory financials — check per issuer, never assume.** (7/27) Delaware Life bundles the **depositor insurer's full statutory financial statements** (with the related-party notes) into its separate-account **Form N-VPFS** filings, making a normally-off-EDGAR statutory note free-text-searchable. **Athene does NOT** — Athene Variable Annuity Account A and ALAC Separate Account 1 (both FY2025, filed 2026-04-30) carry **separate-account statements only**. So: worth checking for any insurer with variable-product separate accounts (Global Atlantic/Commonwealth, Corebridge/American General, Brighthouse, F&G all file N-VPFS) — **but the Athene Schedule-BA wall STANDS.** ⚠️ **And an EDGAR full-text screen for a statutory-note phrase is a FALSE ZERO as a cohort test** — it measures *which insurers bundle statutory financials onto EDGAR*, not which insurers use the practice; most statutory financials aren't on EDGAR at all (`finding_discovery_tool_wrong_slice_false_zero`).

Same class as 7/27 retraction #5 (*"the FABN canary can't be refreshed until 8/4"* — also an untested assumption about when an artifact exists). **Generalized rule: before registering a grade date, ask (i) which instrument carries this number, (ii) what is its publication cadence, (iii) does it actually disclose this field.** *(Corollary from the same day's ARCC grade: **(iii) fails on its own** — SHADE registered "non-accruals concentrated in software," and ARCC discloses non-accruals **only as an aggregate %, benchmarked to year-end, with no industry mix at all.** That leg was never testable on the instrument named. NOT-SUPPORTED ≠ REFUTED.)* Kin to `finding_prereg_verdict_boundary_must_be_a_number` and `finding_read_the_artifacts_own_header_first`; the transferable kernel is a candidate for auto-memory.
- **The ARI $9B landing has a SECOND primary — and it is Athene's own 10-Q, which is also the registered resolution instrument.** (8/4) ATH **Q1-26** 10-Q `0001527469-26-000028`, related-party note: *"Apollo Commercial Real Estate Finance, Inc. (ARI) – On January 27, 2026, **we** entered into a definitive agreement to acquire an approximately $9 billion portfolio of commercial mortgage loans from ARI. The purchase price is based on **99.7%** of the total commitment amounts of the loans… **The transaction closed on April 24, 2026.**"* SHADE previously had the close date and the 99.7% from the 8-K `0001193125-26-177686` + EX-99.1 **only**. **Why it matters beyond corroboration: AHL names ITSELF as acquirer, in its own name, with no designation or portion language** — the first time the registered resolution instrument has spoken on this deal. ⚠️ **It does NOT resolve §2.8.** Designation to an ACRA/third-party landing entity is by **private notice** and can occur at or after close, and a related-party note is not an allocation disclosure. **Register as a WEAK PRIOR toward the LANDED branch, never as evidence for it** — and note the asymmetry that makes this easy to over-read: the note is the strongest-looking artifact SHADE has, and it is still silent on the only question §2.8 asks.
- **Statutory-disclosure visibility tracks PRODUCT MIX, not materiality — and state examination is not the forcing function SHADE assumed.** (7/27) Two lessons from the same event. **(a)** Delaware Life's related-party restatement is public **only because it sells variable products**, which forced its statutory financials into an SEC N-VPFS filing. **Clear Spring Life — same parent, same subpoena, same restatement — writes fixed/FIA only, has no separate accounts, no EDGAR registration, and is therefore invisible.** Before concluding an insurer "didn't disclose," check whether it has any SEC-filing obligation at all. **(b)** The Delaware DOI's **multi-state statutory examination of Clear Spring (as of 12/31/2023, adopted 6/23/2025) reported "no significant findings" and "no recommendations"**, and **AM Best revised the outlook to POSITIVE on 10/10/2025** — then a **Feb-2026 federal grand jury subpoena** produced a 5.1× related-party restatement. **Neither the examiner nor the rating agency found it.** This directly weakens kill-path #2 (AG 55 forced disclosure), which assumes supervision eventually forces disclosure: the realistic discovery channels here were **prosecutorial or product-mix-accidental**. Do not treat a clean exam or an affirmed/upgraded rating as evidence an affiliated-asset book is sound.

- **A fully-disclosed affiliated transfer can still be silent on WHERE the assets landed — check the assignment clause, not just the price and the vote.** (7/27) ARI→Athene is disclosed on every axis SHADE scores (8-K, special committee with BofA Securities + Fried Frank, stockholder vote 4/21/26, cleared at **99.7% of commitments**) — and the Purchase Agreement's **§2.8 "Designation of Buyer Affiliates"** lets the buyer designate *"one or more of its **Affiliates, Managed Accounts or Portfolio Companies**"* to acquire *"**all or any portion** of the Assets"* **by private written notice 10 business days before closing**, with **Athene Co-Invest Reinsurance Affiliate Holding Ltd. / Holding 2 Ltd. named as Buyer subsidiaries.** **The split was never disclosed and no public filing carries it.** So the benchmark is **four-of-five** (mechanism/size/disclosure/price ✅, landing entity ❌). **Practical rule: for any insurer asset acquisition, read the assignment/designation clause of the purchase agreement — it determines statutory entity, regulatory regime, RBC treatment, and whether the exposure is countable in ANY US series at all.** ⚠️ Routine structuring — **not** a conduct allegation. ⚠️ Primary: **Athene Holding Ltd. is a DELAWARE corporation**; the offshore question lives at the **ACRA** layer, not the parent — do not let "Athene = Bermuda" propagate.
- **🔑 ALLOCATION DISCRETION is SHADE's unifying mechanism — the categories are set by the entities being measured.** (7/27, from two independent findings the same day) The filer sets the related-party test (**SSAP 25**, self-defined ">50%" — Delaware Life); the **buyer** sets the landing entity (**§2.8**, above); and the **measurement systems** set the perimeters (Moody's $807B illiquid vs Chicago Fed $849B life-insurer PC vs MBA $775B CM/MF — **three unreconciled perimeters that must never be summed**; the first two likely measure substantially the same balance sheets). **This is a stronger claim than "the data is incomplete."** It is why no "combined insurer sink" total can be stated, and it should be SHADE's through-line rather than any headline size. Corollary for framing: **the sink currently absorbs at PAR (99.7%), not at a discount — so the exposure is invisible FUTURE deterioration, not hidden PRESENT impairment.** Detail: `research/COMBINED_INSURER_SINK_WELD2_2026-07-27.md`.
- **Insurer-flow series are seasonal — never baseline a falsifier on the latest single quarter.** (7/27) MBA life-insurer CM/MF net change: **H1-2025 +$4.4B across two quarters** vs **H2-2025 +$23.6B** (5.4×); Q3-25 **+$12.1B**, Q4-25 **+$11.5B**, Q1-26 **+$3.3B**. A falsifier written as *"materially above the +$3.3B Q1 run-rate"* is anchored to the **seasonal trough** and resolves TRUE on a perfectly normal +$11B print. Stack four quarters before setting any insurer-flow threshold. Transferable kernel promoted to auto-memory: [[finding_seasonal_trough_baseline_resolves_true_on_normal]].

- **A "past due" test cannot detect contractually DEFERRED interest — and the reassuring number will look like it can.** (7/27) Delaware Life non-admits accrued investment income *"over 90 days past due or where collection is uncertain"*, and reported only **$225 (2025) / $46 (2024)** against a **$45.9B** general account and **$2.45B** of NII. That reads as near-total collectibility. **But the company also holds "trust notes that allow for deferred interest" and recorded $159,580 of deferred interest in 2025 — and deferral is the contract, so such interest is NEVER past due and can never trip the non-admission test, however doubtful the ultimate collection.** **Rule: before citing a delinquency/non-admission statistic as evidence a book performs, ask what fraction of the book is contractually incapable of becoming delinquent.** Kin to the SSAP-25 self-graded test above: both are cases where the *test's own definition* excludes the exposure you care about.
- **When testing an anomaly, hunt for SAME-PERIOD, SIZE-MATCHED controls inside the same filing before reaching for outside data.** (7/27) The DLIC accrued-interest anomaly (9.77% accrued-to-book on a 2025 CSLAC purchase) looked like it needed CUSIP-level data to test. It did not — **the same filing disclosed five other affiliate securities transactions** (CSLAC 2023 **0.88%** / 2024 **1.03%**, Barbco 2024 **0.43%**, GLIC 2025 purchase **1.86%**, and a **nearly size-matched same-year GLIC sale at 0.95%**). Those five controls killed the rate-environment, accounting-change and year-end-timing explanations in one pass, and the *mechanism* (deferred-interest trust notes) was named elsewhere in the same document. **Read the whole note for siblings of the transaction before declaring an anomaly unresolvable.**
- **Retire your own flags out loud when they fail.** (7/27) The accrued-interest anomaly was **my** 7/27 flag, and it did not survive its own test — it has a disclosed contractual explanation and PIK moved the *opposite* way (−55.9%). Retired explicitly in STATUS, SCRATCH and `board_log.tsv` rather than quietly dropped. **What replaced it was better than the original claim** (the undisclosed deferred-interest denominator; 96% of the $16.37B structurally undescribed) — which is the usual pattern, and the reason retiring cleanly costs nothing.

- **When a threshold is wrong, the defect is almost always the BASELINE, not the level — and the fix usually inherits the same error inverted.** (7/27, caught in both directions inside one session) CREED baselined `PRED-006` on **one prior quarter** (+$3.3B) — a seasonal trough, so a normal +$11B print would have resolved it TRUE carrying zero information. I proposed **~+$20B** instead, anchored to **Q3/Q4 (+$11–12B)** — but those are **H2** quarters and **Q2 is an H1 quarter**, so my bar would have resolved FALSE **even if the entire $9B landed.** A false negative reading as *"the thesis is refuted"* is worse than the false positive it replaced. **Rule: anchor to a DISTRIBUTION and to the MATCHING SEASON, and state the baseline's percentile. Then check the counterfactual explicitly — "what does this print if the event does NOT happen, and what if it fully does?" Both bars failed that one-line test.** Also: **a threshold that derives its delta by subtracting a previously-recorded stock is fragile** — MBA rounds to the nearest $B and revises priors, so measure the change **as printed**. Transferable kernel: [[finding_seasonal_trough_baseline_resolves_true_on_normal]].
- **A binary bar over a partitionable quantity cannot distinguish "partial" from "none."** (7/27) `PRED-006` fires at ≥+$10.0B on a $9B transfer — but **§2.8 permits designating *"all or any portion"*** to different entities, so a ~$4–5B partial landing prints ~+$7–9B, resolves FALSE, and looks **identical to full exclusion.** **Whenever a threshold tests an event that can happen fractionally, say what fraction the bar actually detects, and phrase the negative branch as "wholly or largely not X" rather than "not X."**
- **⚠️ Read the methodology note before using a published series as a perimeter.** (7/27) I used MBA's **$775B life-insurer line** as *"insurer CRE exposure"* in a document about measurement integrity. **MBA attributes holdings to whoever holds the NOTE** — so insurer-held **CMBS sits in the CMBS/CDO/ABS bucket (~$637B), not the insurer bucket.** $775B is a **FLOOR on insurer CRE exposure, not a measure of it**, and the two buckets split by **the legal form of the instrument, not by holder type** — an insurer buying a CMBS bond prints as CMBS. ⚠️ **I had extracted that exact passage myself and did not act on it: a read-it-and-missed-it, not a data gap.** **Rule: when a series is load-bearing for a perimeter claim, read its attribution rule and ask "could my subject appear in a different bucket?"** Kin to `finding_read_the_artifacts_own_header_first`.
- **n=1 on the null-hypothesis case supports nothing.** (7/27) I wrote *"the sink is absorbing at PAR, not at a discount"* off ARI→Athene's **99.7%**. But that is **one transaction**, on **performing** paper — and **performing paper clearing at par is the null hypothesis, not a finding.** The alarming version would need *impaired* paper clearing at par. Worse, the datum's owner had logged 99.7% as a **counter**-datum against "CRE marks are fictional," so my use **inverted the role he gave it**. **Rule: before a datum carries a general claim, ask (a) what the null outcome would look like, and (b) what the originating agent recorded it AS.** The mechanism survived once decoupled from price entirely.

- **A self-reported metric can be independently rebuilt from holder filings — test that before declaring a refresh impossible.** (7/27) I told Will the FABN peer canary "cannot be refreshed until the Q2 deck." **That was a path assumption I had never tested.** NPORT-P reports every registered fund's holdings at **fair value with par, coupon and maturity** — enough to compute a price, a YTM and a Treasury spread. **The design that makes it valid: restrict to funds holding the target AND a peer IN THE SAME FILING** (same fund, same date, same pricing vendor), so methodology differences cancel and only issuer credit remains. Result: Athene's peer penalty ≈ **+40bp** (within-fund paired, n=15 funds, 11/15 positive) — **bracketing the deck's +43–48bp.** **Two durable takeaways: (a) an issuer's self-chosen peer set is a curation choice, and rebuilding it with your own peer set is an upgrade, not a duplicate; (b) levels from marks are indicative, but a matched-tenor DIFFERENCE is robust because the estimation error is systematic.** Instrument: `research/FABN_PEER_SPREAD_NPORT_2026-07-27.py`, ~4 min, rerunnable quarterly, reusable for any insurer-vs-peer funding-cost question. Instance of `finding_audit_resolution_path_before_reattempt`.
- **Report every estimator when they disagree, and carry the strictest.** (7/27) The same FABN dataset gave **+55.4bp** pooled, **+58.8bp** per-CUSIP, and **+40.2bp** within-fund paired. Quoting the pooled number would have been the most dramatic and the least defensible. **The within-fund paired design is strictest, so it is what SHADE carries — along with its dispersion (11/15 positive, IQR −1 to +52), because 4 funds showing zero-or-negative is part of the finding.** Corollary that saved a bad citation: **always check unique-instrument counts before quoting a bucket** — the 6–11y "penalty" rested on **4 holdings of ONE CUSIP.** One bond is not a tenor bucket.

## 2026-08-13 — lessons from the M-11 dual FINAL grade

- 🔑 **A band derived as a PERCENTAGE of an unverified quantity must be registered as the RATIO, not as a LEVEL.** The M-11 leg-2 LANDED floor was frozen as "$7.0B ≈ 78% of $9B." The deal turned out to be **$8.7B** (ATH Q2 10-Q, first time priced). 78% × $8.7B = $6.786B, and Δ = $6.897B = **79.3%** — so the **letter** graded PARTIAL while the floor's own **stated intent** graded LANDED, on a $103M margin. A level silently hard-codes an unverified input; a ratio resolves its denominator at grade time. **Grade the letter anyway** — but report the divergence at every surface, or "PARTIAL" propagates as "the assets didn't show up" when 79% of the book was sitting in the registered line. *(Promotion candidate for auto-memory — flagged to PROME rather than appended, since `MEMORY.md` is near its byte cap.)*
- ⚠️ **"Could not verify at primary" must distinguish MY QUERY MISSED IT from THE ISSUER DIDN'T DISCLOSE IT.** The 8/4 proxy flagged $34.5B FABN / $28.2B FHLB / $2.0B FABN issuance as unverifiable after searching the Q1 10-Q. **All three were in that filing** — two in Note 11, one **MD&A-only**. Nine days of a false "undisclosed" flag. **Fix: search MD&A separately from the notes, and grep the bare program name (`FABN`) rather than a phrase.** (`finding_verification_zero_is_ambiguous`.)
- ⚠️ **A price-only canary is confounded by supply withdrawal.** Athene's FABN peer penalty **narrowed 11.7bp** in the same six months FABN issuance fell to ~$1.2B/qtr and the program **shrank**. A secondary spread is a price struck on a float; withdrawing supply mechanically supports it. **"Credit improved" and "the issuer stopped issuing" are indistinguishable on this instrument.** Same class as the Q9b identification defect — no number of quarters fixes it; only a different instrument does.
- ⚠️ **Peer-set composition is chosen by the entity being measured.** Athene added **LNC** (its widest peer, T+89) to the Q2 comparison, cutting the reported penalty by **3.0bp**. Immaterial to the band and no accusation is warranted — but **always compute like-for-like against the prior edition's peer set and quote both.** Fifth instance of the **allocation-discretion** through-line.
- ⚠️ **A dated carry-item is a string; reading it never re-evaluates it.** The "HY OAS level leg is MET" state went live 8/4 and **died on 8/3** — the very next session after the pull. It was carried on STATUS, SCRATCH and `NEXUS_BRIEF` for 9 days. **Anything asserting a *sustained* condition needs a re-pull date, not just a pull date.**
