Structural Dynamics and Liquidity Management of the Funding Agreement Backed Notes Maturity Wall: A Quantitative Analysis of the 2026-2027 Rollover Concentration
The evolution of the global retirement services market has been increasingly defined by the sophistication of capital markets-based funding mechanisms. Central to this transformation is the Funding Agreement Backed Notes (FABN) framework, a structural innovation that allows insurance entities to access institutional liquidity by securitizing deposit-type contracts. Within this ecosystem, Athene Global Funding has established a dominant presence, utilizing a statutory trust structure to manage an issuance capacity of $35,000,000,000.1 While this capacity provides a robust foundation for organic growth and asset accumulation, it also creates a complex liability profile characterized by periodic maturity concentrations. As the financial landscape moves toward the 2026-2027 window, the intersection of scheduled debt retirements, shifting credit spreads, and benchmark interest rate volatility establishes a critical trigger mechanism for the issuer’s capital management strategy.
The Architecture of the Athene Global Funding Program
The structural integrity of the FABN program is predicated on the legal and economic separation between the issuing trust and the underlying insurance provider. Athene Global Funding (the Issuer) is organized as a statutory trust in series under the Delaware Statutory Trust Act.1 This organizational form permits the issuance of senior secured medium-term notes in separate series and tranches, each of which is specifically secured by one or more funding agreements purchased from Athene Annuity and Life Company (AAIA), an Iowa-domiciled insurer.1 This mechanism ensures that noteholders possess a senior secured claim on the specific collateral—the funding agreement—while the insurance company gains access to diverse institutional capital.
The $35 billion program capacity is not merely a theoretical ceiling but a dynamic reflection of Athene’s market-leading position in the retirement services sector. In 2024 alone, Athene achieved nearly $35 billion in total fixed market sales, reinforcing its status as the top-ranked provider according to LIMRA surveys.2 This massive influx of capital is channeled through various avenues, with funding agreements (including FABN and FABR programs) serving as a primary pillar of organic growth.3 The scale of this operation requires a meticulous approach to duration matching and liquidity planning, particularly as the "maturity wall" for legacy issuances begins to converge.
Tranche Granularity and Issuance Mechanics
The notes issued under the AGF program are designed to be opportunistic and responsive to prevailing market conditions. Each series may consist of multiple tranches, allowing the issuer to segment risk based on rate type (fixed or floating), currency, and maturity date.1 These notes rank pari passu with other senior secured obligations of the issuer, providing a uniform credit profile to institutional investors while maintaining the flexibility to tailor specific terms through pricing supplements.1
The acquisition of funding agreements by the issuer constitutes the core investment activity of the trust. These agreements are deposit-type contracts that provide the holder with a guaranteed return of principal and periodic interest payments.4 Crucially, they are not insurance policies but are considered senior obligations of the insurance company, often ranking equal to policyholder claims in certain insolvency regimes. This senior status is the primary driver of the attractive risk-adjusted funding costs that characterize the FABN market.4
The 2026-2027 Maturity Wall: A Quantitative Breakdown
The primary challenge facing the AGF program is the concentration of liabilities scheduled for retirement between 2026 and 2027. Financial reporting from the 2025 supplements indicates a significant escalation in interest-sensitive contract liabilities during this period. The maturity schedule identifies a substantial "wall" that must be navigated to preserve liquidity and minimize refinancing risk.

The August 2026 window is particularly dense with floating-rate notes, including a $400 million tranche maturing on August 27.10 These notes utilize a SOFR-linked rate, which has undergone significant upward pressure as benchmark rates normalized.10 The 2027 maturities are dominated by fixed-rate notes issued during the height of the low-interest-rate era, with coupons as low as 2.450%.8 These legacy rates sit far below current market yields, creating a stark contrast for any potential refinancing activity.
Economic Sensitivities and the Cost of Capital
The "trigger mechanism" of the maturity wall is the interplay between the scheduled expiration of these notes and the prevailing cost of capital in the primary markets. If credit spreads for insurance-linked debt "blow out" (widen significantly), the issuer faces a choice between refinancing at a higher cost or allowing the notes to mature unfunded using internal liquidity.
Refinancing Cost Projections
The current cost of funds for underwritten new business in the retirement services sector is estimated at approximately 5.3%.13 This rate accounts for the dynamic pricing of options, guarantees, and institutional policy issuance.4 When juxtaposed against the legacy fixed-rate notes maturing in 2027, the financial implications of refinancing are substantial.
The potential increase in interest expense can be quantified by the difference between the legacy coupon and the current market yield. For a $500 million tranche such as Series 2022-6, which carries a 3.205% coupon 8, refinancing at 5.3% 13 would result in an annual interest expense increase of approximately $10.475 million. Across the entire $16.5 billion 2026-2027 maturity block, a 200-basis-point increase in the weighted average cost of funds would translate to an additional $330 million in annual interest obligations.

The Unfunded Maturity Alternative
Athene maintains a significant liquidity buffer, often referred to as a "fortress balance sheet," to mitigate the risks associated with market dislocations. As of early 2025, the entity reported $69 billion in available liquidity, including $7.7 billion in cash and cash equivalents and a $49.9 billion portfolio of highly liquid assets.13
Note: Liquidity data as of Q1 2025.13
This $69 billion liquidity position provides the issuer with the strategic flexibility to let the $16.5 billion 2026-2027 maturity wall "mature unfunded" if the cost of refinancing exceeds the internal hurdle rate. By paying off the notes with cash or by liquidating high-quality assets, the company can avoid locking in high-cost debt during periods of spread volatility. While this strategy reduces the "dry powder" available for opportunistic acquisitions or new business, it preserves the net interest margin by eliminating expensive interest payments.13
Proactive Liability Management and Market Signals
Historical behavior by the Athene Global Funding issuer suggests that the maturity wall is managed proactively rather than reactively. In September 2022, the issuer launched "any-and-all" tender offers for $500 million of its Series 2022-6 notes and its Series 2020-5 notes, both of which were due in 2027.8
Strategic Rationale for Early Retirement
The decision to tender for notes years before their scheduled maturity serves as a multi-faceted de-risking tool:
Rollover Concentration Smoothing: By retiring debt early, the issuer reduces the absolute dollar amount that must be addressed during the 2026-2027 window.8
Credit Metric Validation: Management stated that the tender offer reflected their belief that secondary market spreads did not accurately represent Athene’s strong credit metrics or recent rating upgrades from agencies like Fitch.8
Capital Efficiency: Tendering for debt when it trades at a discount in the secondary market allows the issuer to retire liabilities at a gain, enhancing the statutory capital of the insurance subsidiaries.
This proactive stance indicates that the "dates" provided in the maturity schedule are the absolute boundaries of the obligation, but the actual refinancing activity is a fluid, continuous process that leverages market inefficiencies.
The Role of Floating-Rate Debt and the SOFR Transition
A significant portion of the FABN program involves floating-rate notes, which introduce a different set of dynamics compared to fixed-rate debt. For instance, the $400 million AGF note maturing in August 2026 is linked to SOFR.10 These instruments provide a natural hedge for the insurance company’s floating-rate asset portfolio, but they also mean that the cost of funds adjusts in real-time with central bank policy.
Hedging and Interest Rate Risk
Athene manages the risk of rising interest rates by strategically allocating a meaningful portion of its investment portfolio into floating-rate securities.15 This creates a "net floating rate position" that the company monitors closely. In a rising rate environment, the income from the floating-rate assets typically offsets the increased interest expense on the floating-rate FABNs.15 However, as these notes approach maturity, the decision to refinance into new floating-rate debt or fixed-rate debt depends on the company's outlook for the yield curve.
The SOFR transition remains a critical operational factor for the 2026 maturities. Notes issued prior to the full implementation of SOFR may have legacy fallback language that requires careful management during the retirement process. The concentration of SOFR-linked maturities in August 2026 10 suggests that the issuer has successfully migrated its institutional funding base to the new benchmark, reducing the risk of basis volatility during the rollover.
Second-Order Implications: Capital Generation and Ratings
The management of the FABN maturity wall is inextricably linked to the broader strategic goals of the parent company, Apollo Global Management. Apollo’s 2024 Investor Day targets include reaching ~$1 trillion in AUM by 2026 and generating more than $15 billion in annual capital.16 The FABN program is a vital engine for these targets, contributing to Spread Related Earnings (SRE) by capturing the delta between institutional funding costs and the yields on Apollo-originated assets.4
Impact on Spread Related Earnings (SRE)
The "net spread" earned by Athene is the primary driver of its profitability. In the third quarter of 2025, the entity reported a net spread impact of 0.05% from alternative investment returns alone.17 A successful refinancing of the 2026-2027 wall at favorable rates would preserve these margins. Conversely, if the trigger mechanism forces the issuer into high-cost debt, the SRE could be compressed. However, because Athene prices its new business dynamically—as evidenced by the October 2025 rate changes for products like AccuMax 5 where crediting rates were lowered by up to 75 basis points 18—the company can adjust its cost of liabilities in other channels to compensate for higher institutional funding costs.
Rating Agency Perspectives
Credit rating agencies, including Moody’s, S&P, and Fitch, maintain a close watch on Athene’s liquidity and leverage.8 The ability to navigate the $16.5 billion maturity wall without a significant spike in leverage or a depletion of the $69 billion liquidity buffer is essential for maintaining the "A" range ratings that allow for attractive FABN pricing. The 2022 upgrade from Fitch was specifically cited as a reason for the company's confidence in its credit metrics during the tender offer process.8
Parallel Market Analysis: Fiber Asset-Backed Notes (FABN)
It is critical to distinguish the insurance-linked FABN from the Fiber Asset-Backed Notes issued by telecommunications companies like Frontier Communications, as they share the same acronym and are often grouped in aggregate ABS research. Frontier’s fiber securitizations demonstrate a different approach to maturity walls, centered on asset-heavy collateral rather than deposit-type contracts.
Frontier's 2027 Trigger
Frontier Communications has reported having "no long-term debt maturities prior to 2027".19 Like Athene, Frontier has been proactive in using securitization to address this upcoming wall. In 2024, Frontier completed a $750 million fiber securitization, using $400 million of the proceeds to repay term loan commitments that were due in 2027.21

However, the strategy remains the same: push out the maturity wall to the 2030s to avoid a liquidity crunch in 2027.
Regulatory Outlook and Accounting Shifts
The regulatory environment for FABNs is currently undergoing refinement. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) has prioritized FABN reporting, with the Statutory Accounting Principles (E) Working Group exploring revisions to SSAP No. 52.23 The goal is to ensure concurrent exposure of these liabilities in financial blanks and to improve transparency regarding the "defined maturities" of institutional funding agreements.3
For Athene, these regulatory shifts could lead to more stringent reporting requirements for the 2026-2027 maturities. If the NAIC requires insurance entities to hold higher reserves or capital against FABNs, the "cost of refinancing" could increase due to higher capital charges, even if market spreads remain stable. This regulatory trigger is as important as the calendar-based maturity wall, as it affects the long-term viability of the $35 billion program capacity.
Strategic Synthesis and the 2026-2027 Outlook
The "FABN Maturity Wall" is a manageable but significant structural hurdle. The concentration of $16.5 billion in liabilities maturing between 2026 and 2027 serves as the primary trigger for the issuer’s capital allocation decisions.
Summary of the Trigger Mechanism
The trigger consists of three primary dates where significant blocks of capital require action:
August 2026: Maturity of SOFR-linked floating-rate notes, including a $400 million tranche.10
March 2027: Retirement of Series 2022-6 3.205% fixed-rate notes.8
August 2027: Expiration of Series 2020-5 2.450% legacy fixed-rate notes.8
If credit spreads widen significantly (the "blow out" scenario), the issuer is expected to pivot to its "unfunded maturity" strategy, utilizing its $69 billion liquidity pool.13 This allows the entity to bypass expensive capital markets while continuing to generate organic inflows from the retail channel, which reached $36 billion in 2024.2
Conclusion
The architecture of the Athene Global Funding program is designed for resilience. The $35 billion capacity and the $69 billion liquidity buffer provide the necessary "shock absorbers" for the 2026-2027 maturity wall. While the cost of refinancing legacy 2% debt at 5% spreads will inevitably compress some margins, the company’s ability to dynamically price its retail products and its proactive use of tender offers to flatten the rollover curve suggest that the maturity wall will be navigated as a routine exercise in liability management rather than a systemic liquidity event. The specific dates in 2026 and 2027 are well-known to the market, and the issuer’s "fortress balance sheet" remains the primary defense against spread volatility.
