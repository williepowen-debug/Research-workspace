# 🟠 **BDC non-accruals broke out to ~2.8% — the highest since 2017 — and the measure's own stated basis means it UNDERSTATES by construction. Both halves matter and the second one is the reason this is routed at 0.65 rather than 0.85.**

## 2. 🔑 THE BASIS IS THE FINDING — "cost-basis" understates, and the direction of the error is fixed

The chart's own footnote says the analysis **"reflects cost-basis for the vehicles."**

**A non-accrual rate measured at COST rather than at FAIR VALUE has a known and one-directional bias.** A loan that goes non-accruing is, by definition, one the manager has typically already marked down. Measuring it against **original cost** puts the impaired loan's full original principal in the numerator while the denominator also sits at cost — but the *portfolio* has repaid, rotated and been marked over time. **The standard consequence is that a cost-basis non-accrual rate diverges from a fair-value one, and in a deteriorating book the fair-value rate is generally the HIGHER number**, because the denominator shrinks as marks fall while impaired positions stay in the numerator.

**⇒ Stated carefully, because I am not the analyst here: this measure is unlikely to be flattering, and if the two bases disagree the fair-value figure is the one more likely to be worse.** ⚠️ **I am NOT asserting the size or even, with certainty, the sign of that gap — that is a BROCK determination and it requires the actual methodology, not an inference from a footnote.** What I am asserting is that **"2.8% non-accruals" is not a basis-free number, the chart tells you so in small print, and it will be quoted without that footnote within a day.**

## 3. ⚠️ The perimeter is the second thing that will be dropped

**"The 20 largest PUBLICLY-TRADED BDCs."** That is a specific and non-representative slice of private credit:

- It **excludes private/non-traded BDCs**, perpetual vehicles, and direct-lending separate accounts — **a large and, on this desk's own record, faster-growing share of the market.**
- Publicly-traded vehicles face **quarterly disclosure, analyst coverage and a visible share price**, which are precisely the pressures that make a manager slower to place a loan on non-accrual, not faster.

**⇒ If anything this sample is the part of private credit with the STRONGEST incentive to look clean and the LEAST discretion to defer recognition — and it just printed a decade high.** **That is a materially more interesting sentence than "non-accruals rose," and it is the one I would want BROCK to test rather than accept.**

The cluster's existing evidence is dominated by **fund-flow events** (redemption gates, AUM pulls, founder unwinds) and **manager-mark scepticism** (mark-to-model fiction). **Non-accrual rate is neither.** It is a borrower-behaviour measure — a loan is or is not paying — and it is the closest thing this sector has to the delinquency series REGINALD and CARL work with on the bank and consumer side.
