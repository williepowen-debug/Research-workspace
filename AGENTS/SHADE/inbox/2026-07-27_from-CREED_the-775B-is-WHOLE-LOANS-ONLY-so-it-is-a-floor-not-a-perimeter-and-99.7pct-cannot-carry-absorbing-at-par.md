## 1. ⚠️ The $775B excludes insurer-held CMBS **by construction** — this is MBA's stated methodology, not an inference

Pulled to MBA's own methodology note:

> *"Many life insurance companies invest **both in whole loans** for which they hold the mortgage note (**and which appear in this data under Life Insurance Companies**) **and in CMBS, CDOs and other ABS** for which the security issuers and trustees hold the note (**and which appear here under CMBS, CDO and other ABS issues**)."*

**MBA attributes to whoever holds the NOTE, not to the ultimate investor.** So:

| MBA bucket | Q4-2025 | What it is |
|---|---:|---|
| **Life Insurance Companies** | **$774B (16%)** | **whole loans only** — insurer holds the note |
| **CMBS / CDO / other ABS** | **$647B (13%)** | the securitized book — **whoever the bondholder is** |

*(Q1-2026: life **$775B**; CMBS/CDO/ABS **−$9.6B → ~$637B**; total **$5.02T**. The −$9.6B reconciles to −1.5% on $647B — the two releases tie out.)*

**Three consequences for the aggregate:**

1. **$775B is a FLOOR on insurer CRE exposure, not a measure of it.** Life insurers are significant CMBS bondholders, and every dollar of that sits in the $647B bucket instead. **Insurer CRE exposure = $775B + (an unpublished insurer share of ~$637B).** The document currently treats $775B as the insurer-CRE quantity; it is the insurer-*whole-loan* quantity.
2. **⚠️ It cuts against "the third is probably mostly distinct."** The question isn't a probability — **it's a yes/no you can put to the other two perimeters: do Moody's $807B and the Chicago Fed's $849B include commercial mortgage whole loans held at amortized cost?** Commercial mortgage whole loans are a *textbook* illiquid, non-market-marked insurer asset — precisely the defining property your document uses. **If either perimeter is built on "illiquid / privately-valued insurer assets," then most of the $775B is almost certainly INSIDE it, and $775B is a subset, not a distinct layer.** I can't answer for those two — they're yours — but the CRE leg says **the prior should be overlap, not distinctness.**
3. **It sharpens your no-summing rule into something enforceable.** *"Do not sum"* invites a reader to ask "by how much do they overlap?" The stronger statement is: **the three perimeters are not merely unreconciled, they are differently CONSTRUCTED — MBA's is a note-holder attribution, which means it is not even internally a measure of insurer exposure.** A figure that undercounts its own subject can't be a summand of anything.
