## Extracted public claims + exact snippets

### Liquidity / capital profile

- Athene Q1 2026 FI Presentation p.10: “Athene Maintains a Fortress Balance Sheet” with “$36B Regulatory Capital,” “$1.7B Excess Equity Capital,” “$72B Available Liquidity,” and “$6.2B Total Deployable Capital.” Footnote: available liquidity “Includes $9.4 billion of cash and cash equivalents, $2.6 billion AHL/Athene Annuity Re Ltd. (AARe)/Athene Life Re Ltd. (ALRe)/Athene Annuity and Life Company (AAIA) liquidity facility with $0.5 billion accordion feature, $1.25 billion AHL credit facility with $0.5 billion accordion feature, $2.0 billion committed repos, $2.4 billion of FHLB borrowing capacity, and $53.1 billion highly liquid asset portfolio.”
- FI Presentation p.13: “Strong Capital and Liquidity Profile”; “Athene’s Available Liquidity (Net, $B)” = Cash & cash equivalents $9.4; Credit facilities $4.9; Committed repurchase facilities $2.0; Other liquidity $2.4; Highly liquid assets $53.1; “Total Available Liquidity $71.8.” Footnote 8: “Athene holds $94.6 billion of total available liquidity inclusive of the noncontrolling interests in ACRA.”
- 10-Q liquidity section: “Our investment portfolio is structured to ensure a strong liquidity position over time to permit timely payment of policy and contract benefits without requiring asset sales at inopportune times or at depressed prices.” It defines liquid assets but says they “exclude pledged assets, mainly associated with funding agreement and repurchase agreement liabilities.” Carrying value of these liquid assets excluding modco/funds-withheld portfolios was “$130.0 billion”; liquid assets in modco/funds-withheld portfolios were “$9.9 billion.”

### Funding agreement capability / definitions

- FI Presentation p.8: “Differentiated, Multi-Faceted Funding Agreement Capabilities”; “Disciplined approach illustrated by last syndicated issuance in September 2025”; “Simple, predictable liability with known maturities and no customer behavior or refinance risk”; “No FABNs are puttable by investors and Athene does not use short-term FA-backed commercial paper”; “Global issuance across 5 currencies”; “$33B 1Q‘26 LTM” funding agreement inflows by type: FABN / FABR / FHLB / Direct FA / Muni Prepays.
- FI Presentation p.6 / Financial Supplement footnote: “Funding agreements are comprised of funding agreements issued under our FABN program, secured and other funding agreements, which include our funding agreement backed repurchase agreement program (FABR) and direct funding agreements, funding agreements issued to the Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB) and long-term repurchase agreements.”
- 10-Q Note 11: “We have a funding agreement backed notes (FABN) program, which allows Athene Global Funding, a special-purpose, unaffiliated statutory trust, to offer its senior secured medium-term notes. Athene Global Funding uses the net proceeds from each sale to purchase one or more funding agreements from us.” As of 2026-03-31: “$34.5 billion” FABN outstanding and “$10.5 billion of board-authorized FABN capacity remaining.”
- 10-Q Note 11 FABR/direct: “Secured funding agreements issued under our funding agreement backed repurchase agreement (FABR) program involve special-purpose, unaffiliated entities entering into repurchase agreements with a third party, the proceeds of which are used by the special-purpose entities to purchase funding agreements from us.” As of 2026-03-31: “$27.6 billion” secured and other funding agreements outstanding, of which “$21.5 billion” FABR and “$6.1 billion” direct funding agreements.

### FHLB capacity / borrowings

- FI Presentation p.9: “Disciplined FHLB Funding Strategy”; “Fully collateralized, matched funding structure that supports disciplined spread generation and FHLB mission.” Membership highlights: “$1.3B Capital Stock,” “~135% Overcollateralization Ratio,” “$38B Pledged Collateral,” “$28B Outstanding Borrowings,” “8% of total liabilities,” and “65% Weighted Average LTV.”
- 10-Q Note 11: “We are a member of the Federal Home Loan Bank of Des Moines (FHLB) and, through membership, we have issued funding agreements to the FHLB in exchange for cash advances.” As of 2026-03-31 and 2025-12-31: “$28.2 billion and $23.3 billion, respectively, of FHLB funding agreements outstanding.”
- 10-Q liquidity section: “As of March 31, 2026, our total maximum borrowing capacity under the FHLB facilities was limited to $68.2 billion.” But after eligible-collateral/other limits: “we had the ability to draw up to an estimated $31.4 billion, inclusive of borrowings then outstanding.” Reconciliation math: $31.4B inclusive capacity less $28.2B outstanding ≈ $3.2B gross headroom; FI presentation available-liquidity footnote uses $2.4B available FHLB borrowing capacity.

### Public vs private funding agreements / spread discipline

- Apollo Q1 2026 transcript (third-party): “At Athene, inflows in the quarter totaled $20 billion, driven by activities across retail, flow reinsurance and funding agreements.”
- Same transcript, Q&A funding agreement answer: “We did no public funding agreements in the first quarter, given where spreads were. And given where spreads are today, they’ve come back in meaningfully from the wides, but they’re still not the level. They’re probably 15 to 20 basis points wide of where they need to be, depending on the tenor for us to find that attractive. We were, on the other hand, able to access private funding agreements in a way that compensated for that attractive spreads.”

### CLO / levered lending / private credit exposure

- FI Presentation p.16: “High Quality Asset Portfolio Generates Safe Yield”; “Net Invested Assets $300B”; asset mix includes “CLO 8%.” Note says 98% of $220B AFS securities designated NAIC 1 or 2.
- 10-Q net invested assets table: CLO = “$23,611” million and “7.9%” of total net invested assets at 2026-03-31, down from “$25,401” million / “8.7%” at 2025-12-31.
- Apollo Q1 2026 transcript: “CLO exposure is now down below 8%, and we expect it to be even lower in the coming quarters.” Also: “We had, at one point, on Athene’s balance sheet, north of $40 billion of investment-grade tranches of CLOs, some 11% of Athene’s balance sheet, by the way, 99.8% investment grade.”
- Apollo Q1 2026 transcript on levered lending/private-credit framing: “Our exposure to levered lending, which people sometimes call private credit is de minimis, rounds closer to 0 than to 1%, … at 0.4%. Our exposure to software, 0.1%.” Note: transcript text has a likely transcription artifact (“Cayman in at 0.4%”); reconcile against official audio/deck before using exact wording.

### AMAPS

- Apollo Q1 2026 transcript: “AMAPS, Apollo multi-asset prime securities essentially takes the benefit of CLO and adds greater number of issuers, less leverage better structure for investors with a FIC single A tranche versus a skinny single A tranche and 40% to 50% investment-grade assets.”
- Same: “Athene now has $11 billion of investment-grade exposure or 3% of the portfolio, essentially making up the decline of our CLOs. We would expect this to double over the coming months. as our CLO exposure comes down.”

## Reconciliation checklist for statutory audit

1. Tie public liquidity bridge: $71.8B net available liquidity / $94.6B including ACRA NCI / $130.0B liquid assets in 10-Q. Confirm what statutory entities hold the cash and highly liquid bonds and what is pledged/restricted.
2. FHLB: reconcile FI p.9 $38B pledged collateral, $28B outstanding borrowings, ~135% OC, 65% WA LTV, and $2.4B available FHLB liquidity to 10-Q $28.2B outstanding, $68.2B maximum capacity, $31.4B draw capacity inclusive of outstanding.
3. Funding agreements: build statutory roll-forward by entity for FABN ($34.5B outstanding, $10.5B remaining capacity), FABR ($21.5B), direct FAs ($6.1B), FHLB ($28.2B), and long-term repo; map each to pledged assets/restricted assets.
4. Public issuance discipline: preserve Apollo Q1 2026 statement that no public FAs were issued in Q1 due spreads and private FAs were used instead; verify against official webcast/audio before final publication.
5. CLO: reconcile public “CLO 8%” / 10-Q 7.9% / transcript “below 8%” to Schedule D/BA or statutory asset categories; test IG percentage and maturity/runoff claim.
6. Levered lending/private credit: verify the 0.4% management claim against underlying statutory/private-credit classifications; transcript wording needs audio/deck confirmation.
7. AMAPS: identify statutory asset category, CUSIPs/issuer vehicles if visible, NAIC designations, ratings, affiliate/related-party treatment, and whether $11B / ~3% exposure sits in Athene-only NIA or includes ACRA economics.
8. Restricted assets: 10-Q total restricted assets were $111.646B at 2026-03-31 and are “primarily related to reinsurance trusts … and the FHLB and secured funding agreements”; tie this to entity-level statutory Notes 5/11 and collateral schedules.
