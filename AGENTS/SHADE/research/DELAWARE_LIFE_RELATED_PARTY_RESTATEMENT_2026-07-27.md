### Audit opinion — read carefully, there is a trap here
- **Unmodified opinion on the statutory basis** of accounting.
- **Emphasis of Matter:** *"certain 2024 related party investment disclosures have been restated due to an error correction. Our opinions are not modified with respect to this matter."*
- ⚠️ The filing also contains an **"Adverse Opinion on U.S. GAAP."** **This is boilerplate in every statutory-basis insurance audit** (SAP ≠ GAAP; the variances are routinely described as "material and pervasive"). **It is not a finding about Delaware Life and must not be reported as one.**

**Net:** the auditor restated the disclosure and flagged it — but did **not** qualify the statutory opinion. That is a genuine dampener on severity.

## 3. 🔑 THE MECHANISM — a self-set classification threshold

This is the forensic core, and it is stated in the company's own accounting policy:

> "Related-party investments are identified pursuant to **SSAP No. 25**… the Company considers investments to be related party investments where the investment return is **predominantly contingent on the performance of related parties**. **The Company defines predominantly contingent as greater than 50%.**"

**The related-party disclosure line — the single number that tells a regulator, policyholder or counterparty how independent an insurer's asset side is — rests on a contingency test whose threshold the filer sets itself.** Delaware Life's $16B did not hide behind an offshore entity or a captive; it sat in the general account, in the bond line, on the wrong side of a self-graded 50% test.

**Generalizes to the whole cohort.** Any SSAP-25 filer applies the same judgment. This is a **measurement-integrity finding**, not just a single-name event — see §8 for the consequence to SHADE's own ratios.

### 4c. Reconciling the widely-quoted "$17B / 39%"
**$16,371,945 + $871,114 = $17,243,059 → $17.24B.** ✅ **The media "$17B" reconciles to primary.**

Denominator: **Total General Account assets 12/31/25 = $45,903,193 ($45.90B)**. $17.24B / $45.90B = **37.6% of general-account assets**; against *invested* assets (a smaller denominator) **~39% is consistent.** ✅

### 4d. ⚠️ The divergence that does NOT reconcile — do not propagate "12×"
The **Correction of Errors** note quantifies the restatement itself, and it is a **different year and a different measure** from the media framing:

> "general account related-party investments as of **December 31, 2024**, excluding investments in subsidiaries, **increased from $2,261,658 to $11,558,601**."

→ **FY2024: $2.26B → $11.56B = +$9.30B, a 5.1× revision.**

The circulating "**$1.4B (3%) → $17B (39%)**, ~12×" compares an **originally-reported FY2025** figure (**$1.4B is not in this filing**) against the **restated FY2025** total. Both statements can be true — they are different vintages — but:

**✅ Cite as primary:** FY2024 restatement **$2.26B → $11.56B (5.1×)**; FY2025 restated related-party total **$17.24B (~37.6% of GA assets)**.
**⚠️ Cite as media-sourced, unreconciled:** the "$1.4B / 3%" starting point and the "~12×" multiple.

**⚠️ The 10-hit FTS result is a FALSE ZERO if read as a cohort screen.** It measures *which insurers bundle statutory financials into EDGAR filings*, **not** which insurers use a self-set contingency test. Statutory financials for most insurers live with state regulators / NAIC and are **not on EDGAR at all**. Per auto-memory `finding_discovery_tool_wrong_slice_false_zero` + `finding_count_measures_intake_not_domain`.

**Consequence:** the **Athene Schedule-BA / statutory wall STANDS.** Delaware Life was reachable by accident of its filing practice. ## 9. Open questions (ranked)

1. **Is the $16.4B affiliate-contingent book impaired, or merely mis-labelled?** The restatement is a *disclosure* correction — **no write-down was taken and surplus was not restated for it.** Classification ≠ credit loss. **This is the difference between an accounting event and a SHADE kill path, and it is currently unresolved.**
2. **What does the remediation plan actually require** — size, timetable, forced-sale or run-off? Not public.
3. **Clear Spring Life ($16B) numbers** — this document covers Delaware Life only. CSLAC's own restatement is not yet pulled.
