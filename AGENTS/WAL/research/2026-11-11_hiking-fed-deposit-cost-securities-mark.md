# WAL — what a hiking Fed does to deposit cost and the securities mark (2026-11-11)

**Desk:** WAL · **Written:** 2026-11-11 ~09:55 ET · **Task:** PROME catch-up wake (prome-2b, Will 09:24 ET). Research only; no score, weight, EV, gate, threshold or card change.

## Bottom line

1. **A December 25bp hike is already inside management's Q4 guide.** On the 10/20 call the CFO's Q4 NII guide assumed a December +25bp move (KB-250, two B2 transcripts). Only the part of current pricing beyond one hike (more than one full 25bp move, or Waller's 50bp option) is outside the guide.
2. **By WAL's own model, a hike is roughly neutral for pretax earnings.** NII rises (+4.7% per +100bp shock, Q3 10-Q), but the earnings-credit (ECR) deposit cost, most of which sits in non-interest expense outside that NII figure, rises by a similar amount. Per 25bp that is roughly +$38M a year of NII against up to ~$58M a year of ECR cost (about three-quarters of it in expense). Both figures are linear scalings of a model, not forecasts.
3. **The Q3 10-Q made WAL's deposits look more rate-sensitive than at Q2.** Assumed betas rose: non-term deposits average 53% → 58%, ECR deposits 70% → 77%, all non-maturity deposits 59% → 65%. NII upside per +100bp fell from +5.9% to +4.7%, and the economic value of equity falls more when rates rise (−4.4% → −5.3% at +100bp).
4. **The securities mark is probably better today than the printed 9/30 mark, despite the hike repricing.** The −$1,223M available-for-sale loss was struck when the 5-year Treasury yield was 5.09% and the 10-year 5.29%. On 11/10 they were 4.81% and 4.98%. A rough one-quarter fit puts that at about +$170M pretax (~$1.2 per share of tangible book) since quarter-end. The 11/5 → 11/10 hike repricing gave back about +22bp of the 5-year, roughly $130M pretax of that recovery. AOCI is not in WAL's regulatory CET1, so the ratios do not move with the mark.
5. **The WAL-specific risk from higher-for-longer is credit, not funding or marks.** Floating-rate CRE and hotel borrowers pay more as the front end rises: 2027 CRE-NOO maturities are $2,972M (Q2 10-Q) and hotel franchise finance criticized loans rose from $75M to $185M in Q3. This is the thesis tail's mechanism, but nothing graded today, and long rates (cap rates) are lower than at quarter-end.

## Arithmetic (derived; every line is an estimate)

- **NII per 25bp:** 4.7% × ¼ × Q3 annualized NII ($807.6M × 4 = $3,230M) ≈ **+$38M a year**. Linear scaling of a non-linear, dynamic-balance-sheet shock.
- **ECR cost per 25bp:** 25bp × 77% × ≤$30.4B ≈ **≤$58M a year** of total earnings credits. Q3 split: about 74% in the expense line, 26% netted in interest income, so the expense-side piece is ≈ $43M. ⚠️ The interest-income piece is already inside the NII sensitivity, so never add the full $58M to the NII figure.
- **Net:** of the same order and opposite sign, so roughly neutral. The issuer's own all-in measure (earnings-at-risk including ECR expense and mortgage) was +0.8% for a +100bp ramp at Q2 (KB-220). The Q3 10-Q says only "within limits"; no Q3 figure is printed.
- **AFS sensitivity:** Q2 → Q3 the loss grew $537M while the 5-year rose 90bp, about **$6M per bp** (implied duration ~3.9 years on ~$15.2B of fixed-rate AFS). ⚠️ This one-quarter fit is contaminated by $2.0B of fair-value hedge terminations with the hedged securities sold, and by MBS spread moves. REGINALD's 3–5 year duration range remains an assumption, because no effective-duration figure is printed.
- **Since 9/30:** 5-year −28bp × ~$6M ≈ **+$170M pretax**, ≈ $125–130M after tax at ~25%, ≈ **$1.2 per share** on 107.55M shares (10/26 cover count), against TBV $62.22. 11/05 → 11/10: 5-year +22bp ≈ −$130M pretax given back.
