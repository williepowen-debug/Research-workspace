## SCENARIO — what the prior implies for the $99M mark

**Mechanical value compression, cap-rate leg alone:** 1 − (4.4 / 6.6) ≈ **−33%** at flat NOI. Layer in falling NOI (rents −8%, vacancy at a record) and a weak-submarket asset could be **−40% to −50%** off a 2021–22 peak basis; a **core-submarket, leased** asset far less (**−10% to −20%**).

**WAL-specific modifiers (why this is NOT a straight-line charge-down):**
1. **Loan, not equity.** WAL's loss depends on **collateral value vs. loan balance (LTV)**, not on the equity wipeout the sponsor already took by walking. A conservative origination LTV absorbs a large value decline before WAL is impaired.
2. **Leasing progress.** Borrower **brought it current end-June with a prospective tenant for a sizable piece** (Q2 call) — the asset is not dark; occupancy is the single biggest driver of a lab value.
3. **Two-speed market.** If the building sits in a **core submarket** (South SF core, San Diego core: sub-10% vacancy), the 23–34% headline does not apply.
4. **Q2 corroborants:** mgmt said office LTVs improved and OREO valuation losses were **zero** at Q2 — the book's marks were holding as of the last print.
5. **Timing:** the sector may be at a turning point; an appraisal dated late-2026 could catch either the trough or an early recovery.

**Net lean:** the sector prior is **clearly negative** and **raises** the probability of a downside appraisal mark → consistent with **holding bear-medium at 16%**, NOT with confirming a charge-down. Dispersion is wide because the WAL-specific facts (LTV, location, lease-up) are unknown.
