**Read:** WAL's ~$319/SF sits **above** the two suburban conversion trades ($172–$303) and **below** the urban deed-in-lieu mark ($514) and the stale assessment ($410). A new, purpose-built Class-A building with 2.4/1,000 structured parking should price above older conversions. But it is 100% vacant in a submarket with 25–27% vacancy, so an appraiser will run a long lease-up with heavy TI and free rent. **The appraisal near WAL's carrying value is genuinely a coin flip; the comparable set does not decide it.** For an "A" outcome (charge-off over $49.5M), value would need to fall below ~$49.5M (~$160/SF), below even the worst conversion trade. That is possible only as a forced sale.

## 4. Forecast

### 4.1 What WAL's own data already rules in or out (from desk files, A1/A2)

- **Q3 gross charge-offs for the whole bank were $48.0M** (net $45.8M; `STATUS.md` WAL-02 row, from the 10/19 release). **A charge-off over $49.5M on this loan in Q3 is therefore arithmetically excluded.** "A" can only come from a post-9/30 event (sale or write-down) disclosed as a subsequent event. Even a Q3 A′ charge-off would have had to absorb most of the bank's Q3 charge-offs.
- **Q3 nonaccrual fell 562 → 485 (−77)** while OREO rose 126 → 165 (+39). Management said 4 of the 6 Investor-Day credits were resolved, and this loan is **not** one of the six (KB-WAL-112). A ~$99M exit would likely have pushed nonaccrual lower and been mentioned. **The loan was most likely still nonaccrual at 9/30 (my estimate ~85%).** The +39 OREO move is too small to be this loan taken in at full value.
- **Q2 posture:** CFO, 7/22: "we have that property fairly valued on our balance sheet"; the appraisal was not yet in. Nonaccrual "with an allowance" in Construction & land development was only $1M at 6/30, against $108M with no allowance (10/30 dry-run note F2, A1). If the loan sits in C&LD, **WAL carried it at 6/30 with no reserve**, i.e. management's own internal valuation was at or above carrying. The bucket is unresolved (KB-WAL-150), so this is suggestive only.
- **Sponsor behaviour:** the borrower brought the loan current at end-June. CBRE then marketed a fee-simple sale (B&T 7/10/2026), and a prospective tenant was evaluating "a sizable piece" (Q2 call). A sponsor paying interest while marketing suggests it sees some value above the debt. This reading is weak, because sponsors also pay to control a sale process.
- **Disclosure habit:** WAL named the loan in the Q1 10-Q (subsequent event) and in the Q2 10-Q MD&A, only because it drove the NPL increase ("the migration of a life science laboratory/office CRE loan, as previously disclosed"). In Q3 the NPL change ran the other way. **If nothing happened to the loan, the Q3 MD&A has no reason to name it.** The Q3 release, deck and call were all silent (KB-WAL-238, KB-WAL-248).

### 4.2 Two-stage estimate

**Stage 1: the economic outcome by the 10-Q date (my judgment, evidence above):**

**Stage 2: the chance the 10-Q names the outcome.** I estimate ~85% if the loan exits (C or A, since that would move NPLs or charge-offs). It is ~45% for a reserve or partial charge-off (it could be described only in aggregate as a specific-ACL change, which frame F2 logs but does not grade). It is ~35% for "unchanged".
