## 4. Valuation assumption and ambiguity

Use one internally consistent, simplified payout model instead of selecting unrelated earnings and book multiples. Let **E** be sustainable annual common EPS available to ordinary shares, **B** the ending TBV/share, **g=3%** stable growth requiring **g×B** retained per share annually, and **k=13%** required equity return. Then sustainable distributions are **E−gB** and conditional value is:

`P = (E − gB)/(k − g)`; equivalently `P/B = (E/B − g)/(k − g)`.

**Both 13% and 3% are WAL judgment assumptions**, not measured discount rates, market beliefs or issuer forecasts. They assume durable earnings, sufficient regulatory capital, a distributable payout and a stable future share basis. k of 12–14% is shown to expose valuation uncertainty; g is a conservative steady-state capital-growth assumption, not the issuer's much faster near-term growth. Applying this steady-state model to a transition year is a limitation: the time needed to reach sustainable earnings is unknown and could reduce present value. Thus the outputs are conditional normalized values, not precise present targets.

| **13%** | **~$62** | **~$79** | **~$90** |
| 14% | ~$56 | ~$72 | ~$82 |

Central implied P/TBV multiples are approximately **1.02× / 1.24× / 1.38×**, respectively. They derive from the stipulated profitability/growth/required return; they are not peer comps. **At unchanged sustainable earnings and these assumptions, a smaller book does not mechanically create a proportionate valuation loss:** this model values distributable income, and lower capital-growth needs can offset the smaller book. An AOCI hit becomes more economically consequential if it impairs earnings, capital flexibility, growth/payout or raises the required return. None of those effects is assumed merely from the mark.

**Reverse calculation, explicitly not a market-belief claim:** with k=13%, g=3%, B=$60–65, price $73.73 is compatible with sustainable EPS roughly **$9.2–9.3**. With E=$11 and B≈$65, it is also compatible with a required return around **15.3%**. These alternatives demonstrate non-identification: the same price fits different earnings, payout and discount assumptions. The hypothetical $8 case is below both analyst FY2027 EPS and that chosen-model earnings reference, but this does not establish that the market has overlooked it.
