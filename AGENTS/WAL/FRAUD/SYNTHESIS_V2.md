## 1. Executive Summary

V2 fraud thesis confirmed in print Apr 21, 2026. WAL took **$152.5M of fraud-related charge-offs** ($126.4M LAM + $26.1M Cantor Group V), with management explicitly using the words *"two fraud-related credits"* and asserting the matters are *"now largely behind us."* Two of three pre-print vectors are publicly resolved. **Vector 3 (First Brands) and Vector 4 (Tricolor) remain SILENT** — neither was discussed in the call or press release, leaving their WAL-specific exposure unquantified.

The biggest pre-print analytical gap: **LAM was net-new** to the REGINALD watchlist. The pre-print SYNTHESIS_V2 had three vectors (Cantor, First Brands, Tricolor), all anchored to the RSM-auditor reserve-credibility thesis. The actual print delivered:

- A 5x larger charge-off (LAM $126.4M) on a credit we hadn't named, on the same Jefferies/Leucadia rail we'd flagged via First Brands
- The Cantor charge-off we had predicted, at the magnitude that directionally validates the ZION 83% benchmark (89% of WAL's specific reserve was utilized)
- Silence on the two vectors we had named

**Thesis status: V2 framework correct (collateral fraud + Jefferies/Leucadia transmission + WAL underwriting failure pattern). Specific named credits partially wrong (Cantor right, First Brands/Tricolor unnamed; LAM unnamed but materially the largest hit).**

## 3. The Auditor Case (RSM) — Demoted to Tertiary

The pre-print thesis: PCAOB Release 104-2025-100 found RSM 100%-deficient on ACL collateral verification at "Issuer B" (likely WAL); WAL's 30% Cantor reserve was therefore unreliable; expected forcing event.

**Post-print verdict:**

- Cantor charge-off ($26.1M / 89% utilization of $29.6M reserve) **directionally validates** the ZION 83% benchmark — i.e., WAL's reserve was almost-but-not-quite sufficient. Mgmt: *"the $29.6 million specific reserve established in Q3 has been validated by current as-is appraisal values."* Self-validating language.
- LAM ($126.4M) was a **previously-reserved credit** ("remaining balance") on the Jefferies/Leucadia rail, NOT an RSM-collateral-verification failure. The reserve was already there; the charge-off realized it.
- The "RSM under-reserved" thesis applies only to the Cantor leg, where it was directionally correct (WAL needed all of the reserve, not just 30%).

**Forward read:** RSM angle is no longer the sharpest edge. The cleaner thread is **whether the Jefferies/Leucadia rail has additional unidentified credits** (see §4).

## 4. Open V2 Threads

### 4a. Other LAM/Leucadia-era credits (PRIMARY)

LAM = Leucadia Asset Management = Jefferies subsidiary post-2013 merger. The disclosed credit was specifically *"the loan to a fund of Leucadia Asset Management"* (singular fund). LAM operates **multi-strategy funds** (per pre-print AUDITOR_NEXUS research). If WAL has additional facilities to other LAM funds — or to other Leucadia-era credits originated during the platform's pre-Jefferies expansion — those are latent.

**Mgmt lockdown:** *"Given the nature of this process, the outcome may take time to resolve, and we will not provide further commentary while the matter is ongoing."* No near-term incremental disclosure expected.

**Tests:**

### 4b. Cantor residual recovery realism

~$70M residual on book post-charge. Recovery sources per transcript:

1. **$13M senior liens** acquired during litigation (collateral protection)
2. **Springing guarantees from ultra-high net worth guarantors** — collectibility depends on guarantor solvency and willingness to litigate
3. **Mortgage fraud policy** — depends on policy terms and insurer's willingness to pay

**Scenarios:**
- Bull: 60-80% recovery → $14-28M additional loss over 2026-2027
- Base: 30-50% recovery → $35-50M additional loss
- Bear: <30% recovery → $50-70M additional loss (full residual writedown)

Mgmt: *"complex and potentially of long duration"* — implies multi-quarter resolution, not Q2-Q3.

### 4c. First Brands & Tricolor silence

Both were unquantified pre-print and remain unquantified post-print. Two interpretations:

| Interpretation | Implication |
|---|---|
| WAL has zero / immaterial direct exposure | "Largely behind us" framing is honest; cohort risk landed at Apollo (Atlas SP $6.9B PFSI), not WAL |
| WAL has exposure but it sits inside $87M ex-LAM provision | Stress is hidden in the line; future quarters may realize incremental losses |

**WAL's silence pattern** echoes RITM's Apr 28 silence on broader non-bank servicer stress — coverage Q&A focused on disclosed credits only, no proactive sector commentary. Sector-silence-is-data.
