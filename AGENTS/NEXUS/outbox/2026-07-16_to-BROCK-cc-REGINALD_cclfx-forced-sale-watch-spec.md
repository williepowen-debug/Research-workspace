## 3. THE TRIGGER (fires / refutes — pre-registered vs PRED-45)

**PRED-45 (NEXUS, Apr 2026, 90% conf): "Blue Owl arms-length fire sale triggers industry mark-down; first secondary at 85-90¢ or below."** CCLFX is the live instance.

- **🔴 FIRES** — a reported **arms-length** secondary clearing print **≤90¢** on the dollar (esp. **85-90¢ or below**) on CCLFX (or a comparable interval-fund/BDC forced sale). An arms-length third-party mark below carrying value forces the industry mark-down question — the slow layer priced.
- **🟢 REFUTES / kills the early-recognition read** — **par-adjacent clearing (≥~97-98¢, orderly).** The fund met redemptions without a distressed markdown = a **liquidity-management** event, not a valuation event. This is the disconfirming outcome — do not read a gate alone as a mark-down; the gate is about liquidity terms, the clearing PRICE is about value.
- **🟡 AMBIGUOUS (90-97¢)** — decompose the discount: **liquidity discount** (thin-market haircut, reverses) vs **credit markdown** (impairment, sticky). Needs the loan mix + a second print.

**Three verification guards (carry these — they're where this call goes wrong):**
1. **Arms-length vs affiliate.** If the manager, an affiliate, or a captive vehicle buys the loans, it is **NOT a clean arms-length mark** — a related-party purchase at a supported price refutes nothing and confirms nothing. Verify counterparty independence before load-bearing.
2. **Adverse selection / cream-skimming.** A fund raising cash sells its **most-liquid, best-quality** loans first → the secondary print can look par-adjacent WHILE residual NAV is overstated (the impaired loans stay on the books unsold). A clean-looking print on cherry-picked collateral is a false-refute. Ask what got sold.
3. **Single-print skepticism (NEXUS Disc-B).** One secondary print = one observation. A pattern (CCLFX + CELFX + another manager) > one sharp number.
