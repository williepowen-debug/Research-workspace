Tenth District manufacturing growth accelerated, with expectations for further increases in the next six months. The month-over-month price increases for raw materials eased slightly but continued to outpace finished product price growth. November 19, 2026 Manufacturing Manufacturing Survey Subscribe Share by: Cortney Cowley and Megan Williams Download Article Factory Growth Accelerated The month-over-month composite index was 18 in November, up from 11 in October and 14 in September (Tables 1 & 2). The composite index is an average of the production, new orders, employment, supplier delivery time, and raw materials inventory indexes. Durable manufacturing activity increased, driven primarily by strengthening growth in the transportation equipment manufacturing. In contrast, nondurable manufacturing growth slowed, driven by weaker activity in the printing sector. All month-over-month indexes were positive except for the finished good inventory index at -1. Most month-over-month indexes saw higher readings, but the indexes for raw materials price and inventories declined modestly. The production and employment indexes reached readings of 23 and 20, respectively, both multi-year highs. The year-over-year composite index increased from 11 to 19, its highest level since November 2022. All year-over-year indexes were positive, with capital expenditures continuing to grow from last year. Expectations for future activity remained expansionary with the 6-month composite index at 21, and the employment index rising slightly. Over a third (38%) of firms reported they are raising starting wages and/or salaries for only selected job categories to attract new hires, 30% are raising starting wages and/or salaries for most job categories, 18% are not raising starting wages and/or salaries, and 14% of firms reported they are not actively hiring (Chart 2). Firms were also asked about their employment expectations over the next twelve months. Over half (57%) of firms expect their firm to leave employment unchanged over the next twelve months, 38% expect to increase employment, and 6% expect to decrease employment (Chart 3).
