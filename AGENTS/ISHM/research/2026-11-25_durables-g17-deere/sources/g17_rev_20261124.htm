Industrial Production and Capacity Utilization: The 2026 Annual Revision PDF  ASCII RSS DDP FRED

Release Date: November 24, 2026

The Federal Reserve has revised its index of industrial production (IP) and the related measures of capacity and capacity utilization. The most prominent features of the revision are the incorporation of comprehensive production data for 2023 and 2024 from the Census Bureau's Annual Integrated Economic Survey (AIES), the integration of survey data on utilization rates for 2025, and an update of the base year to 2022 for the production and capacity indexes.[1]

Total IP in October 2026 was 3.6 percent above its level at the end of 2022, unchanged from the previous estimate. The annual rates of change in 2023 and 2024 have been revised up 1/2 percentage point per year, on average, and the annual rate of change in 2025 has been revised down 1/2 percentage point.[2] Annual growth rates before 2023 have been little revised. Notably, output in the industrial sector is now estimated to have been little changed, on net, over 2023 and 2024, whereas output was previously estimated to have contracted modestly before expanding in 2025 and over the first 10 months of 2026.

Industrial capacity is now estimated to have expanded at a slightly slower pace in recent years than previously estimated. Estimated capacity growth averaged 1.0 percent per year after 2022, which has been revised down from 1.2 percent per year, and remained below the long-run (1972–2025) average pace of 1.9 percent. Annual capacity growth in 2023 through 2025 has been revised down each year an average of 0.4 percentage point, while growth in 2026 has been revised up 0.3 percentage point. Capacity growth before 2023 has been largely unrevised.

With little change, on net, to production and somewhat lower capacity growth, capacity utilization has been revised up in recent years. In the fourth quarter of 2025, capacity utilization for total industry stood at 76.5 percent, 0.9 percentage point above its previous estimate and 2.9 percentage points below its long-run (1972–2025) average. For the 2023–25 period, capacity utilization has been revised up 0.8 percentage point on average (annual utilization rates are reported as of the fourth quarter). Capacity utilization has been revised down modestly in the previous 10 years, from 2013 through 2022, an average of 0.1 percentage point.

This revision to IP, capacity, and capacity utilization incorporated newly available annual data on both output and prices. The updated IP indexes incorporated data from the newly introduced AIES for the manufacturing sector and the publishing industry. Some content that was collected for the predecessors to the AIES, such as the Annual Survey of Manufactures (ASM), including beginning-of-year inventories and detailed information on equipment capital expenditures, is not collected as part of the AIES. Furthermore, a greater share of estimates in the manufacturing sector were suppressed for the 2023 AIES than had been typical for its predecessor.[3] The 2024 AIES had fewer data suppressions than the 2023 AIES. Where necessary, we impute results for data that were discontinued or withheld using the available data. The IP index for logging is based on special calculations provided by the U.S. Forest Service (USFS) that extended previously published data; the IP index incorporated new data for 2023 through 2025. In addition, the indexes for metallic and nonmetallic minerals were updated with revised annual data for 2021 through 2023 and with new data for 2024 and 2025 from the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). Data on prices from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) were also incorporated into most of the manufacturing indexes.

Revisions to IP indexes for the post-2024 period are based partly on adjustments to the correction factors that are intended to align the input and product data used for estimating monthly IP with the annual benchmark data. In addition, the monthly estimates of production have been updated to include late-arriving or revised quarterly or monthly indicator data, including information from the BLS's most recent benchmark revisions to the Current Employment Statistics. The monthly IP estimates reflect updated seasonal factors.

The revised estimates of capacity and capacity utilization incorporate data from the Census Bureau's Quarterly Survey of Plant Capacity Utilization for the fourth quarter of 2025 and new data on capacity from the USGS, the Energy Information Administration (EIA), and various industry organizations. The revised capacity estimates also include new data on capital spending from the 2023 and 2024 AIES and new data on fixed assets in 2025 from the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA).

RESULTS OF THE REVISION

Industrial Production

Production by Industry Group

Since 2022, manufacturing output has oscillated between growth and contraction, but it has risen on net. The annual rates of change in manufacturing output have been revised up in 2023 and 2024 and revised down in 2025. The cumulative effect of these revisions leaves the level of manufacturing output in October 2026 3-1/4 percent above its level from December 2022, close to its position before the revision.

Revisions across manufacturing industry groups were mixed. The output of durable goods rose 4-1/4 percent, on net, from December 2022 through October 2026, 3/4 percentage point less than the previously estimated increase. Over the same period, the output of nondurable goods rose 4 percent, on net, 3/4 percentage point more than the previously estimated increase. The index for industries in scope for manufacturing IP that are not part of manufacturing under the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS)—that is, logging and publishing—has been declining for much of its recent history; growth of output in this industry group has been revised down.

The output of mining and utilities both continued to show gains, on net, from December 2022 through October 2026, and the output indexes for both industries were revised up.

Production by Market Group

The index for consumer goods is estimated to have declined 3/4 percent, on net, from December 2022 through October 2026. However, the decline over that period has been revised up 1-1/4 percentage point, on net, as upward revisions to the durable consumer goods index more than offset modest downward revisions to the nondurable consumer goods index.

The index for business equipment is estimated to have risen 4-1/2 percent, on net, from December 2022 through October 2026, less than half as much as previously estimated. The index for defense and space equipment is estimated to have risen nearly 20 percent, on net, over the same time period, which is a bit less than previously estimated.

The index for construction supplies is estimated to have risen 1-1/2 percent from December 2022 through October 2026, 3/4 percentage point less than previously estimated. Over the same period, the output of business supplies is estimated to have risen 1-3/4 percent, little revised from its previously estimated growth rate over that period.

The index for materials is estimated to have risen 6-1/2 percent from December 2022 through October 2026, 3/4 percentage point more than previously estimated. The indexes for durable, nondurable, and energy materials each rose, on net, over this period; on net, an upward revision to the index for nondurable materials more than offset a downward revision to the index for durable materials, while the index for energy materials is little revised.

Capacity

Total industrial capacity is estimated to have risen at a modest pace from 2023 through 2026, with capacity increasing, on average, in manufacturing, mining, and utilities. Overall capacity is expected to rise 1.4 percent in 2026. Annual industrial capacity growth has been revised down an average of 0.2 percentage point per year from 2023 through 2026, with the largest downward revision of 0.5 percentage point occurring in 2025.

Manufacturing capacity is estimated to have grown 1/2 percent per year, on average, from 2023 through 2026, a pace that is well below the long-run (1972–2025) average pace of 2 percent per year but a turnaround from the average decline of 1/2 percent per year from 2008 through 2022. Manufacturing capacity is expected to rise 1 percent in 2026. Average annual growth is revised down 1/2 percentage point per year from 2023 through 2026, on average, with downward revisions to capacity growth for each of durable goods, nondurable goods, and other manufacturing.

Mining capacity is estimated to have risen each year from 2023 through 2026, resulting in an average annual growth rate of 1-1/4 percent, which is above its long-run (1972–2025) average pace of 3/4 percent. Mining capacity is expected to rise 1/4 percent in 2026. The annual rate of change for mining capacity has been revised up 3/4 percentage point per year, on average, from 2023 through 2026, with the largest upward revision in 2025.

Capacity for electric and gas utilities rose at a solid pace of 3-3/4 percent per year, on average, from 2023 through 2026, a pace that is faster than the long-run (1972–2025) average growth rate of 2-1/4 percent per year. Utilities capacity is expected to grow 4-1/2 percent in 2026. Revisions to the annual rates of utilities capacity growth were small from 2023 through 2025, but growth in 2026 has been revised up 1-3/4 percentage points.

Capacity Utilization

Capacity utilization for total industry moved down from 77.7 percent in 2022 to 76.5 percent in 2025, 2.9 percentage points below its long-run (1972–2025) average of 79.4 percent. On average, utilization has been revised up 1/2 percentage point from 2022 through 2025, with upward revisions of 1/2 percentage point in 2023 and 1 percentage point in both 2024 and 2025, along with a small downward revision in 2022. In October 2026, estimated capacity utilization stood at 76.8 percent.

Capacity utilization at manufacturers moved down from 76.8 percent in 2022 to 76.4 percent in 2025. Manufacturing utilization from 2022 through 2025 has been revised up about 3/4 percentage point on average. In October 2026, manufacturing utilization stood at 77.1 percent.

Within manufacturing, revisions to capacity utilization rates in 2025 were mostly positive across industry groups. Utilization in durables manufacturing has been revised up 1 percentage point, on average, from 2022 through 2025. Utilization in nondurables manufacturing has been revised up 3/4 percentage point, on average, over the same period, with most of the upward revision occurring in 2025.

The capacity utilization rate for mining rose from 81.4 percent in 2021 to 84.7 percent in 2023, moved down to 83.0 percent in 2024, and was little changed at 82.9 percent in 2025, 2-1/4 percentage points below its long-run (1972–2025) average rate of 85.1 percent. Utilization at mines has been revised down 1/2 percentage point, on average, from 2022 through 2025, largely reflecting a downward revision of 1-3/4 percentage points in 2025; utilization in oil and gas extraction has been revised down in each year.

The operating rate for electric and gas utilities has been below its long-run (1972–2025) average of 84.1 percent since 2008 and stood at 71.7 percent in 2025; utilization in utilities has been revised down slightly, on average, from 2022 through 2025.

TECHNICAL ASPECTS OF THE REVISION

The IP indexes represent the level of real output relative to a base year. At the monthly frequency, movements of the indexes are based on indicators that are derived using industry-specific data from a variety of government and private sources.

The monthly production indexes are anchored to annual benchmarks that are less timely but typically based on more comprehensive data. In most cases, the annual benchmark is nominal gross output reported by the Census Bureau deflated by a suitable price index.

Annual revisions to the IP and capacity measures generally involve (1) incorporating new and revised annual benchmark data on output, prices, and value-added proportions; (2) incorporating new monthly or quarterly data that were revised or that arrived too late to be included in the regular six-month reporting window for monthly IP; (3) updating seasonal adjustment factors; (4) updating the methods used to construct the indexes; and (5) introducing changes to the industry- or market-group structure of the indexes based on changes to underlying data sources.

Annual Benchmark Data on Output, Prices, and Value-Added Proportions

Output

The annual benchmark output indexes for IP are measures of real gross output at the six-digit NAICS (2022) level. The Census Bureau provides annual figures for value-added and the cost of materials for manufacturing industries, which can be summed to obtain nominal gross output. The benchmark indexes for this revision incorporated information for 2023 and 2024 from the AIES. The AIES replaced seven annual Census Bureau business surveys, including the ASM—which was previously used to construct benchmark production indexes for manufacturing industries—and the Service Annual Survey, which was previously used to construct benchmark indexes for publishing.

New annual data were also incorporated into many other indexes not in the scope of the AIES. The benchmark indexes for metallic and nonmetallic mineral mining were updated with any newly available data from 2021 through 2025 from the USGS, and the benchmark index for logging was advanced through 2025 based on data from the USFS.

Prices

To obtain individual benchmarks of real gross output, the measures of nominal gross output are deflated by annual price deflators. In general, the benchmark industry price deflators consist of price indexes from the BEA through 2011 that are extended through 2024 with the related producer price indexes (PPIs) from the BLS.[4] However, for a few selected industries, the annual price deflators are constructed by the Federal Reserve.[5]

Value-Added Proportions (Weights for Aggregation)

The IP system is organized as a hierarchical structure where the individual production indexes are combined using a version of the Fisher-ideal index formula to construct broader measures of production. The weights that are used to combine individual IP measures into more aggregate measures are based on the value-added from the industry, calculated as gross output less cost of materials. For IP indexes that are defined at the six-digit (or more aggregate) NAICS level, the value-added weights are derived from the Economic Census in years ending in ``2'' or ``7'' and from either the AIES or its predecessor, the ASM, in other years. For IP indexes that cover only part of a six-digit NAICS industry, the aggregation weights were constructed by allocating value-added (as defined by the Census Bureau) for a six-digit industry across the various components of IP that compose that industry.

The allocation of value-added across each IP component in an industry was determined by that component's share of the industry's overall product shipments. In principle, the annual revision uses data on product shipments based on the 2022 North American Product Classification System (NAPCS). However, the published results of the 2023 and 2024 AIES did not include survey results for product shipments. Instead, the Census Bureau published an experimental data product extending the value of shipments of products by industry for 2023 and 2024 using shares from the 2022 Economic Census. As such, allocation of value-added across components is generally unchanged from the previous annual revision. Missing values for specific NAPCS-based products were imputed where necessary.

The Federal Reserve derives estimates of value-added for the electric and gas utility industries from annual revenue and expense data issued by other organizations. For electric utilities, the measures of value-added incorporate data from the EIA of the U.S. Department of Energy and from the Edison Electric Institute. For gas utilities, the value-added estimates incorporate data from the American Gas Association. The weights for aggregation for mining industries are derived from value-added data from the Economic Census. Figures for value-added for mining industries in the years between the quinquennial Economic Censuses are estimated based on both output and price changes for the industry.

The weights for aggregation, expressed as value-added per unit output, were estimated with data on producer prices for the period after 2024.

Revised Quarterly and Monthly Data

This revision incorporated source data on production, shipments, and inventories that became available or were revised after the regular six-month reporting window for monthly IP was closed. These data were released with too great of a lag to be included with monthly IP estimates but were available for inclusion in the annual revision.

Revised Seasonal Factors

IP indexes are adjusted to remove from the underlying data the predictable movements related to timing, holiday, workday, and monthly or quarterly seasonal patterns. Individual indexes are adjusted using the Census X-13ARIMA-SEATS seasonal adjustment program. The seasonal factors are based on the full history of data back to 1972, where available.

Seasonal factors for indexes based on production-worker hours were updated with data through August 2026. The updated factors for the physical-product-based indexes used data through August 2026 where available.

Seasonal factors for unit motor vehicle assemblies have been updated, and projections through December 2028 are available on the Board's website at https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/mv_sales_sf.htm. These factors are based on production data through April 2026 and were revised back to January 2021. The seasonal factors explicitly incorporate the holiday schedule for the vehicle assembly lines specified in the latest collective bargaining agreements with domestic manufacturers. The seasonal factors identify production data from September 2025 and January 2026 as outliers because of changes in tax policy and a winter storm, respectively.

Methodological Changes to Individual Production and Capacity Indexes

Consolidation of Individual Industrial Production Indexes for Residential Air-Conditioning Equipment Manufacturing

Production estimates for room air conditioners (NAICS 333415pt.) and for residential unitary air conditioners (NAICS 333415pt.) have been consolidated into a single index of residential air-conditioning equipment (NAICS 333415pt.). This consolidation does not alter the availability of any IP indexes, as the individual indexes for room air conditioners and for residential unitary air conditioners were previously unpublished, and the consolidated index will remain unpublished.

Consolidation of Individual Industrial Production Indexes for Manufacturing of Major Appliances Excluding Water Heaters

Production estimates for household cooking appliance (NAICS 335220pt.), for clothes dryers (NAICS 335220pt.), for washing machines (NAICS 335220pt.), for dishwashers and disposers (NAICS 335220pt.), for home freezers (NAICS 335220pt.), and for refrigerators (NAICS 335220pt.) have been consolidated into a single index of major appliances excluding water heaters (NAICS 335220pt.). These changes more closely align the structure of the production indexes with the 2022 NAICS structure, which eliminated detailed NAICS codes for categories of major appliances. The index of major appliances excluding water heaters will be released as an auxiliary index; previously, auxiliary indexes for household laundry equipment, for other major household appliances, and for home refrigerators and home freezers were available.

No Methodological Changes to Individual Capacity Indexes

There were no changes to the methodology for constructing individual capacity indexes in the 2026 annual revision to the Federal Reserve Statistical Release G.17, ``Industrial Production and Capacity Utilization.''

Data Availability and Publication Changes

Files containing the revised data and the text and tables from this release are available on the Board's website at https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17, as are updated data for the annual revision and for all of the regularly issued series on IP, capacity, and capacity utilization. Other changes are listed on the Board's website at https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/g17_revision_series.htm.
