Preliminary North American Class 8 Net Orders for October Disappoint at 15,600 Units

FTR reports North American (N.A.) Class 8 preliminary net orders for October totaled 15,600 units, down 24% month over month and 36% year over year (y/y). Year-to-date orders reached 278,201 units, up 74% y/y. October orders were nearly 50% below the 10-year October average of 31,089 units, marking a notably weaker-than-expected start to the 2027 order season. Combined September-October orders totaled 36,002 units, down 20% from the comparable period last year.

The disappointing October results likely reflect ongoing uncertainty about the Environmental Protection Agency’s 2027 NOx regulations and their impact on model year (MY) 2027 truck pricing and production schedules. While OEMs have begun opening their MY 2027 order boards, some may not yet be fully releasing build slots or finalizing pricing pending greater regulatory clarity.

Meanwhile, fleets may be delaying purchases while awaiting final regulations and potentially more favorable pricing. Under the proposed framework, nonconformance penalties could add an estimated $6,000 to $7,000 per engine if passed through to buyers, compared with an estimated $8,000 to $12,000 premium for fully compliant engines. Actual costs will depend on the final rule and OEM pricing decisions.

Dan Moyer, senior analyst, commercial vehicles, commented, “Other factors may also be contributing to the slower ordering pace. Fleet capital spending priorities may be shifting toward trailers rather than tractors in the near term. Also, while freight rates are strong, so are costs for fuel, insurance, and labor, pressuring margins and possibly limiting purchases for now.

“Underlying replacement needs, tighter capacity, and firmer freight rates continue to support Class 8 demand. However, with the earlier EPA 2027 pre-buy now complete, orders are increasingly tied to fleet profitability, capital spending priorities, and regulatory certainty. Weak October results suggest that some purchases may be deferred rather than canceled, potentially allowing orders to strengthen as emissions requirements, production schedules, and equipment pricing become clearer.”

Dan Moyer

Senior Analyst, Commercial Vehicles
