Regulatory Uncertainty Weighs on Class 8 Orders as Medium-Duty Demand Remains Resilient

11/18/26

Share this

Final North American Class 8 net orders totaled 13,755 units in October, down 44% year-over-year, according to ACT Research’s latest State of the Industry: NA Classes 5-8 report. While Class 8 activity softened amid ongoing regulatory uncertainty around the EPA’27 engine transition, total Classes 5-7 orders rose 12% year-over-year to 17,731 units, signaling continued resilience in the medium-duty segment.

“Regulatory ambiguity regarding the upcoming EPA’27 engine standards is prompting fleets and OEMs to delay Class 8 order activity, as the industry seeks clarity on future equipment costs and compliance requirements,” said Carter Vieth, Research Analyst at ACT Research. “Despite this caution, underlying demand remains, with many fleets looking to refresh aging equipment and take advantage of supportive freight rates. Meanwhile, medium-duty order strength appears linked to dealer inventory stocking ahead of anticipated 2027 regulatory cost increases.”


State of the Industry: NA Classes 5-8 report provides a monthly look at the current production, sales, and general state of the on-road heavy and medium duty commercial vehicle markets in North America. It differentiates market indicators by Class 5, Classes 6-7 chassis and Class 8 trucks and tractors, detailing measures such as backlog, build, inventory, new orders, cancellations, net orders, and retail sales. Additionally, Class 5 and Classes 6-7 are segmented by trucks, buses, RVs, and step van configurations, while Class 8 is segmented by trucks and tractors with and without sleeper cabs. This report includes a six-month industry build plan, backlog timing analysis, historical data from 1996 to the present in spreadsheet format, and a ready-to-use graph package. A first-look at preliminary net orders is also published in conjunction with this report.

Additional Resources

Preliminary North America Class 8 net orders totaled 13,500 units in October, down 45% year-over-year, according to ACT Research. The decline reflects a combination of unresolved regulatory questions ahead of EPA 2027 implementation, ongoing cost pressures, and midterm political uncertainty, despite continued strength in spot freight rates. Complete industry data for October, including final order results, will be published by ACT Research in mid-November.

Medium-duty demand showed resilience, with preliminary Classes 5-7 net orders rising 14% year-over-year to 18,000 units in October, continuing the segment’s recovery from last year’s soft volumes.

“While spot rates remain supportive of equipment demand, the lack of clarity around EPA 2027 rules and associated costs has left OEMs and fleets unable to plan for next year,” said Carter Vieth, Research Analyst at ACT Research. “It’s not a lack of desire for new equipment holding fleets back, but rather the inability to make informed investment decisions in the face of regulatory and economic uncertainty.”
