Manufacturing activity in the region was less widespread 
but continued to expand overall, according to the firms 
responding to the November Manufacturing Business 
Outlook Survey. The survey’s indicator for general 
activity fell to a six-month low but remained positive. The 
new orders index fell to a near-zero reading, while the 
shipments index moved higher. The employment index 
rose, continuing to suggest overall gains in employment. 
Both price indexes increased from already elevated 
readings. Most future indicators declined this month but 
continued to suggest expectations for overall growth 
over the next six months. 

Current Indicators Are Mixed 

The diffusion index for current general activity fell from 
20.9 in October to 6.3 in November, its lowest reading 
since May (see Chart 1). More than 36 percent of the 
firms reported increases in activity, while 30 percent 
reported decreases; 33 percent of the firms reported no 
change in activity. The current new orders index dropped 5 
points to -0.4, its lowest reading since May and the index’s 
fourth consecutive decline. More than 32 percent of the 
firms reported decreases in new orders (down from 34 
percent last month), while 32 percent reported increases 
(down from 39 percent); 35 percent reported no change 
(up from 22 percent). The shipments index rose 10 points 
to 25.7, offsetting most of the previous month’s decline.  

The employment index rose 3 points to 11.6, its first 
increase in three months. More than 21 percent of the 
firms reported an increase in employment levels, 10 
percent reported decreases, and 69 percent reported no 
change. The average workweek index rose from 7.5 to 
11.2.  

Price Indexes Remain Elevated 

Both price indexes rose further above their long-run 
nonrecession averages in November. The prices paid 
index rose 15 points to 58.2, its highest reading since April

Most Future Indicators Weaken  

The diffusion index for future general activity was little 
changed at 33.6 this month, following two consecutive 
declines (see Chart 1). More than 50 percent of the firms 
expect an increase in activity over the next six months, 
and 17 percent expect a decrease; 23 percent expect no 
change. The future new orders index declined 5 points to 
31.7, and the future shipments index fell 10 points to 
25.4, each reaching its lowest reading in more than a 
year. The firms continue to expect increases in 
employment over the next six months, but the future 
employment index declined 5 points to 24.9, its lowest 
reading since February. Both future price indexes were 
little changed this month, well above their long-run 
averages. The future prices paid index edged up to 63.3, 
and the future prices received index ticked up 1 point to 
62.9. The index for future capital expenditures fell 16 
points to 18.9, its third consecutive decline and lowest 
reading since February.    

Summary 
Responses to the November Manufacturing Business 
Outlook Survey suggest that the region’s manufacturing 
activity continued to expand overall but was less 

widespread this month. The indicators for current activity 
and new orders declined to six-month lows, while the 
shipments index increased this month. The employment 
index rose, continuing to suggest overall gains in 
employment. Price increases were more widespread this 
month, and both price indexes remained well elevated. The 
survey’s broad indicators for future activity declined but 
continued to suggest expectations for growth over the next 
six months. ◼
