Manufacturing Business Outlook Survey
October 2026 Report
Note: Survey responses were collected from October 5 to October 12.

Manufacturing activity in the region continued to expand  overall, according to the firms responding to the October
Manufacturing  Business Outlook Survey
. The survey’s indicators for general activity, new  orders, and shipments all declined but remained positive this month. The  employment index edged down but continued to suggest overall increases in  employment. The prices paid index moved down, while the prices received index  moved higher this month; both continued to indicate overall increases in  prices. The firms continued to expect overall growth over the next six months, but  most future indicators declined.
Current Indexes Fall but Remain Positive
The diffusion index for current general activity declined  for the second consecutive month, falling from 37.8 in September to 20.9 in October  (see Chart 1). More than 41 percent of the firms reported increases in activity  (down from 45 percent last month), and 20 percent reported decreases (up from 8  percent); 27 percent of the firms reported no change in current activity (down  from 45 percent). The new orders index fell 25 points to 4.3 this month. Almost  39 percent of the firms reported increases in new orders, 34 percent reported  decreases, and 22 percent reported no change. The current shipments index moved  down 12 points to 15.3.
The employment index continued to indicate an increase in  employment overall but edged down 3 points to 9.1 in October. Eighteen percent  of the firms reported increases, 9 percent reported decreases, and 68 percent reported  no change in employment. The average workweek index declined 11 points to 7.5, its  lowest reading since June.
Price Indexes Remain Elevated
The firms continued to report overall increases in prices.  The prices paid index moved down 5 points to 43.2 in October (see Chart 2). More  than 43 percent of the firms reported increases in input prices, 52 percent  reported no change, and no firms reported decreases. The current prices  received index rose 9 points to 40.1, its highest reading since May 2025. More  than 41 percent of the firms reported increases in the prices of their own  goods (up from 33 percent last month), 52 percent reported no change (down from  66 percent), and 1 percent of the firms reported decreases (unchanged from last  month).
Firms Anticipate Higher Capital Expenditures Next Year
In this month’s
special question
, manufacturers  were asked about their plans for different categories of capital expenditures  next year. More than 52 percent of the firms  expect higher total capital spending, and no firm expects lower capital  spending next year; 48 percent of the firms expect total spending to stay the  same. When this question was asked last year, 36 percent expected to increase  spending, and 19 percent expected to decrease spending. Expectations for higher  capital expenditures were most widespread for noncomputer equipment, software, and  computer and related hardware. However,  at least half of the firms expect capital expenditures to stay the same across  all categories.
Firms Continue to Expect Growth
The diffusion index for future general activity fell 20  points to 33.0 this month, its second consecutive decline and lowest reading  since January (see Chart 1). More than 51 percent of the firms expect an  increase in activity over the next six months, 18 percent expect a decrease,  and 18 percent expect no change. The future new orders index fell 25 points to 37.0,  and the future shipments index fell 26 points to 35.2. The firms continue to  expect increases in employment over the next six months, although the future  employment index more than offset its increase from last month, falling 21  points to 30.1. Both future price indexes also decreased this month but remained  well above their respective long-run averages. The future prices paid index fell  8 points to 62.9, while the future prices received index fell 10 points to 62.1.  The index for future capital expenditures edged down 2 points to 34.9 this  month.
Summary
Responses to the October
Manufacturing Business  Outlook Survey
suggest overall expansion in the region’s manufacturing  activity. The indicators for current activity, new orders, and shipments all  moderated but remained positive this month. The firms continued to indicate  overall increases  in prices. The survey’s broad indicators for future activity continued to  suggest expectations for growth over the next six months, although those  expectations were less widespread this month.
