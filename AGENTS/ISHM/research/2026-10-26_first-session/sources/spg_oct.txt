Comment

Chris Williamson, Chief Business Economist at S&P 
Global Market Intelligence:

“October’s flash PMI data indicate that the US economy 
continues to boom at the start of the fourth quarter. The 
output readings from the survey are consistent with GDP 
growing at a 5% annualized rate after a 4% expansion 
was signalled for the third quarter. Especially healthy 
corporate earnings and profits are also indicated, and 
hiring has surged as companies seek to boost capacity to 
fulfil stronger customer demand.  

“If the reopening of the economy after COVID is excluded, 
recent months have seen the best growth spell for US 
businesses since early 2015.

“However, cost pressures remain a key concern, notably 
as the renewed surge in oil prices reverberates through to 
impact a wide variety of goods and services. Supply chain 
bottlenecks have meanwhile intensified, and order book 
backlogs are growing, making it easier for companies 
to pass higher costs on to customers. The result is the 
sharpest increase in selling prices for over four years to 
a pace that indicates consumer price inflation is likely to 
rise further above the Fed’s target in the coming months.”

October 2026
Flash US Composite PMI Output Index: 57.6 
(September: 58.4). 2-month low.

Flash US Services PMI Business Activity Index: 
57.9 (September: 58.8). 2-month low.

Flash US Manufacturing Output Index: 55.8 
(September: 56.2). 2-month low.

Flash US Manufacturing PMI: 56.0 (September: 
55.9). 53-month high. 

US business activity growth slowed in October but 
remained close to September’s recent high in response to 
a further improvement in order books. Growth rates in both 
manufacturing and services remained among the best seen 
over the past five years as robust domestic demand was 
accompanied by renewed signs of export growth. Hiring also 
remained strong, with employment increasing at the second-
fastest pace for over four years as firms boosted capacity to 
meet demand. However, selling prices rose at the sharpest 
rate since August 2022 as companies sought to pass on a 
further steep rise in costs to customers.

Output and demand
The headline flash S&P Global US PMI Composite Output 
Index slipped from 58.4 in September to 57.6 in October but 
remained firmly in strong growth territory. Despite the fall, the 
index still signalled the second-sharpest increase in activity 
since March 2022. Growth dipped lower in both manufacturing 
and services, but in both cases the rates of expansion were 
among the highest seen over the past five years.

New orders growth meanwhile ticked higher from September’s 
elevated reading and was the highest in over four-and-a-half 
years. However, a weakened rate of increase in manufacturing 
new orders contrasted with an improved rate of growth of 
services new business to the strongest since March 2022.

While the upturn in orders was again principally a reflection 
of rising domestic demand, boosted partly by precautionary 
stock building, October saw export sales gain traction, 
registering the strongest gain since May 2022. Export orders 
for goods rose for the first time since June of last year, 
accompanied by the largest rise in services exports since July 
2023. 

Employment and operating capacity
Companies’ backlogs of uncompleted orders, a key indicator 
of capacity utilization and future business growth, rose 

© 2026 S&P Globalsharply again, the latest increase being the largest since 
May 2022. Backlogs accumulated at stronger rates in both 
manufacturing and services.

The rise in backlogs of work again encouraged firms to take 
on more staff. Employment rose in October at the fastest rate 
since June 2022, barring only the jump in payrolls reported in 
September. Both service sector and manufacturing payrolls 
increased, the latter notably at the fastest rate since March 
2012, while the service sector jobs gain was below that seen in 
September but still the third largest for over four years. 

Future sentiment
Business output expectations for the 12 months ahead cooled 
compared to September but were still the second highest 
seen this year. A paring-back of optimism in the services 
economy was countered, however, by a marked improvement 
in sentiment in the manufacturing sector. Business expansion 
plans reflected reports of confidence being buoyed by 
signs of ongoing demand growth and economic resilience. 
Manufacturers remained more upbeat than service providers, 
and factory confidence has more or less returned to its 
long-run average. In contrast, service providers’ sentiment 
remained well below its trend level amid worries over the cost-
of-living, higher borrowing costs and political uncertainty.

Prices
Selling price inflation picked up further momentum in October. 
The overall rise in prices across goods and services was the 
largest since August 2022. The upturn in price pressures was 
also broad-based: while services inflation edged up to the 
steepest since August 2022, manufacturers’ selling prices 
rose at a pace not seen since July 2022, with the rate of 
inflation accelerating markedly.

Higher prices reflected a further sharp increase in companies’ 
costs. Average input costs measured across both goods 
and services rose at the fastest pace since November 2022, 
excluding only the rise seen in September. The surge in cost 
pressures was most prominent in manufacturing, though 
service sector costs also rose sharply again.

Respondents commonly cited oil-based inputs, energy, fuel 
and freight rates as having pushed costs higher, but various 
other raw materials including steel, aluminum and copper 
were also up in price, often due to tariffs or shortages. 
Supplier delivery times lengthened to the greatest extent 
since 2022 amid material shortages, low stock levels, 
overloaded suppliers, labour shortages, rising back orders and 
shipping or import delays. Service providers also widely noted 
higher wage rates and increased utility and insurance costs. 

Manufacturing PMI
The S&P Global US Manufacturing PMI rose from 55.9 in 
September to 56.0 in October, according to the flash reading, 
registering the strongest improvement in operating conditions 
since May 2022.  Although production and new orders growth 
rates slowed, both remained elevated to register sustained 
strong expansions. Jobs growth meanwhile accelerated 
further to reach the highest since March 2012, and input 
inventories rose at the fastest rate for four months. Suppliers’ 
delivery times meanwhile lengthened to the greatest degree 
since July 2022.

Methodology

Final October data are published on 2 November for manufacturing and 4 November for 
services and composite indicators.
The S&P Global Flash US Composite PMI® is compiled by S&P Global from responses to 
questionnaires sent to survey panels of around 650 manufacturers and 500 service providers. 
The panels are each stratified by detailed sector and company workforce size, based on 
contributions to GDP. The services sector is defined as consumer (excluding retail), transport, 
information, communication, finance, insurance, real estate and business services. 

Survey responses are collected in the second half of each month and indicate the direction of 
change compared to the previous month. The following variables are monitored:

Manufacturing: Output, new orders, new export orders, backlogs of work, stocks of finished 
goods, employment, quantity of purchases, suppliers' delivery times, stocks of purchases, 
input prices, output prices, future output.

Services: Business activity, new business, new export business, outstanding business, 
employment, input prices, prices charged, future activity. 

A diffusion index is calculated for each manufacturing and services variable. The index is 
the sum of the percentage of ‘higher’ responses and half the percentage of ‘unchanged’ 
responses. The indices vary between 0 and 100, with a reading above 50 indicating an overall 
increase compared to the previous month, and below 50 an overall decrease. The indices are 
then seasonally adjusted. 

Composite indices for are calculated by weighting together comparable manufacturing and 
services indices using official manufacturing and services annual value added.

The headline figure is the Composite Output Index. This is a weighted average of the 
Manufacturing Output Index and the Services Business Activity Index. It may be referred to as 
the ‘Composite PMI’ but is not comparable with the headline Manufacturing PMI, which is a 
weighted average of five manufacturing indices (including the Manufacturing Output Index).
The headline manufacturing figure is the Manufacturing Purchasing Managers’ Index™ (PMI®). 
The PMI is a weighted average of the following five indices: New Orders (30%), Output (25%), 
Employment (20%), Suppliers’ Delivery Times (15%) and Stocks of Purchases (10%). For the PMI 
calculation the Suppliers’ Delivery Times Index is inverted so that it moves in a comparable 
direction to the other indices. 

The headline services figure is the Services Business Activity Index. This is a diffusion index 
calculated from a single question that asks for changes in the volume of business activity 
compared with one month previously. The Business Activity Index is comparable to the 
Manufacturing Output Index. It may be referred to as the ‘Services PMI’ but is not comparable 
with the headline Manufacturing PMI. 

Disclaimer

Flash data are calculated from around 80-90% of total responses and are intended to provide 
an accurate early indication of the final data. Since flash data were first processed, the 
average differences between final and flash index values for the headline indices are:

Composite Output Index = 0.1 (absolute difference 0.4)

Services Business Activity Index = 0.1 (absolute difference 0.4)

Manufacturing PMI = 0.0 (absolute difference 0.3)

S&P Global do not revise underlying survey data after first publication, but seasonal 
adjustment factors may be revised from time to time as appropriate which will affect the 
seasonally adjusted data series. Historical data relating to the underlying (unadjusted) 
numbers, first published seasonally adjusted series and subsequently revised data are 
available to subscribers from S&P Global.
