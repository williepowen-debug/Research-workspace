Cass Freight Index® - Shipments
The shipments component of the Cass Freight Index rose 2.3% m/m and 1.9% y/y in September, the second straight y/y gain after a 3.5-year downturn by this measure, the longest on record.
In seasonally adjusted (SA) terms, shipments rose 1.4% m/m in September, building on the 5.0% sequential gain in August.
With modest retail and industrial growth, and some inventory restocking as container imports surge, freight demand is recovering. The combination of a smaller private fleet and rising new equipment sales also supports higher for-hire volumes.
While two months generally does not make a trend, and rising oil prices and interest rates are limiting factors, the cycle is on a positive track.
The normal seasonal pattern would put the shipments component of the Cass Freight Index up 4% y/y in October.

Cass Freight Index - Expenditures
The expenditures component of the Cass Freight Index, which measures the total amount spent on freight, accelerated to 20% y/y growth in September to 3.970, up from a 19% gain in August.
The acceleration in the past two months, from a prior 5.5% YTD rate, was driven by higher shipments, rates, and fuel surcharges.
In SA terms, the index rose 4.0% m/m after a 6.0% gain in August.
After declines in 2023, 2024, and 2025, the expenditures component of the Cass Freight Index is on track for a 12% gain in 2026. With normal seasonality, the index would rise 23% y/y in October.

Cass Truckload Linehaul Index®
The Cass Truckload Linehaul Index rose to 154.8 in September, up 0.6% m/m and up 10.1% y/y.
The sequential increase is slower than the 0.9% YTD average, likely because spot rates retrenched somewhat and contract rates slowed with few bids this time of year.
Even as spot rates slow with modest sequential declines, the much larger contract market is still adjusting higher.
This index reflects the whole for-hire truckload market, both spot and contract rates.
The Cass Truckload Linehaul Index fell 10% in 2023, another 3.4% in 2024, and turned up to a 1.8% increase in 2025. In 2026, the index is on pace for a 7% increase.

ACT Freight Expectations
As evidence that the freight demand cycle is improving and this could be the start of a trend, we present recent trends and our forecasts for intermodal loads on the North American Class I railroads for the rest of this year and 2027.
In 2026, NA intermodal volumes are on track to break the 2018 record. We estimate 19.3 million intermodal loads in 2026, breaking the previous record of 18.9 million in 2018. And we expect 2027 to be another record year, with the intermodal cost advantage above average.
The biggest question to us is how the railroads will execute on the higher demand level. At industry conferences this month, executives assured us there’s a cushion of capacity and they’re stacking chassis to be ready to serve record volumes in the coming months.

About the Author: Tim Denoyer, ACT Research
Tim Denoyer joined ACT Research in 2017 after spending fifteen years in equity research focused primarily on the transportation, machinery, and automotive industries. Tim is a senior analyst leading ACT’s transportation research effort and the primary author of the
ACT Freight Forecast, U.S. Rate and Volume OUTLOOK
. Research associate, Carter Vieth, who joined ACT in early 2020 after graduating from Indiana University, also contributes to the report. This report provides supply-chain professionals with better visibility on the future of pricing and volume in trucking, the core of the $1.2 trillion U.S. freight transportation industry, including TL, LTL, and intermodal.
Tim also contributes to ACT’s core Classes 4-8 commercial vehicle (CV) data analysis and forecasting; powertrain development, such as electrification analysis; and used truck valuation and forecasting. Tim has supported or led numerous project-based market studies on behalf of clients in his time with ACT on topics ranging from upcoming emissions and environmental regulations to alternative powertrain cost analyses, to e-commerce and last-mile logistics, to autonomous freight market sizing.
ACT’s freight research service leverages its expertise in the supply-side economics of transportation and draws upon Tim’s background as an investment analyst, beginning at Prudential and Bear Stearns. Tim was a co-founder of Wolfe Research, one of the leading equity research firms in the investment industry. His experience also includes responsibility for covering the industrial sector of the global equity markets, including with leading investment management company Balyasny Asset Management.
