Market Update - Dealers & Leasing

October 2026 Update

The September sales and inventory picture differed by application. North American Class 8 retail sales rose 25% year over year, led by tractors. Vocational sales were softer and inventories remained elevated. A higher total retail count does not mean every stock unit is turning at the same pace; dealers and lessors need to examine customer demand and inventory age by application.

Match Inventory to the Customer’s Operating Need

U.S. trailer net orders reached 42,700, up sharply against a very weak year-earlier comparison, while build rose 15%. Dealers should distinguish a customer commitment from a delivery date, especially as OEMs and suppliers work through production ramp requirements. Specs, application and the timing of price changes can influence when a fleet chooses to accept equipment.

Rental and Leasing Decisions Depend on Utilization and Flexibility

Used Class 8 trucks averaged $64,007 at retail, up 12% year over year and down 1.9% from August. Same-dealer retail transactions fell 5.7% for the month but were 6% higher than a year earlier. Average age, miles and vehicle mix influence the price reading; the market average is not a value for an individual trade. Dealers and lessors can use ACT's State of the Industry and used-truck reporting to compare inventory age, stocking needs, utilization, financing and residual exposure before adjusting purchase or lease terms.
