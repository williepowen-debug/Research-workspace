S&P Global US Manufacturing PMI®

Manufacturing sector sustains marked growth in October and 
employment rises sharply, but inflationary pressures intensify

October 2026
Production and new orders increase albeit at 
slower rates

Price pressures intensify noticeably as supply-
chain disruption persists

Jobs growth at five-and-a-half-year peak

The US manufacturing sector continued to expand markedly 
in October, albeit to a slightly softer degree. Output and 
new orders continued to rise, although headwinds remained 
apparent. 

These were most notably related to input costs, with 
inflation at its highest since May and well-above trend, and 
supplier delivery times, which lengthened to the greatest 
degree since August 2022. 

Nonetheless, firms retained sufficient confidence in the 
outlook to expand employment steeply, with growth the 
highest since April 2021. 

The seasonally adjusted S&P Global US Manufacturing 
Purchasing Managers’ Index™ (PMI®) recorded 54.9 in 
October. Posting above the critical 50.0 no-change mark for 
a fifteenth successive month, the PMI signaled a further 
improvement in operating conditions. Growth was again 
also comfortably above trend, although by falling from 
September’s 52-month high of 55.9, the headline index 
pointed to a slight softening of growth. 

Rising levels of output and new orders continued to underpin 
the sector’s expansion, although like the PMI, both registered 
slower growth rates compared to September. Production 
rose for the seventeenth successive month and was closely 
associated with an upturn in new orders. Although new 
work increased at the slowest rate since March, growth 
was still solid and supported by generally stronger market 
demand and a stabilization of international sales (new export 
business rose fractionally for the first time since June 2025 
although tariffs limited gains). There remained some reports 
of clients seeking to secure orders and lock in prices given 
widespread inflationary pressures.

On the price front, input cost inflation surged higher in 
September, reaching its greatest level since May’s near four-
year high. Manufacturers widely reported that energy, fuel 
and transportation expenses had risen since September, 
and were key in driving up wider vendor prices. Tariffs also 
added to upward cost pressure, and metals like aluminum

Comment

Chris Williamson, Chief Business Economist at S&P 
Global Market Intelligence: 

“US factories continued to report strong expansion in 
October, sustaining the sector’s best growth spell since 
early 2022. The upturn is also generating jobs at a pace 
not seen for five-and-a- half years, during the surge in 
demand seen during the pandemic. 

“However, growing pains are clearly evident, with 
factories struggling to meet strong demand, especially 
for AI-related goods and equipment, amid supply chain 
disruptions. Delivery delays, often linked to shipping and 
logistics issues, are more widespread than at any time 
since 2022.

“With demand often exceeding supply, producers 
have been increasingly able to pass higher costs 
on to customers, notably for energy-related inputs. 
October consequently saw another leg-up in terms of 
manufacturers’ selling price inflation to the steepest 
since September 2022. Such steep growth in factory 
gate prices will inevitably feed through to consumers 
in the coming months, keeping inflation well above the 
Fed’s target and adding to pressure on policymakers to 
raise interest rates further.”

Faced with another atypical increase in input costs, many 
manufacturers saw little choice but to increase their own 
charges in October. Output price inflation overall picked up 
and was the highest since September 2022. 

The short supply of inputs was another source of inflationary 
pressure. Vendors were widely reported to be struggling to 
keep up with demand, especially for any products related to 
AI data center build outs, whilst simultaneously dealing with 
tariffs and the disruption caused to supply chains by the 
conflict in the Middle East. Subsequently, average lead times 
for the delivery of inputs deteriorated to the greatest degree 
since August 2022 and at a historically elevated pace. 

With production and new order book requirements 
continuing to increase, manufacturers raised their 
purchasing activity. It was the tenth successive month in 
which buying has increased, with growth again marked 
as firms sought to secure stock and present prices 
wherever possible. Manufacturers also added to their input 
inventories, with modest growth recorded for a seventh 
successive month. 

Delays in the delivery of certain inputs added to the pressure 
on plant capacity caused by rising new orders in October. 
Backlogs of work overall increased for an eighth successive 
month, with the latest net increase the steepest since April’s 
near four-year peak. In response, manufacturers added to 
their staffing levels at a historically strong pace. Overall, 
growth in payroll numbers was the steepest in five-and-a-
half years. 

Looking ahead to the next 12 months, manufacturers are 
confident of a rise in production levels, with sentiment 
overall picking up from September to a level broadly in line 
with its trend. Firms signaled positive business expansion 
plans and are hopeful of a pick-up in market demand despite 
some ongoing headwinds related to tariffs and broader 
global economic uncertainty.

Methodology
The S&P Global US Manufacturing PMI® is compiled by S&P Global from responses to 
questionnaires sent to purchasing managers in a panel of around 600 manufacturers. The 
panel is stratified by detailed sector and company workforce size, based on contributions to 
GDP. Data collection began in May 2007.

Survey responses are collected in the second half of each month and indicate the direction 
of change compared to the previous month. A diffusion index is calculated for each survey 
variable. The index is the sum of the percentage of ‘higher’ responses and half the percentage of 
‘unchanged’ responses. The indices vary between 0 and 100, with a reading above 50 indicating an 
overall increase compared to the previous month, and below 50 an overall decrease. The indices 
are then seasonally adjusted. 

The headline figure is the Purchasing Managers’ Index™ (PMI). The PMI is a weighted average of 
the following five indices: New Orders (30%), Output (25%), Employment (20%), Suppliers’ Delivery 
Times (15%) and Stocks of Purchases (10%). For the PMI calculation the Suppliers’ Delivery Times 
Index is inverted so that it moves in a comparable direction to the other indices. 

Underlying survey data are not revised after publication, but seasonal adjustment factors may be 
revised from time to time as appropriate which will affect the seasonally adjusted data series.
