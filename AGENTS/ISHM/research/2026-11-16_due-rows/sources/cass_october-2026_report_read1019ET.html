Freight Cycle Finds a Higher Gear

  October 2026 Year-over-year change 2-year stacked change Month-to-month change Month-to-month change (SA*)
Cass Freight Index - Shipments 1.056 5.9% -2.3% -0.6% 1.7%
Cass Freight Index - Expenditures 4.010 26.5% 26.3% 1.0% 2.9%
Truckload Linehaul Index 156.2 9.9% 13.2% 0.9% NA

 

* SA = seasonally adjusted

Cass Freight Index® - Shipments

  • The shipments component of the Cass Freight Index rose 5.9% y/y in October, accelerating from 1.9% in September aided by an easy comparison to negative trade war effects from late 2025.

  • In SA terms, shipments rose 1.7% m/m in October, the third straight month of recovery.

  • The recovery in freight demand continues, with several factors at work. Slow growth in retail and industrial activity, inventory restocking ahead of the holidays, and to some extent, the IEEPA tariff refunds are all supporting activity.

  • Still, demand will be limited by elevated inflation and rising interest rates squeezing consumers and businesses, and the temporary nature of the IEEPA tariff refunds.

The normal seasonal pattern would put the shipments component of the Cass Freight Index up 3% y/y in November.

Cass Freight Index - Expenditures

The expenditures component of the Cass Freight Index, which measures the total amount spent on freight, accelerated to 27% y/y growth in October to 4.01, up from a 20% gain in September.

The acceleration in the past few months was driven by higher shipments, rates, and fuel surcharges.

  • In SA terms, the index rose 2.9% m/m, the third straight strong m/m increase, with a 1.7% gain in shipments and a 1.2% increase in inferred rates in October.

After declines in 2023, 2024, and 2025, the expenditures component of the Cass Freight Index is on track for a 13% gain in 2026. With normal seasonality, the index would rise 24% y/y in November.

Cass Truckload Linehaul Index®

The Cass Truckload Linehaul Index rose to 156.2 in October, up 0.9% m/m and 9.9% y/y.

  • The sequential increase is in line with the 0.9% YTD average, as both spot and contract rates moved higher in October.

  • With a tight market balance before the holiday season tightens things further, the near-term direction seems higher still.  

  • This index reflects the whole for-hire truckload market, both spot and contract rates.

The Cass Truckload Linehaul Index fell 10% in 2023, another 3.4% in 2024, and turned up to a 1.8% increase in 2025. In 2026, the index is on pace for a 7% increase. 

 

 
 
 

See the Methodology for the Cass Truckload Linehaul Index

 

 

ACT Freight Expectations 

We continue to see the freight demand cycle gaining steam, and with record container imports and intermodal volumes, and growing LTL tonnage, the improvement has broadened out some in recent months.

Some of the recent improvement in y/y comparisons is certainly due to tariff implementations this time last year, and some is one-time IEEPA refunds. But with lean inventories and a resilient economy, we think freight volumes have entered the growth phase of the cycle.

After about a year in the early cycle phase, we see the freight cycle shifting into the mid cycle phase heading into 2027.

About the Author: Tim Denoyer, ACT Research

Tim Denoyer joined ACT Research in 2017 after spending fifteen years in equity research focused primarily on the transportation, machinery, and automotive industries. Tim is a senior analyst leading ACT’s transportation research effort and the primary author of the ACT Freight Forecast, U.S. Rate and Volume OUTLOOK. Research associate, Carter Vieth, who joined ACT in early 2020 after graduating from Indiana University, also contributes to the report. This report provides supply-chain professionals with better visibility on the future of pricing and volume in trucking, the core of the $1.2 trillion U.S. freight transportation industry, including TL, LTL, and intermodal. 

Tim also contributes to ACT’s core Classes 4-8 commercial vehicle (CV) data analysis and forecasting; powertrain development, such as electrification analysis; and used truck valuation and forecasting. Tim has supported or led numerous project-based market studies on behalf of clients in his time with ACT on topics ranging from upcoming emissions and environmental regulations to alternative powertrain cost analyses, to e-commerce and last-mile logistics, to autonomous freight market sizing.  

ACT’s freight research service leverages its expertise in the supply-side economics of transportation and draws upon Tim’s background as an investment analyst, beginning at Prudential and Bear Stearns. Tim was a co-founder of Wolfe Research, one of the leading equity research firms in the investment industry. His experience also includes responsibility for covering the industrial sector of the global equity markets, including with leading investment management company Balyasny Asset Management.
