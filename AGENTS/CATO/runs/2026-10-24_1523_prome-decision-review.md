### PRD2 — Regional-put cost recovery presented as a necessary profit condition (material)

**Witness:** PROME says the cluster 'only breaks even if every bank name falls about 11.8% by expiry.' The script actually solves one stylized scenario: all underlying names fall an equal percentage from their October 23 closes by each different expiry, terminal intrinsic equals the original $4,646.62 premium basis. It includes no pre-expiry time value/volatility. 'Only' and 'every name must' are not warranted.

**Independent arithmetic on the saved dated inputs:** sum 28 contracts; original basis $4,646.62; October 22 marks $1,563.00. Bisection of the script's intrinsic function gives 11.7561431% for original-cost recovery. The same model reaches the old $1,563 mark at 8.6224632%; that is an alternative dated comparison, NOT a current trade target. Counterexample: KRE alone falling 20% by its respective expiries, other names flat, produces $10,851.20 gross intrinsic—above original total cost without every name falling. These checks verify the arithmetic and disprove necessity, not the realism/probability of either scenario.

Reproduction: load `cluster.py` using `runpy.run_path` under `python3 -B`; sum LINES basis/marks/quantity; bisect `intrinsic(d)` on [0,100] for the two targets; for the counterexample sum `max(strike - PX[ticker]*(1-drop[ticker]/100),0)*100*qty` with drop KRE=20 and all others=0. No source edits or live fetch required. The script's rounded 11.76% point returns $4,650.792; ordinary rounding, not an arithmetic failure.

**Other inference limits:** 4.9% is a KRE historical endpoint frequency over overlapping 19-session windows, not the conditional probability that a multi-name/multi-expiry option portfolio profits. The view also supplies 39-session and within-window figures; selecting only the four-week number drops scope. The +160bp 'required rate move' extrapolates a 57-session regression with R² 0.13 and treats a statistical association as a causal requirement. It supports saying the fitted relationship alone does not explain a large payoff; it cannot prove that both paths cannot pay or that a crisis is the sole profitable route. A falling Treasury yield is not a universal bank-put falsifier; bank-specific credit/funding stress may coexist with a Treasury rally. Preserve any owner-registered scenario test, but state its empirical limit.

**Correction/owner:** TERRY labels the original-cost expiry scenario; PROME removes the necessity/profit-probability language and carries weak-fit limits. Current value and attainable pre-expiry P&L require current quotes; no new model or broad backtest commissioned. OCC/OIC independently explains that option premium includes time value and responds to volatility: https://www.optionseducation.org/optionsoverview/options-pricing ; https://prd-web.optionseducation.org/strategies/all-strategies/long-put .

### PRD3 — GLD hedge conclusion tests the wrong side of the portfolio (material)

**Witness:** MIDAS stress sample asks whether GLD rises on stock-market declines/VIX jumps. TERRY §1/§4 says that refutes GLD's purpose as protection for the bearish put book; PROME repeats the binary choice. The sample is relevant to gold as a hedge against losses in long equities. Bearish puts generally benefit from falling underlying prices, all else equal; their loss conditions include equity rallies and time decay. Counting GLD up-days during equity stress does not establish whether GLD offsets those put losses or reduces the combined portfolio's losses.

**Counterexample in logic:** if equities fall, bearish puts gain and GLD falls, those directions can offset at the portfolio level; the same observation counted as failed long-equity protection does not by itself show failed diversification of bearish puts. This is not a claim GLD actually hedges this book. Exact sizes, daily option P&L, regimes and other holdings determine that. The dated observation that both lost on October 22 is relevant to that day, not a universal hedge verdict. Twelve selected stress days and a low-R² rate model do not settle the portfolio question.

**Correction/owner:** MIDAS/TERRY/PROME should say 'gold did not reliably hedge equity declines in this sample; its hedge value for this bearish-option portfolio is unestablished.' Remove 'purpose already broken ⇒ trim branch' unless supported by the actual intended purpose and applicable portfolio evidence. TERRY's mapping of a withdrawn positive lean to 'thesis broken' also needs care: moving to neutral does not by itself establish that every premise is false. No action on GLD is recommended here, and no new portfolio study commissioned. This question can be clarified without changing any held line.
