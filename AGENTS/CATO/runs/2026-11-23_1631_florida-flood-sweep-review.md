## Recommended shape of the existing sweep

Use one compact table, with rows for affected places/property types or useful named examples: hazard/damage, existing financial pressure, incremental cost, who pays, evidence/date, and next observation. Distinguish observed flooding from watch areas. The existing five questions remain useful inputs.

1. **Insurance and household cash flow:** distinguish ordinary property coverage, separate NFIP/private flood coverage, association coverage and unit-owner exposure. Seek actual limits, deductibles, uncovered repair/displacement costs, claims and renewal evidence. Do not turn a flood watch into a forecast of a particular homeowners-premium increase. Florida DFS explains the separate flood-policy channels and NFIP exclusions, including additional living expenses and business interruption ([official FAQ](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers/storm/flood-disaster-faqs), opened November 23). Single-family transmission should not be restricted to future renewals; immediate owner costs are a research question too.
2. **Condos/HOAs:** test whether damage, reserve/repair obligations and financing constraints overlap on the same buildings. Show association bills, reserve position, collections and assessments where available, followed by saleability and price evidence. A hypothetical uninsured common-area repair added to an existing assessment illustrates the mechanism; no such named case was verified here. Keep ordinary HOAs distinct from condominium associations. DBPR identifies SIRS scope by building characteristics, including three habitable stories, and describes subsequent legislative changes ([official FAQ](https://condos.myfloridalicense.com/faqs/), opened November 23). Wider effects on condo demand can be investigated without implying identical legal obligations for every condo. CORAL must verify applicable law/deadlines for the actual building examples.
3. **Home values and demand:** use county/property-type observations and the pre-flood baseline. Listings, concessions, failed transactions and financing availability may answer sooner than a clean estimate of flood-caused price change.
