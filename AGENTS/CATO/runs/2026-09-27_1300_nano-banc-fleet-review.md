## Findings requiring bounded correction

### NB1 — HIGH: scenario arithmetic is promoted to an FDIC asset mark and unconditional ceiling

**Evidence:** REGINALD report §3's cite block attributes approximately $120M to what FDIC expects to lose; its own table separately says a deposit premium and junior-creditor shortfalls increase implied asset loss, while administrative costs decrease it. Synthesis §1 calls the FDIC loss the assets and gives a retained-pool ceiling of 51–56%; CREED §2 and WALTER -006 propagate the interpretation. The FDIC published estimate is **$114M DIF cost**, not an observed $120M CRE mark. [FDIC release](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USFDIC/bulletins/42c8901). The $120M is approximately $114M plus $5.661M of equity on three-day-earlier books, before unknown adjustments. FDIC's discussion of estimated resolution losses also distinguishes asset/liability values and receivership expenses: [FDIC explanation](https://www.fdic.gov/news/speeches/2023/spmay1523.html).

**Counterexample:** the zero-adjustment case is `119.661 / 214.868 = 55.6905%`. A hypothetical $10M deposit premium and $5M expenses, zero acquired-asset discount and no other adjustments gives `(119.661 + 10 - 5) / 214.868 = 58.0175%`. This is a logical counterexample, not an estimate of actual terms. REGINALD itself acknowledges the premium direction. Therefore 56% is not an unconditional upper bound while those terms are unknown. The denominator also mixes September 22 books and closing purchase figures.

**Correction/closure:** call $114M the FDIC estimate and ~$120M/17% the desks' conditional reconciliation, not a measured asset or CRE haircut. Label 51–56% a zero-premium/other-adjustment scenario, with stated assumptions, or omit it from the headline. Preserve the inability to allocate between acquired and retained assets. Do not promise that a P&A alone necessarily supplies every loan schedule, valuation or cost needed. Close when REGINALD's cite block and the synthesis/CREED/WALTER live consumers preserve these distinctions. No substitute loss estimate needed.

### NB4 — MEDIUM: the below-8% debt-yield bucket is not wholly below 1x at every spread

**Evidence:** CREED §4 gives a 6.93% coupon at the low spread, with a 30-year amortizing debt constant of 7.93%, then treats the full <8%/≤8% bucket as below 1x at every spread. Recomputed with monthly amortization: `c = 12*(r/12)/(1-(1+r/12)^(-360))`; at `r=.0693`, `c=.0792729572`. A 7.99% debt-yield loan has `DSCR=.0799/c=1.00791`, above 1x. The coarse bucket cannot establish how much lies in that narrow interval. This is a bounded overstatement; its magnitude is unknown, not necessarily large.

**Correction/closure:** retain at least ~27% of September below 1x throughout the band; the ~51% bucket supports that bound at the 225/275bp amortizing cases, not unconditionally at 175bp. The >half-below-1.25x conclusion still follows conditionally from the supplied September bucket. Carry the full-year 36% count-versus-balance uncertainty into PROME's summary; do not imply 36% of dollar principal. Close CREED and synthesis wording; no new 2027 data acquisition or model build needed.
