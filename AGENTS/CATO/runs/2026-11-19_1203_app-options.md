## Three candidate products

### 1. Research explorer — best first investment

**User experience:** type a company, theme or question, such as “What did we conclude about Cable One, and what would change that conclusion?” See the relevant owner conclusion, dated evidence, contrary argument and subsequent corrections, with the original passages one tap away. Save a few topics and return to them without remembering desk names or filenames.

**First useful slice:** extend the existing research browser to a deliberately selected set of owner records, including NEXUS and TERRY; provide readable results, topic/ticker aliases, source dates and links. Separate current owner assessment from historical studies. Begin with source-backed search; a generated conversational answer is optional later and must cite its supporting passages, preserve disagreement and disclose incomplete coverage. A recently edited file alone does not establish recent evidence.

**Benefit:** reduces repeated archaeology and lets Will use work the system has already paid to produce. His preceding phone-plan question is one ordinary example of the retrieval need, though it does not measure its frequency.

**Effort/limits:** small-to-medium for a bounded local search/reading extension; medium or larger for reliable question answering and private phone delivery. Main difficulty is correction/current-versus-historical handling and collection coverage. No quantitative time-saving claim. Proposed stopping observation: Will can recover a real prior conclusion and its decisive caveat without an agent reconstructing it for him.

### 2. Phone research reader — best for away-from-desk convenience

**User experience:** open a home-screen icon to read recent owner briefs, browse new published research since the last viewed snapshot, and see the existing research calendar and recorded decisions. Mark a passage to discuss when back at the desk. “Newly published” means a document changed, not that the underlying world changed.

**First useful slice:** a private reading-only snapshot of selected existing records with a visible publication time, source evidence dates, and links to full context. Reuse Calendar and Decisions readers. No extra daily summary-writing assignment, approval submission, remote agent control or automatic research commission in the first slice. Offline use shows the last downloaded snapshot.

**Benefit:** gives Will access without opening terminal windows or asking PROME to repeat a briefing. It addresses access; it does not create timely research while desks are offline.

**Effort/limits:** medium. Phone layout is straightforward; private access, selecting safe-to-publish material, publication ownership and dependable updates need an agreed design. The existing local servers are not phone-ready. A remote authenticated snapshot can remain available while the research computer is off, but no new content arrives unless a publisher runs. Hosting choice and cost are unresolved. Proposed stopping observation: Will can read an actual report away from his computer and correctly identify its age.

### 3. Interactive exposure map — most distinctive analytical option

**User experience:** choose an existing theme, such as AI financing, then follow borrower → fund → lender/guarantor relationships. Select a connection to see the dated source, size if known, competing evidence, and the event required for stress to transmit. Missing or inferred connections look different from documented ones.

**First useful slice:** render one existing NEXUS edge map as a dated interactive case, with links to its supporting desk reports. Preserve correction and inference labels. This reuses a completed case; it does not commission another exposure-chain investigation or automatically maintain a fleet-wide knowledge graph.

**Benefit:** fits Will's causal, systems-oriented thinking and makes it easier to distinguish an interesting story from a demonstrated exposure. Possible later question: which missing link prevents an investment conclusion? TERRY retains the tradeability judgment.

**Effort/limits:** medium for one frozen case, large for a maintained multi-theme system. Source upkeep and overconfident connections are the real costs; graph drawing is the easy part. Similar company mentions do not establish a financial relationship, and a plausible link does not establish material loss. Proposed stopping observation: the map helps Will identify the pivotal unknown more readily than the original table.

## Platform feasibility and sequencing

Default suggestion is a browser application that works on desktop and phone. Apple's [home-screen web-app guidance](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/bookmark-a-website-iph42ab2f3a7/ios) confirms the iPhone can open a website as an app. MDN's [offline guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Guides/Offline_and_background_operation) describes cached offline reading and explicitly notes that offline news remains only as fresh as its last update. Checked November 19. This supports the delivery shape, not a specific hosting/security implementation or a device test.

Recommendation: choose research retrieval first; phone delivery can later expose the same collection. Choose the phone reader first if access away from the desk is the principal need. Choose the map if Will wants a visual analytical workspace. Do not commission all three together. A small interactive example using already-written research would be the next proposed step after Will selects a direction; none is built by this discovery assignment.

No remote terminal controller, new autonomous coordinator, new broker aggregator, new forecast study or replacement decision ledger recommended. Those add operational or semantic scope before the reading/retrieval benefit has been established. This is a design judgment, not a finding that those capabilities could never be valuable.
