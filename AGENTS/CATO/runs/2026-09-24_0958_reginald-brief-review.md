## RG2 — Medium: AOCI opt-out is not a finding of capital safety

**Exact sources:** pasted brief §1, “so no bank's capital is in danger”; AOCI report §§1/3 (“reported CET1 is unaffected by any of this today” / “No capital ratio breaks”).

**VERIFIED distinction:** the [FDIC opt-out guidance](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2015/fil15012.html) supports excluding specified unrealized marks from regulatory capital. It does not exempt realized losses or establish capital adequacy under funding and credit stress. Regulators expressly explain that selling AFS securities can realize losses and reduce regulatory capital, including when sales meet liquidity needs ([joint agencies' discussion, Federal Register p.64036](https://www.fdic.gov/news/board-matters/2023/2023-07-27-notice-dis-a-fr.pdf)). The latter passage was available in indexed primary-source search text; direct PDF fetch returned 403. No claim of reading the whole rule or of that proposal becoming final.

**Counterexample:** a bank opts out, then realizes a securities loss to meet withdrawals. The unrealized-loss opt-out does not preserve the capital consumed by that realized loss. This is a logical scenario, not a prediction that any named bank will do so. REGINALD's own report already lists sales at losses as a watch item.

**Correction / close when:** replace the safety assurance with “These unrealized marks do not directly reduce reported CET1 under the current opt-outs; the modeled scenario alone does not establish an immediate regulatory-capital breach.” Retain economic-value, liquidity, realized-loss and credit risks without implying observed distress. No new full solvency audit is required to make the summary accurate.

**Related timing limit:** WAL's management expectation to exceed $100B spot assets by end-Q1 2027 is not the date it meets the applicable four-quarter-average test. WAL KB-198 records average assets $95.325B at Q2 and an illustrative later crossing; the management quotation remains B2 in KB-196. Proposed AOCI inclusion also remains contingent on the rule. Preserve those qualifications; REGINALD's “when, not an if” wording goes beyond a still-proposed rule. This review does not determine future applicability or reverify the original webcast.
