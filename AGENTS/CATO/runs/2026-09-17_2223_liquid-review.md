## 1. High — the FRED “unsupported/provisional” diagnosis is refuted by the documented source

**Evidence:** `workbook/KB.tsv:134` (KB-LIQ-133); September 17 report §7; STATUS's eight-defect narrative; the original and amendment packets to WALTER, now under WALTER's `inbox/processed/`.

LIQUID observes T10YIE/T5YIFR dated September 17 while FRED DGS/DFII pages stop at September 16. It concludes the new cells postdate their own inputs and are unsupported/provisional. The observation about different publication dates can be true while the conclusion is false.

[FRED T10YIE documentation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10YIE) and [T5YIFR documentation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T5YIFR) say the Treasury inputs have been obtained **directly from Treasury since June 21, 2019**. These derived series need not wait for the separate FRED component pages.

The September 17 rows of [Treasury's nominal table](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026) and [real table](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_real_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026) provide:

| Maturity | Nominal | Real | Breakeven |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 years | 4.78% | 2.46% | 2.32% |
| 10 years | 4.94% | 2.61% | 2.33% |

Using FRED's published forward formula, `100 * (((1.0233**10 / 1.0232**5)**0.2) - 1) = 2.34000098%`, which rounds to **2.34%**. Both supposedly unsupported cells reproduce. The repeated 2.33 was neither proof of carry-forward nor proof of a provisional observation. The replacement “structural” tell also fails if it mistakes another dissemination channel for the producer's actual dependency.

**Correction:** withdraw the unsupported/provisional conclusion and replace it with: “FRED series publish on different schedules; these September 17 spread observations reproduce from Treasury's same-date inputs.” Retain cross-date guards for calculations that actually combine mismatched dates. Follow the declared producer lineage before rejecting a newer datum. LIQUID should correct its KB/report and already-delivered WALTER claims; CATO has not sent those corrections. This review does not establish the precise availability timestamp of Treasury's pages during LIQUID's earlier request or certify unrelated derived series.

## 2. High — repaired row objects lose their dates and warnings at rendering

**Evidence:** `scripts/boot.py:552` (price date capture), `:821`–`:843` (renderer). See the accompanying offline probe and output.

The September 17 repair puts `asof` into each price row, but `render()` displays it only inside `if verbose and r['trend']`. Price rows have no trend. Consequently, **neither default nor verbose output displays their observation dates** when the batch shares one date. The probe passes September 16 dates to all seven rows; neither rendering contains that date.

Worse, the renderer suppresses non-headline white rows alongside green ones. White now means both “reference only” and “ungradeable,” so the new fail-closed warnings disappear. With a stale IORB basis, the generated 079 ARM row correctly says UNGRADEABLE, but the ordinary rendering hides it. With all price dates missing, the price-only credit and domestic dashboards print **all clear** despite the rows being DATE UNVERIFIED. This is an isolated consumer test, not a claim that an actual full live boot printed that exact dashboard combination.

**Correction:** print `asof` independently of trend/verbosity; preserve observation date and basis on every displayed number. Separate data validity from alert severity and reference-only display status. A required ungradeable row must remain visible and must prevent an all-clear summary. Test the final text a user receives, not just the helper's returned row.

## 3. High — unattended HY counting is not the canonical published-observation count

**Evidence:** `scripts/hy_oas_watch.py:75`–`:103`, `:244`–`:259`; canonical `workbook/KILL_MEMO_HY_OAS_260.md:47` and conventions at `:54`–`:60`.

The watcher fetches two observations but consumes only `obs[0]`. Its streak advances whenever the newest observation date differs from the previously polled date. Counterexample: published values **259 Friday, 270 Monday, 259 Tuesday**; skip Monday's watcher run. Tuesday falsely reports two consecutive sub-260 observations because the intervening reset was never consumed. A missed run on a shared operator machine is an ordinary operating case.

The state also lacks a terminal fired flag. After a valid fire, a recovery and another pair of sub-260 observations produce another fire, contrary to the approved terminal/no-recount convention. Calling it again on the same fired observation creates another local fired message; the delivery function has separate same-date deduplication, so that probe is **not** evidence of duplicate external delivery. New-date re-fires remain possible. The message also hard-codes BROCK as contested as of August 23, although the canonical memo explicitly supersedes that state on August 28.

**Correction:** consume all unseen published observations in order, preserve the first-published values/count history, distinguish missing publications from missed polling, persist the terminal result, and reference the current arbiter disposition rather than a frozen string. If history cannot be recovered, report unknown instead of joining two sampled endpoints. The shared latest-revised fetch limitation is already acknowledged by LIQUID/DAEDALUS; it is not a newly discovered repair here.

The watcher's existing selftest passes **all eight checks** while these counterexamples fail its contract. No actual historical false kill or current live gate fire is claimed.

## 4. Medium — the “every composite uses one helper” completion claim is false

**Evidence:** KB-LIQ-132 (`KB.tsv:133`); `boot.py:289`–`:302`, `:442`–`:445`, `:490`–`:500`.

Two other unprotected composites remain. SOFR99−SOFR computes across dates while printing only the percentile date. ## 7. Medium — analytical certainty exceeds what the measurements identify

**Credit discrimination:** KB-LIQ-127 and the REGINALD packet correctly disclose unresolved index composition and sector attribution, but their headlines say duration is refuted/proven by isolation. The table measures changes in **OAS**, not duration-adjusted bond total returns. Longer duration translates a yield change into greater price sensitivity; it does not require IG OAS to widen more than CCC OAS. The ladder supports “spread widening concentrated in CCC while higher-quality OAS tightened.” It does not isolate credit deterioration from composition/liquidity/option effects or rule out rates as an economic cause. [FRED's CCC methodology](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A3HYC) describes market-cap-weighted constituent OAS versus the spot Treasury curve.

**Base rates:** the effort to compare changes with history is useful. But a 17% unconditional historical frequency does not establish “almost no information,” and neither rarity nor a percentile establishes predictive usefulness. The 15-observation rolling windows overlap. FRED's accessible history is now limited to three years; “series max” must be “maximum in the available three-year sample.” Preserve both level and dispersion, with their respective questions, rather than “never quote the level.”

Reproduction limit: the two existing `fred_BAMLH0A3HYC_1400.json` / `fred_BAMLH0A2HYB_1400.json` payloads, cached at 01:49:02/03 UTC September 18, contain 787 aligned observations through September 16. Integer-bp differences produce 772 overlapping 15-observation changes: CCC ≥45 in 131 (16.97%); CCC−B ≥49 in 56 (7.25%). This does **not** reproduce the report's n=771 and gap 51/771 (6.6%). Different filtering/tie/window choices may explain it; CATO has not established LIQUID's exact original procedure. File the actual calculation, date-selection rule, tie convention and input digest before treating the exact percentile as independently certified. The broad “not rare enough by itself to establish a crisis” judgment is not overturned by this small discrepancy.

**TIC:** report §4 says other officials absorbed China's supply. KB-LIQ-122 already accepts that reported country/holder class does not identify beneficial ownership or counterparties. July's aggregate official purchases plus China sales do not establish who bought whose supply. Correct to “the reported aggregate official coupon bucket was positive while China-reported holdings had net sales.” This inference was inherited from ZHAO's packet, but LIQUID reasserted it after its own earlier cap. No recomputation of the July TIC totals was performed here.
