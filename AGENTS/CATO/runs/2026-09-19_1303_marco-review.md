## Findings and suggested acceptance conditions

### F1 — High: the adjustment does not establish that demand weakness is absent

**Sources:** `AGENTS/MARCO/CLAUDE.md:190,198,202`; `STATUS.md:26,82`; `thesis/PREDICTIONS.tsv`, MAR-24 September 19 note; both September 19 CARL/REGINALD airport packets, especially their opening, “only genuinely negative leg” and unconditional “threshold-true and mechanism-false” instruction.

Ex-Spirit growth measures traffic on surviving carriers. It is not traffic with the effect of liquidation removed. Passengers who would have flown Spirit can switch to another carrier, raising the adjusted series even while aggregate passenger demand falls. Counterexample: last year 100 passengers = 30 Spirit + 70 others; this year 90, all on others. Raw traffic is −10%, ex-Spirit is +28.6%. Those observations are consistent with demand declining to 90; they are also consistent with demand staying at 100 and capacity limiting realised traffic to 90. The carrier split alone cannot distinguish the cases.

Likewise, MIA having no direct Spirit service removes that direct exposure, not every supply, carrier-substitution, airport-substitution or passenger-composition effect. BTS enplanements are not a count of Florida visitors. A passenger series need not identify demand separately from available service. BTS distinguishes passenger traffic from available capacity in its [T-100 documentation](https://www.transtats.bts.gov/DatabaseInfo.asp?QO_VQ=EED).

**Consequence:** MARCO correctly challenged the inference “raw decline proves boycott,” then overcorrected to “positive survivors prove no demand withdrawal.” The blanket packet instruction could suppress a real future deterioration. The current MAR-24 note even decides the mechanism of unpublished Q3 observations in advance.

**Suggested fix:** retain the approved adjustment as an ex-Spirit traffic screen, with a causal limit that travels beside it: “raw weakness is confounded by liquidation; these data do not identify the demand contribution.” Describe MIA as free of direct Spirit exposure. Keep raw forecast scoring separate and assess its mechanism on the resolving evidence; do not preassign mechanism-REFUTED to every future fire. Acceptance: the 100/30/70→90 counterexample is not labelled proof of absent demand weakness, and a future all-three headline is not automatically declared mechanism-false without its own vintage and carrier evidence.

### F4 — Medium: “84% of seats backfilled” is the wrong measure and stale vintage

It is an accounting offset in enplanements, not seats supplied or a causal measurement of backfill. At the independently fetched September 19 vintage, MCO May's total decline is **5,967**, making that same passenger-offset calculation **97.84%**. For June, the offset is **35.56%** (170,307 total decline / 264,278 Spirit decline). These are different observation months, not alternative estimates of one capacity fraction.

**Suggested fix:** withdraw “84% seats backfilled” from current charter/scratch and issue a dated correction to the two recipient artifacts through the authorized owner route. If retaining history, label it May passenger-offset accounting at the August 21 vintage. Leave seat-capacity replacement unsized until an actual capacity series is measured. Acceptance: every cited replacement percentage names its unit, observation month, vintage and arithmetic; none of these passenger offsets is represented as seats.

### F6 — Medium: ID-01 compares different event windows and calls an in-sample frequency a false-positive rate
