# RP-OTT-2.3: Immigration-Employment-Auto Payment Transmission Chain

## Objective
Map the transmission mechanism from immigration enforcement/fear → employment loss → auto loan distress, with focus on timing and leading indicators.

## Research Questions

1. **Employment Collapse in Vehicle-Dependent Industries**
   - MARCO found: Construction -93%, Transportation -96% in Latino immigrant employment
   - Both sectors = vehicle-dependent for work
   - What's the lag from job loss → first missed auto payment → default?
   - Source: BLS QCEW by industry + demographic, academic research on employment-default timing

2. **Geographic Correlation**
   - Map: Counties with highest Latino employment decline → subprime auto DQ rates
   - Does DQ spike follow employment decline with predictable lag?
   - Key test areas: TX border counties, CA Central Valley, AZ Maricopa
   - Source: BLS county-level data, ABS geographic performance data

3. **Remittance Behavior as Leading Indicator**
   - Central America remittances UP 26% = liquidation mode (selling assets, sending money home)
   - Mexico remittances DOWN 5% = already left or underground
   - **Hypothesis**: CA remittance surge precedes loan payoffs/sales; TX remittance decline precedes abandonment
   - Can we correlate remittance trends with auto loan prepayment/default by state?
   - Source: Banxico, World Bank remittance data, Fed remittance research

4. **Fear Premium — Which Behavior Dominates?**
   - Option A: Prioritize car payment (need mobility to flee/relocate) → lower DQ
   - Option B: Stop paying entirely (cutting financial ties, going underground) → abandonment
   - Option C: Liquidate (sell car, pay off loan, take cash) → elevated prepayments
   - Which behavior is dominating? Does it vary by geography/enforcement intensity?
   - Source: Survey data, legal aid organizations, community reporting

5. **Cash Economy Shift**
   - If immigrants shift to cash employment (off-books), income appears to disappear
   - But they may still have capacity to pay
   - Are we seeing "strategic default" where borrowers can pay but won't?
   - Source: Academic research on informal economy, immigrant financial behavior studies

6. **Timing Analysis**
   - When did Trump 2.0 enforcement rhetoric intensify? (Late 2025)
   - When did MARCO see employment/remittance shifts? (H2 2025)
   - When should auto DQ spike? (Q1-Q2 2026 if 3-6 month lag)
   - Are we in the window now?
   - Source: Timeline construction from MARCO data + auto performance data

## Output Requested
- Employment-to-default timing estimates (academic/industry research)
- Geographic correlation data if available
- Remittance-to-auto behavior hypothesis testing
- Assessment of which "fear response" (pay, abandon, liquidate) is dominating
- Timeline projection for when immigration stress should appear in auto data

## Cross-References
- MARCO: Employment data, remittance data, enforcement geography
- LABOR: Latino employment tracking
- CARL: Consumer payment hierarchy

## Why This Matters
This is **LABOR → CARL → OTTO in real time**, but with a specific demographic channel. If we can establish the transmission timing, we can:
1. Predict which ABS vintages/tranches get hit hardest
2. Identify geographic concentrations before losses materialize
3. Understand whether this is a recoverable default wave or permanent collateral loss

## Key Thesis
**The Invisible Transmission**: Aggregate employment data misses the immigrant channel because much of it is informal/cash. Remittances are a better signal. When Central American remittances peak and reverse, that's the liquidation complete → abandonment begins signal.
