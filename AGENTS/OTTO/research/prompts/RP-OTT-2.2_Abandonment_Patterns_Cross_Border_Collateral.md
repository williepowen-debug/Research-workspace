# RP-OTT-2.2: Abandonment Patterns & Cross-Border Collateral Risk

## Objective
Investigate whether subprime auto is experiencing elevated "skip" and abandonment rates (vs traditional default), and assess the cross-border collateral enforcement problem.

## Research Questions

1. **Abandonment vs Default — Operational Difference**
   - Traditional default: Borrower stops paying → repo → auction → recovery
   - Abandonment/Skip: Borrower disappears → car disappears → no recovery
   - What are current skip rates in subprime auto? Trending?
   - How do servicers classify/report these differently?
   - Source: Servicer data, repo company reports, ABS trustee reports

2. **Recovery Rate Trends**
   - Are recovery rates declining faster than vehicle depreciation explains?
   - Recovery on "located" repos vs "skip" repos — what's the delta?
   - Are lenders writing off more loans as "unrecoverable"?
   - Source: ABS performance reports, Manheim data, lender earnings calls

3. **Cross-Border Lien Enforcement**
   - Can a U.S. lien be enforced if vehicle crosses to Mexico?
   - What legal mechanisms exist? (Practically: none)
   - Are there reports of vehicles being driven south and not returned?
   - What would this look like in servicer data? (Skip + $0 recovery)
   - Source: Repo industry publications, border state AG reports, legal analysis

4. **Repo Industry Intelligence**
   - Repo companies have ground-level visibility
   - Are they reporting higher skip rates in border states?
   - Any reports of vehicles "last seen heading south"?
   - What's the cost/feasibility of cross-border recovery?
   - Source: American Recovery Association, repo industry trade publications, forwarding company data

5. **GPS/Telematics Evidence**
   - Many subprime lenders require GPS trackers
   - Do any report vehicles crossing the border?
   - Starter-interrupt devices — are lenders disabling cars pre-emptively?
   - Source: Telematics company reports, state AG investigations into starter-interrupt

6. **Geographic Pattern in Skip Rates**
   - Are skip rates elevated in TX, AZ, CA border regions vs national?
   - Does skip rate correlate with immigration enforcement intensity?
   - Source: Lender/servicer geographic breakdowns, state-level data

## Output Requested
- Current skip rate data for subprime auto (if available)
- Recovery rate trends with breakdown by recovery type
- Legal analysis of cross-border collateral enforcement
- Any repo industry reporting on border-related skip patterns
- Geographic distribution of skip/abandonment if available

## Cross-References

- OTTO: Overall loss severity, recovery rates by vintage

## Why This Matters
Traditional loss models assume you can repo and sell the car. If the car is in Mexico, recovery = $0. A spike in abandonments (not defaults) would explain loss severity exceeding expectations. This is a **model blind spot** — ABS investors may not be pricing this risk.

## Key Hypothesis
**The "Invisible Exit"**: Immigrants facing deportation risk or employment collapse don't default — they leave. The loan doesn't go 90 DPD, it goes from current to "skip" with no recovery. This breaks traditional roll-rate models.
