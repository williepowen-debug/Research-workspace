# Western Alliance Bancorporation: Liquidity & FHLB Position Analysis

## Executive Summary

Western Alliance (WAL) has meaningfully **reduced its reliance on FHLB borrowings** through 2025, driven by exceptional deposit growth of $10.8 billion (+16.3% YoY) that has dramatically improved its funding profile. Total borrowings stood at **$5.24 billion** at year-end 2025, down from $5.57 billion a year earlier, but the composition has shifted significantly toward short-term instruments and away from longer-dated FHLB advances. The company's own Q4 2025 earnings presentation explicitly states that the "cost of liability funding decreased 18 bps primarily due to **reduced reliance on FHLB borrowings**".[^1][^2]

***

Borrowings are volatile quarter-to-quarter, reflecting WAL's use of short-term wholesale funding to manage timing mismatches between loan originations and deposit flows. The **Q3-25 trough at $3.86B** coincided with a massive $6.1B deposit surge, while Q4 saw borrowings rebound to $5.24B as deposits experienced seasonal mortgage-related outflows of ~$3.1B.[^3][^2]

## FHLB Advances: What We Know

### Direct Disclosure from Earnings Materials

WAL does **not break out FHLB advances as a separate line item** in its quarterly earnings presentation or press release. However, several critical disclosures provide strong indirect evidence:

1. **"Cost of liability funding decreased 18 bps primarily due to reduced reliance on FHLB borrowings"** — Q4 2025 earnings presentation, slide 9[^1]
2. **Borrowing interest expense fell from $284.0M (FY2024) to $257.5M (FY2025)**, a 9.3% decline, even as the bank's balance sheet grew 15%[^2]
3. **Insurance expense dropped from $164.8M (FY2024) to $117.5M (FY2025)**, driven by "a reduction in brokered deposit levels and FDIC special assessment charges" — corroborating the shift away from both brokered deposits and FHLB funding toward organic deposits[^1]
4. **Long-term borrowings decreased $1.0B YoY** while short-term borrowings increased $696M — consistent with letting longer-dated FHLB advances mature without full replacement[^2]

### Q4 2025 Borrowing Composition Shift

The Q4 2025 press release provides this key breakdown:[^2]

- **Linked-quarter (vs Q3-25):** Borrowings up $1.38B, driven by **+$2.9B in short-term borrowings** partially offset by **-$1.5B in long-term borrowings**
- **Year-over-year (vs Q4-24):** Borrowings down $333M, reflecting **-$1.0B in long-term borrowings** partially offset by **+$696M in short-term borrowings**

This pattern — declining long-term, rising short-term — is consistent with WAL actively allowing FHLB term advances to roll off and replacing residual funding needs with shorter-duration instruments (likely overnight Fed Funds purchased, repo, or short-dated FHLB overnight advances).

### Industry Context: FHLB Usage Trend

In Q4 2025, the Federal Reserve told examiners not to discourage banks from using FHLB advances for liquidity management. Some regionals increased FHLB borrowings in Q4 (e.g., Old National +10.1% to $6.2B). However, WAL appears to have been moving in the **opposite direction** — deleveraging FHLB exposure as deposit growth reduced the need for wholesale funding.[^4]

### Historical Context

WAL dramatically reduced FHLB borrowings by $6.1B in Q2 2023 during the post-SVB recovery period as deposits stabilized. The 2024-2025 trajectory has continued this theme of replacing wholesale funding with core deposits.[^5]

## Deposit Flow Dynamics

### Full-Year 2025 Deposit Growth: $10.8B (+16.3%)

WAL's deposit franchise substantially strengthened through 2025:[^6][^7]

| Deposit Type | Q4-24 | Q4-25 | Change | % of Total (Q4-25) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-interest bearing | $18,846M | $24,353M | +$5,507M | 31.5% |
| Interest-bearing demand | $15,878M | $18,416M | +$2,538M | 23.9% |
| Savings & MMA | $21,208M | $24,586M | +$3,378M | 31.9% |
| Certificates of deposit | $10,409M | $9,804M | -$605M | 12.7% |
| **Total** | **$66,341M** | **$77,159M** | **+$10,818M** | **100%** |

*Source: WAL Q4 2025 Press Release*[^2]

### Key Deposit Quality Observations

- **Non-interest bearing deposits grew $5.5B YoY** to $24.4B, now 31.5% of total deposits — up from 28.4% a year ago. This is a significant improvement in funding quality.[^2]
- **CDs declined $605M**, consistent with the strategy to reduce higher-cost funding[^2]
- **32% of total deposits are non-interest bearing**, with approximately 39% of those carrying no ECR obligations[^1]
- Deposit growth sources: **Regional Banking** (including $500M from Innovation Banking in Q4), **Specialty Escrow Services** (+$850M in Q4), and **HOA** (+$400M in Q4)[^3]
- **Brokered deposits are being actively reduced**, evidenced by the $47.3M decline in insurance expense (which includes FDIC assessments on brokered deposits)[^1]

### KBRA Assessment

KBRA affirmed WAL's ratings in May 2025, noting that "WAL's $66 billion of total deposits at YE24 were up $11 billion from the prior year; ~3x the $3.4 billion year-over-year increase in loans HFI." KBRA highlighted the "considerably larger, and what we consider to be more durable deposit base that incorporates substantially lower uninsured/uncollateralized balances" alongside "enhanced earning asset liquidity".[^8]

## Liquidity Position Assessment

### Balance Sheet Liquidity Metrics

| Metric | Q4-24 | Q3-25 | Q4-25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Securities + Cash | $19.2B | $24.6B | $24.0B |
| Securities + Cash / Total Assets | 23.7% | 27.0% | 25.9% |
| HFI Loan-to-Deposit Ratio | 80.9% | 73.3% | 76.0% |
| Total Borrowings / Total Assets | 6.9% | 4.2% | 5.6% |
| Cash & Due from Banks | $4.1B | $5.8B | $3.6B |

*Source: WAL Q4 2025 Balance Sheet*[^1][^2]

### Assessment

WAL's liquidity position is **meaningfully stronger** than a year ago:

- **Securities + Cash at $24.0B** (25.9% of assets), up $4.8B or 25% YoY, providing substantial liquidity buffer[^1]
- **Loan-to-deposit ratio improved to 76.0%** from 80.9% a year ago, reflecting deposit growth outpacing loan growth[^2]
- **Borrowings/total assets at 5.6%** — moderate and lower than the 6.9% a year ago[^1]
- The **$5.8B cash position at Q3-25** was likely elevated by the massive deposit surge; Q4 cash at $3.6B is more normalized[^1]
- WAL estimates its interest rate sensitivity as modestly asset-sensitive, with -100 bps reducing NII by 2.7% but EaR only 0.5% (after accounting for ECR deposit cost relief and mortgage banking offsets)[^1]

## FFIEC Call Report Data Availability

### Current Status

The **Q4 2025 Call Report (as of December 31, 2025) has likely not yet been filed** with the FFIEC as of mid-February 2026. Call Reports are typically due 30-45 days after quarter-end, placing the Q4-25 filing deadline around late January to mid-February. The most recent Call Report available on the FFIEC CDR would be the **Q3 2025 (September 30, 2025)** filing.[^9]

### Where to Check

- **FFIEC CDR:** [https://cdr.ffiec.gov](https://cdr.ffiec.gov) — Search for Western Alliance Bank (RSSD: 3138146, Certificate: 57512)[^9]

The Q3 2025 Call Report (last available on iBanknet, updated October 30, 2025) would show the FHLB advance breakdown as of September 30, 2025, when total borrowings were at their lowest point of $3.86B.[^10]

## 2026 Outlook

Management's 2026 guidance calls for:[^3][^1]

- **Loan growth: +$6B** (up from $5B in 2025)
- **Deposit growth: +$8B** (vs $10.8B in 2025)
- **NII growth: +11% to 14%**, supported by ongoing funding cost relief and loan growth
- **CET1 target: ~11%** (flat to current)
- **Assumes 2 rate cuts of 25 bps each**
- **Deposit costs expected to decline further** as rate cuts flow through ECR-related balances, though offset by ongoing growth investments[^1]

The $8B deposit growth target exceeds the $6B loan growth target, suggesting **continued improvement in the loan-to-deposit ratio** and **further potential to reduce wholesale funding reliance** including FHLB advances through 2026.[^3][^1]

## Analytical Takeaways

1. **FHLB Deleveraging is Real but Lumpy:** WAL has been systematically reducing FHLB reliance since the 2023 crisis, but quarter-to-quarter borrowing levels fluctuate significantly based on deposit seasonality (especially mortgage warehouse flows).

2. **Deposit Franchise Transformation:** The shift from 28.4% to 31.5% NIB deposits, combined with $605M in CD runoff and declining brokered deposit levels, represents meaningful improvement in core funding quality.

3. **Short-Term Borrowing Substitution:** The structural shift from long-term to short-term borrowings suggests WAL is using more flexible (and currently cheaper) funding instruments rather than locking in term FHLB advances. This improves flexibility but introduces refinancing risk.

4. **The Q4-25 Call Report (Schedule RC-M) will be the key disclosure** to watch for exact FHLB advance levels — likely available on the FFIEC CDR by late February/early March 2026. The Q3-25 Call Report showing the $3.86B total borrowings quarter would show the lowest FHLB levels.

5. **2026 trajectory favors further FHLB reduction** given deposit growth guidance ($8B) substantially exceeding loan growth guidance ($6B), though the pace of any reduction will depend on the interest rate environment and mortgage-related deposit seasonality.
