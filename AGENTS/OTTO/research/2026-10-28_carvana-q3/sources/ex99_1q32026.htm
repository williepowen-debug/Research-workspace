Dear Shareholders,
The third quarter was another excellent quarter for Carvana.
It was another big step on our path to selling 3 million cars at 13.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin per year.
Starting with the strides we are making toward 3 million: In the quarter, we sold 217,000 units - our first quarter over 200,000.
That’s up just under 40% from a year ago, and with eight straight quarters of growth in that same range, we are now double the size we were just two years ago.
For context, the industry was once again approximately flat compared to last year. This makes clear that our growth is coming from market share gains as customers continue to migrate to Carvana because of the experience we offer, the selection we provide, and the value we deliver.
We are the fastest-growing automotive retailer for deep, sustainable reasons.
Moving to the margins we are generating: in the third quarter we produced just under $500 million of Net income and $800 million of Adjusted EBITDA resulting in a 6% Net income margin and a 10% Adjusted EBITDA margin. We achieved these results despite a backdrop of rapid increases in both interest rates and fuel prices.
For the 10th quarter in a row, our margins are about 2x that of the industry average. These results reflect a business built differently from the ground up.
We have a business model that benefits from a different supply chain, a different technology stack, and greater vertical integration, creating a fundamentally different customer experience and the economics that come with it.
We are the most profitable automotive retailer for deep, sustainable reasons.
Over the last two years, we have driven up our Net Promoter Score by 9 points and provided our customers with twice as much selection, delivery times that are 2 days faster, lower interest rates on their loans relative to benchmarks, and a continually simplifying process.
The moat is widening.
And we’re nowhere near done improving the machine. We still see opportunity in every step of the customer experience and in every line item of our financial statements.
Our current retail unit run rate puts us at a scale of 870,000 units per year. Excitingly, this is about 2% share of the used market and about 1.5% of the market overall. We have built a powerful machine and we are early in the process of realizing its potential.

A Note on 2027 Reporting
Over the past several years, we have focused our reporting and targets on non-GAAP financial metrics, including Non-GAAP GPU, Non-GAAP SG&A expense, and Adjusted EBITDA, which we believe have provided the clearest illustration of our business performance. As we marched toward industry-leading profitability, these metrics served as useful markers of our progress.
At our current scale and profitability, GAAP profitability metrics, including Operating income and Net income, have become a simpler and clearer framing for our targets and execution. Accordingly, in 2027, we plan to shift our primary reporting focus from our historical Non-GAAP metrics to their GAAP counterparts, including Total GPU and its components, SG&A expense, Operating income, and Net income.
In line with our focus on GAAP metrics, in 2027 we will convert our mid-term goal to selling 3 million retail units annually at a 12.5% Operating margin between 2030 and 2035. We view this margin target as equivalent, on a GAAP basis, to the 13.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin target we introduced in early 2025.
When we simplify our financial metrics in 2027, we will also simplify our approach to outlook. Now that we are solidly in our long-term profitable growth phase, we will align our financial outlook more closely with the mid-term and long-term goals that guide our strategy. Beginning with our Q4 2026 earnings call, we do not plan to provide quarterly or annual financial outlooks. Instead, we will focus our forward-looking communication on our longer-term goals and our progress toward achieving them.

Third Quarter Results
Q3 2026 was another record-setting quarter for Carvana, and again demonstrated the efficiency and scalability of our vertically integrated model. In Q3, we set new company records in retail units sold, revenue, gross profit, GAAP Operating income, and Adjusted EBITDA.
Retail units sold reached a new all-time high of 217,385, increasing 39% year-over-year, while the broader industry was approximately flat. We again paired industry-leading growth with industry-leading profitability, generating Net income margin of 6.0% and Adjusted EBITDA margin of 10.0%.

GAAP and Non-GAAP Total GPU decreased by $365 and $410 year-over-year, respectively. The decreases were primarily driven by our decision to give back to customers in the form of lower rates on their loans relative to benchmark rates as well as lower wholesale GPU as we scaled retail units sold faster than wholesale marketplace gross profit, partially offset by higher spreads between retail and wholesale prices.
Our overall results in Q3 were also impacted by elevated fuel prices and rapidly rising benchmark interest rates. In total, we estimate that overall unit economics were negatively impacted by ~$200 per unit from these two factors.
We continue to see significant opportunities to improve efficiency, deliver more value to customers, and grow into our existing infrastructure. These opportunities support continued progress toward our mid-term and long-term goals.

Bigger Machine, Better Machine
Q3 2026 marks our eighth consecutive quarter of approximately 40% retail unit growth. A look back to Q3 2024 underscores our scalability, the benefits of our infrastructure investments, and the durability of our unit economics across a range of macroeconomic and industry conditions.
Over the last 2 years, we have focused on balancing growth, customer experiences, and unit economics. That focus has resulted in doubling the scale of our business since Q3 2024 while also delivering better customer experiences and consistent unit economics, excluding our wholesale marketplace, which does not scale at the same rate as our retail offering.
It is worth noting that the comparison above understates the leverage and fundamental gains achieved over these periods for three reasons:
1.Q3 2024 was positively impacted by ~$250 per unit due to loan sale timing and falling benchmark rates
2.Q3 2026 was negatively impacted by ~$200 per unit due to higher fuel prices and rising benchmark rates
3.We have given back to consumers in multiple ways, including lowering interest rates on customer loans by over 100 basis points relative to benchmark rates.
This combination of growth, better customer experiences, and strong unit economics, all realized in a dynamic environment, reflects the power of our platform and the opportunities we see to keep building on it.

Building the Machine
The foundation of our business is the platform that we developed over the last 14 years: the shared infrastructure, technology, and capabilities that support our customer experience and business economics. This platform continuously evolves to support our business.
Growth strengthens the machine by creating more opportunities to improve and apply these platform capabilities. A stronger machine, in turn, helps us serve more customers, operate more effectively, and support new offerings with less incremental time and investment, which drives further growth.
An example of this is our recent introduction of new vehicles on Carvana.com from our seven Stellantis franchise dealerships. Much of the infrastructure required to bring a new category of vehicles to customers already existed: a nationwide customer base, national merchandising, personalized financing, digital checkout, customer support, fulfillment infrastructure, and title and registration workflows. Rather than create the capabilities necessary to support new car transactions from the ground up, we
3

applied the capabilities we had already built and refined, significantly reducing the runway, complexity, and investment required to launch this offering, while also enhancing the quality.
This example cements the principle: growth strengthens our machine and a stronger machine enables more growth and opportunity.

Expanding Production Capacity
In Q3, we made continued progress expanding production capacity across our nationwide footprint. In the quarter, we integrated retail production capabilities at two ADESA locations, bringing our total integrated sites to 21. We also began construction on two additional full buildouts at ADESA locations. This brings the total number of full buildout sites currently under construction to three, all of which we expect to begin production in 2027.
Our current footprint gives us fully built out annual capacity for approximately 1.5 million retail units, with real estate to support annual retail production of 3 million retail units.

Looking forward, we will continue investing in our infrastructure ahead of future growth. By building excess reconditioning capacity, we expect to benefit from near-term operational flexibility while laying a clear foundation to support growth over the medium and long term.

The above measures are supplemental measures of operating performance that do not represent and should not be considered an alternative to net income, gross profit, or SG&A expenses, as determined by GAAP.

Adjusted EBITDA is defined as net income plus income tax provision (benefit), interest expense, net, other operating expense, net, other expense, net, depreciation and amortization expense in cost of sales and SG&A expenses, share-based compensation expense in cost of sales and SG&A expenses, and loss on debt extinguishment, minus revenue related to our Warrants. Adjusted EBITDA margin is Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of total revenues. Adjusted EBITDA per unit is defined as Adjusted EBITDA divided by retail vehicle unit sales.

Gross profit, non-GAAP, Retail gross profit, non-GAAP, Wholesale gross profit, non-GAAP, and Other gross profit, non-GAAP are defined as the respective GAAP gross profits plus depreciation and amortization expense in cost of sales and share-based compensation expense in cost of sales, minus revenue related to our Warrants. Total gross profit per retail unit, non-GAAP, Retail gross profit per retail unit, non-GAAP, Wholesale gross profit per retail unit, non-GAAP, and Other gross profit per retail unit, non-GAAP are the respective gross profits, non-GAAP divided by retail vehicle unit sales.

SG&A expenses, non-GAAP is defined as GAAP SG&A expenses minus depreciation and amortization expense in SG&A expenses and share-based compensation expense in SG&A expenses. Total SG&A expenses per retail unit, non-GAAP is SG&A expenses, non-GAAP divided by retail vehicle unit sales.

We use these non-GAAP measures to measure the operating performance of our business as a whole and relative to our total revenues and retail vehicle unit sales. We believe that these metrics are useful measures to us and to our investors because they exclude certain financial, capital structure, and non-cash items that we do not believe directly reflect our core operations and may not be indicative of our recurring operations, in part because they may vary widely across time and within our industry independent of the performance of our core operations. We believe that excluding these items enables us to more effectively evaluate our performance period-over-period and relative to our competitors. These non-GAAP measures may not be comparable to similarly titled measures provided by other companies due to potential differences in methods of calculations.
