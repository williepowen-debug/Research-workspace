## Academic Framework: The Immigration-Credit Nexus

### "Positive Selection" Hypothesis

Once immigrants enter U.S. credit system, they exhibit scores **20-27 points higher** than non-immigrants of same age/location. Gap *widens* with age = long-term assimilation into conservative credit behavior.

**BUT** this is masked by "credit invisibility":
- 26M credit invisible + 19M unscorable = 45M Americans
- 15% Hispanic vs 9% White credit invisible

### Credit Access Gaps (by age 37)

| Product | Gap vs Non-Immigrants | % of Mean |
|---------|----------------------|-----------|
| Auto Loans | -4.7 percentage points | 20% |
| Mortgages | -5 percentage points | 20% |
| Credit Cards | Converges faster | N/A |

### Sanctuary Policy Effect

Sanctuary policies (2010-2021) led to:
1. **Increased demand** — signal of acceptance increases perceived security
2. **Lower rejection rates** — reduced default probability associated with deportation

**"Bifurcation of risk"** — portfolios in sanctuary jurisdictions should outperform enforcement-heavy states

## Key Conclusions

### Is This a New Risk?

**NO** — but magnitude in auto sector is novel:
- 2008: Subprime auto was fraction of mortgage market
- 2025: $280B+ subprime auto outstanding, sufficient scale for systemic ripples
- BBB spreads widened 50bps post-Tricolor

### Where's the Edge?

1. **"Sanctuary Alpha"**: Portfolios in sanctuary jurisdictions should outperform enforcement-heavy states

2. **"Documentation Premium"**: Move up-market within immigrant demographic — target ITINs/SSNs with seasoning, not "no-FICO/no-license" BHPH

3. **"Verification and Custody"**: $800M fraud enabled by lack of third-party control. Surviving lenders will have enhanced due diligence.

### The Forgotten Risk

The immigration-credit nexus was well-documented in 2008-2011 academic research. The 2012-2022 period of relative policy stability allowed lenders to remove these variables from models.

**The "forgotten risk" is now the defining risk of the 2026 credit cycle.**
