# Subprime Auto Lending — February 2026 Developments

## Executive Summary

The subprime auto lending sector continues to face cascading stress across multiple dimensions: fraud-driven bankruptcies at Tricolor and First Brands, liquidity crises at PrimaLend, regulatory tightening via Fed stress tests, and emerging rating agency concerns about immigration enforcement impacts on borrower pools. This report covers developments through February 14, 2026.

***

## 1. PrimaLend Capital Partners — Sale Process Update

### Background
PrimaLend Capital Partners filed Chapter 11 on October 22, 2025 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of Texas (Case No. 25-90013), listing assets and liabilities each in the $100–500 million range. The company is one of the largest national asset-based lenders financing "buy-here, pay-here" (BHPH) auto dealerships, holding approximately $280 million in dealer-borrower loans.[^1][^2][^3]

### Sale Process Status
- **January 5, 2026**: PrimaLend gained court approval for bidding procedures to sell its assets in a market test, with the process aimed at maximizing value of its loan portfolios.[^2][^4]
- **January 7, 2026**: CEO Mark Jensen and PrimaLend were sued by Prime Asset LLC, a Texas-based auto loan servicer, for allegedly saddling it with nearly $45 million in debt and underperforming portfolios. Prime Asset alleges breach of fiduciary duty and bad faith, characterizing itself as a "dumping ground" for PrimaLend's distressed assets.[^5]

### Key Gap — Auction Outcome
**No publicly available reporting confirms that a January 23 auction took place or was concluded.** Court-approved bidding procedures were established in early January 2026, but specific auction results, buyer identity, purchase price, creditor plan vote (referenced deadline of February 9), and recovery rates have not appeared in public sources accessible outside of PACER. The case remains in the sale process phase.

**Market implication**: As noted by Joseph Cioffi of Davis+Gilbert, "A significant sign to watch for the industry is just how much interest the sale generates and the price generated, and the impact on whether lenders going forward may find it attractive to come in to support a troubled company with an eye toward owning".[^2]

## 2. First Brands Group — Escalating Crisis

First Brands Group's bankruptcy has become the most consequential case in the subprime auto lending / auto parts space, with developments accelerating dramatically in January–February 2026.

### Criminal Indictments (January 29, 2026)
Former CEO Patrick James (61) and his brother Edward James (60), a former senior VP, were indicted on nine federal counts by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York. Charges include:[^6][^7]

- Running a continuing financial crimes enterprise
- Wire fraud
- Bank fraud
- Conspiracy to commit money laundering

The indictment alleges a multi-year fraud scheme (2018–2025) involving:[^8][^9]
- **Inflating accounts receivable invoices** and securing loans against them multiple times without lender knowledge
- **Double- and triple-pledging collateral** — approximately $2 billion in undisclosed off-balance sheet debt through entities controlled by Patrick James
- **Falsifying financial statements** and hiding substantial liabilities
- Operating the company "like a Ponzi scheme, using proceeds from new loans to pay off previous lenders"

Former VP of Finance Peter Andrew Brumbergs pled guilty. At the time of filing, First Brands had $12 million in cash and over $9 billion in liabilities.[^10][^6]

**Source**: DOJ press release, SDNY, January 29, 2026; CBS News; CNBC; Reuters

### Examiner Investigation — Martin De Luca
- **January 9, 2026**: U.S. Bankruptcy Judge Christopher Lopez approved the appointment of Martin De Luca (partner, Boies Schiller Flexner) as independent examiner, with a $7 million budget.[^11][^12][^13]
- De Luca was selected from over 70 applicants by the U.S. Trustee's office.[^12]
- **Scope**: Investigate the disappearance of up to $2.3 billion from factored accounts receivable and third-party factoring irregularities, plus transfers related to inventory financing arrangements.[^14][^15]
- **Report due date**: February 25, 2026 — **no preliminary findings have been publicly released as of February 14**.[^12]
- Alvarez & Marsal, the financial advisor, has already reported discrepancies between invoices and corresponding cash receipts of approximately $2.5 billion.[^15]

### Creditor Allegations — Onset Financial Kickback Scheme
Creditors allege that Onset Financial and Edward James were involved in a kickback scheme tied to financing arrangements described as "usurious." Onset allegedly advanced no more than $2.5 billion, has already received approximately $2.9 billion, and is still asserting $1.9 billion more in bankruptcy claims. If litigated successfully, this could reshape priority of claims.[^16]

### Emergency Customer Financing (January 28–29, 2026)
After warning it had reached a "breaking point," First Brands secured approximately $48 million in emergency financing from major customers:[^17][^18]

| Customer | Role |
|---|---|
| Ford Motor Co. | Lead participant; most exposed |
| General Motors | Co-lead participant |
| Harley-Davidson | Participant |
| Honda, Polaris, Audi, Nissan, VW, BMW, Mercury, International Motors | Additional participants |

[^17]

Funding was structured as customer prepayments tied to future product deliveries, with the group potentially extending week-to-week financing. Ford was described as "likely the most vulnerable" participant.[^19][^17]

### DIP Loan Market Distress (January 2026)
Oaktree Capital Management and Anchorage Capital purchased stakes in First Brands' $1.1 billion DIP loan at distressed prices. In December, the company sought as much as $800 million in fresh capital, but advisory fees, declining loan values, and the breadth of fraud allegations diminished lender appetite.[^20][^21]

### Wind-Down and Chapter 7 Discussions

**January 26, 2026**: First Brands commenced wind-down of three North American business units — Brake Parts Inc., Cardone, and Autolite — after failing to secure funding or find buyers. Other North American and international operations continue.[^22][^23]

**January 30, 2026**: First Brands and main creditors entered confidential mediation with U.S. Bankruptcy Judge Marvin Isgur to resolve disputes over collateral ownership, third-party financier claims, and how to keep operations alive long enough to sell factories.[^24]

**February 12, 2026**: Bloomberg reported that First Brands and creditors are weighing shifting certain units into Chapter 7 liquidation as cash runs critically low. Key data points:[^20]

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Senior DIP debt trading level | 13–16 cents on the dollar |
| Second-most-senior loan | 0.375–0.625 cents |
| Pre-bankruptcy first-lien loan | 0.0625 cents |
| Operating model | Week-to-week with customer advance payments |

[^20]

A litigation trust may be created as part of the mediation to reach compromise with the unsecured creditors committee.[^20]

### Section 363 Sale Process
- **January 7, 2026**: First Brands launched a formal Section 363 sale process to market and sell the entire business, in whole or in parts.[^25][^26]
- The company is in discussions with an Ad Hoc Group of lenders to provide additional DIP financing and act as stalking horse bidder for selected divisions.[^26]
- Target completion: Q1 2026, subject to court approval.[^25]
- Brands on the block include FRAM, Raybestos, Trico, Autolite, ANCO, Luber-finer, and others.[^27][^28]

**Market implication**: First Brands represents the largest ABL fraud case in recent memory. The case has catalyzed a "renewed focus on diligence of borrower fundamentals, collateral tracing, and SPV structuring" across U.S. credit markets. A&O Shearman noted that the case has "ramifications for lenders and investors across the U.S. market that are still being worked through".[^29]

## 3. Flagship Credit Acceptance — InterVest Acquisition

### Transaction Status
On November 20, 2025, Flagship Credit Acceptance LLC announced a definitive agreement to sell its operating assets and business operations to InterVest Capital Partners, a New York-based specialty finance investment firm. Key terms:[^30]

- **Buyer**: InterVest Capital Partners (SEC-registered investment adviser)
- **New entity**: Flagship Financial Group LLC
- **New CEO**: Jim Landy (upon closing)
- **Estimated valuation**: ~$400 million (per Bloomberg, citing sources from a year-earlier process)[^31]
- **Flagship's scale**: Over $16 billion in lifetime originations, 500+ employees[^30]
- **Advisors**: Deutsche Bank (seller financial), McDermott Will & Schulte (seller legal); Piper Sandler (buyer financial), Willkie Farr (buyer legal)[^30]

### What's Not Yet Public
- **Final closing date**: Not confirmed in public filings as of February 14, 2026.
- **Forensic audit findings**: No publicly disclosed forensic audit has been reported.
- **Mass layoffs in 2026**: Flagship confirmed layoffs in April 2024 "citing challenging market conditions", but no 2026 layoff reports have surfaced.[^32]
- **Collateral discrepancies**: No public reporting of Tricolor-style collateral gaps at Flagship. However, S&P Global Ratings took various rating actions on 34 classes from 12 Flagship Credit Auto Trust (FCAT) transactions in September 2025, and the California DFPI settled with Flagship in October 2025 over deceptive practices related to loan modifications and unlicensed repossession agents, requiring refunds.[^33][^34]

**Market implication**: The InterVest deal represents a notable private equity bet on non-prime auto lending at a time when the sector is under severe stress. The absence of fraud allegations is notable compared to Tricolor and First Brands. The deal's closing will be a key signal of market confidence.

## 4. Fed 2026 Stress Test Scenarios (Published February 4, 2026)

The Federal Reserve finalized its 2026 supervisory stress test scenarios on February 4, 2026, testing 32 banks against a severe global recession.[^35][^36]

### Severely Adverse Scenario — Key Parameters

| Variable | Scenario Detail |
|---|---|
| Unemployment rate | Rises 5.5pp from 4.5% (Q4 2025) to **10% peak** (Q3 2027) |
| Equity prices | Fall **~58%** through Q3 2026 |
| House prices | Decline **30%** from Q4 2025 through Q4 2027 |
| Commercial real estate prices | Decline **39%** through Q4 2027 |
| VIX peak | **72%** (Q2 2026) |
| BBB corporate bond spreads | Widen 4.7pp to **5.7%** (Q3 2026) |
| 3-month Treasury rate | Falls to **0.1%** by Q2 2026 |
| 10-year Treasury yield | Falls to **2.3%** by Q4 2026 |
| Real GDP decline | **-4.6%** from Q4 2025 to trough (Q2 2027) |

[^37]

### Auto Lending–Specific Scenarios
**There are no auto lending–specific stress scenarios.** The 2026 stress test uses 28 general macroeconomic variables applied uniformly across all tested banks. Auto loan portfolios would be stressed through the general macro deterioration (unemployment, asset price declines, rising delinquencies) rather than sector-specific overlays.[^37]

### Comparison to Prior Years
The Board noted that scenario variables were calibrated "near or in the upper one-third of their ranges of severity". The 58% equity decline reflects that equity prices rose ~16% in 2024–2025, triggering the principle that "equity prices in the scenario fall more after periods when they have risen more". The stress capital buffer requirements are frozen until 2027 pending public feedback on model transparency.[^35][^37]

### Notable Feature — Japanese Yen
The U.S. dollar appreciates against all currencies in the scenario **except the Japanese yen** — a notable exception that may reflect the Fed's view of yen carry trade unwind risks in a stress environment.[^37]

## 5. Tricolor Holdings — Post-Indictment Updates

### Criminal Proceedings
- **December 17, 2025**: Indictment unsealed charging former CEO Daniel Chu and former COO David Goodgame with a seven-year, nearly billion-dollar fraud scheme.[^38][^39]
- CFO Jerome Kollar and senior finance director Ameryn Seibold pled guilty on December 16 and are cooperating with the government.[^38]
- The scheme involved double-pledging approximately $2.2 billion in purported assets against only $1.4 billion in real collateral — an $800 million gap.[^40][^38]
- Executives fabricated customer payments, manipulated loan delinquency data, and created fictitious auto loans.[^38]

### February 2026 Developments
- **February 4, 2026**: A bankruptcy judge ruled that Chu can tap his directors & officers (D&O) insurance to fund his legal defense against the federal fraud charges. Chu has pleaded not guilty.[^41]

## 6. Other Subprime Auto Lender Developments

### New Bankruptcy Filings
No new major subprime auto lender bankruptcy filings have been reported in January–February 2026 beyond the existing cases (Tricolor, PrimaLend, First Brands).

### Rating Agency Actions on Subprime Auto ABS

**S&P Global Ratings — Immigration-Related CreditWatch (Late September 2025)**
S&P placed ratings on CreditWatch Negative for a significant number of subprime auto ABS classes tied to immigrant borrower pools:[^42]
- **11 classes** from 5 Lendbuzz Securitization Trust transactions (LBZZ 2024-1, 2024-2, 2024-3, 2025-1, 2025-2)
- **4 classes** from SAFCO Auto Receivables Trust 2025-1
- S&P cited "the cumulative effect of immigration law enforcement actions" and stated it was "reassessing our projections for future losses, our loss-timing forecast, and other assumptions"[^42]
- This followed CreditWatch placements on **11 ratings** from 3 First Help Financial (FHF) Issuer Trust transactions on September 9, 2025

**Despite the CreditWatch**, Lendbuzz closed a $246 million securitization (LBZZ 2026-1) on February 10, 2026, rated by KBRA at K1+, AAA, AA, A, and BBB-, with J.P. Morgan as lead bookrunner.[^43][^44]

**S&P — Flagship Credit Auto Trust**
S&P took rating actions on 34 classes from 12 FCAT transactions in September 2025. Specific details on the direction of those actions are behind the S&P paywall.[^34]

**KBRA — FHF Trust Watch Downgrade (October 2025)**
KBRA placed ratings on 7 classes from 4 First Help Financial Trust and FHF Issuer Trust subprime auto loan transactions on Watch Downgrade due to deteriorating cumulative net loss volumes.[^45]

**KBRA — 2026 U.S. ABS Sector Outlook**
KBRA's 2026 outlook noted that "subprime auto and unsecured consumer loans continue to exhibit the most stress" and that "divergence in performance between subprime and prime may increase as the so-called 'K-shaped' economy provides broad support for the latter". Through October 2025, KBRA reported 77% affirmations, 19% upgrades, and 4% downgrades, with downgrades concentrated in non-prime auto and solar ABS.[^46]

## 7. Systemic Risk Assessment

The convergence of fraud at Tricolor ($800M collateral gap), First Brands ($2.3B+ missing from factored receivables), and stress at PrimaLend represents an unprecedented cluster of failures in asset-based and specialty auto finance. Key systemic implications:

1. **Collateral tracing failures**: Both Tricolor and First Brands involved double-pledging and fabricated collateral — suggesting that audit and verification mechanisms across the sector were inadequate.[^52][^29]

2. **Private credit exposure**: First Brands' collapse has raised alarms about "potential instability in the opaque private credit markets". Major Wall Street banks, specialty lenders, and factoring companies are absorbing losses.[^11]

3. **K-shaped credit performance**: The divergence between prime and subprime auto loan performance continues to widen. Deep subprime vintages are experiencing cumulative delinquency rates exceeding 30% by month 24, while overall delinquency growth is slowing for higher-income borrowers.[^48][^49]

4. **Immigration enforcement as a new risk factor**: S&P's CreditWatch actions on Lendbuzz, SAFCO, and FHF transactions represent a novel credit risk vector — potential disruption to borrower pools from immigration enforcement actions. This could affect a meaningful subset of subprime auto ABS.[^42]

5. **Regulatory response**: The Fed's 2026 stress test scenarios, while not auto-specific, model a severe downturn (10% unemployment, 58% equity decline) that would substantially stress auto loan portfolios. The stress capital buffer freeze until 2027 adds uncertainty about future capital requirements.[^35][^37]
