## Executive Summary

The landscape of American corporate credit has undergone a radical transformation in the first quarter of 2026, as evidenced by a statistical surge in large-scale bankruptcy filings that mirrors the most distressed periods of the 21st century. By mid-February 2026, market data confirmed that nine major corporate entities with liabilities exceeding $50 million filed for court protection in a single week ending approximately February 11, 2026. This spike contributed to a three-week rolling average of 18 large bankruptcies, a figure representing the highest frequency of significant corporate failures since the peak of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. Historically, this level of distress has only been surpassed during the 2001 dot-com collapse, the 2008-2009 Global Financial Crisis, and the 2020 lockdowns.

## Assessment: Systemic Stress vs Normal Cycle

### Case for Normal Cyclical Adjustment
- Spec-grade default rate: 3.75-4.25% projected by June 2026 (elevated but within norms)
- Economy growing at 1.8%
- Unemployment relatively low
- "Unfit" legacy players being purged

### Case for Systemic Stress
1. **Volume spike**: 9 filings/week, 18 in 3 weeks = 2008/2020 levels
2. **Debt concentration**: Count down 19% but VOLUME persistent = larger average failures
3. **Fraud contagion**: Multi-billion entities on fraudulent collateral for years = due diligence breakdown

**Conclusion:** Not merely cyclical — this is a "cleansing process" exposing systemic risks in shadow banking and securitized debt markets of early 2020s.
