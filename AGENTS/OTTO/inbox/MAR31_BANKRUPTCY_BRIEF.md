# Mar 31 Bankruptcy Deadline Brief
**Date:** 2026-03-26 | **Deadline:** Mar 31, 2026 (5 days)

## 1. Tricolor Auto Group (Ch. 7 — N.D. Texas, Case 25-42659)

### Current Status
- Filed **Chapter 7 liquidation** (not Ch. 11) on Sep 10, 2025. No reorganization attempted.
- ~10,000 vehicles across ~60 locations, ~100,000 loan accounts, 25,000+ creditors
- Ch. 7 Trustee Anne Elizabeth Burns overseeing liquidation
- **Vehicle Sales Deadline: March 31, 2026** — all remaining inventory must be liquidated

### The Fraud
- Daniel Chu (founder/CEO) and COO David Goodgame charged Dec 2025 with running a "financial crimes enterprise"
- **$800M collateral gap**: ~$2.2B in assets pledged against ~$1.4B actual collateral
- Core scheme: **double-pledging** — same auto loans pledged as collateral to multiple warehouse lenders simultaneously; some loans potentially fabricated entirely

### Bank/Fund Exposure (CONFIRMED — all nine-figure losses)
| Lender | Exposure | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **JPMorgan Chase** | **$170M charge-off** | Dimon called it "not our finest moment" |
| **Fifth Third Bancorp (FITB)** | **$170-200M impairment** | Regional bank, material hit |
| **Barclays** | **Nine-figure loss** (undisclosed exact) | Named in Feb 2026 investor lawsuit |

- All three banks sued by ABS noteholders (Feb 27, 2026) for "missing giant red flags"
- **Apollo exposure: NOT IDENTIFIED** in public filings. Tricolor's warehouse lines were traditional bank facilities (JPM, Barclays, Fifth Third + at least one other unidentified "Lender-4")
- No WAL (Western Alliance) exposure identified

### Recovery Outlook
- **Very low.** Ch. 7 liquidation of depreciating used vehicles with disputed lien positions = fire-sale pricing
- $800M gap means unsecured creditors likely recover **near zero**
- Secured warehouse lenders fighting over same collateral — recovery depends on which liens are valid
- Vehicle liquidation by Mar 31 is the hard deadline; proceeds then distributed per court-adjudicated priority

### Chu Criminal Trial
- **Trial date: October 19, 2026** (set Mar 19, 2026 by Judge Kevin Castel)
- Originally expected Aug, now pushed to Oct
- Chu and Goodgame pleaded not guilty
- Charges carry potential **life imprisonment**
- No significant pre-trial motions reported in public filings as of 3/26

## 2. First Brands Group (Ch. 11 — S.D. Texas)

### Current Status
- Filed **Chapter 11** Sep 28, 2025. 100+ affiliated entities.
- Auto aftermarket conglomerate (brakes, spark plugs, filters, wipers, remanufactured parts)
- **Cash nearly depleted** by early 2026; DIP financing proving insufficient
- Multiple divisions already shutting down (manufacturing, distribution centers)
- **Chapter 7 conversion actively under consideration** for some units (Bloomberg, Feb 13, 2026)
- Next hearing adjourned to **April 9, 2026**

### Auction / Sale Status
- 363 sale process launched but **buyer interest has narrowed significantly**
- Operational shutdowns across key units signal the viable-going-concern pool is shrinking
- Some divisions likely headed to Ch. 7 liquidation rather than sale
- Cash compromise with lenders: reduced factoring account release request from $65.3M to $18.1M (Jan 2026) — shows weakened negotiating position

### Capital Structure — Private Credit Dominated
| Lender/Holder | Exposure | Type |
|---------------|----------|------|
| **Marathon Asset Management** | **$280M+** | Largest debt holder |
| **Monroe Capital** | Significant | Private credit |
| **Antares Capital** | Significant | Private credit |
| Off-balance-sheet BREs | $205M total | Broad Street ($45M ABL), Carnaby Inventory II ($60M), Carnaby III ($100M) |

- **This is a private credit story, not a bank story.** Layered, aggressive yield-seeking structure (per Neuberger Berman analysis)
- **Apollo exposure: NOT DIRECTLY IDENTIFIED** in public filings, but Apollo is active in private credit and the full lender list spans "dozens of major private credit names"
- **WAL (Western Alliance) exposure: NOT IDENTIFIED** in available filings
- No traditional bank warehouse lines like Tricolor — financing was ABL revolvers and private credit facilities

### Recovery Outlook
- **Secured creditors:** Partial recovery likely, but declining as operations wind down and asset values erode
- **Unsecured creditors:** Recovery approaching **zero** if Ch. 7 conversion proceeds for major units
- Ch. 7 conversion = trustee shutdown = liquidation value only (typically 10-30 cents on manufacturing assets)
- Going-concern sale of remaining viable brands could yield better recovery but buyer pool is thin

## Implications for Positions

### KRE (Regional Bank ETF)
- **Tricolor is the direct risk.** Fifth Third's $170-200M hit is the template for how subprime auto fraud transmits to regionals. The ABS investor lawsuits (Feb 2026) add litigation overhang.
- First Brands is NOT a regional bank exposure story — it's private credit. Minimal KRE relevance.
- **Mar 31 vehicle liquidation deadline** will crystallize final loss numbers for warehouse lenders. Watch for revised charge-off disclosures in Q1 earnings.

### WAL (Western Alliance)
- **No direct exposure identified** to either Tricolor or First Brands in available filings.
- Indirect risk: WAL has warehouse lending exposure in other verticals; Tricolor's fraud raises due diligence scrutiny across all warehouse lenders. Regulatory tightening possible.

### APO (Apollo)
- **No confirmed direct exposure** to either case, but:
  - First Brands' private credit lender list includes "dozens of major names" — Apollo could have indirect exposure via fund holdings or CLO tranches
  - Apollo's broader private credit book faces mark-to-market risk if First Brands' Ch. 7 conversion triggers reassessment of similar auto aftermarket credits
- Worth monitoring Apollo's next earnings call for any mention

### Net Assessment
The Mar 31 Tricolor deadline is the more consequential event — it forces final loss recognition at major banks and crystalizes the fraud narrative heading into Q1 earnings. First Brands is a slower burn, private-credit-centric, with the April 9 hearing as the next inflection point. Neither case shows confirmed APO or WAL exposure, but Tricolor's bank losses (JPM/Barclays/FITB confirmed nine-figure) reinforce the OTTO thesis on subprime auto stress transmission to traditional finance.
