## What the video gets right, and what it leaves out

- **Right:** the comparison is apples to oranges, and it partly says so. Office-only CMBS (30+ days late, matured loans included) is compared with all bank CRE (90+ days late or nonaccrual), smaller loans and a different property mix. The extension mechanism is real: per CREED's reading of the Fed's large-bank paper (Glancy, FEDS 2026-025; not re-read by me), office loans paid off at maturity only ~20% of the time in 2023–25, against >40% before the pandemic.
- **Left out:** banks report a stage the video names and then never uses: **criticized loans** (flagged weak while still paying). That is where bank stress shows before delinquency, and it is what this desk reads. **What my 14-bank evidence shows [9/30]:** the cohort's total-CRE bad-loan rate fell 2.23% → 2.13% while problem loans rose at 7 of 10 names; banks cutting reserves while problem loans rose were 9.6% of all banks (8.8% in Q2); the breadth test reads BROADER only on its foreclosure leg (banks **taking** buildings, the opposite of extending). Specific extend-and-pretend cases are already live: Valley's ~$108M block apparently modified again, the EagleBank Q4 maturity wall (~$525M), Preferred Bank's $150.2M 60–89-day CRE bucket. My standing **concentrated, not tier-wide** judgment rests on that separate distress evidence: a 14-bank cohort (not the population), one population count of the reserve-cut shape (9.6% of banks), and an 11/07 breadth test that reads BROADER on its letter, weak. It stays open until the FDIC Q3 QBP. **The concentration count in the Addendum measures exposure, not distress, and is not evidence for or against that judgment.**
- **Unanswered by the video and by us:** the "who holds it" question. None of the named loans is tied to a bank on my list; they are CMBS. Holders of the named CMBS bonds, and any bank participation in those loans, are UNKNOWN. The fleet's next real test of the bank side is the FDIC Q3 QBP (~11/25 ESTIMATE).

## Addendum, corrected (11/16 19:3x ET): the "3× capital" claim, measured on the supervisory basis

**Correction.** The first run (kept verbatim below) used total risk-based capital for banks that report it and tier 1 + allowance only for community-bank-leverage banks: two denominators in one count. **SR 20-8** (Federal Reserve, 3/30/2020, revised 4/2/2020; joint statement with FDIC and OCC; read at federalreserve.gov 11/16, sha256 `aac916b8…`) sets the supervisory concentration denominator for **all** banks from 3/31/2020: tier 1 capital plus the allowance for credit losses on loans and leases (CECL), or plus the entire ALLL (non-CECL). It "do[es] not affect the calculation of total capital in other areas." Rerun on that one basis. CATO's denominator-only sensitivity (175 of 889, 19.7%, median 226%) was **not copied**: my rerun reached the same three figures independently from the same saved files.

**Screen, read at source** (SR 07-1 attachment 2, the 2006 interagency guidance, sha256 `0a8b6b9d…`): an institution "approaching or exceeds" (1) construction, land development and other land loans ≥ 100% of total capital, or (2) total CRE ≥ 300% of total capital **and** CRE growth ≥ 50% over the prior 36 months "may be identified for further supervisory analysis": a preliminary step, not a limit. Its scope excludes nonfarm nonresidential loans repaid from the owner's own operations (owner-occupied). Its footnote 6 lists RC-C items 1a, 1d, 1e and Memo 3; 1e was not yet split by occupancy in 2006, so the non-owner-occupied line (F161) is the convention that follows the scope text. The "total capital" footnote is superseded for supervisory calculations by SR 20-8.

**Data:** 9/30/2026 Call Reports for 4,205 insured banks (FFIEC bulk SDF saved by DEWEY 11/11, `AGENTS/DEWEY/research/2026-11-11_003R-population/call-reports-sdf.tar.gz`; OI/NC excluded as in DEWEY's method). Script (corrected in place, header notes the change): `reports/2026-11-16_cre_concentration_9-30.py`.
**Measures:** supervisory CRE = F158 + F159 (construction and land) + 1460 (multifamily) + F161 (non-owner-occupied nonfarm nonresidential) + 2746 (CRE not secured by real estate, where reported). Broad CRE adds F160 (owner-occupied). Denominator = tier 1 capital (8274) + allowance on loans and leases (3123), every bank.
**Method check, qualified:** EagleBank's components reproduce its Q3 release on the **old** total-capital basis (245.6% vs 245.8%; construction 62.1% vs 62.2%; release figures from a search summary, release not opened). On the SR 20-8 basis EagleBank is **242.0%** (construction 61.2%): its published ratio uses total risk-based capital. The check validates the numerator, not the denominator.

**Reading:** the video's broad figure is roughly right: a typical $1–10B bank holds commercial property loans of about three times its capital when owner-occupied loans count (305%). Equating that with the supervisory screen is the problem: on the screen's loan definition the typical bank is at 226%, and 175 of 889 (19.7%) are at or above 300%. The growth leg was not computed, so 19.7% is an upper bound on banks meeting the full screen. The "$100 set aside for losses" line confuses capital with the allowance: CRE is about 43× the allowance at the median $1–10B bank.
**What this count is not:** exposure, not distress. It says where a downturn would bite, not that one is biting, and it is not evidence for my concentrated-not-tier-wide judgment, which rests on separate credit evidence and its own population limits (§ What the video gets right).

**Reading:** the video's "about three times capital" matches only the broad measure that includes owner-occupied loans (306% median), which the 300% screen does not use. On the screen's own measure, the typical $1–10B bank sits at 226%, and 177 of 889 (19.9%) are at or above 300%. That is a real minority, more common in this size band than any other, but not "the typical bank is already over it." The full screen also requires ≥ 50% CRE growth over 36 months, not computed here, so 19.9% is an upper bound on banks meeting both legs. The video's "$100 set aside for losses" line confuses capital with the allowance: CRE is about 43× the allowance at the median $1–10B bank, which is why a few large losses can hurt earnings fast, but capital, not the allowance, is what the 300% line measures.
**Consequence:** none for scores or thresholds. It supports my standing "concentrated, not tier-wide" claim: concentration risk sits in a minority of $1–10B banks, not the median one.
