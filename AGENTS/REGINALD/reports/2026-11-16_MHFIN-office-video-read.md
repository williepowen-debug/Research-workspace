## What the video gets right, and what it leaves out

- **Right:** the comparison is apples to oranges, and it partly says so. Office-only CMBS (30+ days late, matured loans included) is compared with all bank CRE (90+ days late or nonaccrual), smaller loans and a different property mix. The extension mechanism is real: the Fed's own large-bank paper finds office loans paid off at maturity only ~20% of the time in 2023–25, against >40% before the pandemic.
- **Left out:** banks report a stage the video names and then never uses: **criticized loans** (flagged weak while still paying). That is where bank stress shows before delinquency, and it is what this desk reads. **What my 14-bank evidence shows [9/30]:** the cohort's total-CRE bad-loan rate fell 2.23% → 2.13% while problem loans rose at 7 of 10 names; banks cutting reserves while problem loans rose were 9.6% of all banks (8.8% in Q2); the breadth test reads BROADER only on its foreclosure leg (banks **taking** buildings, the opposite of extending). Specific extend-and-pretend cases are already live: Valley's ~$108M block apparently modified again, the EagleBank Q4 maturity wall (~$525M), Preferred Bank's $150.2M 60–89-day CRE bucket. **Concentrated, not tier-wide** still stands.
- **Unanswered by the video and by us:** the "who holds it" question. None of the named loans is tied to a bank on my list; they are CMBS. The fleet's next real test of the bank side is the FDIC Q3 QBP (~11/25 ESTIMATE).
