## Disposition

The report is a credible **corroborative policy appendix** to REGINALD's existing Q3 work. Its central distinction is right: the 2025–26 policy direction can alter lending capacity, examiner intervention, ratings and failed-bank resolution options, but it does not establish concealed CRE losses, an accounting holiday or protection for current shareholders.

It does **not** change the live conclusion that deterioration is concentrated rather than tier-wide. It creates no new bank score, cohort member, threshold, prediction, trade instruction or monitoring task. The existing frozen Q3 read plan remains the decision surface.

| CAMELS revisions and broad capital revisions | Proposals | No current capital or rating benefit should be booked. Any later rating change must be checked against cash collection, realized losses, capital and liquidity because the measurement framework itself may have changed. |
| Fed asset-threshold reform | Announced future work | Not a current exemption. WAL's simple four-quarter company-report asset average of **$95.3245B** is a useful proxy, not a verified FR Y-9C regulatory calculation. |
| FDIC private-capital buyer-policy rescission | Effective March 23, 2026 | Broadens potential failed-bank bidders and resolution options. It is a **post-failure resolution** mechanism, not evidence that a going-concern bank or its shareholders will be protected. |

The durable inference is narrow: supervisory easing can change the timing or form of examiner action. It cannot, by itself, establish better collateral, debt service, cash recovery, accounting cure or shareholder value.

## Empirical-paper limitation

The report correctly presents conflicting findings on CRE extensions, but its sample caveat must apply to **both** papers:

- Crosignani and Prazad's New York Fed Staff Report 1130 uses FR Y-14 data for **22 large domestic banks**, averaging about **$0.96T** in assets, over 2022Q1–2025Q4.
- Glancy's FEDS 2026-025 also uses FR Y-14 loan-level data: non-owner-occupied CRE commitments above $1M at banks above $100B, over 2016Q1–2025Q4.

Neither sample directly establishes how OZK or VLY behaves. The findings can coexist—vulnerable lenders may extend strategically at the margin while many extensions address temporary friction—but neither paper proves a Trump-policy cause or an instruction to conceal losses.

The 2023 interagency CRE-workout statement is the necessary pre-policy baseline: prudent modifications could already avoid criticism when repayment was supportable, while banks still had to maintain risk review, reporting and allowances.

## Current-evidence overlay the report misses

### OZK / RaDD

The report was already stale on the day's most consequential OZK development. OZK's October 12 8-K says the October 9 agreement extended its largest San Diego credit—a **$915M commitment**—to **December 2029**. Sponsor/capital partners contributed **$100M** of new capital; that and **$21.3M** of an existing reserve reduced principal by **$121.3M**. The whole reduction may be redrawn for approved interest and other budgeted costs. Funded debt after the reduction was **$579.1M**, with **$335.9M** unfunded.

That is affirmative disconfirmation of a near-term maturity-failure catalyst, not proof of a permanent exposure reduction or a current collateral cure. The as-stabilized appraisal, redraw capacity and missing credit-specific recognition amount remain limits. REGINALD's existing consumption report already preserves that distinction; no second OZK thesis should be built from this document.

### VLY

The report's ratio/denominator point is sound but incomplete. REGINALD's existing primary-file work records a Q2 cluster: multifamily past dues rose about **$92M** (**$9.4M → $101.5M**), CRE modifications to borrowers in financial difficulty reached **$116.0M**, including **$108.2M** of other-than-insignificant payment delays with an eight-month weighted deferral, and VLY disclosed a decline in quantitative reserves largely within unspecified CRE categories. Those facts are an early bank-specific flag, not a loss conclusion. The October 22 release and later 10-Q already test cure/roll, new modifications, re-default and reserve direction.

### WAL

The report improves the bucket-transition read and the $100B-threshold framing, but it does not convert LAM/Cantor-specific losses into a CRE breadth signal. WAL stays in the existing mid-pack breadth test and owner-desk Q3 grade.

## How REGINALD should use this

1. Keep the report as the **policy lens** behind the frozen Q3 bank checks; do not add a parallel scorecard.
2. At each print, record the financial outcome first—cash paydown, sponsor capital, maturity, reserve/charge-off, nonaccrual, foreclosure and retained/re-advanced balance—then separately ask whether supervisory treatment changed.
3. Use FDIC industry aggregates as a system countercheck, not as the answer to the regional-bank/CRE breadth question. REGINALD's original-basis 14-bank cohort and L180 missing-data rule remain controlling.
4. Treat rules and proposals according to their legal state. November 2 is context, not a new catalyst calendar item; CAMELS and broad capital proposals confer no present benefit.
5. At the Q3 read, a more accommodating supervisor is neither a bullish result nor a bearish concealment result without bank-level accounting and cash evidence.
