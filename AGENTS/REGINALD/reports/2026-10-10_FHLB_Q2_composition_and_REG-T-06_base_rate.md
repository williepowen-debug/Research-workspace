# FHLB advances at 6/30/26: who borrowed the $136B, and what the $700B alarm means

**REGINALD · 2026-10-10 Sat (Will-directed: NEXT SESSION ★2). Vintage of every figure: 6/30/2026 (published Aug 2026). Nothing here can date a tape move after 6/30.**
**Question (MEMORY ★2, ROADMAP `REG-T-06` thread):** Was the jump to $810.7B broad, or PNC only? Read the FHLB Atlanta and San Francisco 10-Qs before the Q3 print, so that `REG-T-06` does not fire as a bare level.

## 1. Answer

| Question | Answer [6/30/26] | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| PNC only? | **No.** PNC is +$27.4B of +$136.2B (20%). | PNC 10-Q (ML-REG-160) |
| Broad? | **No.** It is concentrated in **four very large banks: PNC, Citibank, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo = +$67.5B = 49.5%** of H1 growth (par). Three districts (Pittsburgh, New York, Cincinnati) = 73.5%. | each FHLBank's own 10-Q borrower table (§3) |
| What about the banks I cover? | **Flat.** Across 109 SEC-filing borrowers, **41 up, 49 down, 19 flat, median change 0.0%**; excluding the top three movers, the other 106 added +$2.3B in total. My six Q3 banks were ~6% of Q2 growth (WQ-318, 10/7). | borrower-side XBRL (§4) |
| Atlanta and San Francisco (the two districts asked about) | **Atlanta +6.9% (par), with Truist paying DOWN $3.8B. San Francisco −10.6%.** Neither carries the build. | §5 |
| Is >$700B a crisis level? | **No.** The system was >$700B in **12 of the last 26 quarters**, nine in a row (Q4-22 → Q4-24). It peaked at **$1,044.6B [3/31/23]**. As registered, `REG-T-06` would have fired in mid-2023 and **never cleared** (exit ≤$650B ×2 has not printed since 2022). | §6 |

⚠️ **The purpose of the borrowing is read for ONE bank only, PNC:** asset growth plus an acquisition, funded wholesale as organic deposits ran off; credit clean (ML-REG-160, ~0.85). **For Citibank, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo I have NOT read their 10-Qs. No purpose is asserted. Concentration in the largest banks is a FACT; what it means is UNREAD.**

## 6. What the registered alarm would say

`REG-T-06` (registry/THRESHOLDS.tsv): `FHLB-ADVANCES > 700` for 3 consecutive quarterly prints ⇒ **`EARLY-CRISIS`**; exit `≤ 650` on 2 consecutive prints. **A Q3 print over $700B fires it (~late Oct / early Nov).**

**Bold = >$700B.**
- **Backtest of the letter:** fires at Q2-23, after Q4-22 / Q1-23 / Q2-23. It **never exits**, because every quarter from Q3-22 on has been above $650B (the low is $655.0B [9/30/22]; since then $676.7B [12/31/25]). On its own letter it would have read EARLY-CRISIS from Q2-23 to today, including the whole of 2024.
- **It did catch the 2023 episode,** but the 3-quarter sustain means it would have confirmed only with the June-2023 figure, published about 4–5 months after SVB failed (3/10/23). It cannot separate that episode from the 2024 plateau.
- **How the line got here:** the threshold was set while this row read ~$480B from the wrong publisher (Fed H.8) for 190 days (STATUS FHLB row). **The $700B line was never base-rated against the real series.**

## 7. Caveats (each could change the read)

1. **Vintage 6/30.** Q3 is unknown. The 10/7 discount-window high ($9.965B) and the September selloff are AFTER this data.
2. **Purpose unread for Citi, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo.** Large-bank FHLB growth can be cheap funding, a liquidity buffer or replacement of deposit runoff. Their own 10-Qs would decide it, and they belong to the proposed large-bank desk's perimeter.
3. **Named-member coverage:** the borrower tables name about half the growth. Chicago (+$10.1B) names no one, and the Atlanta top ten beyond Truist and BofA is undisclosed.
4. **Borrower-side frame perimeter:** §4 above.
5. **Insurers** (TIAA, Athene) are a separate channel (funding agreements); not bank stress, and not mine.
