## 2. Reverse the expense budget

Using Yahoo's FY2027 revenue, the average quarter has $1,059.66M revenue. Keep Q2-based assumptions of 19% tax, 108M diluted shares and $13.6M quarterly deductions for noncontrolling interest, preferred dividends and participating awards. This is a **conditional reconstruction**, not the analysts' actual model.

`Annual common EPS = 4 × [(revenue − operating expense − provision) × 0.81 − 13.6] / 108`

$11 requires $383.46M pretax per quarter. Q2 pretax was $332M, total operating expense $583.3M, and provision $80.4M. The comparison is **FY2027 average quarter versus Q2 2026**, not expense growth over a full FY2026 forecast and not a Q3 grading threshold. ## 3. Deposit costs: test the whole profit statement

Q2 NII rose $30.6M to $796.9M. But earnings-credit-related amounts outside NII increased $16.1M, leaving $14.5M of that improvement before other changes. Q2 earnings credits were split across **$52.5M reducing NII, $8.4M reducing fees and $173.3M in operating deposit expense**. Other deposit expense adds $5.9M to reach the $179.2M expense line. Subtracting the full $234.2M again would double count. The correctly reconciled Q2 pre-provision net revenue is $412.4M. [Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1212545/000162828026051418/wal-20260630.htm), Note8 Deposits and MD&A deposit-cost/net-revenue tables; extracted HTML tables 59/98/134.

There is meaningful contrary evidence. Total earnings-credit cost rose from $214.3M to $234.2M while rounded average qualifying balances rose from $29.4B to $31.6B. Adjust for 90 versus 91 days: aggregate annualized cost is approximately 2.956% versus 2.973%. At Q1's cost per dollar per day, Q2 cost would be $232.90M. Thus balances/calendar explain about **93.44%** of the increase; the remaining $1.30M contains rate, mix and rounding effects. This is an aggregate diagnostic across changing populations, **not a same-customer price or deposit-beta measure**.

June-end qualifying balances were $29.9B, below Q2's average. At unchanged unit cost, that difference alone represents roughly $12.6M lower quarterly gross earnings-credit cost. Replacement funding, foregone asset income and related fee changes are unknown, so it is not a net profit forecast. Nondeposit operating expense also fell $7M sequentially to $404.1M, although it remained roughly 10% higher year over year. These facts support the possibility of optimization; they do not prove its full-year net benefit.

The issuer's +100bp ramp model gives NII +3.2% but earnings-at-risk +0.8%, with mortgage and deposit costs explaining attenuation. Different bases prevent subtracting the percentages as a cost estimate. Hedges are already included. Higher rates can help NII without an equally large benefit to common EPS. [Q2 deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1212545/000162828026049001/walq22026earningspresent.htm), slides 7–11 and 17.
