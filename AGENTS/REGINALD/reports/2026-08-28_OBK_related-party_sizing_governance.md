## 2. Reg O sizing — the key ratio, and it is unusually concentrated

Regulation O (12 CFR 215) caps loans to a single insider at **10% of unimpaired capital**:

- Reg O individual-insider cap ≈ **10% × $1,301,623k ≈ $130.2M**
- OTTO's disclosed OBK-to-Tricolor exposure: **$74.7M** ($16.2M executive mortgages + $30.1M commitments + $28.4M charge-off already through P&L)

⇒ **Residual live exposure ≈ $46.3M**; if the Tricolor Holdings LLC lending is **attributed to Chu** under 12 CFR 215.2(g)'s control rules (director + operational role = likely control), the **aggregate insider exposure is 57% of Reg O cap consumed by a SINGLE insider**.

- **Not a Reg O violation on face** — still within the 10% ceiling
- **Not typical either** — a single director consuming >50% of a bank's individual-insider Reg O cap is a concentrated position that a routine audit would flag. Community-bank Reg O books usually see per-insider utilization <10-15% of the cap.
- **$16.2M executive mortgages alone** = 12.5% of Reg O cap, 1.3% of equity — small.
- **$74.7M aggregate** = 5.8% of equity, ~5.5% of tangible common equity (est).

## 4. Governance read — what the SEC filing does to OBK's disclosure posture

The **"borrower fraud" / "ordinary banking relationships"** framing was defensible on 2026-03-13 when the proxy was filed. **The SEC v. Chu complaint (2026-08-18) makes it materially harder to sustain** because:

1. **Chu is alleged to have DIRECTED the fraud from Tricolor's CEO seat.** If Chu had "control" of Tricolor (typical for a founder-CEO), then under Reg O attribution rules Tricolor's loans from OBK are **loans to an insider**, not to an unrelated party. That reclassifies the transaction from "ordinary" to "related party" retroactively.
2. **The pricing/terms standard bites HARDER on a director-controlled entity.** Reg O requires "substantially the same terms as prevailing for comparable transactions with unrelated persons." If Tricolor got favorable pricing or waived conditions BECAUSE Chu sat on OBK's board, that is a Reg O violation of the pricing standard, independent of the concentration.
3. **The $16.2M executive mortgages line** — a personal mortgage to Chu — becomes a per se related-party loan. Not a violation if terms were arm's-length, but the disclosure regime for it changes: it needs to appear separately in the 10-K's related-party section, not lumped with "ordinary banking."

**Enforcement risk vector, ranked:**

- 🟠 **Regulatory (OCC/Fed Reg O enforcement)** — plausible on the CONCENTRATION vector (57% of individual cap in one insider is atypical) and on the PRICING vector if the SEC's discovery surfaces preferential terms. Typical resolution: MRA / MRIA / consent order with capital add-on; low $ hit but a governance overhang.
- 🟡 **Caremark oversight class-action (Delaware Chancery)** — plausible on the theory that a sitting director committing fraud AT the company he oversees crosses the "utter failure of oversight" bar. Requires plaintiff to show OBK's board knew or willfully ignored red flags in Chu's Tricolor activities. Low-conviction but non-zero.
- 🟢 **Audit-committee credibility / restatement risk** — low. The charge-off is recognized. The 10-K's related-party section could be revised on the reclassification, but there is no missing dollar to restate.
- 🟢 **D&O insurance** — Chu's own D&O coverage is likely voided by fraud exclusion; OBK's institutional D&O and its E&O policies remain intact for corporate defense costs. Not a P&L event for OBK.

## 5. The 10-Q silence — the one thing I would ask OBK's counsel about

The Q2 10-Q filed 8/5 is silent on Tricolor's residual $46.3M exposure and on Chu's related-party status. That silence was defensible at filing time because the "ordinary banking" framing was still uncontested. **After 8/18 (SEC complaint), any Form 8-K item 8.01 disclosure updating the position would be prudent** — waiting to Q3 10-Q or the annual 10-K puts OBK 2-6 months behind an active federal securities-fraud proceeding involving a former director.
