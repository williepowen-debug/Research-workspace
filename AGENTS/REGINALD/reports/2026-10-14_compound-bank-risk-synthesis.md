## 3. FHLB connects funding channels, but borrowing is not another loss

[FHFA’s current system explanation](https://www.fhfa.gov/about/federal-home-loan-bank-system), read October14, establishes secured advances and eligible collateral. An advance supplies cash, can prevent a bond sale, and has interest and collateral requirements; it does not repair a defaulted borrower. Whether it worsens earnings depends on the funding it replaces, amount, rate, hedges and timing. More advances with stable deposits can be ordinary balance-sheet management; a rise paired with lost cheap deposits, shrinking usable collateral and higher costs would be more consequential. A pledge ratio alone cannot measure undrawn capacity.

Existing Office of Finance Q2 Table3 work puts insurer advances principal at$202.820B,24.9% of system advances. It is a dated composition fact, **not evidence banks and insurers are simultaneously running out of cash**. Source: [Q2 Combined Financial Report](https://cdn.fhlb-of.com/files/2026/2026Q2CFR.pdf), verified October10, carried in `reports/2026-10-11_FHLB_insurer-channel_handoffs.md`. No system borrower-type Q3 total has been measured here. Bank FHLB advances are already within other borrowed money; do not add both as separate debt.

The existing faster funding work already identified increased smaller-bank borrowing through the September30 H.8 week without a parallel aggregate deposit decline; see the FHLB hub and MEMORY’s existing October15 H.4.1/October16 H.8 read obligation. That is a reason to investigate funding purpose, not an identified loss at any named bank. No new October14 macro observation was pulled in this synthesis, and aggregate stability would not rule out an individual bank’s outflows.

Sources: [2026 F-2 p1](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/bf_f2_0630.2026.pdf), [2025 F-2 p1](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/bf_f2_0630.2025.pdf). This is not a small-business-only series, a count of unique firms, default dollars or a current October rate. All-bankruptcy12.2% growth is a different denominator. Cases with predominantly business debt also include multiple chapters and entity types.

The channels worth distinguishing are direct business loans; SBA-guaranteed versus unguaranteed retained interests; a tenant failing and reducing a landlord’s rent; and a borrower held by a private-credit fund financed by a bank. A single closure can affect several claims, but there are not automatically several bank losses. The Picklr bankruptcy lead has no authenticated cohort lender, property-loan or rent-loss mapping; alleged personal lease guarantees do not close that gap.

## 6. Pensions need a separate cash-demand test

[Bank of England’s2023 gilt-market case study](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2023/2023/financial-stability-buy-sell-tools-a-gilt-market-case-study), read October14, establishes the historical2022 mechanism: leveraged liability-driven investing generated cash collateral demands and forced sales during a rate shock. It is an example of how pensions can amplify markets, not evidence of a current US pension-bank event.

For a defined-benefit pension, rates can change both assets **and the present value of promised benefits**. Funding solvency and cash available for tomorrow’s margin call are separate questions. Determine the scheme, leveraged fund, derivatives/repo counterparties, collateral terms, cash calls and actual sales. A portfolio valuation loss alone is neither cash paid nor a bank credit loss. Direct holdings of bank debt and sponsor contribution obligations are additional distinct paths; no measured cohort allocations were recovered here.

The October13 Chilean pension/swaps lead is a secondary teaser with unverified regulator/position data. It does not establish Treasury cash sales, margin-call amounts or losses at our banks. Existing BOND/LIQUID routing remains relevant; insurers and pensions are not collapsed into one SHADE balance sheet.

## 7. Cushion and incremental-loss bridge

For each named bank, use its **existing** Q3 outcome work to bridge:

1. Remaining collateral value and loan exposure **after prior charge-offs**, senior claims and costs.
2. Additional credit provision/write-down, allowing only legitimately applicable reserves; charge-offs against an existing allowance are not a second new earnings loss.
3. Incremental funding expense relative to replaced funding, with hedges and full-period timing; do not add ECR already captured elsewhere.
4. New AFS OCI versus existing AOCI and any realized recycling. The same loss moving from OCI to earnings is not another total-equity hit. HTM economic sensitivity has different recognition rules.

All14 June banks elected AOCI opt-out; securities OCI is not automatically an equal CET1 deduction. Credit losses can still affect capital through earnings.
