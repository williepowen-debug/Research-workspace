# One-time covered-bank securities and funding screen

**Authority:** Will approved “Run the bounded cohort screen” on October13,2026, after relaying PROME’s assessment. Existing14-bank cohort only; no national population re-run, new monitor, prediction prior, frozen grading rule, specialist launch or trade authority. Research completed October13 late evening ET. Balance-sheet observations are March31/June30, not October positions.

## Judgment

**The screen does not corroborate a new20%-of-equity loss cliff in these14 names.** For a common, hypothetical60bp parallel rise and unhedged five-year duration, additional AFS+HTM economic losses range from2.0% to8.8% of matched June bank GAAP equity before tax; at90bp the range is3.0%–13.2%. Those assumptions do not bound actual outcomes. Portfolio duration, hedges, changing balances, spreads and convexity can change results. No inference about the nationwide95-bank count follows.

**Amerant and Seacoast merit attention to securities marks in their existing Q3 reads; BankUnited is useful contrary evidence.** Amerant has a high securities/equity ratio and disclosed4.7-year duration; Seacoast has5.2-year AFS duration and no securities fair-value hedges duringH1. Seacoast’s tangible-equity denominator makes the exposure more consequential than its GAAP-equity ratio alone suggests. BankUnited has the largest raw securities/equity ratio but only2.02-year duration. None establishes a forced-sale problem or mispricing. Their improving June funding evidence must travel with these findings.

WAL and FLG remain joint securities/funding/credit questions, with securities hedges and missing net durations. Zions’ large existing AOCI is partly a legacy transferred-HTM balance, not a measure of the next rate shock. EGBN’s deposit shrink and thin quarterly income make even smaller book marks consequential, but its named credit outcomes remain the existing decisive test. No blanket conclusion about option availability, prices or upside is supported.

**Disposition:** bounded screen complete; no new specialist or TERRY launch. WAL’s pricing-edge finding cannot be generalized to other banks or option markets.
