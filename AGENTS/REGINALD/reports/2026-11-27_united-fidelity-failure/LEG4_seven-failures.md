## Answer

**They are separate stories, with one thread running through them.** Real-estate credit is the stated or documented cause at 2 of the 7: Nano Banc and United Fidelity. Those two hold 90% of the failed assets ($6.65B of $7.36B) and 94% of the estimated fund cost ($1.94B of $2.07B). The other five failed on five different things: unsecured loans against private-company shares, a government-guaranteed lending push, earnings, mortgage-banking overhead, and a tiny thrift's losses. **The thread is not the kind of asset. Every bank was a long-known problem bank:** 6 of 7 had a formal enforcement action on record before closing (Kentland's history UNKNOWN), and at least 4 of 7 had an insider or management-dominance finding. None was a surprise failure of a bank regulators thought was sound.

**So what for the "is this spreading" question:** the 2026 failures do not show a common real-estate mechanism spreading across small banks. They show a supervisory pipeline of weak, concentrated, owner-dominated banks being closed one at a time; United Fidelity is the large one. The test that would change this is not more of these; it is a failure of a bank that had NO prior enforcement action, or a second bank whose book looks like United Fidelity's (DEWEY's leg 3 screen).

## Reading

1. **No common asset class.** Real estate is the cause at 2 of 7. The five others name unsecured and stock-secured loans (1), a guaranteed-lending push (2), small-thrift losses (3), operating losses (4) and mortgage-banking overhead (5). A shared real-estate cause, the condition that opens branch (b) of the plan, is NOT shown.
2. **A common governance thread.** Prior formal action at 6 of 7 (one unknown), insider or dominance findings at 4 of 7. These are banks examiners had flagged for years. That fits the FDIC problem-bank list (39 at 9/30) draining slowly, not new stress showing up at banks with clean records.
3. **Two scales.** The four small closures cost 7.5–8.1% of assets except Kentland and West Georgia (about 32%). The two real-estate failures cost 15.5% and 30.9%. Severity tracks how concentrated the book was and how it was booked, not the year.
4. **What would change the read:** a failure or formal action at a bank with no prior enforcement history; a second bank with a United Fidelity-shaped book (DEWEY's leg 3 screen); a West Georgia material loss review naming real-estate losses (it would move the count to 3 of 7, still not a pattern on its own).
