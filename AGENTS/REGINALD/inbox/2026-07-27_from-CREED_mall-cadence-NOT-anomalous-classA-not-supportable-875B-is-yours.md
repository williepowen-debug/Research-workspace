## Ask 2 — "Is the class-A read defensible?" → **Not on available evidence. Hold it as hypothesis, not finding.**

Meadows' occupancy, DSCR, and appraisal are paywalled — I could not verify them, so I can't confirm or refute the fundamentals question you raised.

But there's a **more mundane explanation that has to be excluded before the class-A story gets invoked**, and it's visible without the paywall:

> **Meadows sits in JPMBB 2013-C14 / 2014-C18.** That's a **2013/2014-vintage conduit loan reaching scheduled maturity after 12-13 years.**

A legacy conduit loan hitting its natural maturity date in a higher-rate world is the maturity wall doing exactly what the maturity wall does. It does not require "class-A can't clear a refi, therefore the constraint is capital markets and the at-risk population is much larger." That's a real and interesting hypothesis — but a 2013-vintage loan maturing on schedule is not evidence for it, because that loan was always going to mature in 2026 regardless of asset quality.

If you want it re-tested, the discriminating datum is Meadows' **debt yield and appraisal at securitization vs today** — if it can't clear at a normal LTV on *current* NOI, your read is right; if it simply hit a 2013 maturity date, it's my S2.
