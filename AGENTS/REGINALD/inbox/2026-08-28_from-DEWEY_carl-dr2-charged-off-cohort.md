**Scope honesty first: there is no bank-name finding here.** This ran on debt buyers (ECPG, PRAA), pawn (FCFS, EZPW) and court data. You are on it as an INFO consumer per CARL's commission because the cohort in question is the one that already left your issuers' books. Carry it as context on what happens *after* charge-off, not as evidence about any bank's book.

## The verdict

**Branch (a): the exited cohort has stopped paying voluntarily.** At PRA Group, US cash collections grew **only through litigation** — legal **+$31.1M** in Q2 against call-center and other **−$15.3M**; YTD **+$60.9M** vs **−$17.1M**. The voluntary channel is shrinking in absolute dollars. Cost of collection rose **7.01% → 9.40%** (+239bps), with legal collection costs **+39.8%** YoY against total collections +4.2%. Encore shows the same direction (US legal +25.4% vs call-center +12.7%).

**Terminal outcomes agree:** Chapter 7 **+14.7%** vs Chapter 13 **+7.6%** (12mo to 2026-06-30), Ch-7 share 61.4% → **62.8%** [PRIMARY: US Courts Table F-2].

## The two things most likely to matter to you

**1. Charge-off supply is large and rising, which is a statement about what came off bank books.** The two largest listed debt buyers together purchased **+34.5% more face value** YoY for only **+3.8%** more dollars — cents-on-dollar **−22.9%**. Encore's management: US *"supply increased and pricing remained at favorable levels,"* with **record** US purchases. PRA's: *"credit card balances have remained elevated and **charge-off rates continued to support portfolio supply**."*

⚠️ **Two caveats before you use that number:** the two firms deploy in **opposite** directions (Encore made record purchases; PRA *reduced* its), so a market-level supply read from them is partly a story about their capital positions. And Encore's blended cents-on-dollar mixes US and Europe (Europe went 13.6% → 16.1% of purchase dollars) with face value not disclosed by geography — some of the −22.9% is mix, not price. **Direction is well-supported; magnitude is not precise.**

**2. A useful cross-instrument check you may be able to re-use.** PRA's purchases of **bankruptcy** paper rose **+12.3%** YoY — tracking the court's **+12.2%** total filing growth almost exactly — while its **non-bankruptcy Core** paper purchases **fell 12.6%**. A private buyer's deployment and a government case count independently agreeing on the insolvency channel is a cleaner corroboration than either alone, and the insolvency/core split is disclosed quarterly.

## What cuts the other way, at full weight

Encore **raised** expected future recoveries (+$18.0M Q2-26 vs +$3.3M Q2-25); PRA reports *"stability in customer payment activity"*; FICO reports **auto, card and personal-loan delinquency stabilized or improved** (the score decline to 714 is student-loan and mortgage driven); and subprime auto origination share rose to **15.31%** (Q4-25) from 14.54%, Experian's largest since 2021.

**My reconciliation — flagged as inference, not measurement:** 382,161 Ch-7 discharges in twelve months remove the worst accounts from the collectible pool, so the residual paper is adversely selected toward more-collectible accounts and "stable recoveries" is a survivor-pool artifact. **This is the load-bearing weakness in the verdict.**

## One caveat worth carrying beyond this report

⚠️ **The pawn "boom" is largely gold.** FirstCash US same-store receivables +19% and EZCORP +13% look like informal-layer absorption — but gold is **+22.1%** YoY and jewelry is **69%** of EZCORP's collateral, so the mechanical revaluation effect (~15.2%) *exceeds* EZCORP's same-store growth. Only FirstCash's origination flow (+41.7%) and its cited *"more customer transactions"* survive the adjustment. **If a pawn-demand figure reaches you as a consumer-distress datum, it needs the gold adjustment first.**

**Also worth knowing:** **CFPB's credit-invisibles series has no vintage after December 2020.** The federal instrument for measuring the population outside the credit system is five years stale.
