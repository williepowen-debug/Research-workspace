## THE CREDIT-RELEVANT MECHANISM

The Record of Decision (Interior, signed 2026-08-21) cuts Lower Basin water **1.25 maf in each of 2027 and 2028 — AZ 760 kaf · CA 440 kaf · NV 50 kaf** — *"in accordance with a Lower Basin implementing agreement(s)."*

**Those agreements are not executed.** The ROD defines what happens if they never are, and **that path is the credit story**:

> *"In the event that shortages to the Lower Division States are required in the absence of a Lower Basin agreement, the Secretary will consult with the Lower Division States and Contractors. If no agreement is reached, the Secretary will **apportion shortages according to the Law of the River**…"*

**Apportionment by the Law of the River means by PRIORITY.** That is a materially different distribution from the negotiated 760/440/50 split: **California's senior rights are shielded and Arizona's junior CAP priority absorbs the shortage.** The negotiated split is a *sharing* outcome; the default is a *seniority* outcome. **Same total, very different incidence** — and the incidence is what touches AZ ag districts, CAP-dependent municipalities, and the state-fiscal picture.

## ⚠️ AND THE CLAUSE CARRIES AN APPARENT ERRATUM — reported, NOT adjudicated

The ROD states this same provision **twice, with opposite negation**:

- **§10.6 area:** *"…and the assumptions contained in the Final EIS **shall not** preclude or predetermine such apportionments."*
- **§10.7:** *"…and the modeling assumptions contained in the Final EIS **shall** preclude or predetermine such apportionments."*

The phrase `preclude or predetermine` occurs **8 times** in the document; **7 carry "not"**, including the near-identical twin sentence above. Read literally, the §10.7 instance reverses the document's stated policy everywhere else — i.e. it would make the EIS modelling *binding* on apportionment.

🔴 **I am not asserting Interior's intent, and you should not either.** I am telling you the signed decision contradicts itself on a load-bearing clause. **If any credit work turns on whether the EIS modelling constrains apportionment, that question is currently unsettled at the primary — do not build on the literal §10.7 reading alone.**
