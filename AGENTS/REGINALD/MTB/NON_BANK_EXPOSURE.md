## TL;DR — One Page

- **Total NDFI = $13.4B at Q1 2026, 10% of loans** (peer median 12%). Grew from $12.6B Q4 2025 (a $1.3B jump in Q4 driven partly by RECATEGORIZATION of C&I into NDFI — material disclosure-quality flag).
- **Three pillars = ~2/3 of NDFI:** fund banking/capital call lines, residential mortgage warehouse, institutional CRE/REIT lending. **Individual pillar sizes NOT disclosed.**
- **Smaller NDFI buckets disclosed on Q1 call (Slide 19):** $700M wholesale lender finance, $600M business leasing, $400M BDC loans.
- **Bayview Lending Group:** 20% equity stake, held at equity method, $300M original 2007 investment. $33M distribution in Q1 2026 = noninterest income. **BLG is small-balance CRE securitization/servicing, NOT residential non-QM.** This is a CRE transmission line, not a Ginnie/FHA MSR line.
- **Fund banking origin is ambiguous.** CFO Daryl Bible said on Q1 call "acquired from Webster" but also "grown from People's United size" — MTB never acquired Webster. People's United (closed Apr 2022) is the likely legacy source; "Webster" is either CFO misspeak or a sub-team reference.
- **No warehouse counterparties disclosed.** No mention of PFSI, PennyMac, LDI, RITM, COOP, Rocket, UWM, Apollo, Atlas SP, First Brands, MFS. MTB has the same disclosure opacity as WAL.
- **Back-door transmission channels identified:** (1) mortgage warehouse → same non-bank servicer ecosystem as Apollo Atlas SP, (2) BDC $400M → Blue Owl / Ares / Blackstone Credit pool (names not given, but MTB flagged <15% software concentration), (3) wholesale lender finance $700M → consumer/specialty non-bank lenders (opaque), (4) Bayview equity → small-balance CRE securitization cycle exposure.

## 1. BDC / Private Credit Exposure

- **Direct BDC loans: $400M** (disclosed Q1 2026 call, confirmed on Slide 19).
- **No names disclosed.** Management stated "Software exposure within our BDC portfolio is less than 15%" — implies they lend to diversified public BDCs (Blue Owl OBDC, Ares ARCC, Blackstone Credit BXSL, Golub GBDC, Main Street MAIN are all <15% software).
- **No equity stakes or JVs with BDC managers** disclosed.
- **Commitments vs drawn** — not broken out. MTB did say "advance rates vary but are calibrated to asset quality, historical recovery data, and collateral performance" → suggests structured NAV-based lending, not balance-sheet unsecured.
- **Back-door transmission risk: LOW-MEDIUM.** $400M is small in MTB context. If BDCs mass-gate or PIK explodes (BROCK's thesis), MTB's direct exposure is contained. But they likely have indirect warehouse exposure to BDC SPVs that is not separately categorized.

## 2. Wholesale Lender Finance ($700M)

- **Disclosed as "business credit intermediaries"** on Q1 Slide 19.
- **Who the borrowers are: NOT disclosed.** Typical wholesale lender finance counterparties: non-bank consumer finance (Oportun, Enova), specialty small-business lenders, equipment finance subsidiaries, fintech balance-sheet lenders (Affirm/Upstart-type SPVs), insurance premium finance.
- **Structure:** Management cited "borrowing bases, independent valuations, field exams" — implies secured-against-portfolio advance-rate structure.
- **Back-door transmission risk: MEDIUM-HIGH.** This is the bucket most analogous to **MFS UK-template fund finance fraud**. If any counterparty mis-states receivables or pledges same collateral twice (First Brands template), MTB is exposed. No single-name disclosure makes diligence impossible from outside.

## 3. Mortgage Warehouse

- **Book size: NOT separately disclosed.** Bundled into "~2/3 of NDFI" with fund banking + REIT lending. Inference: ~$2-4B range.
- **Counterparties: NOT disclosed.** No mention of PFSI, PennyMac, LDI, RITM/NewRez, COOP, Rocket, UWM.
- **Concentration: NOT disclosed.**
- **Management tone:** "Really safe credit business if you run strong operations." Standard warehouse talking points — 98% advance rates against saleable loans, haircuts on non-conforming, short duration.
- **Back-door transmission risk: HIGH.** Mortgage warehouse is **the exact channel where Apollo's Atlas SP built $6.9B / 78% concentration to PFSI.** MTB runs an older, more-diversified-by-reputation warehouse book, but without counterparty disclosure we cannot rule out exposure to stressed non-banks. The MTB warehouse is a **plausible next landing point** if PFSI, LDI, or other Atlas SP counterparties move books.
- **CRITICAL FOLLOW-UP:** Pull MTB FY2025 10-K Schedule of Financing Receivables and FFIEC Call Report Schedule RC-C Item 9 subcategories when filed. The 10-K may give warehouse concentration disclosure even if the earnings call does not.

## 4. Fund Banking / Capital Call Lines

- **Book size: NOT separately disclosed** (part of ~2/3 NDFI bucket).
- **Origin:** CFO said **"acquired from Webster"** on Q1 call — but MTB has NEVER acquired Webster Bank (Webster is a separate public company, WBS). MTB acquired **People's United** (Apr 2022, $8.3B). CFO's follow-up "grown from People's United size" suggests this is the legacy People's United fund banking book and "Webster" was misspeak or a reference to a team leader surname. **REGINALD FLAG: confirm with 10-K acquired-business disclosure.**
- **LP/GP counterparties: NONE disclosed.** Standard subscription-line structure lends against LP capital commitments for buyout/secondaries/PE funds. Private-credit fund sponsors (Apollo/Ares/Blackstone/KKR/Blue Owl) all heavily use subscription lines.
- **Back-door transmission risk: MEDIUM.** Subscription lines are typically safe (secured by LP commitments from investment-grade institutions), but the **MFS UK template** showed that subscription-line fraud can occur when the same LP commitment is pledged across multiple lenders or when LPs default. MTB gives zero transparency into sponsor mix.

## 5. REIT Lending (Institutional CRE)

- **Balance: NOT broken out** (part of ~2/3 NDFI bucket).
- **Type: "Institutional CRE, primarily REIT lending"** per Q1 call.
- **Public names: NONE disclosed.** Could be equity REITs (lending against stabilized CRE portfolios) or mortgage REITs. Unlikely to be heavily mortgage REITs at a conservative shop like MTB — probably equity REIT corporate revolvers/bridge.
- **Management: "Portfolio that's been growing nicely and will continue to grow."** Rare positive-growth language on a CRE-adjacent book — contrast with MTB's overall CRE shrink story ($24.1B down from $29B).
- **Back-door transmission risk: LOW-MEDIUM.** Equity-REIT corporate lending is generally resilient; mortgage-REIT lending would be higher-risk but there's no evidence MTB is in that lane.

## 6. Bayview Lending Group

- **Stake: 20% minority equity, acquired Feb 2007 for $300M.**
- **Accounting: equity method** (income flows through noninterest income as distributions/equity earnings).
- **Q1 2026 distribution: $33M** (Q3 2025: $20M). Distributions are lumpy but material to MTB's fee income in quarters they occur.
- **What Bayview is:** Per SEC filings, "a privately-held commercial mortgage lender that specializes in originating, securitizing and servicing **small balance commercial real estate loans** in the United States." **NOT a residential non-QM originator. NOT a Ginnie/FHA MSR holder.**
- **Bayview-parent (Bayview Asset Management) has a broader footprint** including some residential exposure and **a 2008 Blackstone stake sale** — but MTB's stake is specifically in **Bayview LENDING Group LLC (BLG)**, the small-balance CRE arm.
- **Back-door transmission risk: MEDIUM (CRE cycle, not residential).** If small-balance CRE defaults accelerate (CREED thesis — 2020-2022 vintage 5-year maturity wall hitting now), Bayview distributions fall → MTB noninterest income takes a hit. Not a warehouse-style counterparty credit risk, but a fee-income resilience risk. **NOT a back-door to PFSI/Ginnie MSR stress.**

## 7. NDFI Total (Schedule RC-C Item 9 Basis)

- **Total: $13.4B at Mar 31, 2026** (10% of loans).
- **Growth: $12.6B Q4 2025 → $13.4B Q1 2026 = +$0.8B QoQ.**
- **Q4 jump: +$1.3B from Q3 → Q4 2025, partly recategorization of C&I into NDFI.** This is a transparency negative — MTB is moving loans between buckets, echoing the WAL C&I-relabeling-as-CRE concern (MI3 discovery) but in the opposite direction.
- **Management characterization (Q1 call):** Three core portfolios are "low loss profile," NDFI is "smaller than peers," "visibility into collateral is strong." Confident tone. No acknowledgment of sector concern post-First Brands / MFS.

## 8. Management Tone / Disclosure Quality

- **Disclosure quality: POOR on counterparties, MEDIUM on aggregate.** MTB breaks out 3 small buckets ($700M / $600M / $400M) and gives 2/3 concentration in three pillars — but does NOT size the three pillars individually or name a single counterparty. Same pattern as WAL.
- **Management did not address:** Apollo Atlas SP concentration news, MFS UK fund-finance fraud, First Brands, private credit default cycle.
- **Management tone:** Defensive-confident. CFO Bible proactively front-ran NDFI questions on prepared remarks, suggesting they knew investors would ask. No tonal stress or hedging.
- **No volunteered commentary on non-bank mortgage servicer stress.** Silence is itself a signal — either MTB is not exposed to stressed servicers (good), or they don't see a reason to disclose (opacity).

## 9. Acquisition History Check

- **People's United (closed Apr 2, 2022, $8.3B all-stock):** Most likely origin of fund banking book. People's United had commercial capabilities including "capital market capabilities" and commercial lending to PE-backed middle market.
- **Webster Bank (WBS):** **Never acquired by MTB.** CFO's Q1 transcript reference to "acquired from Webster" is either (a) misspeak for People's United, (b) reference to a team/person surname Webster, or (c) a sub-business acquired from Webster Financial that is not widely documented. **REGINALD CANNOT VERIFY "from Webster" CLAIM** — flag to Will as disclosure anomaly.
- **REGINALD's original hypothesis (Webster acquisition = fund banking):** **REJECTED** at bank-entity level. Corrected to People's United attribution with Webster-wording flagged.
