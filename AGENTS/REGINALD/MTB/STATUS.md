## POST-EARNINGS VERDICT (Apr 15)

**Headline-beat, tape-fade.** EPS beat by 10¢, NCO improved, but stock -1.55% on elevated volume — the market did not reward the print. Three things likely drove the fade:

1. **CET1 dropped 51bps Q4→Q1 (10.84% → 10.33%)** — capital consumption material despite shrinking CRE. Cat III/IV AOCI rewrite (Jun 18) makes this worse.
2. **NDFI grew $12.6B → $13.4B QoQ**, and the prior Q4 jump was partly C&I-to-NDFI recategorization — a disclosure-quality negative, not clean growth.
3. **No counterparty transparency** on mortgage warehouse / fund banking / REIT lending despite ~$8.9B in those three pillars (see `NON_BANK_EXPOSURE.md`). Same opacity as WAL.

**Read-through framing:** The "turning the corner on CRE" narrative partially held (NCO 31bps down from 54bps, criticized CRE presumably still declining), but NDFI is where the pressure is shifting. Banks that leaned into NDFI to offset CRE shrink (MTB, WAL) now face the same opacity question.

## Why MTB Matters

MTB is the first major regional to report Q1 2026. It sets the tone for the entire earnings wave (CFG+KEY Apr 16, RF Apr 17, WAL+OZK Apr 21).

- **Bull read (stock beat, NCO down):** "CRE is peaking" narrative gets partial cover. Thesis names need new catalysts.
- **Bear read (stock faded, CET1 down, NDFI growing, warehouse opacity):** The de-risking story isn't free — capital is being consumed and opacity is shifting channels. WAL/OZK have less capital buffer to absorb the same pattern.

**Our read is bear-leaning.** The tape (-1.55% on a beat) is the strongest signal. Market is discounting the EPS beat for the balance sheet concerns. If the MTB poster-child of de-risking can't sell its own story, the story is harder at names that didn't de-risk.

## CRE De-Risking Story — Partial Validation

- Pre-Q1: CRE 183% → 148% → 128% of Tier 1 (18 months). Sold $661M residential builder portfolio Jun 2025. Criticized CRE declining ~$671M/qtr.
- Q1 read: NCO improvement (54→31bps) validates the de-risk was real. But CET1 dropped 51bps simultaneously — the de-risk isn't free capital.
- NDFI is the offset: as CRE shrinks, NDFI grows ($12.6B → $13.4B), with Q4's $1.3B jump partly from recategorization.

**Sector implication:** OZK hasn't meaningfully shrunk RESG; WAL is relabeling MI3 (15.5% → 24.2%). MTB shows the de-risk path leaves scars even when executed well. OZK/WAL haven't paid those scars yet.

## NDFI Deep-Dive

Full deep-dive in **`NON_BANK_EXPOSURE.md`** (169 lines, Apr 15). Headline conclusions:

- **Total NDFI $13.4B / 10% of loans** (peer median 12%)
- Three undisclosed pillars (~$8.9B): **fund banking + mortgage warehouse + REIT lending**
- Disclosed smaller buckets: $700M wholesale lender finance, $600M leasing, $400M BDC
- **Highest back-door risk:** mortgage warehouse (Apollo Atlas SP adjacency — MTB is plausible next landing point if PFSI flips) and wholesale lender finance (MFS template)
- **Bayview Lending Group:** 20% equity stake, CRE-securitization servicer, **NOT residential MSR** (prior hypothesis rejected). Q1 $33M distribution.
- **Disclosure anomaly:** CFO said fund banking "acquired from Webster" — MTB never acquired Webster. Likely People's United (Apr 2022). Flag.
