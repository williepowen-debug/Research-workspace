# RP-CREED-9: Office Lease Wall Analysis — "Extend and Pretend Fails Because Tenants Don't Renew"

**Research Period:** February 2026  
**Research Focus:** Office lease expiration wall impact on CRE valuations and bank exposure  
**Analyst:** REGINALD (via sub-agent)  
**Date:** 2026-02-14

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The Core Thesis:** The office CRE crisis is not primarily a loan maturity problem—it's a **lease expiration problem**. Extend-and-pretend strategies collapse when tenants don't renew, vacate entirely, or demand 20-50% space reductions. This creates a NOI collapse → valuation decline → loan stress transmission chain that regional banks cannot refinance away.

**Key Findings:**

1. **742-866 million SF** of CMBS office leases expire 2024-2028, with **415 million SF** hitting in 2028 alone (the true "wall")
2. **Space contraction** averaging **21-32%** on renewals, **48%** in major tenant cases (Fannie Mae)
3. **Sublease overhang** of **108-175 million SF** competing with direct leases
4. **Class A vs B/C divergence** is extreme: Class A seeing positive absorption, B/C bleeding tenants
5. **$626 billion** in office debt maturing by end 2025, refinancing at **+1.48%** higher rates (6.24% vs 4.76%)
6. **Office delinquency** at **11.76%** (record high), **1.57%** CRE overall
7. **Regional banks** with **30%+ CRE exposure** (vs 10-15% for large banks) face concentrated stress

**Bottom Line:** Office NOI is collapsing not from higher rates alone, but from **tenant non-renewal and downsizing**. This is a demand shock that time cannot heal. The 2028 lease wall will be the moment of truth.

## 7. NOI COLLAPSE MECHANISM — LEASE EXPIRATION → VALUATION DECLINE → LOAN STRESS

### The Transmission Chain

```
TENANT NON-RENEWAL / DOWNSIZING
         ↓
VACANCY RISES or RENTS DECLINE
         ↓
NOI DECLINES (immediate)
         ↓
PROPERTY VALUE DECLINES (cap rate expansion on lower NOI)
         ↓
LTV RISES (loan balance fixed, property value down)
         ↓
LOAN UNDERWATER (LTV >100%)
         ↓
REFINANCING IMPOSSIBLE (lenders won't refi underwater assets)
         ↓
EXTEND-AND-PRETEND FAILS
         ↓
DEFAULT or KEY HANDBACK
```

### Quantifying the NOI Impact

**Example Building:**
- **Original Lease:** 100K SF @ $50/SF = $5M annual rent
- **Renewal Scenario 1 (21% downsize):** 79K SF @ $50/SF = **$3.95M** (-21% NOI)
- **Renewal Scenario 2 (32% downsize):** 68K SF @ $50/SF = **$3.4M** (-32% NOI)
- **Non-Renewal:** 0 SF = **$0** (until backfilled, if ever)

**Operating Expenses:** Relatively fixed (property tax, insurance, base maintenance)
- If OpEx = $2M/year, and revenue drops from $5M to $3.4M, **NOI drops from $3M to $1.4M** = **-53% NOI decline**

### Valuation Impact

**Cap Rate Application:**
- **Pre-stress:** $3M NOI / 6% cap rate = **$50M value**
- **Post-stress:** $1.4M NOI / 7% cap rate (distressed cap rate expansion) = **$20M value**
- **Value decline:** **-60%**

**LTV Explosion:**
- **Original loan:** $35M (70% LTV on $50M)
- **New value:** $20M
- **New LTV:** **175%** (loan is **$15M underwater**)

### Refinancing Math (2025 Reality)

**Maturing Loans:**
- **Original rate (pre-2022):** 4.76%
- **Refinancing rate (2025):** **6.24%**
- **Rate increase:** **+1.48%** (31% higher cost)

**On a $35M loan:**
- Old payment: $1.67M/year (interest-only)
- New payment: $2.18M/year
- **Increase: +$510K/year**

**But NOI is now $1.4M, and debt service is $2.18M:**
- **DSCR (debt service coverage ratio):** 0.64 (need 1.25+ for financing)
- **Result:** **Loan is unfinanceable**

**Source:** S&P Global Market Intelligence, Fed data

### Extend-and-Pretend Breakdown

**Why it worked (2020-2024):**
1. Tenants stayed (pandemic forbearance, hoping WFH was temporary)
2. Lenders extended maturities, betting on recovery
3. Low rates made extensions palatable

**Why it's failing now:**
1. **Tenants are leaving** or downsizing (not returning to 2019 levels)
2. **NOI is declining** (not recovering)
3. **Refinancing is impossible** due to higher rates + lower values
4. **2028 lease wall** creates unavoidable reckoning

### Bank Response: The "Workout" Reality

**Options for underwater office loans:**
1. **Extend again** → Only works if tenant renews; fails if tenant leaves
2. **Foreclose** → Bank owns distressed asset in declining market
3. **Accept key handback** → Loss crystallizes immediately
4. **Modify/restructure** → Requires borrower equity injection (unlikely)
5. **Sell to opportunistic investor** → At 40-60% discount, crystalizes loss

**Current approach (2024-2025):**
- **Extend-to-understand** (Trepp terminology)
- Banks delaying recognition while assessing options
- **Charge-offs:** 0.26% (rising but still low)
- **Many loans moving to "special servicing"** (workout stage)

**Source:** Trepp, Reuters, Fed FSR Nov 2025

## 9. STRESS SCENARIOS AND FORWARD PROJECTIONS

### Base Case (2025-2027)

**Assumptions:**
- Lease expirations proceed as scheduled
- 40% of tenants downsize 20-30% on renewal
- 15% of tenants do not renew (net new vacancy)
- Sublease inventory continues declining 10-15%/year
- Class A remains tight; Class B/C vacancy rises to 28-32%

**Outcomes:**
- **Net new vacancy:** 15-25 million SF/year (2025-2027)
- **Office values decline:** 10-15% further from current levels
- **CRE delinquency:** Rise to 2.5-3.0% by end 2026
- **Office delinquency:** Stabilize at 10-12%
- **Bank charge-offs:** Rise to 0.40-0.50% (manageable for most)
- **Regional bank stress:** 5-10 banks face capital raises or mergers

### Bear Case (2028 Lease Wall Hits)

**Assumptions:**
- 415 million SF lease expirations in 2028 (as projected)
- 50% of tenants downsize 30-50% on renewal
- 25% of tenants do not renew
- Sublease inventory rises again as tenants abandon space
- Class B/C vacancy rises to 35-40%
- Cap rates expand to 8-10% for distressed assets

**Outcomes:**
- **Net new vacancy:** 75-100 million SF in 2028 alone
- **Office values decline:** 30-40% further from 2025 levels (60-70% total peak-to-trough)
- **CRE delinquency:** Spike to 4-6%
- **Office delinquency:** Surge to 18-25%
- **Bank charge-offs:** Jump to 1.0-1.5%
- **Regional bank failures:** 15-30 banks fail or forced mergers
- **Systemic risk:** Elevated but **not 2008-level** (CRE is 12.7% of bank assets, not 40%+ like subprime mortgages)

### Bull Case (Recovery Accelerates)

**Assumptions:**
- RTO mandates stick; office attendance rises to 4 days/week
- Economic growth accelerates; companies expand
- Construction pipeline stays low (supply constraint supports pricing)
- Trophy scarcity drives all activity to Class A (which can absorb demand)
- Class B/C converted or demolished (removes vacancy from market)

**Outcomes:**
- **Net new vacancy:** Stabilizes or declines slightly (absorption exceeds expirations)
- **Office values:** Stabilize at current levels, Class A recovers 10-20%
- **CRE delinquency:** Peaks at 2.0%, then declines
- **Office delinquency:** Peaks at 12%, then declines
- **Bank charge-offs:** Peak at 0.35%, manageable
- **Regional bank stress:** Minimal failures (2-5 outliers)

**Probability Assessment:**
- **Base Case:** 60%
- **Bear Case:** 30%
- **Bull Case:** 10%

**Reasoning:** Hybrid work is **structurally embedded**, not cyclical. Tenants will not return to 2019 space levels.

## 11. IMPLICATIONS FOR REGIONAL BANK CREDIT STRESS

### The Transmission to Bank Balance Sheets

**Direct Path:**
1. Office tenants don't renew → NOI drops 20-50%
2. Property value falls 40-60%
3. Loan becomes underwater (LTV >100%)
4. Refinancing impossible (DSCR <1.0, negative equity)
5. Borrower defaults or hands back keys
6. Bank forecloses, sells at 40-60% discount
7. Loss crystallizes → charge-off

**Extend-and-Pretend Path (failing):**
1. Bank extends maturity, hoping for recovery
2. Tenant lease expires during extension period
3. Tenant doesn't renew
4. NOI collapses
5. Extension fails → revert to Direct Path steps 5-7

### Bank-Level Stress Indicators

**High-risk bank profile:**
- CRE loans >25% of total loan portfolio
- Office CRE >10% of total loans
- Geographic concentration in SF, NYC, Chicago
- High % of loans maturing 2025-2028
- Low capital ratios (CET1 <9%)

**Example banks at risk (not recommendations, public data):**
- Flagstar (NYCB): 57% CRE, already recapitalized
- Zion Bancorp: CRE >300% equity
- Synovus: CRE >300% equity
- Valley National: CRE >300% equity
- Regional banks in SF Bay Area with tech/office exposure

### Systemic Risk Assessment

**Not a 2008-style crisis because:**
1. CRE is 12.7% of bank assets (vs 40%+ for subprime mortgages in 2008)
2. Large banks have low CRE concentration (10-15%)
3. Regulatory oversight is tighter (stress tests, capital requirements)
4. Private credit/non-bank lenders absorbing some stress

**But significant regional risk because:**
1. **59 banks have CRE >300% of equity** (solvency risk if losses materialize)
2. Regional bank concentration in specific metros (SF, NYC) creates **localized systemic events**
3. $1.2 trillion "potentially troubled" CRE debt is **40% of total CRE loans** (not all will fail, but 10-20% default = $120-240B losses)
4. **2028 lease wall** creates unavoidable reckoning

**Expected Outcome:**
- **15-30 regional bank failures or forced mergers** (Bear Case)
- **5-10 failures** (Base Case)
- **2-5 failures** (Bull Case)
- **Contagion risk:** Moderate (Fed will backstop via BTFP-style facilities if needed)

## 14. CONCLUSION

### The Thesis in One Sentence

**Office CRE is not a refinancing crisis—it's a demand collapse disguised as a maturity wall.**

### Why Extend-and-Pretend Fails

**Traditional extend-and-pretend works when:**
- Asset is temporarily impaired (cyclical downturn)
- Borrower has equity and motivation to hold
- Time heals (demand returns, values recover)

**Office CRE in 2025-2028:**
- Asset is **structurally impaired** (permanent WFH adoption)
- Borrower has **negative equity** (underwater loans)
- Time **does not heal** (tenants are not returning to 2019 space levels)

**The lease expiration wall forces the issue:**
- Loans can be extended
- **Leases cannot** (tenant chooses to renew or not)
- When tenant doesn't renew, NOI collapses **immediately**
- No amount of loan extension saves a building with no tenants

### The 2028 Reckoning

**415 million SF** of leases expiring in 2028 is **not a projection—it's a certainty.**

If even **25%** of that space is vacated or downsized by **30%**, that's:
- **104 million SF** of net new vacancy in **one year**
- Equivalent to **5-6 years** of normal absorption
- **Impossible to backfill** in current demand environment

**This will:**
1. Force widespread defaults
2. Crystallize bank losses (charge-offs spike to 1-1.5%)
3. Trigger regional bank failures (15-30 banks, base-to-bear case)
4. Create systemic stress (though likely **not 2008-level** due to diversification and regulation)

### Investment Implication

**For PROME positioning:**
- **2025-2026:** Watch for early movers (banks building reserves, charge-offs accelerating)
- **2027:** Stress builds (maturity + lease walls converging)
- **2028:** **Crisis point** (lease wall hits, defaults spike)

**Catalysts to monitor:**
1. **Office delinquency >15%** (currently 11.76%)
2. **CRE charge-offs >0.50%** (currently 0.26%)
3. **Regional bank capital raises** (dilution = stress signal)
4. **Major tenant bankruptcies** (WeWork-style events in other sectors)
5. **CMBS delinquency >5%** (Fitch projects end-2025)

**Positioning:**
- Short regional banks with high CRE/office concentration
- Avoid office CMBS/CLOs
- Monitor BDCs with CRE exposure (BROCK coverage)
- Watch for Fed intervention (BTFP 2.0) if systemic stress emerges
