## 1. Trepp CMBS Special Servicing (January 2026)

**Overall CMBS:**
- Special servicing rate: **10.91%** (+20 bps MoM)
- CMBS 2.0 (post-GFC): **10.84%**
- CMBS 1.0 (legacy pre-GFC): **60.87%**
- Total CMBS 2.0 in special servicing: **$63.6B**
- January transfers to special servicing: **$2.3B** across 38 loans

**By Property Type:**
- **Office: 17.11%** (+47 bps MoM) — 59% of January transfers
- **Retail: 11.76%** (-23 bps MoM)
- **Multifamily: 8.14%** (+6 bps MoM)

**Key Context:**
- Special servicing ≠ delinquency (different metrics)
- Special servicing includes maturity defaults, covenant breaches, distressed borrowers (pre-default)
- Office special servicing rising faster than overall — stress concentrating

## 3. Willis Tower Case Study (Blackstone/Chicago)

**Capital Structure:**
- Total investment since 2015: **$2B+**
- CMBS debt outstanding: **$1.3B**
- 2024 appraisal: **$1.03B** ← UNDERWATER by ~$270M
- **LTV:** ~126% (debt exceeds value)

**Operational Performance:**
- NOI: **$129.8M** annually
- Occupancy: **89%**
- **Operating fine but can't exit at break-even**

**Current Status:**
- Loan extended to **2028** (maturity pushed out)
- Blackstone exploring:
  - Asset split (separate trophy floors from base building?)
  - REIT conversion
  - Partner search (equity injection)
  - Lender handback (strategic default)

**Significance:**
- Even institutional sponsors with strong operations can't exit underwater
- Negative equity trap: $2B invested, worth $1.03B
- Extension to 2028 = adding to the 2028 maturity wall concentration
- NOI coverage is fine (debt service OK) but principal repayment impossible
