# RQ-CREED-003: Refinancing Capacity Gap Analysis

**Date:** 2026-01-27 | **Status:** COMPLETE

---

## THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Can the market refinance $936B in maturing CRE debt? **Mostly yes, but with $140-250B in forced recognition.**

---

## CHANNEL-BY-CHANNEL CAPACITY

| Channel | 2026 Capacity Est. | Confidence | Notes |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|-------|
| **Banks** | $275-325B | High | Run rate ~$303B annualized. SLOOS: standards stabilizing Oct 2025. |
| **CMBS** | $155-183B | High | KBRA: $183B (post-GFC high). Deutsche: $155B. |
| **CRE CLO** | $40B | High | KBRA projection. Near 2021 peak ($45B). |
| **Life Insurance** | $76-96B | Medium | 16% market share. Growing 6.1% YoY. Conservative LTV. |
| **Agency (GSEs)** | $176B | High | FHFA caps: $88B each Fannie/Freddie (+20% from 2025). MF only. |
| **Private Credit** | $200-300B | Medium | $585B dry powder. 37% of non-agency closings. 72% surge Q4 2024. |
| **Foreign Capital** | $20-30B | Low | Marginal. More equity than debt focused. |
| **TOTAL THEORETICAL** | **$942B-$1,150B** | | |

### Realistic Capacity: $500-700B
MBA forecast: $709B total originations (refi + new). Refi share ~60-70% = **$425-496B.**
The theoretical-to-realistic gap reflects competition for same deals, risk limits, and deployment speed.

## WHAT HAPPENS TO THE GAP (~$336B BASE CASE)

| Outcome | Est. % | Amount | Description |
|---------|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Extensions/Modifications** | 45-50% | $151-168B | Push out 1-3yr. Many already extended once — final extension. |
| **Equity Injection + Refi** | 20-25% | $67-84B | Owner injects 10-20% of loan balance to bridge LTV gap. |
| **Discounted Payoff / Note Sale** | 10-15% | $34-50B | Lender accepts less than par. |
| **Foreclosure / REO** | 10-15% | $34-50B | Concentrated in office + overleveraged 2021-22 MF. |
| **Default / Workout** | 5-10% | $17-34B | In process; outcome not yet determined. |

### By Property Type
- **Office:** 20-30% foreclosure/REO rate. 44% in negative equity.
- **Multifamily:** 5-10% distress rate. Agency backstop ($176B) covers most qualifying borrowers.
- **Retail/Industrial:** 3-7% distress rate. Strongest fundamentals.

---

## RATE DIFFERENTIAL IMPACT

**148bps gap** (6.24% new vs 4.76% maturing) = **16.3% debt service increase.**

| Original DSCR | Refinanced DSCR | Passes 1.25x? |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| 1.50x | 1.29x | Yes (barely) |
| 1.40x | 1.20x | No |
| 1.30x | 1.12x | No |
| 1.25x | 1.07x | No |
| 1.20x | 1.03x | No |
| 1.10x | 0.95x | **Cash flow negative** |

**30-40% of otherwise refinanceable loans fail DSCR tests** even with willing lender.

To make the math work: borrower needs 17% NOI growth OR 20-30% equity injection.

---

## FORCED RECOGNITION ESTIMATE

**$140-250B over 2026-2027**, concentrated in:
- Office (structural, permanent repricing)
- Regional/community banks with >300% CRE/equity (1,788 banks)
- 2021-2022 vintage overleveraged deals

---

## WHY THIS IS NOT 2008

1. Banks better capitalized post-Dodd-Frank
2. No derivatives contagion (CRE losses stay in CRE)
3. Residential market healthy
4. Office is 15-20% of CRE, not 100%
5. Agency backstop for MF ($176B)
6. Private credit has $585B dry powder
7. Bank charge-offs at 0.3% (near historic lows)

## WHY THIS IS STILL SERIOUS

1. $140-250B forced recognition = largest since 2009-2012
2. ~2,000 banks at risk of failure
3. Office is a permanent structural repricing, not cyclical
4. Extended loans compound into 2027 ($1.26T peak)
5. 30-40% DSCR failure rate on refinanceable loans

---

## THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT VARIABLE

**Fed rate path.** Each 50bps cut = ~$50-75B additional refinancing capacity (DSCR relief). Fed currently at 3.50-3.75%; dot plot suggests 1 more cut 2026.

If Fed cuts 100-150bps → gap narrows to 15-20% = manageable stress.
If rates hold → gap stays at 36% = significant forced recognition.

---

## VERDICT

**Survivable at systemic level. Not survivable for office or ~2,000 regional banks.**

The maturity wall will not cause systemic financial collapse but will force selective recognition of $140-250B in losses. Credit markets are reopening (CMBS spreads 77bps, CLO +374%) fast enough to handle most of the wall. The residual — concentrated in office, Sunbelt MF, and FL condos — faces genuine reckoning in H2 2026 through 2027.

**CREED's revised thesis:** "Selective reckoning, not systemic collapse. H2 2026 is the forcing window."
