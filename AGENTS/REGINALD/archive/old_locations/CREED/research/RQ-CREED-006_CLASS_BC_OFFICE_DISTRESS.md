# RQ-CREED-006: Class B/C Office Distress Quantification

**Date:** 2026-01-27 | **Status:** COMPLETE (partial — class-level CMBS data requires Trepp subscription)

---

## HEADLINE

Office market bifurcation is extreme and accelerating. Trophy captured **55% of Manhattan leasing** (up from 38% in 2024). Class B share: only **18%**. SF trophy vacancy 15.3% vs overall 35.1% = **20pp gap**. Chicago trophy rents up **26% since 2020** vs broader market up **1%**. Class B/C obsolescence is largely **permanent** for bottom tier.

## 5. NOI VIABILITY

### Breakeven Occupancy (Class B/C)
- Typical Class B: ~**75-80%** occupancy needed for NOI breakeven (with operating expenses $15-20/SF)
- At current Class B vacancy of 20-25% nationally (= 75-80% occupancy), many buildings are **at or below breakeven**
- In worst metros (SF, Chicago, Austin, Seattle): Class B/C well below breakeven

### Capex for B-to-A Repositioning
- Comprehensive renovation: **$100-200/SF**
- Trophy-level repositioning: **$250+/SF**
- At these costs, many B/C buildings cannot justify the investment given current rents

### Permanent vs. Cyclical Obsolescence
Evidence strongly supports **permanent obsolescence for bottom-tier B/C:**
- Described as "massive glut of useless older office buildings that won't easily go away"
- CBRE projects increased conversion and demolition to remove vacant, outdated space
- Flight to quality accelerating (trophy Manhattan share: 38% → 55% in one year)
- However: best-located, most adaptable Class B may recover as trophy becomes scarce
- NAIOP/JLL note selective spillover into Class B in tight trophy markets

### Suburban vs CBD
- CBD distressed transactions tripled in 2024 (faster acceleration than suburban)
- Class B/C suburban historically underperforms in recovery (lack of transit, amenities)

### Where Class B/C is Performing
- Manhattan: selective spillover (18% share, up modestly)
- AI-driven demand in SF could eventually tighten market enough to benefit B (not yet materialized)
- Markets with tight supply + strong population growth (Miami, Nashville) may see better B performance (UNCONFIRMED)

## 7. CONVERSION POTENTIAL

- **7 of 10** converted apartments in 2024 came from **Class A** buildings (counterintuitive — B/C floor plates often unsuitable)
- **10-15%** of Class B/C stock physically suitable for residential conversion (Gensler estimate)
- Conversion cost: **$250,000-$400,000 per unit**
- Break-even rent: **$3,000-$5,000/month** in high-cost markets
- Policy programs: NYC (Office Conversion Accelerator, City of Yes), DC (tax abatements), Chicago (LaSalle Street Reimagined), CA (AB 1490), Federal (GSA surplus buildings)

## CREED IMPLICATIONS

1. Office bifurcation is the most extreme in CRE — two completely different markets under one label
2. Trophy is 2-4% of stock but captures 55% of leasing = extreme concentration
3. Class B (40-45% of stock) is the swing factor — selective recovery possible for best-located buildings
4. Class C (15-20% of stock) faces permanent obsolescence — conversion/demolition only path
5. CMBS office DQ at 11.76% is predominantly B/C — the 11.31% figure understates B/C distress
6. Geographic concentration: SF, Austin, Chicago, Seattle are worst — all tech-heavy metros hit by remote work
7. Conversion is real but limited: only 10-15% of B/C suitable, and costs $250K-$400K/unit
