## Why We Track FITB

Fifth Third Bancorp (NASDAQ: FITB, ~$214B assets, CIK 0000035527) is a super-regional headquartered in Cincinnati. Three reasons it's on the REGINALD tracking list despite not being a position name:

### 1. Tricolor / Auto NDFI Read-Through (Primary Reason)

FITB has the **largest indirect secured consumer (auto) book in our cohort relative to assets** — $17.9B period-end 4Q25 (up from $16.1B 4Q24, +11%). Portfolio composition:
- 49% weighted toward 750+ FICO (prime)
- 19% 720-749
- 32% lower-tier, including specialty RV/Marine
- WAL FICO 772, WAL LTV 88%
- 4Q25 NCO 0.59% (down from 0.66% 4Q24)
- 30-89 DQ 0.72% (down from 0.80%)

Tricolor (subprime auto originator) collapsed in late 2025 and raised concerns about bank auto-ABS exposure and reserve adequacy. FITB's NDFI Slide 19 shows 31% of the $9.5B NDFI book is "Consumer Warehouse / Consumer Finance Securitization Vehicles" — **potential auto ABS conduit**. FITB is the cleanest post-Tricolor tell because:
- Large direct auto book = barometer for prime auto credit trajectory
- Consumer warehouse NDFI = proxy for bank-to-ABS-originator funding
- FITB reports Apr 17 — FIRST in cohort with meaningful direct auto disclosure after the Tricolor event

If FITB's 1Q26 provision or NCO shows an auto / consumer-warehouse bump, it is a transmission signal to other banks with similar warehouse exposure (CFG, WAL, RF, HBAN).

### 2. Sponsor Finance / Leveraged Lending Benchmark

FITB's Shared National Credit (SNC) portfolio is **$31.9B, ~26% of total loans** — a large sponsor-finance exposure. Leveraged lending specifically is $2.6B (~2.1% of loans), management-claimed reduction from 8% in 2015. Direct read-across to:
- Apollo / Atlas SP ecosystem (via OTTO intel)
- Private equity capital call and fund finance chain
- BDC/PC transmission from BROCK

1Q26 watch: SNC criticized rate (currently 17% below 5yr avg), any new disclosure on leveraged book composition, management tone on sponsor finance origination pace.

### 3. Midwest CRE / Manufacturing Labor-Market Transmission

FITB's CRE book tilts Midwest: IL $979M + OH $918M + MI $784M = ~$2.7B (~20% of nonowner-occupied $13.2B). FL and TX are the largest states but Midwest aggregation is material. Key feature: **manufacturing tenant exposure** via commercial mortgage book (~$12.2B avg 2025).

If the LABOR agent channel fires (claims >300K; manufacturing PMI contraction), FITB becomes a canary for Midwest CRE stress through tenant labor-market transmission. This is a different concentration profile from coastal-CRE banks (WAL, OZK with Florida/Texas/Southeast tilt).

## What FITB Is Not

- **Not a position name.** Cleanest balance sheet in the Q1 2026 cohort on headline metrics (CET1 10.77%, NPL 0.62% declining, ACL 1.96% steady, AOCI improved $1.5B YoY to -$3.1B).
- **Not a CRE-concentrated regional.** CRE is ~17% of loans, diversified by geography and property type. Manufacturing tenants are a tilt, not a concentration.
- **Not an FHLB stress story.** FITB actively DE-RISKED FHLB in 2025 (avg 4Q25 $2.6B vs 3Q25 $4.9B = -48% QoQ; period-end short-term borrowings collapsed from $4.7B to $0.9B). If 1Q26 FHLB rebounds hard, thesis flip.
- **Not an NDFI hiding story.** Table 29 shows ZERO reclassification YoY (every YE24 cell matches FY24 10-K exactly). Deck Slide 19 NDFI $9.5B ≈ Table 29 "Financial services and insurance" $9.6B — contained, not thematic.
- **Not an insider / governance story.** Large-cap, institutional investor base, Cat IV.

- **WAL / OZK (Apr 21):** FITB serves as the LOW-NDFI anchor (8% of loans) vs PNC HIGH-NDFI anchor (20%). WAL/OZK disclosure will fall between these and the comparison reveals their NDFI opacity position. If WAL discloses less than FITB's 6-way % breakdown, that's an opacity signal.

- **BROCK / Apollo / Atlas SP:** FITB's $31.9B SNC book and $0.12B Private Capital Warehouse / Fund Finance sub-bucket are direct same-ecosystem exposure. 1Q26 criticized trend = cross-agent confirmation.

## Open Thesis Questions

1. **Is the "Consumer Warehouse / Consumer Finance Securitization Vehicles" sub-bucket (31% of NDFI = ~$2.95B) exposed to subprime auto ABS?** FITB has not disclosed. Management answer on this directly is the highest-value single data point Apr 17.
2. **Why is FITB de-risking FHLB while peers surge?** Deliberate balance-sheet management (cited as driver of NIM improvement), or anticipating liquidity stress and pre-positioning? Will 1Q26 continue the de-risk or reverse?
3. **Is the "Financial services and insurance" Table 29 line going to be split in FY2026 10-K** (following CFG precedent)? FITB currently has a clean YoY reconciliation — any carve-out would be the first.
4. **Will manufacturing PMI / Midwest labor stress translate into CRE commentary?** FITB has not specifically called out manufacturing tenants in the 10-K, but the Midwest geography + C&I manufacturing line ($8.6B) suggests meaningful exposure.
