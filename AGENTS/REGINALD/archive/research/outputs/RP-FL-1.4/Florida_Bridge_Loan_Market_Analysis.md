## Executive Summary

The Florida condo association bridge loan market has emerged as a **critical but opaque leverage channel**. Associations unable to fund mandated reserves/repairs are taking on debt that transforms building-level safety costs into household-level financial stress. This hidden leverage is not captured by traditional credit bureaus or regulatory filings.

### Key Findings

1. **Market Size**: Multi-billion dollar expansion since 2022; ~25,000 associations, 600K+ units in buildings 40+ years old
2. **Inventory Surge**: FL condo listings +56% YoY (early 2025), pending sales -21%
3. **Lender Bifurcation**: Traditional banks (Valley, Popular, Seacoast) vs. private bridge lenders (Nexos, Insula)
4. **Stress Indicators**: Palm Greens bankruptcy ($43.7M debt), Grenadier Lakes lawsuit vs. City National Bank
5. **Assessment Shock**: Special assessments of $30K-$110K per unit becoming common

## The Legislative Catalyst

### Compliance Deadlines

| Requirement | Deadline | Impact |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| Prohibition of Reserve Waivers | Dec 31, 2024 | No more "waiving" reserve funding |
| Milestone Inspection | Dec 31, 2024/2025 | Buildings must prove structural safety |
| SIRS Completion | Dec 31, 2025 | Full reserve funding plan required |
| Mandatory Online Account (DBPR) | Oct 1, 2025 | State-level transparency |
| Mandatory Funding in Budgets | Jan 1, 2025+ | 100% of recommended reserves |

### The Math Problem

Example: 100-unit building, roof needs $2M replacement in 5 years, current reserves = $0

**Old law**: Could waive reserves, pay $8,000/year total = $80/unit/year  
**New law**: Must collect $400,000/year = **$4,000/unit/year** (50x increase)

When multiplied across 9 mandatory structural components, "catch-up" assessments reach $30K-$110K per unit.

## Loan Structure and Collateral

### Debt Terms Comparison (Q4 2025)

| Loan Type | Interest Rate | Term | Collateral |
|-----------|---------------|------|------------|
| Bank Term Loan | 6.5-9.5% | 10-15 years | Assignment of Assessments |
| Private Bridge | 9.0-13.0%+ | 6-24 months | 1st/2nd Lien on Receivables |
| Line of Credit | Variable (Prime+) | 1-5 years | Receivables/Future Assessments |

**Average bridge loan rate (Q4 2025)**: 10.11%  
**Average loan amount**: $600K+ for small repairs, multi-million for structural

### The Collateral Mechanism

Association pledges **future assessment income** as collateral. In default:
1. Lender can step into association's shoes
2. Collect dues directly from unit owners
3. Foreclose on delinquent owners via Fla. Stat. § 718.116

This provides strong security for lenders — explains why banks like Valley and Popular have billion-dollar exposures with low historic losses.

## Underwriting Standards

### Critical Hurdles

1. **Delinquency Ratios**: Most banks require <10% (often <5%) of units 30+ days delinquent. >15% = ineligible for Fannie/Freddie backing.

2. **The Blacklist**: 1,440 FL associations (~6% of total) on Fannie/Freddie "unallowable" list. Private bridge lenders specifically target these buildings.

3. **Reserve Funding**: SIRS "Baseline Funding" requirement analyzed — must never project below $0 over 30 years.

4. **Insurance Viability**: Loss of Citizens coverage = death knell. Forced into private surplus lines at 300-500% higher premiums.

### DSCR Formula for Associations

DSCR = (Total Assessment Income - Operating Expenses) / Proposed Loan Payment

Lenders typically require 1.1x-1.2x coverage. If below, may require "Special Assessment Resolution" — board legally commits to loan-specific assessment.

## Stress Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Refinance Maturity Wall

- Many associations took 1-2 year bridge loans for initial milestone repairs
- 2026 maturities arriving
- If rates haven't fallen OR delinquency has risen → can't refinance
- Balloon payment could bankrupt association

### Scenario 2: Strategic Default Spiral

- Unit values falling (some tiers -33% from 2022 peak)
- HOA fees rising (insurance + reserves)
- Owners strategic default → association loses assessment revenue
- 20%+ revenue loss = association defaults on master loan
- Building enters financial limbo → repairs stall → condemnation

### Scenario 3: Governance Failure

- Board liability increasing → harder to find volunteers
- Buildings can't seat boards → state appoints receiver
- Receiver can levy assessments without owner votes
- Costs increase → faster delinquency → accelerated failure

## Implications for Bank Exposure

### The Hidden Leverage Problem

Association bridge loans are:
- Structured as private commercial contracts
- Not captured in residential mortgage data
- Not reported to traditional credit bureaus
- Creating household debt burden invisible to underwriters

### Bank Loss Severity Chain

1. Insurance premium spike → Assessment increase
2. Owner can't pay → Delinquency rises
3. Association revenue falls → Master loan stress
4. Association defaults → Bank takes loss on commercial loan
5. Individual mortgages also stressed → Double hit

### VLY Specific Exposure

Valley National Bank explicitly mentioned as association lender:
- HOA loans secured by receivables
- Treasury services for reserve accounts
- Direct exposure to this stress channel
