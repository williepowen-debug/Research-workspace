## Executive Summary

The Florida condo market is experiencing a **"structural reset"** — a permanent shift from low-cost ownership in aging towers to a high-carry environment. Developers are acquiring distressed assets at **40-60%+ discounts**, but the Biscayne 21 ruling has frozen many termination deals, creating a legal deadlock that extends bank workout timelines.

### Key Findings

1. **Bifurcated Market**: Luxury new construction thriving; legacy buildings illiquid
2. **Inventory**: 74,000+ units (37% YoY increase), pending sales -21%
3. **Blacklist Scale**: 1,440 associations on Fannie/Freddie unallowable list
4. **Biscayne 21 Impact**: FL Supreme Court upheld 100% consent requirement — developer deals frozen
5. **Pricing Reality**: Heron Pond bulk acquisition at $67K/unit vs. $180-210K individual market values (60%+ discount)
6. **Foreclosure Rank**: Florida #1 nationally (1 in 230 housing units)

## The Biscayne 21 Ruling

### What Happened

- **October 2025**: FL Supreme Court declined to review *Avila v. TRD Biscayne LLC*
- Developer Two Roads acquired majority of units
- Tried to amend declaration from 100% to 80% consent for termination
- Court: Original contract term (100% consent) is binding
- Cannot be amended away by majority vote

### Consequences

1. **Deal Cancellations**: Developers with 80-95% ownership now stuck
2. **Holdout Premium**: Must pay "exorbitant premiums" to remaining owners
3. **Sidelined Capital**: Institutional capital waiting for legislative fix
4. **Restoration Obligations**: Developers who began stripping units may face restoration costs

### Market Impact

- Many termination deals being "renegotiated or cancelled"
- Legal counsel advising "fresh look" at original declarations
- Path to liquidation now requires receivership (judicial) vs. voluntary termination

## Deconversion Strategy

### The Milan Condominiums Success Story

- **Location**: Apopka, FL (Orlando area)
- **Units**: 240
- **Problem**: $4.5M repair bill, insurance dispute
- **Solution**: Sold entire property as single multifamily asset
- **Premium**: 30%+ over individual unit sales value
- **Transition**: No tenants displaced; new owner signed leases with existing occupants

### When Deconversion Works

- Low owner-occupancy (predominantly investor-owned)
- Infrastructure salvageable through centralized capital
- Supermajority achievable (80-90% depending on declaration)
- Building not subject to 100% consent requirement

### When Deconversion Fails

- High owner-occupancy with sentimental attachment
- 100% consent required (post-Biscayne 21 risk)
- Structural issues too severe for renovation
- Insurance unavailable

## Financing Distressed Acquisitions

Traditional bank financing has **"all but vanished"** for distressed condos.

### Current Financing Sources

1. **Private Lending**: Short-term bridges where conventional lenders decline
2. **DSCR Loans**: Qualify based on property rental income, not borrower credit
3. **Non-Warrantable Mortgages**: Specifically for blacklisted buildings

### Implications

- Higher cost of capital for distressed assets
- Longer holding periods to achieve returns
- Risk premium priced into acquisition bids

## Bank Collateral Implications

### The Erosion Problem

1. Building placed on blacklist → collateral "frozen" for residential buyers
2. Bank must hold loan through workout OR sell note at steep discount
3. If bulk purchase at 40%+ discount → creates "comp-driven" downward spiral
4. Adjacent buildings see value marks down

## 2026 Outlook

### Expected Dynamics

1. **Inventory Tightening** in luxury segment
2. **Secondary Tier** remains illiquid until:
   - Legislature provides "economic waste" exception to unanimous consent, OR
   - Market reaches such high distress that 100% of owners desperate to sell

3. **Legal Deadlock** continues — Biscayne 21 not legislatively resolved

4. **Foreclosure Activity** likely to increase as:
   - Bridge loan maturities hit
   - Special assessments continue
   - Strategic defaults accelerate

### The "Structural Reset"

> "The days of low-cost ownership in aging coastal towers are ending, replaced by a high-carry environment where owners must choose between massive special assessments or selling to bulk developers at a fraction of former values."

This is a **permanent shift**, not a cyclical correction.
