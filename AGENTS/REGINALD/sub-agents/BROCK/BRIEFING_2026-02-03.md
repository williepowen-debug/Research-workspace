# BROCK Domain Briefing
**Private Credit and BDC Ecosystem Analysis**  
*February 3, 2026 — Audio Script (~6 minutes)*

---

Alright Will, here's your executive catch-up on what we're seeing in the private credit space through BROCK's lens. This is the domain that sits between REGINALD's regional banks and the broader credit markets — and frankly, what we've found is concerning.

## The Big Picture

The Business Development Company market has grown to over 500 billion dollars in assets. On the surface, it looks healthy — these floating-rate portfolios have been cash machines during the rate hike cycle. But underneath, there's a divergence happening. The industry is bifurcating into two camps: top-tier managers like Ares, Blue Owl, and Blackstone who are maintaining discipline, and a long tail of funds where the credit quality is deteriorating faster than the headlines suggest.

Here's the core finding: **The reported default rate in private credit is about 2.1%. But when you adjust for "shadow defaults" — loans modified to defer cash interest through Payment-in-Kind provisions — the real distress rate is closer to 6%.** That's three times higher than what's being reported.

## The PIK Trap

Why does this matter? PIK income is non-cash. A borrower can't pay their interest in cash, so the lender agrees to add that interest to the principal balance. The loan keeps accruing income on paper, but no actual cash changes hands. 

And here's the trap: BDCs are required to distribute 90% of their taxable income as dividends — including PIK. So they're paying out cash dividends on income they haven't actually received. By late 2025, more than half of all BDCs were paying dividends that exceeded their actual cash flow from operations.

## The Watchlist

At the top of the distress list is **Prospect Capital, ticker PSEC**. Their PIK income hit 35% of total investment income — way above the industry average of around 10%. S&P downgraded them to double-B-plus late last year. They've cut their dividend. Their top five holdings represent 36% of the portfolio, which is dangerous concentration. PSEC is the canary in the coal mine.

Then there's the **BlackRock TCP Capital situation — ticker TCPC**. In January 2026, they disclosed a preliminary 19% decline in net asset value in a single quarter. NAV went from $8.71 to around $7.05. Six portfolio companies drove two-thirds of that loss:

- **Edmentum** — an education technology company, the biggest hit
- **Razor Group and SellerX** — Amazon aggregators that exemplify the failure of the roll-up model
- **HomeRenew** — went into surprise liquidation after being taken off non-accrual just two quarters earlier
- **Hylan** — marked at 94 cents in September, collapsed by year-end

The TCPC event illustrates what we call "cliff risk." Quarterly valuations lag the actual speed of deterioration. A loan can be marked near par one quarter and be a total loss the next.

## Systemic Concentration

The research identified high degrees of portfolio overlap among the largest BDCs. The **Pluralsight restructuring** is the textbook case — a $1.175 billion loan clubbed across Blue Owl, Ares, Goldman, Oaktree, and Golub. When the company faltered, every one of those funds marked down simultaneously. The lesson: diversification across BDC managers may be illusory. The same problem names appear in portfolio after portfolio.

**Edmentum** is on the watchlist at TCPC, New Mountain Finance, Prospect, Ares, and Oaktree. If Edmentum restructures, five major BDCs take a hit at once.

And then there's **First Brands** — the biggest fraud case in recent private credit history. This auto parts manufacturer allegedly fabricated $2.3 billion in receivables. They were double-pledging the same invoices to multiple lenders. Multiple BDCs had exposure: Monroe, Prospect, FS Specialty, Great Elm. The marks ranged from 101% at Monroe down to near zero at Great Elm — showing how subjective these valuations really are.

## The Bank Connection

Here's where it gets really important for our broader thesis. Banks haven't actually decoupled from private credit — they've just changed the form of their exposure.

**Bank lending to non-depository financial institutions has reached $1.2 trillion.** Specifically to private credit participants: about $300 billion outstanding with another $340 billion in unutilized commitments.

The connections run through multiple channels:
- **Fund finance:** Subscription lines and NAV facilities
- **Partnerships:** JPMorgan committed $50 billion. Citi and Apollo have $25 billion. Wells Fargo launched Overland Advantage with $5 billion.
- **CLO holdings:** Banks hold senior tranches backed by BDC portfolios

This creates a **leverage-on-leverage matrix**:
1. Portfolio companies levered 4.6 to 5.7 times EBITDA
2. Funds and BDCs add 1 to 2 times
3. Banks provide fund-level financing
4. Some LPs use leverage for their commitments

Four layers of debt stacked on a single set of underlying assets.

**Citizens Financial Group** is our regional bank case study. They've made fund finance a strategic priority. But if an LP can't meet a capital call, the collateral on Citizens' sub-line is impaired.

## Connection to REGINALD

BDC stress transmits to regional banks through five channels:
1. Credit losses hit warehouse lines
2. Redemption gates trigger liquidity scrambles
3. Software markdowns compound tech loan exposure
4. Fund finance stress hits regionals like Citizens
5. CLO forced selling creates mark-to-market pain

Banks we're watching — Western Alliance, Towne Bank, others with tech exposure — face potential dual hits: direct CRE problems plus indirect BDC contagion.

## Bottom Line

The BDC market has grown to half a trillion dollars on the back of PIK income and extend-and-pretend. The reported 2.1% default rate is fiction — the real rate is around 6%. More than half of BDCs are paying dividends they haven't earned in cash. And banks remain deeply connected through $1.2 trillion in NDFI lending.

**PSEC and TCPC are the early warning signals.** When we see another downgrade at Prospect or another cliff-risk collapse, that's the signal the broader reckoning is accelerating.

**Watch for:**
- PIK-to-income ratios crossing 15%
- LTV migration toward 80%
- Software markdowns forcing synchronized write-downs
- Stress in fund finance desks at the regionals

The private credit ecosystem is more fragile than it looks, and the connections to the banking system are deeper than most people realize.

*End of briefing.*
