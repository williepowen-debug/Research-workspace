## The Florida Thesis

### Core Mechanism: "The Coral Bleaching"

Florida's aging condo stock is undergoing forced recapitalization. Post-Surfside legislation (SB 4-D, HB 913) eliminated reserve waivers and mandated structural inspections. Associations discovering decades of deferred maintenance must now fund repairs immediately or face:

1. **Blacklisting** — Fannie/Freddie won't back mortgages (1,438 buildings)
2. **Insurance Loss** — Carriers refusing master policies for non-compliant buildings
3. **Receivership** — Court-appointed managers liquidating distressed associations
4. **Bank Losses** — Regional banks holding both association loans AND individual mortgages

### Transmission Chain

```
SIRS Deadline (Dec 31, 2025)
    → Reserve Gap Discovery
    → Special Assessments ($30K-$110K/unit)
    → Owner Strategic Defaults
    → Association Revenue Collapse
    → Master Loan Default (VLY, regional banks)
    → Building Enters Receivership
    → Bulk Sale at 40-60% Discount
    → Bank Loss Crystallization
```

### The Biscayne 21 Complication

FL Supreme Court (Oct 2025) upheld 100% owner consent for termination. This **freezes voluntary exits** — distressed buildings can't be sold to developers without unanimous consent. Result: extended workout timelines, receivership becomes primary resolution path, banks hold impaired assets longer.

## Institutional Capital Response (Feb 2026 Research)

### The Opportunity Layer

While distressed owners face forced sales, institutional capital sees **acquisition opportunity**:

**Condo De-Conversions:**
- Institutional buyers acquiring majority of units
- Terminate association → convert to market-rate rental
- Biscayne 21 ruling complicates (100% consent required)
- Result: **Receivership becomes primary exit** for institutions too

**Build-to-Rent (BTR) Pivot:**
- Institutions shifting from scattered-site to purpose-built rental
- AMH: 92% of acquisitions now from in-house development
- Tampa/Orlando: ~4,000 BTR units each in pipeline
- Avoids condo mess entirely — new construction, rental-only

**Migration Normalization:**
- FL net domestic migration: +314,000 (2022) → +23,000 (2025)
- "Pull-forward" effect exhausted
- Demand softening compounds condo inventory surge
- Median DOM: 80 days (up from 73 YoY)
- Strongest buyer's market in decade (30% more sellers than buyers)

### Market Metrics (2025)

| Metric | Value | Context |

| Miami DOM | 69 days | Extended |

### Institutional Insurance Response

Large SFR operators (AMH, INVH) deploying **captive insurance** to manage FL hurricane exposure:
- Pure captive: $250K capital requirement
- Parametric insurance: pays on trigger (wind speed/flood level), not adjustment
- AMH net casualty: $4.0M (2024) vs $16.3M (2022) — effective risk management

**Implication:** Institutional players can self-insure in ways that condo associations and individual owners cannot. Structural advantage compounds over time.

## Stress Scenarios

### Scenario A: Gradual Grind (Base Case)
- Blacklist grows steadily (1,438 → 2,000+ by 2027)
- Association bankruptcies trickle in (5-10/quarter)
- Bank reserves build gradually
- VLY manages down exposure via loan sales
- **Timeline:** Q3 2026 - Q2 2027 recognition

### Scenario B: Cascade Event (Bear Case)
- Major insurer fails (Slide, Universal)
- Forced-place insurance at 5x rates
- Association defaults spike
- Receivership wave overwhelms courts
- Bank losses crystallize rapidly
- **Timeline:** Hurricane season 2026 or policy cluster

### Scenario C: Legislative Relief (Bull Case)
- Legislature loosens SIRS requirements
- "Economic waste" exception to Biscayne 21
- Insurance market continues stabilizing
- Orderly workout of distressed buildings
- **Timeline:** 2026 legislative session
