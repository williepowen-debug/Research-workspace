# **Strategic Assessment of Florida Loan Concentrations Among US Regional Banks: Competitive Positioning and Fiscal Outlook (2025–2026)**

The US regional banking sector has witnessed a profound geographic realignment over the previous five fiscal years, with Florida emerging as the preeminent destination for both capital deployment and deposit acquisition. This structural shift is underpinned by a massive inter-state migration of wealth, often referred to as the Great Wealth Migration, which has seen high-net-worth individuals and corporate entities relocate from the high-tax jurisdictions of the Northeast and West Coast to the pro-growth environment of the Southeastern United States.1 For regional financial institutions with assets between $10 billion and $100 billion, a concentrated presence in Florida is no longer merely a diversification strategy; for many, it has become the primary driver of balance sheet expansion, net interest margin stability, and credit quality resilience.

Florida’s economic landscape, now recognized as the 16th largest market globally, provides a unique operating environment characterized by robust demand for commercial real estate (CRE), high-quality residential mortgages, and a burgeoning middle-market commercial and industrial (C\&I) sector.1 The subsequent analysis provides an exhaustive ranking and evaluation of the regional banks with the highest percentage of their loan portfolios concentrated in Florida. By examining their total loan volumes, geographic distribution metrics, and specific loan-type breakdowns, this report elucidates how these institutions navigate the complexities of interest rate volatility, regulatory concentration thresholds, and the intensifying competition for relationship-based banking in one of the world’s most dynamic markets.

## **Institutional Profile: Seacoast Banking Corporation of Florida (SBCF)**

Seacoast Banking Corporation stands as the largest Florida-headquartered regional bank with a nearly 100% concentration in its home state. Based in Stuart, Florida, Seacoast has successfully executed a strategy that combines organic market share gains with disciplined, accretive acquisitions.3 The bank’s evolution into a regional powerhouse is best illustrated by its ability to maintain a granular, relationship-focused model while scaling its asset base toward the $20 billion threshold.

### **Strategic Impact of the VBI Acquisition**

The 2025 fiscal year was transformative for Seacoast, marked by the closing and integration of the VBI (The Villages) acquisition. This transaction added $4.4 billion in assets, $1.2 billion in loans, and 19 branches in one of Florida’s most demographically attractive markets: The Villages community and North Central Florida.15 Unlike many commercial-heavy regional acquisitions, VBI provided Seacoast with a unique residential mortgage and consumer deposit engine. The integration allows Seacoast to portfolio high-quality mortgage volume originated within The Villages, benefiting from borrowers with exceptional credit scores and shorter-than-average loan durations.3

### **Loan Portfolio Composition and Growth Vectors**

As of December 31, 2025, Seacoast’s total loans reached $12.6 billion, representing a 15% annualized organic growth rate when excluding the direct impact of the VBI addition.3 This growth is largely the result of a multi-year investment in talent. Seacoast has successfully recruited commercial banking teams from larger national competitors that have historically dominated the Florida market but may lack the localized decision-making agility that Seacoast provides. These hires have fueled a robust commercial pipeline that totaled $948 million at the end of 2025\.16

The bank’s non-owner occupied CRE exposure is a point of significant internal management, currently representing 34% of total loans.17 Management maintains that this exposure is highly granular and distributed across varied industries, reducing the idiosyncratic risk associated with single-sector downturns. Furthermore, the credit quality of the Seacoast portfolio remains strong; non-performing loans (NPLs) as a percentage of total loans decreased to 0.57% in Q4 2025, a significant improvement from the 0.90% reported a year earlier.15

### **Fiscal Performance and 2026 Guidance**

Seacoast’s fiscal health is reflected in its net interest margin (NIM) expansion, which reached 3.66% in late 2025\.18 This was driven by a reduction in deposit costs, which fell 14 basis points to 1.67%.15 For 2026, the bank has provided a bullish outlook, anticipating adjusted revenue growth of 29% to 31% and high single-digit organic loan growth.16 The bank’s management expects to continue its aggressive banker recruitment program, planning to increase the banker count by 15% in 2026 to further capitalize on the disruption in the Florida market.14

## **Institutional Profile: City National Bank of Florida (CNB)**

City National Bank of Florida (CNB), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Chilean financial group Bci, represents the second major "pure-play" Florida institution in this asset class. While its parent company provides global scale, CNB operates with a high degree of autonomy, focusing exclusively on the Florida market. With total assets of $28 billion and a total loan portfolio of $19.6 billion as of September 30, 2025, CNB is the largest bank headquartered in Miami once the national mega-banks are excluded.1

### **Dominance in South Florida Commercial Real Estate**

CNB’s strategic identity is inextricably linked to the South Florida commercial real estate sector, which management describes as the premier CRE market in the United States.1 The bank’s CRE portfolio is characterized by ultra-conservative underwriting metrics that contrast with the broader industry. The weighted average loan-to-value (LTV) ratio for CNB’s CRE book stands at 47%, while the weighted average debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) is a robust 1.6x.1

CNB has demonstrated an ability to grow its market share in the face of intense competition. ### **Specialization and Diversification within Florida**

While CRE is the bank's anchor, CNB has diversified into specialized lending verticals that cater to the unique Florida economy. ## **Institutional Profile: BankUnited, Inc. (BKU)**

BankUnited, headquartered in Miami Lakes, Florida, provides a nuanced example of a regional bank that is strategically repositioning its portfolio. With $35.0 billion in assets at the end of 2025, BankUnited has transitioned from its origins as a residential mortgage specialist to a core commercial lender with a multi-state footprint that remains heavily anchored in Florida.24

### **Geographic Dispersion and Florida Concentration**

BankUnited’s loan portfolio is more geographically diverse than those of Seacoast or CNB, with operations in New York, Dallas, Atlanta, and Charlotte.25 However, Florida remains its largest and most productive region. An analysis of the bank’s $24.27 billion loan portfolio reveals a significant concentration in the Florida market, particularly within its core commercial segments.10

Within the Florida CRE portfolio, BankUnited maintains a highly granular sub-market strategy. ### **Strategic Transition: The Shift to Commercial Lending**

The central theme of BankUnited’s current fiscal period is the "remixing" of its balance sheet. Management is intentionally allowing the lower-yielding residential mortgage portfolio to run off while aggressively funding growth in C\&I and CRE.13 In 2025, core loans (CRE, C\&I, and MWL) increased by $769 million, while residential and other non-core portfolios declined by $198 million.10

This transition has direct implications for the bank's net interest margin, which expanded to 3.06% by the fourth quarter of 2025\.10 The bank’s credit quality in Florida remains a differentiator; the Florida CRE portfolio boasts a weighted average LTV of 52.3% and a DSCR of 1.92, indicating significant collateral protection and borrower capacity.13

## **Institutional Profile: SouthState Corporation (SSB)**

SouthState Corporation, headquartered in Winter Haven, Florida, is the fifth-largest regional bank in the South, with approximately $67 billion in assets.9 Following its significant merger with Independent Financial Group, SouthState has established a presence in twelve of the fifteen fastest-growing metropolitan statistical areas (MSAs) in the United States, with a strategic core in Florida.9

### **Portfolio Scale and Geographic Reach**

SouthState does not operate as a Florida pure-play, but the state represents its most critical growth market. As of December 31, 2024, the bank's total loan portfolio reached $33.9 billion, a figure that climbed to $48.6 billion by the end of 2025 as the Independent acquisition was fully integrated.8 Based on regional production trends and legacy branch footprints, Florida accounts for approximately 36% of the bank's total loan exposure.28

### **Managing CRE Concentration Risks**

SouthState has been a subject of industry attention due to its CRE concentration levels. In mid-2024, its CRE concentration reached 320.5% of its total risk-based capital, placing it among a select group of large regional banks exceeding the 300% regulatory threshold.22 However, management has mitigated this risk through portfolio granularity and strong underwriting. In the fourth quarter of 2025, net charge-offs remained remarkably low at 9 basis points, continuing a long-term trend of outperforming peers in credit quality.9

For 2026, SouthState has guided toward mid-to-upper single-digit loan growth, supported by a $5 billion loan pipeline that began to manifest on the balance sheet in late 2025\.27 The bank is also "leaning into" talent acquisition, specifically recruiting commercial relationship managers in high-growth Florida and Texas corridors to drive organic C\&I growth.29

## **Institutional Profile: Valley National Bancorp (VLY)**

Valley National Bancorp, headquartered in Morristown, New Jersey, provides a compelling example of a "transplanted" regional bank that has effectively become a Florida-centric player. Since 2017, Valley has aggressively pivoted its capital away from the saturated markets of the Northeast toward the high-growth environment of Florida.2

### **The "Northeast to Florida" Corridor Strategy**

Valley National’s Florida concentration is the result of a deliberate strategy to follow its client base. As individuals and businesses from New Jersey and New York relocated to Florida, Valley utilized its existing relationships to provide financing in their new locations.2 This strategy has been exceptionally successful: Florida loans accounted for 27.3% of the bank's total loan portfolio by the end of 2024\.7

## **Macroeconomic Themes and Second-Order Insights**

The high concentration of loans in Florida across these six institutions is not merely a geographic coincidence but a reflection of deep-seated economic shifts and regulatory realities. Several broader themes emerge from an analysis of their collective performance and strategic guidance.

### **The "Disruption Hire" as a Growth Mechanism**

A recurring theme among Seacoast, SouthState, and BankUnited is the use of talent acquisition to drive loan growth.3 As national mega-banks undergo organizational restructuring, local relationship managers often seek the autonomy of regional institutions. This creates a causal relationship where regional bank growth in Florida is fueled by the *localized displacement* of national bank portfolios. By hiring entire teams, regional banks "buy" established loan books and deposit relationships that are historically sticky, allowing for faster scale-up in competitive MSAs like Tampa and Miami-Dade.

### **The 300% CRE Concentration Threshold**

Regulatory scrutiny of CRE concentrations remains the primary headwind for Florida-centric banks. Institutions like City National Bank of Florida and SouthState have operated near or above the 300% threshold for CRE loans relative to risk-based capital.22 The response from management across the sector has been a strategic shift toward "Owner-Occupied" CRE.12 Regulators view owner-occupied properties as less risky because the loan is serviced by the primary business’s cash flow rather than volatile tenant rents. This shift allows Florida banks to continue supporting the state’s massive commercial infrastructure while maintaining a risk profile acceptable to the OCC and FDIC.

### **Deposit Beta and NIM Sensitivity**

In the competitive Florida market, deposit retention is as critical as loan origination. The "deposit beta"—the degree to which a bank must increase its deposit rates in response to market changes—is a key differentiator. Seacoast, with its granular deposit base and cost of funds at 1.67%, has demonstrated a superior ability to manage margins compared to banks more reliant on wholesale funding.15 BankUnited’s focus on growing non-interest bearing demand deposits (NIDDA) to 31% of total deposits is another example of a Florida-centric bank building a "funding moat" to protect its NIM during the anticipated 2026 rate cycle.10

### **Insurance and Climatic Risks as Credit Factors**

While the provided research material focuses on financial performance, a second-order risk for Florida-concentrated banks is the spiraling cost of property insurance. This acts as a "silent tax" on Florida borrowers, affecting debt-to-income (DTI) and debt service coverage (DSCR) ratios. Banks like Seacoast are mitigating this by focusing on high-credit-score markets like The Villages, where wealth levels provide a buffer against rising carry costs.3 Conversely, banks with heavy exposure to South Florida’s luxury residential and retail markets must remain vigilant regarding the long-term impact of insurance availability on collateral values.

## **Outlook and Conclusions for the 2026 Fiscal Cycle**

The strategic outlook for Florida’s top regional banks in 2026 is characterized by "guarded optimism." Management teams have broadly guided toward mid-to-high single-digit loan growth, predicated on the continued influx of population and business entities.3

In conclusion, the institutions with the highest Florida loan concentrations have effectively tied their fortunes to the most robust economic engine in the United States. Seacoast and City National Bank of Florida remain the "pure-play" champions, while BankUnited and Valley National demonstrate the power of strategic geographic realignment. SouthState and Ameris utilize Florida as a high-growth component of a broader Southeastern platform. For all these banks, the 2026 fiscal cycle will be a test of their ability to manage the risks of geographic concentration against the undeniable rewards of participating in Florida’s historic economic expansion. The key to success will lie in their ability to maintain conservative underwriting—exemplified by CNB’s 47% LTVs—while aggressively capturing the high-quality relationship business that the national banks are currently vacating.
