## 5. MTM vs HTM / VALUATION TRANSPARENCY

- Most CRE debt funds use **mark-to-model (Level 3)** — illiquid whole loans
- Distressed transactions at only **3% of sales** (vs 20% post-GFC) = losses restructured behind scenes
- FSB identified 3 vulnerabilities: **liquidity mismatches, leverage, valuation opacity**
- Moody's: default rates being **masked** in private credit
- Extend-and-pretend ($384B) delays loss recognition, keeps losses off balance sheets
- No widespread margin calls reported, but SREIT's $1.6B forced sales = redemption-driven selling

- CRE = ~7-14% of broader private credit market

## CREED IMPLICATIONS

1. **37% non-agency market share** = private credit is now the single largest non-bank CRE lender
2. **CRE CLO 43% non-performing matured loans** is the most alarming metric — legacy bridge deals in crisis
3. **40% rate cap gap** creates a ticking clock on floating-rate bridge loans
4. **SREIT NAV -40%** is the visible tip of mark-to-model opacity across the sector
5. **Moody's downgrades** (Blackstone, Apollo, KKR CRE funds) validate credit quality concerns
6. **FSB warning** about non-bank amplification risk is a macro-prudential signal for PROME
7. **$500-600B dry powder** is the strongest bullish counter — capital exists to absorb the maturity wall
8. **Signal to NICK:** CRE private credit is 7-14% of the broader PC market and faces the same liquidity/valuation concerns
