# RP-CREED-10: DC/DOGE Federal Employment Impact on CRE

**Research Date:** February 13, 2026  
**Researcher:** REGINALD (via CREED sub-agent)  
**Focus:** Federal employment as a specific stress vector for DC-corridor regional banks

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

DOGE (Department of Government Efficiency) workforce reductions represent the **largest peacetime federal workforce cut on record**, with 271,000 federal workers cut (9% decline) through November 2025, bringing federal employment to **lowest levels since 1966**. DC metro office vacancy rates have risen to **22.4-22.6%** in 2025, with federal government lease terminations driving occupancy loss. Regional banks with DC corridor CRE concentration—particularly **Eagle Bancorp (EGBN)**—are experiencing acute credit stress, while **Burke & Herbert (BHRB)** shows deteriorating loan quality metrics despite stronger earnings.

**Transmission Timeline:** 6-18 months from workforce cuts to bank CRE metric deterioration. DOGE cuts began Q1 2025; bank stress visible Q1-Q4 2025; full impact expected through 2026.

## 1. FEDERAL EMPLOYMENT IN DC METRO

### Total Employment Base
- **DC Metro Total Employment:** 3,393,700 (April 2025)
- **Government Sector:** 727,400 jobs (21.4% of total employment)
- **Professional & Business Services:** 799,400 jobs (23.6% of total)
  - Many contractor roles dependent on federal spending
  
### Three Metropolitan Divisions:
1. **Arlington-Alexandria-Reston, VA-WV:** 1,630,300 jobs (48%)
2. **Washington, DC-MD Division:** 1,156,800 jobs (34%)
3. **Frederick-Gaithersburg-Bethesda, MD:** 606,600 jobs (18%)

**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Mid-Atlantic Regional Office, April 2025

### Federal Dependency Context
- Government employment represents **21.4% of DC metro jobs** (vs. national avg ~15%)
- Professional services heavily contractor-dependent (defense, consulting, IT services)
- **Multi-order effect:** 1 federal job loss = 1.5-2x indirect job losses in services sector

## 2. DOGE WORKFORCE REDUCTIONS

### Scale of Cuts (as of Feb 2026)
- **271,000 federal workers cut** through November 2025 (9% decline since January 2025)
- **317,000 employees left** vs. 68,000 hired in 2025 (net -249,000)
- **10% reduction** in federal bureaucracy in 2025 alone
- **212,000+ civil servants departed** through voluntary and involuntary mechanisms (as of Jan 27, 2026)
- **Federal employment at lowest level since 1966 (LBJ era)**

**Source:** Cato Institute analysis (Jan 2026), White House DOGE priorities, OPM data

### Announced Targets
- **$1 trillion budget cut target** for 2025-2026 fiscal year
- **4:1 ratio** — four departures for every one new hire
- Focused on **elimination of entire offices** and agency consolidation

### Timeline
- **January 2025:** DOGE initiative launched
- **Q1-Q2 2025:** Initial buyouts and involuntary separations
- **Q3-Q4 2025:** Accelerated departures, agency closures
- **2026:** Ongoing attrition, office lease terminations

**Source:** Fortune, Federal News Network, Atlanta Fed analysis

## 3. FEDERAL OFFICE SPACE FOOTPRINT

### GSA Portfolio Scale
- **Total GSA-managed space:** 363 million sq ft nationwide (8,397 buildings)
- **DC area GSA footprint:** 90 million sq ft
- **National reach:** 1,800+ cities with GSA leases
- **GSA leased space:** ~1.7% of total U.S. office inventory

### Recent Reductions
- **8 million sq ft reduced** in last 4 fiscal years (pre-DOGE)
- **3 million sq ft eliminated** in fiscal 2025 alone
- **90 properties disposed** by GSA in fiscal 2025
- **Over 8,000 leases total**, with 50% coming up for renewal

### Utilization Crisis
- **12-14% utilization** at federal agency HQ buildings in DC (April 2024)
- GSA using **14% of its own HQ space**
- Remote work + DOGE cuts = massive underutilization

**Source:** GSA data, Federal News Network, Global Real Estate (April 2024)

### Projected Impact
- **For every 10% reduction in GSA-leased space → 20 bps increase in national office vacancy**
- DC market impact magnified due to concentration
- **600,000 sq ft** in federal lease expirations (2024-2027) for 6 major agencies (Justice, Treasury, others)

**Source:** CBRE analysis

## 4. DC OFFICE MARKET STRESS

### Vacancy Rates (2025 Progression)
- **Q1 2025:** 20.0% vacancy (Cresa data)
- **Q2 2025:** 22.6% vacancy (CBRE) — **federal government contractions drove uptick**
- **Q3 2025:** 22.4% vacancy (CBRE) — modest improvement from conversions/redevelopment
- **Q4 2024 baseline:** ~22.4% (prior to DOGE acceleration)

### Absorption Trends
- **Q4 2024:** +770,221 sq ft absorbed (positive)
- **Q1 2025:** +367,616 sq ft absorbed (slowing)
- **Q2 2025:** -395,984 sq ft (negative absorption from federal terminations)
- **Q3 2025:** Slight recovery, but office sector "has yet to recover"

**Source:** CBRE, Cresa DC Office Market Reports (Q1-Q3 2025)

### Federal Lease Activity
- **GSA consolidation focus** in National Capital Region (NCR)
- **Reduced procurement activity** for smaller leases
- Major federal lease termination notices in Q2-Q3 2025

**Source:** Lincoln Property Company Q4 2024, Q2 2024 reports

### Property Value Impact
- **DC's most valuable office buildings:** 492 properties (86% of total) projected to experience **10.8% decrease in value** between 2023-2025
- Office distress concentrated in traditional federal tenant buildings

**Source:** DC Office of Revenue Analysis

## 7. HISTORICAL PRECEDENT: PRIOR FEDERAL DOWNSIZING

### 2013 Sequestration Impact on DC CRE

#### Economic Impact
- **Brief slowdown** in DC real estate market
- **Quick rebound** following sequester
- Contracts signed down only **3% vs. normal** during event
- **Fiscal disruptions** (permits, EPA reviews) but limited lasting damage

**Source:** Real Estate Consulting (Oct 2023), Washington Blade (Oct 2025), GAO (2014)

#### Key Differences from DOGE (2025-2026)
1. **Sequester was temporary** — DOGE cuts are **permanent structural reductions**
2. **Scale:** Sequester = belt-tightening; DOGE = **9-10% workforce elimination**
3. **Office utilization:** 2013 preceded remote work trend; 2025 combines workforce cuts with **12% utilization crisis**
4. **Lease expirations:** DOGE coincides with **major lease renewal cycle** (50% of GSA leases)

### 2018-2019 Government Shutdowns
- **Minimal real estate impact** — contracts signed actually increased post-shutdown
- Shutdowns were **temporary furloughs**, not permanent cuts
- No long-term vacancy increases

**Source:** Washington Blade, CoStar

### Why DOGE Is Different (2025-2026)
- **Largest peacetime workforce cut on record**
- **Permanent structural reduction** vs. temporary furlough
- **Coincides with already-elevated vacancy rates** (22%+)
- **Office utilization crisis** amplifies impact
- **Federal employment at 60-year low**

## 8. TIMELINE AND TRANSMISSION: DOGE CUTS → BANK CRE METRICS

### Transmission Chain
```
DOGE Workforce Cuts (Q1 2025)
    ↓
Federal Office Lease Terminations (Q2-Q4 2025, ongoing 2026)
    ↓
Office Vacancy Spike (Q2-Q3 2025: 22.6%)
    ↓
Property Value Decline (2025-2026: -10.8% for prime buildings)
    ↓
Tenant Defaults / Rent Reductions (Q3 2025 - Q2 2026)
    ↓
Bank CRE Loan Non-Accruals (Q4 2024 - Q4 2025)
    ↓
Credit Provision Increases (Q1-Q4 2025)
    ↓
Bank Earnings Compression (Q1-Q4 2025, ongoing 2026)
    ↓
Capital Ratio Stress (2026)
```

### Key Timing Observations

#### Phase 1: Workforce Cuts (Q1-Q3 2025)
- **Jan-Mar 2025:** Initial DOGE buyouts and separations
- **Apr-Sep 2025:** Accelerated departures (317,000 total by Nov 2025)
- **Multi-month lag** in OPM data reporting (FedScope updates delayed)

#### Phase 2: Office Market Deterioration (Q2-Q4 2025)
- **Q2 2025:** DC vacancy jumps from 20.0% to 22.6% (federal termination notices)
- **Q2 2025:** Negative absorption (-395,984 sq ft)
- **Q3 2025:** Slight stabilization (22.4%) but office sector "yet to recover"
- **2025-2026:** Federal lease expirations (600K sq ft from 6 agencies)

#### Phase 3: Bank Credit Stress (Q4 2024 - Q4 2025)
- **Q4 2024:** EGBN $74.9M office loan → non-accrual (early signal)
- **Q1 2025:** EGBN earnings plunge 89% to $1.7M
- **Q2 2025:** BHRB allowance coverage ratio drops 26 bps (104.6% → 78.6%)
- **Q4 2025:** EGBN partial recovery to $7.6M (still 50% below Q4 2024)

#### Phase 4: Ongoing Transmission (2026+)
- **Q1-Q2 2026:** Continued federal workforce attrition (212K+ departed as of Jan 2026)
- **2026:** Full impact of lease terminations on property cash flows
- **2026-2027:** Maturity wall for CRE loans (potential refinancing stress)
- **12-18 month lag** from lease termination to bank charge-offs

**Source:** Atlanta Fed (Apr 2025), Federal News Network, CBRE, bank earnings reports

### Lag Factors
1. **Lease term buffers:** Most federal leases have 6-24 month notice periods
2. **Rent payment continuity:** Tenants pay rent until lease expiration
3. **Property cash flow lag:** 3-6 months from vacancy to missed debt service
4. **Bank recognition lag:** 6-12 months from missed payments to non-accrual status
5. **Charge-off lag:** 12-24 months from non-accrual to write-downs

### Expected Peak Impact: **Q2-Q4 2026**
- Federal leases expired and not renewed
- Property cash flows deteriorating
- Bank CRE loan seasoning complete
- Full credit cycle recognition

## CONCLUSION

**DOGE represents an unprecedented federal workforce reduction with direct, measurable transmission to DC-corridor regional bank CRE portfolios.** Unlike prior sequester/shutdown events, DOGE cuts are:
- **Permanent and structural**
- **Historically large** (largest peacetime cut, 271K workers)
- **Coincident with office utilization crisis** (12-14% usage)
- **Timing with lease expiration cycle** (50% of GSA leases renewing)

**Eagle Bancorp (EGBN)** is already experiencing acute stress (89% earnings drop Q1 2025, $74.9M non-accrual). **Burke & Herbert (BHRB)** shows deteriorating coverage ratios despite stable earnings.

**Peak bank stress expected Q2-Q4 2026** as federal lease terminations flow through to property cash flows and loan defaults.

This is a **live, developing stress vector** with high diagnostic value for regional bank credit monitoring.
