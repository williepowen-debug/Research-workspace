# Texas State Fiscal Health: Sovereign Risk Assessment

**Source:** Deep Gemini research  
**Date:** February 11, 2026  
**Research Prompt:** RP-TEX-4

---

## Executive Summary

Texas maintains AAA ratings across all three agencies with massive liquidity reserves. However, structural vulnerabilities are emerging: pension underfunding, local government debt surge, and reliance on one-time surpluses for permanent tax cuts.

**Assessment:** 🟢 GREEN at state level, but long-term structural risks warrant monitoring.

## Pension Liabilities

### Teacher Retirement System (TRS)

| Metric | Aug 2024 | Aug 2025 | Trend |
|--------|----------|----------|-------|
| UAAL | $60.6B | **$64.9B** | 🔴 +$4.3B |
| Funded Ratio | 77.8% | **77.5%** | 🔴 Declining |
| Funding Period | 31 years | **35 years** | 🔴 Lengthening |
| Fiduciary Net | $210.5B | $226.3B | 🟢 Growing |

**Status:** TRS now "actuarially unsound" under Texas law (>31 year funding period). Requires 1.50% contribution rate increase by Sep 2027 to restore soundness.

**Cause:** HB 2 (89th Legislature) increased educator compensation, adding ~8 years to funding period.

## Assessment for Bank Thesis

### State Fiscal: 🟢 GREEN

**Not a near-term catalyst because:**
1. AAA ratings stable across all three agencies
2. ESF at constitutional cap (26.6% of spending)
3. Strong underlying economic fundamentals
4. No sovereign stress transmission to banks

### Structural Risks to Monitor

1. **TRS pension** — actuarially unsound, needs legislative fix
2. **Property tax buy-down** — structural deficit risk if revenues slow
3. **Local government debt** — $52B increase in one year
4. **OPEB funding** — 15 cents on the dollar

**Timeline:** These are 2028-2030 problems, not 2026 problems.
