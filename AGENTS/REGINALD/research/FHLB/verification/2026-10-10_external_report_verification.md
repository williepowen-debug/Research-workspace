## Bottom line

- **Use the draft as a lead list, never as a source.** It is labelled "Primary-Source Verified" but contains **one fabricated quote** (the Council of FHLBanks, April 2026), **two reversed findings** (GAO's "advances mask stress" is a literature concern GAO's results ran against; Delaware Life's restatement FOLLOWED February 2026 grand-jury subpoenas rather than triggering inquiries), **one reversed trend** (insurer share of FHLB advances FELL 27.0% → 24.9% in Q2 2026 while commercial banks rose 49.7% → 56.4%), and **five filing-level errors** (San Francisco's >10% borrower is Western Alliance at YE2025, not JPMorgan; a misquoted risk factor; a "$14.7B credit unions" figure that matches a different line (MBS investments); Indianapolis is NOT the most insurer-heavy district, since Des Moines is 46%; Athene's $28.2B is attributed to Bloomberg as "system-wide" when it is Athene's own 10-Q, all at Des Moines: ledger A rows E4/E8/E12/E13/E14). Also wrong at the Office of Finance: insurer membership is 9.7–9.9%, not 7.7%; the 2026 commercial-bank share is 56.4%, not ~48%.
- **What holds:** system advances $676.7B / $734.3B / $810.7B; total assets $1,247.6B / $1,436.6B; "growth driven by large members"; Athene $23.3B and 21% of Des Moines; Delaware Life ~97% of its FHLB capacity at YE2025; AB 2024-03 exists and is active; GAO-26-107373 exists; AM Best and S&P Negative outlooks; class actions (two).
- **What it missed:** Q3 likely reversed (FHLB debt −$42.1B; advances nowcast ≈ $770B); Athene's FHLB borrowing FELL in Q2 ($28.2B → $27.7B); Delaware Life $4,963M = 12% of FHLB Indianapolis (#2 borrower) at 6/30; Fitch Rating Watch Negative (7/22); the remediation plan (affiliated 42% → 39% at 6/30, target <10% by mid-2027); Citizens Bank N.A. $450M revolver to Delaware Life (undrawn YE2025; current status unknown); FHFA-OIG dropped member credit risk as "abated" for FY2026; insurer protection in a failure rests on STATE law and collateral delivery, not the federal §10(e) lien (FHFA AB 2013-09).

## What the report omitted or got wrong in direction
1. **Cause and effect reversed (C2).** The February 2026 grand-jury subpoenas *triggered* the internal review and the June 26 reclassification. The probes did not follow the disclosures.
2. **"Restated statutory financial statements" overstates it.** The restatement covered related-party *disclosures* and the affiliated figures in the YE2025 Annual Statement. Capital, surplus and earnings were unchanged, and the audit opinion was unqualified with an emphasis-of-matter paragraph. The open risk is concentration and liquidity, not a solvency restatement.
3. **The current figure is 39%, and a reduction plan exists.** Affiliated assets were 39% ($19.15B) at 2026-06-30. On 2026-08-17 DLIC signed a PSA with TWG Global to swap up to **$6.5B** of affiliated assets for non-affiliated ones (pro forma ≈26%). The targets are 20–25% by YE2026 and <10% by 2027-06-30. Both steps need **regulatory approval**.
4. **CSLAC is more concentrated than DLIC:** 59% affiliated at YE2025 ($5.90B / $9.92B) and 49% at June 2026 (InsuranceNewsNet, 2026-10-09). The report omits it.
5. **Fitch omitted.** Fitch put DLIC (IFS A-) on **Rating Watch Negative** on 2026-07-22. That is a sharper signal than either outlook change.
6. **FHLBI concentration is understated.** DLIC is FHLBI's **#2 borrower (12% of $41.9B advances)** and a ≥5% stockholder. DLIC's +$1.625B in H1 2026 roughly equals *all* of FHLBI's +$1.6B insurance-member advance growth over the period (10-Q). FHLBI's top five borrowers hold 47% of advances.
7. **Utilization peaked and then eased.** It was 97.2% at YE2025 and 93.1% at June 2026 (capacity rose to $5.33B). "Maxed out" describes YE2025 and Q1 2026, not June.
8. **Bank-channel links the report missed:** **Truist and Fifth Third paused sales** of Delaware Life products (Bloomberg/American Banker, 2026-08-29). DLIC also has a **$450M senior revolver from Citizens Bank, N.A.**, effective 2025-08-11 and undrawn at YE2025 (audited FS). The facility is not mentioned in the Q2 2026 notes, so its current status is unknown.
9. **Do not cite the secondary figure "DLIC FHLB $4.42B in 2025"** (WSJ via ProgramBusiness). DLIC's own filing and FHLBI's 10-K both show **$3.338B**.

## Material omissions and directional errors in the report

1. **The 2026 mix shift runs the other way.** Commercial-bank share rose from 49.7% to 56.4% between YE2025 and 6/30/2026. Insurer share peaked at 27.0% in Q1 2026 and fell to **24.9%** in Q2 even as insurer dollars hit a new high of **$202.8B**. Total advances were $813.3B principal, +20% YTD, driven by "large members".
2. **Insurer concentration is visible in the top 10, and the report misses it.** At 6/30/2026, Apollo Global Management (Athene; Des Moines district) was the #4 borrower group at $27.7B (3.4%) and MetLife #9 at $14.2B (1.7%), per 2026Q2CFR Table 5.
3. **Wrong membership figure.** Insurers are 9.7–9.9% of members, not 7.7%.
4. **The GAO finding is inverted.** GAO cites masking and resolution-cost risks as literature concerns that its own results did not support. The report presents them as a GAO finding. The 74% figure is also a bank-only average of outstanding balances over 2015 to Q2 2025, not "advance volume".
5. **OIG de-prioritised member credit risk in FY2026, calling it "abated".** The report cites it as a current priority. Related, not mentioned: FHFA-OIG EVL-2025-005 (9/18/2025) found that several FHLBanks "Faced Challenges Obtaining Timely Supervisory Information" on members.
6. **Fabricated Council quote.** The real letter's argument is about tenor limits, procyclicality and the lack of notice and comment, and it asks for revision through rulemaking. It contains no "emergency discount facilities" claim.
7. **The §1430(e) super-lien does not reliably govern insurer insolvencies.** Protection depends on the domiciliary state's statute (e.g. Iowa's 7-day stay window), on UCC perfection and on collateral delivery. Two FHLBanks (Des Moines, San Francisco; per the coordinator's check) give only the generic §10(e) language.
