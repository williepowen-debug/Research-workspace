# **The Great Contraction: Systemic Credit Risk and Banking Stability in the National Capital Region Amidst the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) Restructuring**

## **Executive Strategic Assessment**

The economic architecture of the Washington D.C. metropolitan area (the National Capital Region or NCR) is undergoing its most profound structural transformation since the post-World War II expansion of the federal administrative state. The establishment of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) in January 2025, and the subsequent implementation of the Deferred Resignation Program and aggressive contract rationalization, has dismantled the "federal put"—the implicit guarantee that the federal government acts as a permanent, counter-cyclical economic stabilizer for the region. As of February 2026, the region confronts the erasure of nearly six years of federal job growth in a span of twelve months, with the District of Columbia and its immediate suburbs shedding over 50,000 direct federal and contractor positions.1

This report provides an exhaustive, forensic examination of the implications of this macro-political shift for the regional banking sector. We focus specifically on four institutions that collectively define the market: **Atlantic Union Bankshares (AUB)**, following its strategic acquisition of **Sandy Spring Bancorp (SASR)**; **Eagle Bancorp (EGBN)**; and **Burke & Herbert Financial Services Corp. (BHRB)**.

The analysis reveals a sharp bifurcation in institutional resilience driven by geographic footprint, asset concentration, and historical balance sheet management. The merger of Atlantic Union and Sandy Spring has created a regional heavyweight capable of absorbing credit shocks through scale and aggressive balance sheet de-risking, primarily by shedding distressed commercial real estate assets inherited from Sandy Spring. Conversely, Eagle Bancorp faces idiosyncratic, existential risks driven by a legacy concentration in downtown Washington D.C. office real estate and a "pure-play" geographic strategy that is now heavily exposed to the epicenter of the fiscal contraction. Burke & Herbert appears structurally shielded by its diversification into West Virginia and a conservative liquidity profile, though its significant municipal bond portfolio requires rigorous monitoring given the deteriorating credit outlook for the District of Columbia.

Furthermore, the municipal credit environment—a critical collateral and investment component for these banks—has fractured. While the District of Columbia faces a negative rating outlook due to plummeting withholding tax revenues and commercial property devaluations 3, wealthy suburban jurisdictions such as Arlington, Alexandria, and Fairfax in Virginia maintain AAA ratings.5 These areas benefit from a "Defense Shield," where the private sector is dominated by national security contractors less susceptible to DOGE's "bureaucracy-focused" cuts than the civilian agencies concentrated in Maryland and D.C. proper.

This document synthesizes financial data, regulatory filings, and market intelligence through the fourth quarter of 2025 to provide a granular assessment of loan portfolio quality, government contractor (GovCon) lending exposure, municipal bond risks, and the long-term viability of the region's banking sector in an era of unprecedented federal austerity.

**1\. The Macroeconomic Structural Break: DOGE and the End of the "Company Town" Model**

### **1.1 The Mechanism of Contraction: From Sequestration to Dismantlement**

To understand the credit risk facing regional banks, one must first dissect the nature of the economic shock. Previous fiscal contractions in the NCR, most notably the **2013 Budget Sequestration**, were characterized by a slowing of the *rate of growth*. The 2013 sequestration imposed across-the-board budget caps that resulted in hiring freezes and furloughs, but rarely mass terminations.7 Federal employment remained relatively sticky; agencies absorbed cuts by deferring maintenance or delaying technology upgrades. The 2018-2019 government shutdown, while disruptive to liquidity, was a temporary cash-flow event where back pay was eventually restored, effectively acting as a forced savings mechanism for federal workers.9

The 2025-2026 DOGE restructuring is fundamentally different. It is not a pause; it is a reversal. The primary mechanism—the **Deferred Resignation Program**—incentivized voluntary separations in early 2025 by offering full pay through September 2025 for employees who agreed to resign.11 This was followed in late 2025 by targeted Reductions in Force (RIF) and the termination of "non-essential" contracts.

By November 2025, the data indicated a structural collapse in federal employment:

* **Virginia:** The Commonwealth lost approximately **23,500 civilian federal jobs** through November 2025, effectively wiping out all federal job gains accrued since 2019\.2  
* **The District of Columbia:** Federal employment declined by **3.2%** year-over-year, with broader regional federal payrolls down **5%**.13  
* **Suburban Maryland:** Counties such as Montgomery and Prince George's saw even steeper declines, with combined federal employment drops approaching **18%** in some sectors.6

The "Deferred Resignation" mechanism created a unique credit latency. Through Q3 2025, consumption and loan performance data remained artificially robust because separated employees were still receiving paychecks. This "severance cushion" evaporated in October 2025\. The subsequent data from the D.C. Office of Revenue Analysis showed a loss of **18,400 jobs** in a single month (September to October 2025), largely driven by the expiration of these arrangements and the immediate knock-on effect on government contractors.14

### **1.2 The Failure of the Multiplier and the Contractor Cliff**

Economic orthodoxy in the NCR relies on the "local multiplier" effect: high-wage federal jobs support a ecosystem of lawyers, consultants, service workers, and retail establishments. Research conducted by the Richmond Fed in mid-2025 initially suggested this multiplier was "muted," as low-skill job openings did not immediately vanish alongside federal positions.15

However, this resilience proved illusory, a lag effect of the deferred compensation. By late 2025, the "Contractor Cliff" arrived. Professional Business Services—the sector code for government contracting—began shedding jobs rapidly.14 Unlike civil servants who have some tenure protections, contractors can be terminated for "convenience of the government" immediately upon contract cancellation. The DOGE initiative targeted administrative and "efficiency" contracts—HR support, DEIA consulting, and general administrative services—sectors heavily populated by small-to-mid-sized businesses that form the core Commercial & Industrial (C\&I) lending base for banks like Eagle Bancorp and Sandy Spring.

### **1.3 Historical Context: Resilience vs. Reality**

Comparing the current environment to historical stress tests reveals the severity of the 2025 scenario.

| Metric | 2013 Sequestration | 2018-2019 Shutdown | 2025-2026 DOGE Restructuring |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Duration** | Structural (10 years) but gradual. | Temporary (35 days). | **Permanent and Structural.** |
| **Federal Job Impact** | Hiring freezes; attrition. | Temporary furloughs (0 jobs lost). | **\>50,000 regional jobs eliminated.** |
| **Contractor Impact** | Delayed payments; scope reduction. | Delayed payments. | **Contract cancellations; business failures.** |
| **Real Estate Impact** | Minimal; vacancy stable. | None. | **Record vacancies; lease terminations.** |
| **Bank Response** | Liquidity management; forbearance. | Fee waivers; short-term loans. | **Charge-offs; capital raising; M\&A.** |

During the 2013 sequestration, banks like **Sandy Spring** noted "disruptions" and slowed loan growth but ultimately reported stable credit quality.18 **Eagle Bancorp** continued to grow its loan book aggressively during this period, pivoting to construction lending.19 In 2025, that playbook is obsolete. The contraction is not a liquidity event to be bridged; it is a solvency event for a segment of the borrower base.

**2\. Regional Sovereign Risk: The Municipal Credit Fracture**

For regional banks, municipalities are both clients (depositors) and investment vehicles (bond issuers). The divergence in municipal credit quality fundamentally alters the risk-weighting of bank investment portfolios.

### **2.1 The District of Columbia: The Epicenter of Stress**

The District of Columbia faces an existential fiscal crisis. Unlike a state, it cannot tax income earned by non-residents (commuters), meaning its revenue base is entirely dependent on residents and commercial property values.

* **Credit Rating Trajectory:** As of January 2026, Moody's assigned a **Negative Outlook** to the District's **Aa1** rating.3  
* **Revenue Collapse:** The District's Chief Financial Officer (CFO) revised withholding tax revenue forecasts downward by nearly **$140 million annually** for FY2026-2029.4 This is a direct consequence of high-income federal workers resigning or relocating.  
* **The Office Tax Loop:** Commercial property taxes historically accounted for a significant portion of D.C.'s revenue. With vacancy rates soaring and assessments being appealed, this revenue stream is collapsing.  
* **Bank Implication:** Banks holding D.C. General Obligation (GO) bonds or Income Tax Secured Revenue Bonds face mark-to-market pressure. While a default remains unlikely due to the oversight of the Financial Control Board, the widening of credit spreads devalues these assets on bank balance sheets, eroding Tangible Common Equity (TCE).

### **2.2 Prince George’s County, MD: The Vulnerable Suburb**

Prince George's County (PGC) has historically held a **AAA** rating but is now under intense pressure.

* **Federal Exposure:** PGC is home to major civilian agencies like the Census Bureau, IRS facilities, and NOAA. It lacks the defense-heavy private sector of Northern Virginia.  
* **Credit Action:** In late 2025, Moody's placed PGC on negative watch or downgraded its outlook, citing "elevated exposure to the federal government" (approx. 9% of workforce).20 S\&P and Fitch maintained the AAA rating earlier in the year 21, but the deteriorating labor market data from late 2025 6 suggests a downgrade is a material risk in 2026\.  
* **Bank Exposure:** **Sandy Spring Bank** has a legacy dominance in PGC. A downgrade here would increase the cost of borrowing for the county and slow the infrastructure projects (schools, roads) that Sandy Spring finances.

### **2.3 Northern Virginia: The Defense Shield**

In stark contrast, Northern Virginia (NoVA) jurisdictions—**Fairfax County**, **Arlington County**, and the **City of Alexandria**—remain bastions of credit stability.

* **Ratings:** All three maintained **AAA/Aaa** ratings through late 2025\.5  
* **The "Beltway Bandit" Buffer:** The DOGE cuts targeted "bureaucracy." They largely spared the "hard power" apparatus of the defense industrial base. NoVA is the Silicon Valley of the defense sector, hosting headquarters for General Dynamics, Northrop Grumman, and a dense web of cybersecurity firms. These entities have diversified revenue streams (foreign military sales, classified intelligence budgets) that are insulated from civil service reductions.  
* **Bank Implication:** **Atlantic Union** (AUB) and **Burke & Herbert** (BHRB) are heavily weighted toward these jurisdictions. This geographic luck—or strategic foresight—provides a critical ballast to their loan portfolios.

**3\. Institutional Deep Dive: Eagle Bancorp (EGBN)**

**Ticker:** EGBN

**Headquarters:** Bethesda, MD

**Strategic Position:** The Pure-Play D.C. Business Bank

Eagle Bancorp represents the highest-beta play on the D.C. economy. Founded on the thesis of being *the* lender to the local business community—specifically real estate developers and government contractors—the bank is now heavily exposed to the specific sectors targeted by the structural contraction.

### **3.1 Balance Sheet and Asset Quality: The Office Albatross**

EagleBank's primary vulnerability is its concentration in income-producing Commercial Real Estate (CRE), particularly office space.

* **CRE Concentration:** As of year-end 2024, income-producing office CRE represented **10.9%** of the total loan portfolio.24 While seemingly manageable, this figure understates the risk because a significant portion of the bank's "owner-occupied" CRE is also tied to government contractors who are downsizing.  
* **Credit Deterioration:** The stress became visible in Q3 2025\. The bank reported **$140.8 million in net charge-offs (NCOs)** for the quarter, a massive spike from $83.9 million in the prior quarter.25 This indicates a capitulation strategy: rather than "extending and pretending," management is recognizing losses on office properties that can no longer support debt service due to vacancy.  
* **Non-Performing Assets (NPAs):** NPAs stood at **$133.3 million** (1.23% of assets) in Q3 2025\.25 While the ratio declined due to the aggressive charge-offs, the absolute level of stress remains high. The "performing office coverage" ratio of **11.36%** suggests the bank has built a fortress reserve against this specific asset class, anticipating further pain.25

### **3.2 Historical Resilience vs. Current Reality**

In the 2013 sequestration, EagleBank grew its loan book aggressively. Between 2013 and 2018, the bank capitalized on the retreat of larger national banks to capture market share in D.C. construction lending.19

* **2018-2019 Shutdown:** During the 35-day shutdown, EagleBank's localized decision-making allowed it to offer forbearance and liquidity lines to affected contractors, cementing client loyalty.  
* **2025 Failure:** That playbook relies on the borrower eventually getting paid. In the DOGE scenario, contracts are cancelled. There is no "back pay" for a terminated contract. Consequently, the bank's C\&I portfolio (historically a source of strength) faces higher correlations with its CRE portfolio than in previous cycles.

### **3.3 Regulatory and Governance Shadows**

EagleBank is navigating this economic storm while still emerging from a significant governance crisis. In 2022, the bank paid **$22.9 million** to settle SEC and Federal Reserve charges regarding undisclosed related-party loans to its former CEO, Ronald Paul.26

* **Operational Impact:** The remediation of these issues likely entails higher compliance costs and a more conservative risk appetite enforced by regulators. At a time when a bank might need flexibility to work out troubled loans, EagleBank is likely under strict regulatory scrutiny, limiting its maneuverability.

### **3.4 Capital and Liquidity**

Despite these headwinds, EagleBank has prioritized liquidity.

* **Liquidity Ratio:** At 6.9%, Eagle maintains one of the highest liquid assets-to-deposits ratios among peers.28  
* **Funding Mix:** In Q3 2025, the bank took a **$2.0 million loss** on the sale of investment securities to reduce its reliance on high-cost brokered deposits.25 This defensive move improves the quality of the liability stack but drags on earnings.

**4\. Institutional Deep Dive: The Atlantic Union (AUB) and Sandy Spring (SASR) Merger**

**Ticker:** AUB

**Headquarters:** Richmond, VA

**Strategic Position:** The Regional Consolidator

The defining financial event of the region in 2024-2025 was Atlantic Union Bankshares' acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp. This transaction serves as a case study in how scale can be used to mitigate localized credit risk.

### **4.1 Sandy Spring: The Fall of a Legacy Franchise**

Sandy Spring Bancorp, headquartered in Olney, MD, was the oldest independent bank in the region. Its demise as an independent entity was driven by an over-concentration in CRE that became untenable as rates rose and the Maryland suburbs slowed.

* **The Impairment Signal:** In Q4 2024, prior to the merger close, Sandy Spring reported a net loss of **$39.5 million**, driven by a **$54.4 million goodwill impairment charge**.29 This was a clear signal that the market value of the franchise had eroded below its book value, necessitating the sale.  
* **CRE Exposure:** At the time of the merger, CRE loans totaled **$7.9 billion**, or **68%** of Sandy Spring's total loan portfolio.30 This concentration was significantly above regulatory guidance thresholds (typically 300% of capital), forcing the bank into a defensive posture where it could not grow.

### **4.2 Atlantic Union's Integration Strategy**

Atlantic Union, headquartered in Richmond, utilized the merger to expand its footprint into the wealthy D.C. suburbs while simultaneously "de-risking" the Sandy Spring balance sheet.

* **Loan Sales:** The merger agreement included explicit provisions for the sale of a portion of Sandy Spring's CRE loan book immediately post-closing.31 This allowed AUB to mark these assets to market and remove them from the combined entity's risk profile.  
* **Performance Post-Merger:** By Q4 2025, the combined entity demonstrated the success of this strategy. AUB reported **net income growth of 21.5%** and an **Adjusted Operating ROA of 1.50%**.32  
* **Credit Quality:** Net charge-offs for the combined bank were a pristine **1 basis point** in Q4 2025\.33 This suggests that the credit marks taken during the acquisition were sufficient, and the remaining portfolio is performing well.

### **4.3 Government Contractor Lending: A Strategic Vertical**

AUB has positioned its Government Contracting (GovCon) vertical as a growth engine, differentiating itself from generalist lenders.

* **Portfolio Size:** As of Q1 2025, the GovCon portfolio stood at **$770 million**.34  
* **Sector Selection:** AUB explicitly targets **national security and defense agencies**, avoiding the civilian bureaucracy targeted by DOGE. Their investor materials highlight active monitoring of contract terminations and stop-work orders.34  
* **Resilience:** The portfolio showed **0.00% Net Charge-Offs** and **0.07% Non-Performing Loans** in early 2025\.34 This validates the thesis that defense spending provides a credit floor even amidst broader austerity.

### **4.4 Geographic Arbitrage**

The merger allows AUB to arbitrage the regional economic divergence.

* **Market Share:** The bank is now the \#1 regional bank in both Virginia and Maryland.31  
* **Risk Transfer:** By using the stable, low-cost deposit base from Sandy Spring's Maryland branches (which have historically sticky deposits) to fund lending in the higher-growth markets of Northern Virginia and North Carolina, AUB optimizes its balance sheet. It effectively extracts liquidity from the distressed zone (MD) and deploys it in the growth zone (VA/NC).

**5\. Institutional Deep Dive: Burke & Herbert Financial Services (BHRB)**

**Ticker:** BHRB

**Headquarters:** Alexandria, VA

**Strategic Position:** The Diversified Conservative

Burke & Herbert offers a contrasting profile to both the aggressive EagleBank and the acquisitive Atlantic Union. As a historic institution (founded 1852), it maintains a conservative culture that prioritized liquidity over growth during the boom years, a strategy that is now paying dividends.

### **5.1 The Summit Merger: Diversification as a Hedge**

In May 2024, Burke & Herbert completed a merger of equals with **Summit Financial Group**, a West Virginia-based bank. At the time, this was viewed as a standard scale play. In hindsight, it was a masterful hedge against D.C. risk.

* **Footprint:** The combined bank operates over 75 branches across Virginia, West Virginia, Maryland, Delaware, and Kentucky.35  
* **Economic Decoupling:** The West Virginia and Kentucky markets are driven by energy, healthcare, and light manufacturing. These economies are uncorrelated with the federal employment cycle. While D.C. contracts, the energy-rich regions of BHRB's footprint provide a counter-cyclical revenue stream.

### **5.2 Commercial Real Estate Profile**

While BHRB has significant CRE exposure (**46.5%** of loans as of year-end 2024 36), its composition differs materially from EagleBank.

* **No Downtown Exposure:** Management explicitly notes "minimal office building exposure within Washington D.C.".37  
* **Suburban Focus:** The CRE portfolio is weighted toward suburban retail, medical offices, and multifamily projects in Northern Virginia and West Virginia. These asset classes have held up better than central business district (CBD) office space because the population in the exurbs has remained more stable than the city center.

### **5.3 Municipal Bond Portfolio Risks**

A key area of scrutiny for BHRB is its substantial securities portfolio.

* **Portfolio Size:** As of Q4 2025, the bank held **$922 million** (fair value) in municipal bonds.35 This represents a significant portion of its total assets (\~$8 billion).  
* **Valuation Pressure:** The portfolio had **$54.5 million in net unrealized losses**.35  
* **Credit Risk:** While the bank classifies these as Available-for-Sale (AFS), it intends to hold them. However, if the underlying issuers (specifically D.C. or Maryland counties) suffer further downgrades, the market value of these bonds could decline further. While this doesn't impact regulatory capital for a bank of this size (due to the AOCI opt-out), it does depress Tangible Book Value (TBV), potentially weighing on the stock valuation.

### **5.4 Financial Performance**

Burke & Herbert's conservatism is reflected in its steady, if unspectacular, metrics.

* **Profitability:** In Q4 2025, the bank reported an annualized **ROAA of 1.49%**.35  
* **Margins:** Net Interest Margin (NIM) remained stable in the **3.50%-3.60%** range.38  
* **Capital:** The bank remains well-capitalized with a common equity tier 1 ratio of roughly **11%**.24

**6\. Comparative Sectoral Analysis & Outlook**

### **6.1 The Office CRE Maturity Wall**

The D.C. region faces a "Maturity Wall" of CRE loans originated between 2015 and 2019 that are coming due in 2025 and 2026\.

* **The Valuation Gap:** These loans were underwritten at cap rates of 4-5%. Today, office cap rates in D.C. are closer to 7-8%, implying value destruction of 30-40%.  
* **LTV Breaches:** Even fully performing loans are breaching Loan-to-Value (LTV) covenants upon appraisal.  
* **Bank Response:**  
  * **EagleBank:** Is taking the pain now (charge-offs).  
  * **Atlantic Union:** Sold the problem (loan sales).  
  * **Burke & Herbert:** Avoided the problem (suburban focus).

### **6.2 The GovCon Lending Nuance**

Lending to government contractors is no longer a monolithic asset class. Banks must distinguish between "Survivors" and "Targets."

| Contractor Type | Risk Profile | Bank Exposure |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Defense/Intel (Hardware/Cyber)** | **Low.** Shielded by national security mandates. | **Atlantic Union (High)**, Burke & Herbert (Moderate) |
| **IT Services/Cloud** | **Moderate.** Efficiency drives may consolidate contracts. | **EagleBank (High)**, Sandy Spring Legacy (High) |
| **Admin/HR/DEIA Consulting** | **Critical.** Primary target of DOGE cuts. | **EagleBank (High)** |

The "Contractor Cliff" of late 2025 suggests that banks with heavy exposure to small, service-based civilian contractors will see a spike in C\&I non-performing loans in Q1-Q2 2026\.

### **6.3 Municipal Dependent Risks**

The correlation between municipal health and bank health is tightening.

* **Deposit Flight:** As municipalities like D.C. and Prince George's County face budget deficits, they may draw down their deposits held at local banks to fund operations. This creates a liquidity outflow for banks just as loan stress is rising.  
* **Taxation:** To plug budget holes, D.C. may increase taxes on financial institutions or commercial transactions, further acting as a drag on bank earnings.

## ---

**7\. Strategic Synthesis and Investment Conclusions**

The 2025-2026 period represents a definitive end to the "Recession-Proof" narrative of the Washington D.C. banking sector. The region is no longer a monolith; it is a fractured landscape of winners and losers determined by their proximity to the "Efficiency" blast radius.

### **7.1 Eagle Bancorp (EGBN): The Value Trap**

Eagle Bancorp is in a defensive crouch. Its concentration in D.C. office space and civilian GovCon lending places it directly in the path of the DOGE restructuring. While its liquidity is strong, its earnings power is impaired by the need to provision for loan losses. The bank is a candidate for consolidation or a prolonged period of below-average returns as it works out its legacy portfolio.

### **7.2 Atlantic Union Bankshares (AUB): The Apex Predator**

Atlantic Union has successfully executed a pivot. By acquiring Sandy Spring and purging its CRE risk, it has secured the region's premier deposit franchise without the associated asset overhang. Its focus on Northern Virginia and defense lending aligns perfectly with the "surviving" sectors of the regional economy. It is the investable proxy for the stabilized post-DOGE mid-Atlantic.

### **7.3 Burke & Herbert (BHRB): The Safe Harbor**

Burke & Herbert offers stability. Its diversification into West Virginia and lack of D.C. office exposure insulate it from the worst of the shock. However, its upside is capped by the slow growth of its new markets. It remains a solid, if unexciting, hold for capital preservation.

### **7.4 Final Outlook**

For the next 12 to 24 months, credit costs in the NCR will remain elevated. The full impact of the federal workforce reduction will ripple through the residential mortgage and consumer auto portfolios in 2026 as severance packages run out. Banks that have already taken their medicine (AUB via purchase accounting, EGBN via charge-offs) may begin to stabilize, but the growth story of the D.C. bank is fundamentally broken. The future belongs to those who can gather deposits in the wealthy suburbs and lend them elsewhere.
