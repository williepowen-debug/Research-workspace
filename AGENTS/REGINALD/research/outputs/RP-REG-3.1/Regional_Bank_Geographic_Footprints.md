# **Strategic Geography and Deposit Dynamics: A Comprehensive Analysis of U.S. Regional Banking Footprints**

## **1\. Introduction: The New Geography of Regional Banking**

The landscape of United States regional banking is undergoing a profound structural transformation, driven by a "flight to quality" in deposits, the normalization of interest rates, and demographic shifts that are redrawing the map of economic power. This report provides an exhaustive analysis of fifteen prominent regional banks—constituents of the KRE Regional Banking ETF—examining their geographic footprints, branch architectures, and deposit concentrations as of late 2024 and early 2025\.

The strategic imperative for these institutions has shifted from pure asset generation to the cultivation of high-quality, granular deposit franchises. In an era where the cost of funds has risen dramatically, the geographic location of a bank’s branches—and the specific economic vitality of the Metropolitan Statistical Areas (MSAs) they serve—has become the primary determinant of net interest margin (NIM) stability and franchise value.

Our analysis reveals a sector bifurcated by strategy. On one side are the "Deposit Fortresses" like Huntington Bancshares (HBAN) and M\&T Bank (MTB), which leverage dense branch networks in the industrial Midwest and Northeast to gather low-beta retail deposits. These institutions are increasingly acting as exporters of capital, using their stable funding bases to finance commercial expansion in the high-growth Sunbelt. On the other side are the "Commercial Specialists" like Western Alliance (WAL) and ServisFirst (SFBS), which eschew heavy retail footprints in favor of branch-light, commercial-heavy models that rely on specialized business deposits.

Furthermore, this report scrutinizes the specific exposure of these banks to four critical regions of interest:

1. **The Capital Region (DC/NoVA/MD):** A recession-resistant fortress of government contracting and affluent professional services.  
2. **The Texas Border Region:** A distinct economic zone driven by cross-border trade and logistics, often distinct from the broader Texas "Triangle."  
3. **Florida:** The primary destination for domestic wealth migration, creating a fierce battleground for deposit market share.  
4. **Southern California / Imperial Valley:** A complex market ranging from the wealth of coastal San Diego to the banking deserts of the agricultural interior.

The following sections provide a granular, institution-level analysis of these dynamics.

**2\. Atlantic Union Bank (AUB)**

### **2.1 Corporate Profile and Strategic Footprint**

**Headquarters:** Richmond, Virginia

Atlantic Union Bank represents the quintessential "state champion" model, positioning itself as the largest independent bank headquartered in Virginia. The bank’s strategy is predicated on dominating the "Golden Crescent" of Virginia—an arc extending from Northern Virginia (NoVA) through Richmond to the Hampton Roads tidewater region. This geography offers a unique blend of government-stabilized employment in the north and defense/logistics activity in the south.

Strategically, Atlantic Union is in the midst of a transformative expansion. The pending acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp is a definitive move to densify its presence in the wealthy Maryland suburbs of Washington D.C., effectively creating a contiguous "Mid-Atlantic Powerhouse" that bridges the Potomac River. This consolidation is driven by the need to capture the full commercial ecosystem of the Greater Washington region, allowing the bank to serve middle-market companies that operate across the DMV (DC, Maryland, Virginia) tri-state area.

### **2.2 Deposit Concentration and Primary Markets**

The bank’s deposit base is heavily weighted toward stable, core retail deposits derived from its deep community roots. By leveraging its "Main Street" brand, AUB competes effectively against national money-center banks by offering superior local decision-making for commercial clients.

* **Primary Operating States:** Virginia, Maryland, North Carolina.  
* **Top 5 MSAs by Deposit Concentration:**  
  1. **Richmond, VA:** The bank's home market and historical stronghold. Atlantic Union commands a dominant deposit market share here, serving as the primary bank for many of the state's legacy businesses and law firms.  
  2. **Virginia Beach-Norfolk-Newport News, VA-NC (Hampton Roads):** A critical market anchored by the largest naval base in the world. The bank's deposits here are bolstered by defense contractors and maritime industries.  
  3. **Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV:** Prior to the Sandy Spring acquisition, AUB’s presence was largely confined to the Virginia side (Northern Virginia). Post-acquisition, this MSA is projected to become the bank's largest source of deposits, leveraging the highest median household incomes in the nation.  
  4. **Roanoke, VA:** A key market in Western Virginia where AUB acts as a dominant community bank, gathering stable, lower-cost retail deposits.  
  5. **Baltimore-Columbia-Towson, MD:** Through the Sandy Spring merger, AUB gains immediate scale in this diverse market, which includes the bio-health corridor and the Port of Baltimore.

### **2.3 Regional Exposure Analysis**

* **DC/NoVA/MD Concentration:** This region is the strategic linchpin of AUB’s future. The acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp is explicitly designed to conquer the "Capital Region." The combined entity will have a dense branch network enveloping the Capital Beltway, providing access to a client base insulated from national economic volatility by federal spending. This exposure is highly favorable for credit quality, though deposit costs in this competitive market can be high.  
* **Texas Border Region:** No disclosed concentration. The bank does not operate in Texas.  
* **Florida:** No retail presence.  
* **Southern California:** No presence.

### **2.4 Branch Network Architecture**

Atlantic Union operates a traditional branch-heavy model, viewing physical presence as essential for gathering low-cost core deposits.

* **Total Branches:** \~129 (Legacy) / \~180+ (Pro Forma post-merger).  
* **Branch Count by State:**  
  * **Virginia:** \~109 locations (Dominant state).  
  * **Maryland:** \~50+ locations (Pro forma via Sandy Spring).  
  * **North Carolina:** \~20 locations (Focused on the Research Triangle and Northeast NC).

**3\. Burke & Herbert Financial Services Corp. (BHRB)**

### **3.1 Corporate Profile and Strategic Footprint**

**Headquarters:** Alexandria, Virginia

Burke & Herbert Bank & Trust Company, established in 1852, holds the distinction of being the oldest continuously operating bank under its original name in the Washington D.C. area. Historically, the bank was a hyper-local institution focused on the affluent, historic districts of Alexandria and Northern Virginia. However, the recent merger with Summit Financial Group has radically altered its identity, transforming it from a dense, urban wealth-management bank into a diversified regional player with a rural and suburban footprint stretching across Appalachia.

### **3.2 Deposit Concentration and Primary Markets**

The integration of Summit Financial has created a "barbell" deposit structure. On one end, BHRB retains its legacy deposits from high-net-worth individuals in the DC suburbs. On the other, it now aggregates deposits from rural West Virginia and Kentucky, which typically exhibit much lower beta (price sensitivity) than metropolitan deposits.

* **Primary Operating States:** Virginia, West Virginia, Kentucky, Maryland, Delaware.  
* **Top 5 MSAs by Deposit Concentration:**  
  1. **Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV:** The legacy core. The bank caters to "Old Virginia" money and established commercial businesses in Alexandria and Fairfax County.  
  2. **Richmond, VA:** A growing commercial hub for the bank.  
  3. **Hagerstown-Martinsburg, MD-WV:** A key market gained from Summit, serving the exurbs of DC and the logistics corridors of I-81.  
  4. **Lexington-Fayette, KY:** A new market entry providing diversification into the equine, healthcare, and university sectors.  
  5. **Charleston, WV:** Provides stability through government-related deposits in the state capital of West Virginia.

### **3.3 Regional Exposure Analysis**

* **DC/NoVA/MD Concentration:** This remains the bank's "crown jewel." BHRB highlights its "Strong presence in Northern VA's affluent, high-income markets" as a primary investment thesis. The bank leverages its historic brand to compete for trust and estate services, a lucrative source of sticky deposits.  
* **Texas Border Region:** No presence.  
* **Florida:** No presence.  
* **Southern California:** No presence.

### **3.4 Branch Network Architecture**

The merger has expanded the branch count significantly, diluting the historically high deposits-per-branch metric of the Alexandria legacy franchise but improving overall geographic diversification.

* **Total Branches:** \~75+  
* **Branch Count by State (Estimated):**  
  * **Virginia:** \~35 locations (Northern VA and Richmond).  
  * **West Virginia:** \~25 locations (Eastern Panhandle and Charleston).  
  * **Kentucky:** \~10 locations (Lexington area).  
  * **Maryland/Delaware:** \~5 locations.

**4\. Citizens Financial Group (CFG)**

### **4.1 Corporate Profile and Strategic Footprint**

**Headquarters:** Providence, Rhode Island

Citizens Financial Group is a "Super-Regional" bank, operating with the scale and sophistication of a national money center bank while maintaining regional retail roots. With assets exceeding $220 billion, its footprint is massive, covering the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Midwest. Citizens operates a "Universal Bank" model, combining a massive retail deposit gathering engine with high-end commercial lending, capital markets, and a newly launched private banking division.

### **4.2 Deposit Concentration and Primary Markets**

Citizens' deposit strategy is focused on density. It aims to be a top-5 bank in every major metro it serves. The bank has been actively remixing its deposit base, letting expensive promotional deposits run off while acquiring relationship-based accounts through expansion in the New York Metro area.

* **Primary Operating States:** Massachusetts, Pennsylvania, New York, Rhode Island, Michigan, Connecticut, New Jersey, Ohio, Delaware, Vermont, New Hampshire.  
* **Top 5 MSAs by Deposit Concentration:**  
  1. **Boston-Cambridge-Newton, MA-NH:** Citizens holds a top-tier market share here (approx. 31% in some sub-markets), competing fiercely with Bank of America. The education and biotech sectors drive commercial deposits.  
  2. **Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington, PA-NJ-DE:** A legacy stronghold. Citizens is ubiquitous in the Delaware Valley.  
  3. **Providence-Warwick, RI:** The bank’s home market, where it enjoys a dominant, quasi-utility status.  
  4. **New York-Newark-Jersey City, NY-NJ-PA:** A major growth priority. Through the acquisition of Investors Bank and HSBC’s East Coast branches, Citizens has bought its way into significant market share in the NYC metro, targeting both retail and middle-market commercial clients.  
  5. **Detroit-Warren-Dearborn, MI:** A legacy footprint from its Charter One days, providing steady industrial and consumer deposits.

### **4.3 Regional Exposure Analysis**

* **DC/NoVA/MD Concentration:** Citizens has a visible retail presence in the Mid-Atlantic, though it is less dominant here than in New England. It views this region as a natural extension of its Northeast corridor strategy.  
* **Florida:** Citizens operates in Florida with a focus on **Wealth Management and Commercial Banking**. Unlike its northern footprint, the Florida presence is "branch-light," targeting high-net-worth retirees and commercial real estate developers rather than mass-market checking accounts.  
* **Southern California:** The bank has recently hired private banking teams in California (San Francisco/LA) to serve ultra-high-net-worth clients, capitalizing on the dislocation caused by the failures of First Republic and Silicon Valley Bank.  
* **Texas Border:** No retail presence.

### **4.4 Branch Network Architecture**

Citizens is optimizing its massive network, closing redundant branches in legacy markets while opening flagship centers in growth markets like NYC and South Florida.

* **Total Branches:** \~1,000  
* **Branch Count by State (Approximate Distribution):**

  * **Rhode Island:** \~70+

**9\. Huntington Bancshares (HBAN)**

### **9.1 Corporate Profile and Strategic Footprint**

**Headquarters:** Columbus, Ohio

Huntington Bancshares is the "King of the Midwest." With a brand built on fairness ("Welcome") and customer-centric products (24-hour grace periods), it has built a fortress of low-cost retail deposits across the Rust Belt. However, recognizing the demographic headwinds of the Midwest, Huntington has launched a bold commercial expansion strategy, establishing verticals in Texas and the Carolinas to lend out its abundant liquidity in higher-growth markets.

### **9.2 Deposit Concentration and Primary Markets**

Huntington’s deposit base is one of the most granular and stable in the industry, heavily weighted toward consumer checking accounts. This low cost of funds gives it a competitive advantage in pricing commercial loans.

* **Primary Operating States:** Ohio, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Indiana, Illinois, Minnesota, West Virginia, Colorado, Wisconsin.

### **9.3 Regional Exposure Analysis**

* **Texas / Carolinas:** Huntington has explicitly targeted these regions for **commercial expansion**. This is an "asset-side" strategy. **10\. M\&T Bank (MTB)**

### **10.1 Corporate Profile and Strategic Footprint**

**Headquarters:** Buffalo, New York

M\&T Bank is widely respected for its conservative credit culture and operational discipline. The bank has steadily expanded from its Buffalo roots to become a dominant force in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic. ### **10.2 Deposit Concentration and Primary Markets**

M\&T’s deposit franchise is characterized by dominant market shares in secondary and tertiary cities (Buffalo, Rochester, Baltimore) where competition is less fierce than in NYC or Boston. This pricing power allows M\&T to maintain a low cost of deposits.

* **Primary Operating States:** New York, Maryland, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Delaware, Virginia.  
* **Top 5 MSAs by Deposit Concentration:**  
  1. **Buffalo-Cheektowaga-Niagara Falls, NY:** \#1 Market Share. M\&T is the hometown bank, dominating local commerce.

  3. **Bridgeport-Stamford-Norwalk, CT:** \#1 Market Share (via People's United). This wealthy corridor serves as a bridge to the NYC market.  
  4. **Rochester, NY:** \#1 Market Share.  
  5. **Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV:** A top-tier presence in the suburbs, supported by a dense branch network.

### **10.3 Regional Exposure Analysis**

* **DC/NoVA/MD Concentration:** **High.** M\&T is a major player in the Capital Region. Unlike EagleBank (commercial focus), M\&T offers a full retail branch network across Maryland and Northern Virginia, capturing consumer deposits to fund its commercial book.  
* **Texas Border/Florida/SoCal:** No retail presence.

### **10.4 Branch Network Architecture**

M\&T operates the third-largest branch network in the Northeast, viewing density as a competitive advantage in its "community banking" model.

* **Total Branches:** \~1,000

**18\. Conclusion**

The analysis of these fifteen regional banks underscores a decisive trend in the U.S. financial sector: **Geography is Destiny.** The banks that have outperformed in the current cycle are those that have either (1) dominated a high-growth, low-tax geography (e.g., **First Horizon** in Tennessee, **ServisFirst** in the Southeast) or (2) established a specialized niche that transcends geography (e.g., **Western Alliance** in tech/HOA banking).

For the specific regions of interest:

* **The Capital Region (DC/NoVA/MD)** remains a fortress of stability. **Eagle Bancorp** and **Burke & Herbert** offer pure-play exposure, while **Atlantic Union** and **M\&T** are using scale to consolidate the market.  
* **Florida** has become the industry's most contested battleground. **Valley National** and **First Horizon** are aggressively moving capital and personnel into the state to capture wealth migration.  
* **Texas** is being approached cautiously. While **Zions (Amegy)** is a distinct local player, outsiders like **Huntington** are choosing "asset-light" commercial expansions rather than building expensive retail networks in a saturated market.  
* **Southern California** sees a split: mass-market retail is ceded to the giants, while lucrative niches (entertainment, tech, private banking) are fiercely contested by **Western Alliance**, **City National** (not in KRE top), and the newly repositioned **Flagstar**.

Investors utilizing this data should prioritize banks with "Deposit Density"—a dominant share in their core markets—as this pricing power is the only durable hedge against the "higher-for-longer" interest rate environment.
