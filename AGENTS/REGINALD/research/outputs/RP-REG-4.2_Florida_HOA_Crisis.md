# RP-REG-4.2: Florida HOA/Condo Assessment Crisis

**Research Prompt:** Florida Condo Special Assessment Crisis and Regional Bank Exposure

**Completed:** 2026-02-05

**Status:** ✅ COMPLETE

---

## Executive Summary

**Florida's post-Surfside condo legislation (SB 4-D, HB 1021) has triggered a wave of special assessments ranging from $10,000 to $224,000+ per unit, forcing owners to sell and flooding the market with aging condos that buyers can't finance.** This creates a secondary stress channel for FL-exposed banks beyond the existing insurance "doom loop" documented in RP-REG-3.5.

**Key Finding:** While Florida is NOT a super-lien state (mortgage liens retain priority), the assessment crisis creates bank stress through:
1. Borrower DTI stress → loan defaults
2. Collateral value impairment → LTV degradation
3. Market dysfunction → reduced origination volume
4. Receivership cascade → distressed asset overhang

**Bank Exposure:** SBCF, VLY, BKU, FHN most exposed. This **compounds** the existing FL insurance channel rather than creating a new independent stress path.

## 1. Post-Surfside Legislation Overview

### The Surfside Collapse (June 24, 2021)

- Champlain Towers South (12-story beachfront condo) collapsed
- 98 deaths — worst structural building failure in US history
- Cause: Decades of deferred maintenance, inadequate reserves, ignored engineering warnings
- Reserve study showed association had 6.9% of recommended reserves ($0.7M vs $10.3M needed)

### Legislative Response: SB 4-D (2022) + HB 913/HB 1021 (2023-2025)

| Requirement | Deadline | Impact |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| **Milestone Inspections** | Dec 31, 2024 (30+ year buildings) | Licensed structural engineer inspection required |
| **SIRS (Structural Integrity Reserve Studies)** | Dec 31, 2024 | 10-year assessment of structural components |
| **Full Reserve Funding** | Jan 1, 2025 | Associations can NO LONGER waive reserves |
| **Digital Transparency** | Jan 1, 2026 | SIRS/budgets must be online for 25+ unit buildings |

### What Changed

**Before SB 4-D:**
- Associations could vote to "waive" reserve contributions (kept dues low)
- No mandatory structural inspections for most buildings
- Deferred maintenance was standard practice

**After SB 4-D:**
- Mandatory reserves for: roofs, load-bearing walls, foundations, fire protection, plumbing, electrical, waterproofing, windows, parking structures
- No waiver option — full funding required
- Milestone inspection failures trigger immediate repair mandates
- Non-compliance = potential fines, occupancy restrictions

### Special Assessment Data Points

### Why Assessments Are So Large

1. **Decades of Deferred Maintenance** — Many 30-50 year old buildings haven't had major repairs
2. **Salt-Air Corrosion** — Coastal buildings face accelerated concrete deterioration
3. **Zero Reserve Starting Point** — Associations that waived reserves have nothing banked
4. **All-at-Once Mandate** — Must fund 10-year SIRS projections immediately
5. **Insurance Premium Shock** — FEMA Risk Rating 2.0 adds 15-18% annual premium increases

### Market Impact

**Florida Policy Project (Jan 2025) Key Findings:**

| Metric | Data |
|--------|------|
| Total FL condo units | ~1.1 million |
| Units 30+ years old | 900,000+ |

| Miami-Dade condos 30+ years | ~65% of inventory |
| Half of Miami-Dade sales <$300K | Previously affordable housing option |

**Market Dysfunction:**
- Buyers can't finance aging condos (Fannie/Freddie warrantability issues)
- Sellers flooding market but no demand
- Developer bulk purchases stalled (legal uncertainty from Two Roads case)
- Receivership expected for associations that can't fund assessments

## 3. HOA Lien Priority in Florida

### Critical Legal Framework

**Florida is NOT a "super-lien" state.**

This means:
- Mortgage liens retain priority over HOA/COA liens
- HOA foreclosure does NOT wipe out the first mortgage
- Buyer at HOA foreclosure sale takes property SUBJECT TO existing mortgage

### Safe Harbor Provisions

**Florida Statutes §720.3085 (HOA) and §718.116 (COA):**

When a first mortgagee forecloses and takes title, their liability for past-due assessments is capped at the **lesser of:**
1. 12 months of unpaid regular/special assessments
2. 1% of the original mortgage debt

**Example:**
- Condo owner defaults with $150,000 in unpaid assessments
- Bank forecloses on $300,000 mortgage
- Bank's liability to HOA: MAX($3,000 or 12 months of dues)
- HOA eats most of the loss

### Implication for Banks

**Good news:** Banks are NOT directly liable for massive special assessments
**Bad news:** The borrower stress from assessments flows through other channels:
- Borrower can't pay $100K assessment → defaults on mortgage
- Property values crater → LTV degrades → loss severity increases
- Market dysfunction → can't sell/refi → foreclosure only exit

```
   ┌─────────────┐      ┌─────────────┐      ┌─────────────┐

   └─────────────┘      └─────────────┘      └─────────────┘
          │                     │                     │

   └─────────────┘      └─────────────┘      └─────────────┘
          │                     │                     │
          └─────────────────────┼─────────────────────┘
                                │
                    ┌───────────▼─────────────┐
                    │  FL Bank CRE/Resi       │
                    │  Portfolio Stress       │
                    └─────────────────────────┘
```

### Channel 1: Borrower DTI Stress

- Owner faces $100K assessment
- Can't pay → defaults on mortgage
- Bank has performing loan go nonperforming
- Most FL condo loans are 1-4 family residential, not CRE

### Channel 2: Collateral Impairment

- 56% YoY surge in listings = supply glut
- Buyers can't finance 30+ year condos
- Prices crater (Miami Herald: 25%+ price cuts)
- Bank's collateral worth less than loan balance

### Channel 3: Market Dysfunction

- Fannie/Freddie warrantability issues for aging condos
- New originations impossible for non-compliant buildings
- Refi not available as escape valve
- Only exits: cash sale or foreclosure

### Channel 4: Receivership Cascade

- Associations that can't fund assessments face receivership
- Courts appoint receiver → all decisions require court approval
- Timeline extends → foreclosure backlog grows
- Buildings may become functionally unlendable

### Geographic Concentration

**Most affected counties (58% of FL condos):**
- **Miami-Dade** — 65% of condos are 30+ years old
- **Broward** — High coastal exposure
- **Palm Beach** — Luxury market, large assessments
- **Pinellas** — Older building stock
- **Collier, Sarasota, Hillsborough, Manatee** — Growing exposure

### SBCF — Purest Expression

**Seacoast Banking (SBCF):**
- ~100% Florida concentration
- Heavy presence in "The Villages" and coastal markets
- Residential real estate = significant book exposure
- NO geographic hedge
- Condo crisis compounds insurance doom loop exposure

**Risk:** Double exposure — insurance assessments (Citizens) PLUS structural assessments (SB 4-D) hitting same borrower population

### VLY — CRE Focus Somewhat Insulates

**Valley National (VLY):**
- 27% FL, but focused on commercial CRE
- Commercial condos face similar issues but different buyer base
- VLY's FL risk is primarily CRE, not 1-4 family condo
- Still 🔴 HIGH due to overall FL concentration

### Quantifying the Exposure Gap

**Data limitation:** Banks don't specifically disclose "Florida condo" loan concentration.

Residential portfolios include:
- Single-family homes (likely majority)
- Condos/townhomes (undisclosed %)
- HOA communities (undisclosed %)

**Proxy indicators:**
- MSA concentration (Miami-Dade, Broward = highest condo density)
- "Attached dwelling" disclosures in some 10-Ks
- Fannie/Freddie condo warrantability issues in pipeline

## 6. Compounding Effect with Insurance Doom Loop

### The FL "Triple Threat" for Condo Owners

| Stress Channel | Source | Magnitude |
|----------------|--------|-----------|
| **Citizens Assessment** | Hurricane deficit | 10-15% of policy premium |
| **Private Insurance Premium** | FEMA Risk Rating 2.0 | +15-18%/year |
| **Structural Assessment** | SB 4-D compliance | $10K-$224K one-time |

### Why This Matters

A FL condo owner in a 30+ year coastal building may face:

| Cost | Annual Impact |
|------|---------------|
| Base HOA dues | $12,000-36,000/year |
| Insurance premium increase | +$2,000-5,000/year |
| Citizens assessment surcharge | +$1,500-3,000/year |
| Special assessment (amortized 5 years) | +$2,000-45,000/year |
| **Total new burden** | **$5,500-53,000+/year** |

For a retiree on fixed income, this is **displacement-level stress**.

### Bank Transmission

Same borrower paying both insurance and structural assessments:
- DTI can blow out from multiple directions simultaneously
- Not sequential stress — hits all at once
- Even borrowers who survived insurance crisis may fail on structural assessment

## 7. Legislative Response

### Special Session (Jan 27, 2025)

Governor DeSantis called special session to address:
- Unintended consequences of post-Surfside reforms
- Affordability crisis for existing owners
- Market dysfunction (can't sell, can't finance)

**Potential relief measures:**
- Extended compliance timelines
- State-backed loan programs (Miami-Dade model)
- Inspector pool expansion
- Reserve funding flexibility

### Miami-Dade Assistance Program

| Program | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Eligibility | <140% Area Median Income |
| Loan amount | Up to $50,000 |
| Term | 40 years |
| Purpose | Cover special assessments |

**Limitation:** Covers only $50K — many assessments exceed this

### HB 913 (2025) — Some Relief

- Allows associations to fund SIRS via loans (not just assessments)
- Provides flexibility in reserve funding schedule
- Requires loan disclosure in resale documents

**Does NOT solve:** The fundamental deferred maintenance backlog
