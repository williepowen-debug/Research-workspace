## Translate the outcome into new earnings cost before valuing it

Use disclosed accounting, not a gross exposure times an assumed haircut masquerading as a result:

1. **Cash and remaining risk:** distinguish fresh third-party/sponsor principal cash, existing escrow/reserves, net exit cash, replacement loans, OREO/HFS assets, unfunded commitments and redraw rights. Principal paid at carrying changes asset composition; it is not profit. Cash interest is also distinct from principal recovery.
2. **Recognized loss:** separate prior cumulative charge-offs, Q3 charge-offs, loan-sale marks/gains, property write-downs and carry expense. A sale at post-mark carrying does not reverse the old loss. Loss of contractual interest through nonaccrual is another earnings channel, not principal impairment.
3. **Loan allowance bridge:** `ending ACL = beginning ACL + provision − gross charge-offs + recoveries + other changes`. Reconcile other changes and reporting perimeter explicitly. A charge-off funded by an existing allowance consumes an asset/allowance already recognized; adding the entire charge-off to provision double-counts current earnings cost. Replenishment required for the remaining book can still create new provision.
4. **OREO/HFS:** follow the issuer's accounting line. Subsequent OREO write-downs/carry costs affect noninterest expense; they are not loan charge-offs protected by the entire loan ACL. Credit marks or gains already included in reported income/expense are not added again.
5. **Capacity and book:** compare **new** provision and other recognition against profit before provisions on a consistent definition. A reported PPNR figure already includes OREO expense; use an explicitly adjusted counterfactual only if removing that expense. For the book bridge, reconcile beginning tangible common equity, common earnings, dividends, buybacks/issuance, AOCI and other changes, then divide by **ending** shares. EPS requires weighted diluted shares instead.

The aggregate allowance is a buffer, not a free deduction from every hypothetical loss. Credit only the allowance allocated to the modeled object, disclose assumptions, and retain enough for the rest of the book. Tax benefit depends on deductibility/realizability; no automatic benefit or CET1 inference is made.
