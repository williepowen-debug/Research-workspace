# Ueno Bank (Paraguay) — forensic failure, contagion and exposure report

**As of:** Thu 5 Nov 2026, ~12:55 ET (17:55 UTC). This is a fast-moving story; anything after that time is not reflected. The Paraguayan Congress sessions on Peña's resignation were scheduled for 17:00 local (15:00 ET), after this cutoff.
**Labels:** **[P]** primary document · **[S]** secondary (press/wire summary of a primary) · **[I]** my inference/arithmetic · **[U]** unknown / not found.
**Slant note on Paraguayan media:** ABC Color (Zuccolillo family) has run a two-year campaign against Ueno and Peña; the same family controls **Banco Atlas**, which was in litigation with Ueno's sister firm ITTI over core-banking software (that dispute killed the Atlas–Familiar merger in Dec 2025). Treat ABC as well-sourced but conflicted. Última Hora (Vierci group) is critical of the government. La Nación/HOY (Cartes-aligned group) have turned against Peña this week. Moody's and Fitch remarks below come through press summaries unless marked [P].

## 1. Answer first (five sentences)

1. **On the evidence available at ~12:55 ET on 5 Nov, this stays a single-bank failure. It is not a Paraguayan banking crisis and not a regional one. Confidence is moderate, about 65%.** The losses fall on Ueno's own capital structure and on a short, named list of local counterparties.
2. **Why it failed:** Ueno did not fail because of a run on a sound bank. Most of its reported capital was made up of assets the regulator allowed it to carry under its 2024 merger forbearance [P]: a G2.1 trillion "merger trust" of capitalised expenses, G761 billion of capitalised software, G877 billion of stakes in group companies, and deferred provisions. On top of that sat about $200M of alleged related-party lending [S]. A G1.62 trillion provision adjustment pushed solvency to 5.56% against a 12% minimum [S]. Then a ~$70M reserve-requirement shortfall on 29 Oct made the liquidity problem visible [S].
3. **The rest of the system looks stronger than Ueno did.** The system reports 16.95–17.1% capital and 32.4% liquid assets to deposits [S]. The only confirmed stress at other banks is app outages from mass account-opening (about 60,000 new accounts at Familiar in 48 hours) and branch queues driven by intervention rumours that BCP called "totally false" [S/P]. No bank has published deposit-outflow data either way [U].
4. **The losses go mainly to people and institutions in Paraguay.** Ueno shareholders and subordinated holders come first. Senior noteholders come next: they sit behind statutory depositor preference, and my estimate is 0–5 cents [I]. Then come non-IPS public-sector depositors, possibly Zeta Banco (it lent Ueno G160 billion, about 26% of its own equity, at 31 Mar 2026; current balance unknown) [P/I], and the deposit guarantee fund (FGD; $1.4–1.6bn against about $0.85bn of insured deposits).
5. **The routes to US and emerging-market credit are real but small.** US registered funds hold $17.9M face (5.1% of the issue, at most 1.22% of any one fund) [P]. OTP has $47M of equity, about 0.3% of its own capital [S]. Development-finance lenders hold about $54M [P]. The sovereign spread widened 19bp to 138bp, and the guaraní fell 6–9%, partly before the failure [S]. The call would flip if one of the other three merger-facility banks (Sudameris, GNB, Continental) turns out to carry similar forbearance assets, if BCP data shows outflows at Familiar, Continental or Zeta, or if the political transition derails the deposit payout.

## 3. How the bank really failed (forensic narrative)

**3.1 What the balance sheet showed (31 Dec 2025, audited by PwC Paraguay; report dated 19 Feb 2026) [P]**
- Total assets: G21,095.5bn (≈$3.21bn at 6,575.7 G/$). Equity: G2,117.7bn (≈$322M). Public-sector deposits: G4,492bn (≈$683M; G1,740bn a year earlier). Private deposits: G10,261bn.
- Assets that existed largely because of forbearance or related parties [P]:
  - **"Derechos fiduciarios" (merger trust): G2,105.7bn.** This is a trust run by **Zeta Banco** as fiduciary, holding post-merger "reorganisation" costs that are amortised straight-line (BCP facility "b").
  - **Capitalised software ("Bienes Intangibles – Sistemas"): G761.4bn,** including G654.8bn added in 2025 alone (≈$100M).
  - **Stakes in related group firms:** Red Digital de Procesamientos G640.5bn plus Red Digital S.A. G236.2bn, total G876.7bn. These were zero at YE24.
  - **"Valores al cobro"** (cash-equivalent cheques in clearing): G802.8bn, of which **G796.2bn was with related parties** at year-end.
  - Prepayments to related software suppliers and others: G385.3bn.
  - Deferred provisions on the absorbed Visión book (G118.1bn, linear over up to 20 years), deferred Visión losses (G83.8bn), and deferred provisioning on foreclosed assets until year 5.
  - **Sum of the merger trust, software, related stakes and related prepayments ≈ G4.13 trillion, about twice reported equity** [I].
- **Earnings depended on the group:** G303.9bn of "other income" came from related parties (including G200.4bn of "Proyectos" and G180bn of rents), plus G582.2bn of related "recupero de reintegros" (recovered rebates), against net income of G313.9bn [P].
- **BCP merger facilities, listed in note c.18.4 [P]:** (a) deferred provisions; (b) the trust; (c) deferred Visión losses; (d) deferred foreclosed-asset provisioning; (e) a 12/18-month grace period on **completing client credit files ("legajos")**. Carvallo's "debilidades en las carpetas" ("weaknesses in the credit files") on 3 Nov is the same item [S].

**3.2 The related-party lending (all allegations; nothing has been adjudicated) [S]**
- Mowi S.A. $41M, Grupo Hendy $41M, Grupo M $39M (≈G743.8bn): unsecured loans with tenors of 7–10 years. Ueno asked BCP in **Oct 2025** to reclassify these borrowers as non-related, got no approval, and kept lending from Sep 2025 to May 2026. Funds allegedly moved within minutes to ITTI, Redisa, Redipro, Vinanzas and Grupo Vázquez, and repayments came back from the same firms.
- To get under the related-party limit, Ueno sold **$74.4M of loans to Vinanzas S.A.** Prosecutors allege Vinanzas is itself related and was financed indirectly by Ueno. The BCP opened an administrative case (sumario) and issued a mandatory order (requerimiento de obligada observancia) dated **6 Aug 2026**.
- Prosecutors put the amount still outstanding at US$166,984,879 plus G343.2bn (≈$56M), together ≈ $222M. They also cite more than $33M lent to Nuevo Mundo Entertainment ("Puerto Madero de Asunción"), with the project budget rising from $5.16M to $33M.
- The banks' association (Asoban) told BCP the bank's group exposure was **G2.94 trillion = 133% of patrimonio efectivo at YE25**. That broader figure probably includes the stakes and prepayments [S/I].

**3.4 Rule change versus asset change [I]**
Most of the visible capital "collapse" is a change in what counted as capital. The underlying assets did not suddenly deteriorate.
- Fitch moved YE25 FCC from 15.2% to 10.3% purely by deducting software intangibles [P].
- Asoban's three demands map exactly onto Ueno's three soft spots: deferred losses, the Art. 56 calculation of patrimonio efectivo, and stakes in group firms [S].
- BCP's G1.62 trillion of extra provisions came from **reclassifying** related debtors into higher-risk categories [S].
- New credit losses inside the June–October window cannot be separated out from public data [U].

## 4. Loss waterfall by creditor class (arithmetic and ranges) [I unless marked]

**Inputs** (USD millions, FX ~6,150 G/$; base balance sheet = Q2-26 deck [P] plus the Q1-26 FS lender list [P]; the 2 Nov balance sheet has not been published [U]):
- Deposits $2,830 [P, Jun-26]. Insured ≈ 30% by value = **$849** (Moody's via Perspectivas [S]). Public ≈ **$869** (G5tn, 29% of deposits, Bloomberg [S]); of that, IPS deposits are assumed ≈ $300 (IPS total exposure $345–380M includes Ueno bonds [S]). Private uninsured ≈ **$1,112**.
- AFD $129 (secured by pledged sub-loans [S]); BNF $38; Zeta $26; Basa $24; Continental $4; foreign DFIs $54 [P, 31 Mar]; global notes $350; local senior bonds ~$49; subordinated ~$85; other ~$150.
- **Legal ranking** (Law 2334 Art. 20 and Art. 26 [P]):
  - First order, sub-rank 1: guaranteed private deposits **and social-security (IPS) deposits**, with amounts above the guarantee transferred in "linear increments".
  - First order, sub-rank 2: cash mandates and tax retentions.
  - First order, sub-rank 3: other public-entity deposits.
  - Second order: credit obligations to BCP. Third order: non-credit BCP obligations, FGD rights, taxes.
  - **Everything else goes to the residual balance and judicial liquidation.** That includes the global notes, local bonds, subordinated debt, and unsecured interbank and DFI loans. The notes' contractual pari passu clause does not override statutory depositor preference.

Caveats:
1. If Law 7745's final text (or a later law) puts IPS ahead of large private savers or covers all public funds, losses shift between local classes. **The notes' outcome does not change.**
2. If BCP extended liquidity to Ueno before 2 Nov (no evidence found), that claim ranks second order, ahead of the residual.
3. **"Valued at zero" under SV Resolution 0016 is a prescribed fund-valuation rule, not a recovery estimate.** My estimate of ~0–5 cents for senior paper comes from the ranking, not from that rule.
4. Fitch's RR6 (0–10% expected recovery) is consistent with this [S].

**Can the FGD pay in cash?** Most likely yes. The FGD had $1.404bn at YE25 (≈5.6% of private-system deposits; 59% in USD, mostly at the New York Fed) and $1.6bn of liquidity at Sep-26 per Moody's [S]. Insured ≈ $0.85bn, so a full cash payout uses about 53–60% of the fund before recoveries [I]. The Treasury backstop is bond issuance against future bank contributions (Law 2334) [S/P]. **Has anyone been paid?** At cutoff, BCP had opened a lookup platform on 5 Nov, and advances were due to start "within 24 hours / within 5 business days" (Law 7745). I found **no confirmation that money had actually been credited** [U].

## 5. Strongest case that it stays local — and what would break it

**The case for "local":**
- The sovereign moved 19bp (to 138bp), not hundreds. The guaraní move started on 9 Oct, before Ueno [S].
- The regulator acted under a hard-coded legal trigger (solvency below 50% of the minimum, with a 24-hour start rule) [P].
- 97% of depositors are fully insured, and the FGD holds roughly twice the insured amount [S].
- Law 7745 speeds up payment [S].
- System capital is ~17%; the four largest banks (Itaú, Continental, Sudameris, GNB) hold ~60% of deposits and are gaining flows [S].
- Asoban says it sees no outflows [S].
- The failure mechanism (group fintech ecosystem, merger trust, government-directed public deposits) is specific to Ueno.
- US holdings are $17.9M across 17 funds, at most 1.22% of any one fund [P]. OTP's write-down is about 1.5% of a year's profit [S].

**What would have to be true for that to fail:**
1. **Another merger-facility bank is similarly propped up.** Asoban's request names the mechanism (deferred losses, group stakes), not the bank. Sudameris is the obvious test: deferrals of up to 5–10 years, top-20 agro borrowers = 18% of its book with only G3.8bn of provisions [S], and Frigorífico Concepción in restructuring (≈$836–842M of debt, about half bank debt) [S]. A Sudameris problem would be a systemic event, because it is a top-4 bank.
2. **Interbank or fiduciary contagion through Zeta Banco.** Zeta lent Ueno G160bn (~26% of its equity) at 31 Mar, administered the merger trust, and processed the alleged "72-hour cheques" [P/S]. If the loan is still outstanding and unsecured, Zeta faces a material hit.
3. **The run broadens.** Queues at Familiar and police at Continental on 4 Nov [S] turn into measurable outflows in BCP weekly data. A "deposit-freeze extension" or capital controls would be the tell (none so far).
4. **The political transition stalls resolution.** That could mean BCP left without a board quorum (it was down to 2 of 5 on 4 Nov [S]), a messy Senate fight, or a populist decision to cover all $850M of public funds, which would cost the FGD or the Treasury roughly $0.5–0.6bn [I].
5. **The sovereign is downgraded or put on a negative outlook** because of governance, which would hit the guaraní global bonds held by foreign investors.

## 8. What would make this tradable (no recommendation)

- **For the notes:** the exclusion balance and the chosen mechanism (P&A vs titularisation) determine whether anything is left over for the residual estate, plus TRACE prints that show where the market is clearing.
- **For the sovereign and guaraní:** the BCP board quorum restored and Vera Bogado confirmed; FGD payouts completed by ~17 Nov; an explicit decision on covering public funds (cost $0.5–0.6bn [I]); agency commentary (especially Fitch, whose BB+ outlook is positive).
- **For Paraguayan bank credit:** BCP weekly deposits by bank; Zeta's and Basa's disclosure of their Ueno exposure; a BCP statement on the other merger-facility banks.

## 9. Corrections to the brief

- **Fitch core capital:** in Fitch's **2 Mar 2026** report, YE25 FCC is 15.2%. In the **16 Oct 2026** RAC, the same YE25 FCC is **10.3% after deducting intangibles**, and 2Q26 is also 10.3%. "15.2% → 10.3% at June 2026" mislabels the change. It was a restatement, not a decline.
- **NPL:** 0.4% at YE25 (Fitch, March), **0.6% / 0.59% at Jun-26** (Fitch Oct; Ueno deck).
- **"14-bank solvency request": kill it.** Asoban *has* 14 member banks. Its request was a solvency report on "all entities that received facilities in merger processes" (four mergers: GNB, Sudameris, Ueno, Continental).
- **6/5/26:** BCP's formal notice of related-limit observations is dated 5 June. The specific mandatory order on the portfolio sale is dated **6 Aug 2026** (dd/mm/yyyy in the BCP text).
- **Senate bill:** now law, **No. 7745** (promulgated 4 Nov): advance of up to 40 minimum wages (G121.7M), starting within 24h and finishing within 5 business days, net only of *overdue* debts.
- **Interim BCP head:** Liana Caballero was briefly interim; **Pedro de Jesús Acosta Bernal** was reported interim on 4–5 Nov; **Raúl Vera Bogado** has been nominated (Senate approval pending).
- **Solvency:** 5.56% vs 12% (secondary, consistent across several outlets).
- **Fitch D / C / RR6:** confirmed through multiple wire reports of the Fitch statement (secondary; Fitch page not retrieved).
- **US funds:** brief verified exactly: 17 series, $17.944M face, 5.13% of the issue, max 1.22% (Gramercy), largest Western Asset EMD $6.6M.
- **Bond prices:** N-PORT marks are 98.86 (31 Mar), 97.48 (30 Jun), 91.02–91.93 (31 Jul), 94.80–95.17 (31 Aug). Bloomberg reported 86 on the morning of 3 Nov.
