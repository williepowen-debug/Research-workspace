# Proposed research to explain KRE and regional bank price action

**Subsequent approval and delivery, October 12:** Will said **“Okay approved. Go ahead”**. Executed as one dated [whole-basket study](2026-10-12_KRE_whole_basket_study.md). Original proposal below preserves the scope at submission; the initial no-data-pass status is historical.

**October 12, 2026. Authority:** Will directly asked REGINALD to identify additional useful investigations. This is a research recommendation, separate from the approved L694 EGBN opportunity pass. No new data pass, bank commission, monitor or trade action has begun.

The first question should be: **is KRE's weakness widespread across its portfolio, or concentrated in particular banks, and how much is ordinary market exposure?** Our ten-heaviest-holdings sample covers only 11.34% of the October 9 portfolio. Its price history alone cannot explain the fund's loss. The [existing pattern study](2026-10-12_KRE_price_patterns.md) records that limitation and the recent decline.

## First investigation and concrete output

Build one dated whole-basket comparison using KRE's published holdings and settled daily prices. Start with the existing July 10–October 9 and September 11–October 9 windows so the results connect to our current research. If a later settled endpoint is used, save it as a separate dated observation. Keep both price returns and dividend-adjusted returns explicit.

| Component | Measurements | What it would answer |
|---|---|---|
| Breadth | Percentage of covered banks declining and percentage of covered portfolio weight declining; median return; dispersion; proportion below 20-day and 50-day moving averages | Whether weakness reaches much of the basket or a small tail |
| Contribution | Largest positive and negative contributors using historical beginning weights, membership and rebalance dates where available | Which holdings actually explain the ETF move |
| Market comparison | KRE versus KBE, XLF, SPY and IWM over identical settled windows; bank-relative returns and calendar-aligned rate changes | Whether bank weakness exceeds broad-market and smaller-company weakness |
| Existing evidence overlay | Match identified price outliers to already-owned earnings, funding and credit records, preserving their observation dates | Whether the price leaders have an independently supported bank mechanism |

Deliver a coverage table, a ticker-level CSV, a contribution chart where historical weights permit it, and a short synthesis with counterevidence. Record missing series, corporate actions, dividends, ticker changes and unobserved historical weights. A current-basket comparison selected at October 9 must be labelled as such; it is not the July 10 portfolio. Current weights can describe exposure or a fixed-basket approximation, but cannot authenticate historical fund contributions. Do not silently substitute missing prices with zeros.

The benchmark comparison is a diagnostic, not a causal estimate. If a statistical factor model is later justified, estimate exposures before the decline and test them out of sample rather than fitting an explanation to the same selloff. Treasury market holidays require the appropriate settled rates; an equity trading timestamp does not establish cash-bond trading.

## Follow on only where the first investigation points

**Check the strength of selling and rebounds.** Use complete daily sessions for KRE and the identified contributors: volume relative to a declared 20-session baseline, down-day versus up-day volume, and persistence of recovery above the chosen moving averages. Treat partial-session volume separately. RSI can supply context, but an oversold reading does not establish a reversal. Volume alone does not prove institutional selling or short covering; those explanations need fresh, dated positioning evidence. The old frozen positioning ledgers remain historical.

**Test price weakness against the existing bank evidence.** For names already covered by a desk, compare the price pattern with deposit changes, borrowing, provision charges, nonaccrual flows and the dated earnings evidence already in its grading frame. Preserve the difference between troubled assets arriving and reserves consumed by recognized losses or disposals. Price-only outliers can be flagged for a separately scoped question; this proposal does not commission new single-bank deep dives.

**Keep improving banks as controls.** EGBN's verified cleanup and relative strength, plus other resilient names in our saved cohort, help test whether the market distinguishes bank outcomes. If weak-price banks have stable or improving credit and funding evidence, a credit-driven explanation loses support. If rebounds broaden and persist, the technical bearish case weakens even while some historical loan problems remain.

## Scope and interpretation

Begin with whole-basket breadth and contribution. Let that result determine whether volume work or a particular existing bank-evidence comparison can answer one remaining question. This is a single dated research package, not a recurring monitoring commitment. It proposes no new registry threshold, capital allocation, option screen, trade construction or owner launch. TERRY's existing EGBN feasibility response remains a separate **PASS now — no trade-ready expression**, with the loan-outcome dependency at the existing issuer events.
