For the second quarter of 2026, net income from continuing operations attributable to Key common shareholders was $472 million, or $0.44 per diluted common share. For the third quarter of 2025, KeyCorp reported net income from continuing operations attributable to Key common shareholders of $454 million, or $0.41 per diluted common share, or adjusted net income of $450 million, or $0.41 per diluted common share (b) . The third quarter of 2025 included a $4 million after-tax benefit related to the updated FDIC special assessment (c) . Comments from Chairman and CEO, Chris Gorman "Our third quarter results reflect the strength of Key's relationship-driven franchise, the disciplined execution of our strategy, and the momentum we are building across the business. We delivered record revenue and record pre-provision net revenue (b) , while generating a return on tangible common equity exceeding 15% (b) . These results reinforce our confidence in achieving and sustaining a return on tangible common equity of 15%+ by the end of 2027. Our performance this quarter demonstrates the power of our balanced business model. Average deposit balances increased $3.6 billion sequentially, supporting continued net interest margin expansion. Our capital markets team delivered strong results with investment banking and debt placement fees increasing 27% sequentially. Concurrently, we maintained our disciplined approach to risk management, evidenced by broad-based improvement in credit quality metrics, including lower net charge-offs and nonperforming assets. Client engagement remains strong, supported by a constructive economic environment and continued confidence among the businesses and communities we serve. We continue to see opportunities to deepen client relationships, capture additional share of wallet, and drive sustainable growth across the franchise. Key is well positioned to perform across a broad range of economic scenarios, supported by our solid capital levels, strong liquidity profile, and well-diversified business mix. The ongoing investments we have made in teammates and technology are translating into stronger performance today while concurrently positioning us for greater value in the future. Looking ahead, we remain focused on executing with discipline, capturing the significant organic growth opportunities across our franchise, and delivering significant value for all our stakeholders." TE = Taxable-equivalent Taxable-equivalent net interest income was $1.31 billion for the third quarter of 2026 and the net interest margin was 2.95%. Compared to the third quarter of 2025, net interest income increased by $115 million, and the net interest margin increased by 20 basis points. These increases reflect a reduction in deposit costs as a result of declining interest rates and proactive deposit beta management, the reinvestment of proceeds from maturing low-yielding investment securities and fixed-rate swaps into higher yielding investments, an expanding balance sheet, and a continued strategic shift towards higher-yielding commercial and industrial loans. These benefits were partially offset by the impact of lower interest rates on repricing earning assets. Compared to the second quarter of 2026, taxable-equivalent net interest income increased by $50 million, and the net interest margin increased by 6 basis points. These increases reflect growth in commercial and industrial loans and higher deposit balances, the reinvestment of proceeds from maturing low-yielding investment securities and fixed-rate swaps into higher-yielding investments, and the impact of higher interest rates on repricing earning assets. Additionally, net interest income benefited from one additional day in the third quarter of 2026 compared to the second quarter of 2026. Taxable-Equivalent The interest income earned on certain earning assets is completely or partially exempt from federal income tax. As such, these tax-exempt instruments typically yield lower returns than taxable investments . Income from tax-exempt earning assets is increased by an amount equivalent to the taxes that would have been paid if this income had been taxable at the federal statutory rate. This adjustment puts all earning assets, most notably tax-exempt loans, and certain lease assets, on a common basis that facilitates comparison of results to peers. Earnings Per Share Equivalent Certain income or expense items may be expressed on a per common share basis. This is done for analytical and decision-making purposes to better discern underlying trends in total consolidated earnings per share performance excluding the impact of such items. When the impact of certain income or expense items is disclosed separately, the after-tax amount is computed using the marginal tax rate, unless otherwise specified, with this then being the amount used to calculate the earnings per share equivalent. The cash efficiency ratio is a ratio of two non-GAAP performance measures. As such, there is no directly comparable GAAP performance measure. The cash efficiency ratio performance measure removes the impact of Key’s intangible asset amortization from the calculation. Management believes this ratio provides greater consistency and comparability between Key’s results and those of its peer banks. Additionally, this ratio is used by analysts and investors as they develop earnings forecasts and peer bank analysis. The adjusted cash efficiency ratio excludes significant or unusual items that management does not consider indicative of ongoing financial performance Adjusted taxable-equivalent revenue or adjusted revenue is a non-GAAP measure in that it adjusts revenue for certain tax-exempt instruments and selected items. The interest income earned on certain earning assets is completely or partially exempt from federal income tax. As such, these tax-exempt instruments typically yield lower returns than taxable investments. To provide more meaningful comparisons of net interest income, we use interest income on a taxable-equivalent basis by increasing the interest income earned on tax-exempt assets to make it fully equivalent to interest income earned on taxable instruments. Additionally, management believes adjusting for the selected items provide investors with useful information to gain a better understanding of ongoing operations and enhance comparability of results with prior periods, as well as demonstrate the effects of the financial impacts related to those selected items. Adjusted noninterest income and adjusted noninterest expense are non-GAAP measures in that they exclude significant or unusual items that management does not consider indicative of ongoing financial performance. Management believes these measures provide a greater understanding of ongoing operations and enhances comparability of results with prior periods. Adjusted income (loss) available from continuing operations attributable to Key common shareholders (or “adjusted net income”) and diluted earnings per share - adjusted (or "adjusted earnings per share") are non-GAAP in that these measures exclude significant or unusual items, net of tax, that management does not consider indicative of ongoing financial performance . Management believes these measures provide investors with useful information to gain a better understanding of ongoing operations and enhance comparability of results with prior periods. Marked CET1 ratio is a non-GAAP measure and is calculated based on Common Equity Tier 1 capital, inclusive of the AOCI impact from securities and pension. The marked CET1 ratio differs from the defined CET1 regulatory capital ratio by including the impact of AFS and pension accumulated other comprehensive income (loss) (AOCI) amounts in the calculation of the capital ratio. These ratios are not defined in GAAP or federal banking regulations. As a result, these non-regulatory capital ratios disclosed may be considered non-GAAP financial measures. Management believes this measure provides useful information in light of the potential for change in the regulatory capital framework. Non-GAAP financial measures have inherent limitations, are not required to be uniformly applied, and are not audited. Although these non-GAAP financial measures are frequently used by investors to evaluate a company, they have limitations as analytical tools, and should not be considered in isolation, or as a substitute for analyses of results as reported under GAAP.
