# RQ-CREED-009: Institutional Strategic Defaults

**Date:** 2026-01-27 | **Status:** COMPLETE

---

## HEADLINE

Major institutional owners have collectively defaulted on **~$15-20B+** in CMBS loans (2023-2025). Brookfield alone: **~$4.0-4.5B**. These are overwhelmingly **rational non-recourse strategic defaults** — not distress signals. RXR CEO explicitly called it "Project Kodak" (keep digital, walk away from film). Institutional defaults represent ~18-20% of total special servicing by dollar volume. The price discovery effect is creating a negative feedback loop for smaller borrowers.

## 4. PATTERN ANALYSIS

### Property Type: **Overwhelmingly office**
- CMBS office SS: 17.3% (all-time record)
- Single-borrower office default rate: 19.7% (up from 1.9%)
- JPM estimate: ~$39B of CMBS office (21%) would default
- Brookfield retail defaults are notable exceptions

### Geographic Concentration
1. **Downtown LA** — Brookfield epicenter (~$1.2B+); vacancy 30%+
2. **Manhattan** — Massive $ but more mixed outcomes; some workouts
3. **DC metro** — Brookfield 12-building portfolio; suburban office hit hard
4. **San Francisco** — PIMCO/Columbia; ~35% vacancy
5. **Atlanta** — Starwood Tower Place 100

### Strategic vs Distressed: **Strategic**
Evidence:
- All loans are non-recourse CMBS — zero corporate liability
- All defaulters remain solvent, continue operating large platforms
- Properties defaulted share traits: negative leverage, declining occupancy, floating-rate, values below loan balance
- Properties retained: trophy/Class A+, prime locations, high occupancy, fixed-rate
- CEOs explicit: Flatt ("not relevant"), Rechler ("Project Kodak")

## 5. PRICE DISCOVERY / NEGATIVE FEEDBACK LOOP

### Comps Being Set
- Bank of America Plaza (LA): appraised $212.5M vs $605M prior = **-65%**
- 1740 Broadway (Blackstone): marketed ~$150M vs $308M loan = **-51%**
- Columbia Property Trust (PIMCO): $2.3B → $1.3B = **-44%**
- Pembroke Lakes Mall (Brookfield): $159M vs $260M loan = **-39%**

### The Loop
1. Institutional owner defaults on non-recourse loan
2. Special servicer markets note/property at distressed price
3. Sale/reappraisal establishes new lower comparable value
4. Comp forces write-downs on neighboring properties
5. Write-downs push more loans underwater → covenant violations → refi impossible
6. More defaults follow

### Asymmetry for Smaller Borrowers
- Small borrowers often have **recourse loans** — cannot strategically default without personal liability
- Institutional defaults set comps that depress their appraised values
- Lack negotiating leverage vs. special servicers
- NBER: CRE deterioration could cause **$160B in bank losses**, disproportionately from smaller borrowers

---

## 6. MARKET SIGNAL INTERPRETATION

**When Brookfield walks away from $4B+, it signals:**
1. Internal valuations well below loan balances
2. Certain office submarkets (Downtown LA, suburban DC) may not recover within any reasonable horizon
3. Non-recourse structures working as designed — losses flow to CMBS bondholders

**Two-tier market crystallizing:**
- Trophy assets holding/recovering value
- Everything else in structural decline
- Institutional defaults accelerating this bifurcation

**Moral hazard:** Normalizing default as routine portfolio management may increase mid-tier borrower defaults

---

## CREED IMPLICATIONS

1. **$15-20B+ in institutional defaults is material** but contained within CMBS (no corporate contagion)
2. **Price discovery loop is actively operating** — distressed sales setting devastating comps
3. **Asymmetry between institutional and small borrowers** = regional bank losses will come from borrowers who CAN'T strategically default
4. **Office bifurcation confirmed from the demand side** — even the largest owners are abandoning B/C assets
5. **Brookfield's $4B+ in defaults while maintaining $925B AUM** = strategic, not systemic distress signal
6. **RXR's "Project Kodak" framework** is the clearest articulation of what every institution is doing
7. **PIMCO/Columbia at $1.9B** is one of the largest CMBS defaults in history — peak-vintage risk confirmed
