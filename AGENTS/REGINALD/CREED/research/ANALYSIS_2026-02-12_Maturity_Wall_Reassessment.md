## QUESTION 1: Does Chicago -71% Change Maturity Wall Assumptions?

**YES — Widens the refinancing gap for secondary/tertiary markets.**

### Original Maturity Wall Model (Jan 2026)
- $936B 2026 maturities
- Realistic refi capacity: $500-700B
- **Base case refinancing gap: $336B (36%)**
- Forced recognition: $140-250B

**Assumption:** Office valuations down -30% to -40% (Green Street CPPI)

### Revised (Post-Chicago Data)
**Chicago -71% is WORSE than modeled:**
- Gateway cities (SF, NYC, Boston): -30% to -50% (Class A stabilizing)
- Secondary markets (Chicago, Houston, Denver): **-60% to -80%** (worse than GFC)
- Tertiary markets: Unknown but likely similar to Chicago

**Impact on Refinancing Capacity:**

**Scenario A: Chicago is Outlier (10% probability)**
- Chicago uniquely bad (high taxes + aggressive remote work)
- Most secondary markets -40% to -50%
- Refinancing gap remains ~$336B

**Scenario B: Chicago is Representative (60% probability)**
- Most secondary/tertiary office markets -60% to -75%
- Only trophy gateway cities -30% to -40%
- **Refinancing gap expands to $400-450B (43-48%)**
- Forced recognition: **$180-300B** (upper end more likely)

**Scenario C: Cascade Effect (30% probability)**
- Chicago transactions reset regional bank appraisals
- Forced reserve builds → credit tightening → wider spreads
- Self-reinforcing: lower refi capacity → more defaults → worse pricing
- Refinancing gap: **$450-550B (48-59%)**
- Forced recognition: **$250-400B** (systemic threshold)

**CREED Assessment:** **Scenario B most likely.** Chicago is NOT an outlier — it's a secondary market facing structural office obsolescence. Most Midwest/Southeast secondary markets will face similar repricing.

**Implication:** Original $140-250B forced recognition estimate was based on blended -30% to -40% valuations. With secondary markets at -60% to -80%, **upper bound ($250B) is now the base case.**

## QUESTION 3: "Extend to 2028" Concentration — What Does It Mean?

**"Extend to 2028" pattern is rational extend-and-pretend creating deferred crisis.**

### Known Extensions (Jan-Feb 2026)

**1. Willis Tower (Chicago) — $1.3B**
- Blackstone sponsor
- Extended to 2028 (likely matured 2025-2026)
- Underwater: $1.3B debt on $1.03B appraisal (-20% LTV)
- Operations fine: 89% occupied, $129.8M NOI
- **Can't refinance** (negative equity) but **can service debt**

**2. One New York Plaza (NYC) — $835M**
- Brookfield sponsor
- Matured (default), extended 24 months to Jan 2028
- Gateway city office (better than Chicago) but still can't exit
- **Classic kick-the-can**

**Total Known:** $2.135B

### Extend-and-Pretend Logic

**Why lenders extend (instead of foreclose):**
1. **Avoid crystallizing loss** — Extension = current loan; foreclosure = charge-off
2. **Time for market recovery** — Hope office values recover 2026-2028
3. **Servicer capacity** — Special servicers overwhelmed ($63.6B backlog)
4. **Price discovery risk** — Flood of office sales crashes comps further
5. **Regulatory arbitrage** — Extensions don't trigger NPL classification

**Why borrowers accept:**
- **No equity left** — Underwater, can't inject capital
- **Operations sustainable** — Can cover debt service from NOI
- **No recourse** — Non-recourse loans = can walk away later if needed
- **Free option** — 24 months to see if market recovers

**Why this is rational but creates bigger problem:**
- Solves 2026 liquidity problem (no forced sale)
- Doesn't solve 2028 solvency problem (still underwater)
- **Concentrates maturities in 2028 instead of spreading across 2026-2027**

### How Big Is This?

**Known:** $2.1B+  
**Estimated:** Unknown, but likely **$50-150B** (5-15% of $936B wall)

**Logic:**
- One New York Plaza + Willis Tower = two of the LARGEST deals
- Dozens/hundreds of smaller deals likely getting same treatment
- Office special servicing 17.11% = $63.6B in CMBS 2.0 alone
- If 50% of special servicing gets extended (vs foreclosed), that's ~$30B+ just in CMBS
- Bank portfolio (48% of wall = $450B): if 10-15% extended, that's $45-68B

**Base Case Estimate:** **$75-100B** of 2026 wall extended to 2028  
**Bull Case (extend-and-pretend succeeds):** $150B+  
**Bear Case (employment breaks, extensions fail):** <$50B

### Timeline Implications

**Original Thesis (Jan 2026):**
- H2 2026 = forcing window
- $336B refinancing gap
- $140-250B forced recognition **in 2026-2027**

**Revised Thesis (Feb 2026):**
- **H2 2026 = partial forcing window**
  - Some loans refinance (~$500-600B)
  - Some loans extend to 2028 (~$75-100B)
  - Some loans default/foreclose (~$200-250B)
  - **Net 2026 forced recognition: $150-200B** (lower than original)

- **2027 = breathing room**
  - $1.26T maturity wall but less concentrated
  - Extensions from 2026 haven't matured yet
  - Employment still key variable

- **2028 = NEW CRISIS CONCENTRATION**
  - Original 2028 maturities (~$800B estimated)
  - + Extended loans from 2026 (~$75-100B)
  - + Re-modified loans from 2025-2026 (~$50B)
  - **Total: ~$900B-$1T+ in 2028**
  - IF office values haven't recovered, forced recognition in 2028 could be **$200-350B** (larger than 2026)

**Key Insight:** Extend-and-pretend doesn't eliminate forced recognition — it **defers and concentrates** it. 2028 becomes the "can't kick it anymore" year.

### Risk Scenarios

**Scenario A: Soft Landing (30% probability)**
- Employment holds through 2028
- Office values stabilize at -50% (no further decline)
- 2028 extensions succeed via: (1) equity injection, (2) discounted payoff, (3) conversion
- Forced recognition spreads across 2026-2028: $50-75B annually = manageable

**Scenario B: Deferred Crisis (50% probability)**
- Employment holds through 2026-2027, breaks 2027-2028
- Office values don't recover (-60% to -70% persists)
- 2028 concentration creates LARGER crisis than 2026 would have been
- Forced recognition: $200-300B concentrated in 2028 = systemic risk

**Scenario C: Employment Breaks Early (20% probability)**
- Employment breaks H2 2026 or 2027
- Extensions FAIL (borrowers can't service debt)
- Maturity extensions become payment defaults
- 2026 + 2028 force simultaneously: $300-500B = systemic crisis

**CREED Assessment:** **Scenario B most likely.** Employment holding (per LABOR/CARL) through 2026 but vulnerabilities building. Extensions buy time but concentrate risk in 2028.

### What This Means for H2 2026-2028 Risk

**H2 2026 Risk:**
- **REDUCED but not eliminated**
- Original fear: $300-400B forced recognition all at once
- Reality: Extend-and-pretend smoothing the curve
- **Still expect $150-200B forced recognition in H2 2026** (mostly non-extendable loans: smaller borrowers, already-extended loans, retail/MF)

**2027 Risk:**
- **REDUCED**
- $1.26T wall but mostly refinanceable if employment holds
- Extensions from 2026 haven't matured yet
- Breathing room year

**2028 Risk:**
- **ELEVATED and CONCENTRATED**
- Original 2028 maturities + 2026 extensions + re-mods
- If office values haven't recovered, this is the "reckoning deferred"
- **Key risk:** 2028 becomes BIGGER crisis than 2026 would have been, because:
  1. More time for employment deterioration
  2. Lender/borrower fatigue (can't extend again)
  3. Regulatory pressure (extend-and-pretend visibility increases)
  4. Servicer capacity exhausted

**Employment Dependency:**
- **If employment holds through 2028:** Scenario A (soft landing, $50-75B annually)
- **If employment breaks 2027-2028:** Scenario B (deferred crisis, $200-300B in 2028)
- **If employment breaks 2026:** Scenario C (dual crisis, $300-500B across 2026+2028)

**Strategic Implication for PROME:**
- H2 2026 forcing window still real but **less severe** than modeled
- **2028 is the new critical monitoring window**
- Employment transmission from LABOR/CARL is STILL the key trigger
- Regional bank exposure to secondary markets (Chicago-like repricing) is the **hidden risk** not yet reflected in market pricing
