## WHY THIS MATTERS

### Gateway vs Secondary Market Bifurcation

**Gateway Cities (SF, NYC, Boston):**
- Class A office: -30% to -40% (trophy stabilizing)
- AI demand providing floor (SF: 35% of leasing)
- Dominated by large banks (JPM, BAC, WFC) and institutional lenders

**Secondary Markets (Chicago, Houston, Denver, Phoenix, Nashville):**
- **Office: -60% to -80% (Chicago proves this)**
- No AI demand offset
- Higher vacancy (20-25%+ vs 15-18% gateway)
- **Dominated by regional banks (<$100B assets)**

**Key Insight:** Original maturity wall models assumed blended -30% to -40% office repricing. If secondary markets are -60% to -80%, regional banks face:
1. **Reserve catch-up provisions** (loan loss reserves inadequate)
2. **Appraisal shock** (2024-2025 appraisals used pre-Chicago comps)
3. **Refinancing capacity collapse** (borrowers can't inject equity at -70% LTV)

## WHAT TO LOOK FOR IN 10-KS

### Critical Disclosures (MD&A and Footnotes)

**1. Geographic Breakdown of CRE Loans**
- Look for: Office CRE by metro area or state
- Red flag: High concentration in Chicago, Denver, Phoenix, Houston, Dallas
- Ideal: Footnote table showing "Commercial Real Estate by Geography"

**2. Property Type Breakdown**
- Look for: % Office vs Retail vs Multifamily vs Industrial
- Red flag: Office >15% of total loans

**3. Class A vs B/C (rare but sometimes disclosed)**
- Look for: Quality tier or vintage disclosures
- Red flag: "Class B/C" or "non-trophy" or "secondary market" language
- Proxy: Loan-to-value disclosures (high LTV = likely Class B/C)

**4. Appraisal Dates and Methodology**
- Look for: "Appraisals updated annually" or "rolling basis"
- Red flag: "Last appraised 2023" or "2022" = pre-Chicago comps
- Look for: "We use external appraisers" (better) vs "internal valuations" (worse)

**5. Reserve Adequacy and Loss Models**
- Look for: "Assumed loss severity" or "stress test assumptions"
- Red flag: Models assume -20% to -40% office losses (Chicago proves -70%+)
- Calculate: Allowance as % of office CRE (should be 3-5%+ if Chicago-exposed)

**6. ASU 2022-02 Modifications**
- Look for: Volume of modified CRE loans, terms, re-default rate
- Red flag: High mod volume (>5% of CRE book) + short extensions (12-24mo)

**7. Non-Performing and Criticized Assets**
- Look for: % office, % secondary markets
- Red flag: Low non-accruals (<1%) but high criticized (>5%) = lagging indicator
- Calculate: Office NPLs as % of total office CRE

### Red Flag Pattern

**High-Risk Bank Profile:**
- CRE concentration: >300% of equity or >30% of loans
- + Secondary market office exposure (Chicago, Phoenix, Denver, etc.)
- + Appraisals dated 2023 or earlier (pre-Chicago price discovery)
- + Low reserves: <2% of office CRE
- + Low but rising non-accruals: 0.5-1.5%

**If this pattern exists → Reserve catch-up provision likely Q1-Q2 2026.**

**Assumptions:**
- Bank has $10B CRE portfolio, 40% office ($4B office)
- 20% of office is secondary markets exposed to Chicago-like repricing ($800M)
