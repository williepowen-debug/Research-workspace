## Weaknesses in the Short Thesis

### 1. M&A Floor (CRITICAL)
CFG at $220B is too big for most acquirers but too clean to be left alone. The convergence score of 9 is driven by fund finance, not balance sheet distress. In a stress scenario, CFG could be acquired by a G-SIB (JPM, BAC) at a premium — creating a floor under the stock that doesn't exist for WAL/OZK/EGBN.

**Risk:** Fire-sale acquisition at 1.5x TBV = significant loss on puts.

### 2. Fund Finance Seniority
Subscription line credit facilities are typically secured by LP capital commitments — arguably the most senior collateral in private equity. Even if the underlying fund is distressed, LPs are legally obligated to fund commitments. CFG may be last-loss, not first-loss.

**Counter:** LP stress (Yale selling PE at discount, 81% DPI shortfall) undermines commitment reliability. But legal obligation =/= actual impairment unless LPs default on capital calls, which is extremely rare.

### 3. Transmission Path is Indirect
CRE → bank losses is direct (loan defaults → charge-offs). Fund finance → bank losses requires multiple intermediate steps (fund distress → NAV decline → collateral impairment → facility review → provision). Each step can be managed, delayed, or absorbed.

**Counter:** Speed is DAYS-WEEKS per FLOW-REG-21.01. But this is theoretical — no historical precedent for this channel at scale.

### 4. No Position Yet = No Urgency
We have existing positions in KRE, SSB, WAL, OZK, ZION, EGBN, FLG, HYG, IWM with clear catalyst timelines (Apr-Jun earnings). Adding CFG dilutes capital and attention without improving the thesis expression on existing names.

### 5. Analyst Coverage Density
11 Strong Buy ratings means any negative development gets rapidly re-priced by well-resourced sell-side analysts. Less likely to find persistent mispricing vs. OZK (heavily shorted, Temple8 thesis public) or EGBN (thinly covered).
