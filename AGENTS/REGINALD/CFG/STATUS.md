## C&I AS HIDING PLACE — Three-Layer Framework

Our Hidden CRE methodology (Memo Item 3) uncovered Layer 1. CFG research uncovered Layers 2 and 3. All three exploit the same structural feature: **C&I is the catch-all bucket where banks hide concentration risk.**

```
LAYER 1: CRE hidden in C&I (Memo Item 3 / RCON2746)
  Mechanism:  Loans financing real estate classified as C&I because
              lien structure doesn't list RE as primary collateral
  Effect:     CRE concentration ratios appear lower
  Regulatory: Call Report Schedule RC-C — changes what REGULATORS see
  Found at:   OZK 37.6%, WAL 24.2% (growing), EGBN 23.7%
  Discovery:  REGINALD, via Metropolitan Capital failure template

LAYER 2: NDFI/fund finance hidden in generic C&I
  Mechanism:  Lending to BDCs, PE funds, mortgage servicers classified as
              C&I. No mandatory sub-category disclosure.
  Effect:     Counterparty concentration invisible in aggregate C&I
  Regulatory: 10-K voluntary (Table 14). Call Report NDFI (RC-C Item 9)
              exists but is a single line — no counterparty detail.
  Found at:   CFG $12.5B (9% of loans), MTB $13.4B (10% of loans)
  Discovery:  REGINALD, via 10-K Table 14 deep-dive

LAYER 3: Reclassification within C&I sub-categories
  Mechanism:  Existing C&I loans relabeled between industry buckets in
              disclosure tables. Inflates/deflates specific categories
              without changing total C&I.
  Effect:     Growth rates distorted. CFG shows 40% YoY growth in fund
              finance (10-K), management claims 5% (verbal). MTB showed
              $1.3B C&I→NDFI recategorization in Q4 2025.
  Regulatory: Voluntary 10-K disclosure taxonomy — no audit of sub-category
              consistency across years
  Found at:   CFG (5% vs 40% discrepancy), MTB (NDFI recategorization)
  Discovery:  REGINALD, via transcript vs 10-K cross-reference
```

**The deeper connection:** Some of CFG's Layer 2 exposure may itself contain Layer 1 risk. CFG lends to a BDC → the BDC holds leveraged loans → the borrower owns commercial real estate. CFG classifies this as C&I (their counterparty is a fund, not a property). But the economic exposure is real estate, separated from CFG's balance sheet by one intermediary. This is not Memo Item 3 (the loan doesn't directly finance CRE), but it achieves the same opacity — **real estate risk wearing a C&I label, with an extra entity in between.**

**Implication for convergence thesis:** The eight channels don't just terminate at regional banks through separate doors. They share the same hiding place. CRE stress (Channel A) hides in C&I via Memo Item 3. Private credit stress (Channel B) hides in C&I via NDFI opacity. Both are invisible in the headline C&I number — and both are invisible to analysts who only look at the CRE concentration ratio. **The convergence isn't just that multiple risks hit the same bank. It's that they're hidden in the same bucket, making them invisible until they detonate together.**
