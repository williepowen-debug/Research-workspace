## Why We Track RF

Regions Financial ($158.8B assets, Birmingham AL) is a Southeast-heavy super-regional with three structural attributes that make it a useful pattern read for the convergence thesis:

1. **Consumer credit leading indicator (PRIMARY REASON).** RF's consumer book is 34% of total loans ($32.6B of $95.6B) — the largest consumer share of any Q1 2026 reporter. Historical pattern: RF's consumer NCO trends lead the broader regional bank sector by 1-2 quarters on turns. If RF Q1 shows consumer acceleration, the CARL consumer-credit transmission channel gets a first-cycle confirmation. If RF consumer stays flat/improving, CARL channel stays weak across peers.

2. **Southeast CRE / Florida condo overlap.** 52% of RF branches (701/1,247) are in FL/TN/AL — FL alone = 270 branches (21.6%). RF doesn't disclose loans by state, but geographic concentration is proxied by footprint. The FL condo/HOA/SIRS crisis (CORAL sub-agent focus) transmits to RF via IRE single-family/condo ($617M loans, 9.2% of IRE at YE2025) and small-business lending secured by FL commercial real estate. Any new Southeast language in RF's deck/call is a CORAL-grade signal.

3. **Overdraft / service charge fee dependency.** RF has historically been more overdraft-reliant than larger regionals. Service charges on deposit accounts ran $639M FY25 (+5.9% YoY), with FY25 10-K noting growth "driven primarily by treasury management services and overdraft fees." Any CFPB overdraft-fee restriction (e.g., revived $3-cap rule) tape-bombs RF non-interest income more than peers. Watch CFPB regulatory calendar against RF NII trajectory.

## What RF Is Not

- **Not a CRE-concentrated regional.** Total IRE = $9.1B (9.5% of loans), vs OZK 70%+, WAL 20%+. Office book $1.0B (1.1% of loans) with 95% secured, 64% South-region, 81% Class A, weighted-avg LTV 65%. Material but not structural.
- **Not an NDFI hub.** 10-K uses NO language about "NDFI," "Non-depository," "fund finance," "capital call," or "private credit." Table 11 shows $8.5B "Financial services" loans (17.3% of commercial) but undifferentiated. A PNC-style rollup would likely show $12-15B true NDFI exposure (inferred); still far below PNC's $73B / 20% of loans.
- **Not a convergence score target.** Does not appear on Tier 1/Tier 2 watchlist. Diversified Southeast deposit franchise, low loan-to-deposit ratio (73%), growing non-interest-bearing deposits.
- **Not an insider/governance story.**
- **Not a short candidate.** No identifiable structural break. If anything, a relative-safety name within the sector if convergence breaks.

## How RF Connects to Position Names

- **WAL/OZK (Apr 21):** Sector tape context — if RF beats-and-fades like MTB/CFG, the Q1 pattern is 4/4 and reinforces the read-through to position names.
- **CFG:** Consumer DQ cross-check. CFG retail NCO improved 70→38bps (auto runoff driven). RF retail NCO flat at 70bps. If CFG's improvement is structurally replicable (auto exits) and RF's isn't, CFG's "credit turning clean" narrative is overstated.
- **MTB:** CRE pattern. MTB $818M CRE modifications FY25 = extend-and-pretend. RF claims resolution via charge-offs, not mods. Different philosophy — watch which cohort's reserve-release ages better.
- **PNC:** NDFI disclosure benchmark. RF at the opposite pole (Table 11 only, no deck carve-out). The 4-bank spread PNC → CFG → MTB → RF = investor-facing granularity gradient.
- **CORAL (Florida sub-agent):** RF's FL branch concentration makes any FL qualitative comments high-signal for CORAL's condo/HOA/insurance thesis.
- **CARL (consumer):** RF consumer NCO is the cleanest Q1 leading-indicator read for CARL's thesis.

## Open Thesis Questions

1. Is RF's "Financial services" $8.5B Table 11 bucket growing (absent new NDFI disclosure) because of fund-finance origination or reclassification from other industries? No FY25 comparable disclosed (FY24 also $8.5B, flat) — but unfunded jumped from $9.3B → $9.8B (+5.4%) and that's where capital-call exposure lives.
2. Does RF's "Real estate (incl REITs)" $18.3B book mark-to-market differently from PNC's All-Other NDFI ($10B, incl REITs)? No public answer.
3. Is RF's Southeast branch density creating concentrated FL commercial lending that would show up if state-level disclosure was required?
4. How does the consumer credit card NCO of 4.08% compare to DFS, COF peer benchmarks? RF's book is small ($1.5B) but the rate is elevated for a prime regional.
5. Does RF have exposure to Home Improvement subprime lenders (GreenSky / EnerBank comps) that flow into "Other consumer"? The 2.97% NCO and 4.9% balance decline suggest managed runoff — track pace.
