# D6: Extend-and-Pretend — The Modification Machine

**Created:** 2026-03-24 | **Sources:** V1–V4 TDR synthesis
**KB Rows:** 096–097, 099–104 | **Group:** `EXTEND_PRETEND` | **Cross-ref:** 098 (→MGMT_CREDIBILITY), 105 (→ACL_THINNING)

## Bottom Line

Bank OZK has executed **590 loan modifications on ~300 RESG loans over 14 quarters** — the average construction loan has been modified twice. Only **10 loans ($96.4M)** were formally classified as "financial difficulty" modifications under ASC 326, and Call Reports show just **$1.5M** in restructured loans. This 98% classification gap shields investors from re-default rate disclosures that would quantify the true stress. When modifications DO get classified, **59% re-default** — and that's on the tiny slice that made it through the filter.

This is the *mechanism* by which OZK has deferred loss recognition. The ACL breach (KB-001–003) and the maturity wall (KB-074–079) are the *consequences* and *catalyst* respectively. The chain:

```
Extend-and-pretend (590 mods) → delays recognition →
ACL consumed by the losses that DO surface → reserve inversion (1.18% NCO > 1.16% ACL) →
2022 vintage maturity wall (H1-H2 2026) forces mass recognition →
Earnings miss / provision spike → re-rating
```

## The Evidence

### 1. Scale: 590 Modifications on ~300 Loans (KB-096, KB-101)

Over 14 quarters through Q4 2025, OZK processed 590 extensions or modifications on its RESG book of approximately 290–320 loans. The average loan has been modified roughly twice. The pace is accelerating — **49 extensions in Q4 2025 alone**, generating $56.7M in reserve deposits and $7.6M in fees.

Cumulative extractions from sponsors over the 14-quarter period:
- **$1.3B** in additional equity contributions
- **$866M** in reserve deposits
- **$429M** in unscheduled principal paydowns
- Floor rates enhanced on **200 loans**, spreads enhanced on 11
- Only **1 floor reduction** and **5 spread reductions** granted (per management)

Management frames this as "pay to stay, don't pay, don't stay" — arguing each modification *improves* the bank's position. The counter: if a project reaches its original maturity without stabilizing, requiring an extension is *by definition* a failure of the original business plan. The additional equity may signal sponsors throwing good money after bad, not financial health.

### 2. The 98% Classification Gap (KB-096)

| Metric | Amount | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Operational modifications (14 quarters) | **590** | Q4 2025 earnings call |
| ASC 326 "financial difficulty" mods (H1 2025) | **10 loans / $96.4M** | Q2 2025 10-Q |
| Call Report RC-C Memo restructured loans | **$1.525–1.764M** | FFIEC RSSD 107244 |

98% of modifications avoided the regulatory classification that triggers:
- Enhanced disclosure requirements
- Re-default rate reporting
- Investor visibility into actual stress levels

**How OZK avoids the classification (V3 analysis):**
1. Sponsors contribute MORE capital at each extension → argues financial capacity, not distress
2. Most mods involve enhanced terms (higher floors, fees) rather than concessions
3. Borrowers remain current on interest payments throughout modification process

This interpretation is aggressive but defensible. ASC 326 doesn't provide a bright-line test for "financial difficulty." But the Fed's own Staff Report #1130 (Oct 2024) — "Extend-and-Pretend in the U.S. CRE Market" — found that banks systematically extended impaired CRE mortgages to avoid capital write-downs.

### 3. The 59% Re-Default Rate (KB-097, KB-104)

Of the $78.3M in loans that WERE classified as financial difficulty modifications (YTD through Q3 2025):
- **$46.3M (59%)** subsequently re-defaulted
- $45.2M C&D loan → nonaccrual
- ~$1.1M residential → nonaccrual

In the residential segment specifically: $1.1M of $1.5M modified = **73% re-default rate** (KB-104). These mods used term extensions (+7-9 years) and 6-month payment deferrals. Even the simplest modifications are failing at extreme rates.

**Critical inference:** If 59% of the 2% that got classified are re-defaulting, what is the true re-default rate across the 98% that avoided classification? We don't know — and that's exactly the point of the classification strategy. No classification = no re-default disclosure requirement.

### 4. The Rate Concession Contradiction (KB-098, KB-102)

Management repeatedly claimed on earnings calls: "no concessions" — no rate reductions, no spread reductions, no floor reductions.

The Q2 2025 10-Q disclosed:
- A **$66.1M C&D loan** received a **261 basis point rate reduction** (to SOFR+300)
- This same loan ($45.2M after write-down) had ALREADY received a **$20.9M charge-off** BEFORE the rate cut
- **Double concession:** charge-off THEN rate reduction on the same credit

This directly contradicts the blanket "no concessions" claim. One confirmed lie in disclosure doesn't mean everything is false — but it means the "no concessions" framing cannot be taken at face value.

### 5. NPL Volatility as Classification Management (KB-100)

| Quarter | NPL Ratio | What Happened |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Q3 2024 | 0.60% | Sterling Bay write-down |
| Q2 2025 | 0.18% | $101M foreclosure transfers (vs $14M in H1 2024) |
| Q4 2025 | 1.06% | Boston $72.4M, losses forced by sponsor withdrawal |

The sharp drop to 0.18% wasn't recovery — it was NPLs being moved to OREO (foreclosed assets surged $69M → $160M in H1 2025). This is classification management through timing of loss recognition, not genuine credit improvement.

### 6. What Happens When Extensions Stop Working

Q4 2025 demonstrated the endgame:
- **$98.3M in charge-offs** (8x Q4 2024's $12.4M)
- Boston office: $72.4M write-down (422K SF, fully vacant, sponsors quit)
- Chicago Lincoln Yards: $9M (modified SIX times since 2019 before foreclosure)
- Santa Monica office: $5.7M (15% leased, sponsor stopped paying)
- Full-year NCOs: **$160M** (nearly 3x 2024's $57.4M)

Lincoln Yards is the textbook E&P lifecycle: modified six times over five years → each time collecting fees and "improving position" → sponsor finally quit → foreclosure → loss. This is the path many of the 590 modifications will eventually follow.

### 7. The Reserve Inversion (KB-001–003, cross-ref)

ACL peaked at ~$680M in Q3 2025. In Q4, it DECLINED to $632M — charge-offs consumed reserves faster than provisioning rebuilt them. The annualized Q4 NCO rate (1.18%) now exceeds the ACL ratio (1.16%).

Management on Q4 call: further ACL builds "probably not my base case." This means the bank is projecting credit losses will decelerate — but the 2022 vintage maturity wall says otherwise.

## The Bull Counter (KB-106)

The bull case argues the Q4 spike DISPROVES extend-and-pretend: the bank IS recognizing losses aggressively. RC-N nonaccrual restructured ($300M+) vastly exceeds accruing restructured ($55M) = bank moves assets to nonaccrual promptly.

**Rebuttal:** Recognition came AFTER years of 590 extensions, and only when sponsors refused to contribute more capital. This is forced recognition, not voluntary conservatism. The bank extended Lincoln Yards six times over five years before taking the loss. "Aggressive recognition" of losses that could no longer be deferred is not the same as proactive risk management.
