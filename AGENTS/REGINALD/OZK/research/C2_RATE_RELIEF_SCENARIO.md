# C2: Rate Relief Scenario — Does the Maturity Wall Soften?
**Created:** 2026-03-24

---

## The Bull Argument
If the Fed cuts rates materially, cap rates compress, property values recover, and CRE sponsors can refinance maturing construction loans. The 2022 vintage maturity wall ($13.8B originated) doesn't hit as hard because borrowers can refi into permanent debt. The "mechanical, not probabilistic" argument in SCENARIOS.md fails because the mechanism depends on a frozen refi market that rate cuts could thaw.

- Current: **4.25-4.50%** (held at Mar 18-19 FOMC)
- March 2026 dot plot: **one 25bps cut in 2026, one in 2027**
- Market pricing: ~50bps total by year-end 2026
- PCE at 3.1% — Fed constrained. No aggressive cutting cycle coming.

**Office cap rates are still RISING even as other sectors stabilize.** This is structural — not rate-driven.

## The Rate Relief Model

### What Rate Cuts Actually Do

Rate cuts affect CRE through two channels:
1. **Lower borrowing costs** → higher DSCR → more loans qualify for refi
2. **Cap rate compression** → higher property values → better LTV → more collateral adequacy

But these channels have **very different power by property type:**

| Property Type | Channel 1 (Borrowing Cost) | Channel 2 (Value Recovery) | Net Relief |
|--------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|-----------|
| **Multifamily** | ✅ Strong — demand exists, lower rates = more affordable | ✅ Strong — cap rates already stable, cuts compress further | 🟢 HIGH |
| **Industrial** | ✅ Strong — tenant demand solid | ✅ Moderate — caps already compressed pre-cut | 🟢 HIGH |
| **Office** | ⚠️ Helps math, but buildings need tenants | ❌ Weak — cap rates still RISING despite flat rates. Structural vacancy problem. | 🔴 LOW |
| **Life Sciences** | ⚠️ Same as office | ❌ Weak — 25-29% SD vacancy is not a rate problem | 🔴 LOW |

### OZK's Construction Book Breakdown

From the Call Report, OZK's $7.8B construction book:
- **$5.4B "Other construction/land"** — this is where the office, life science, and mixed-use CRE construction sits
- **$2.4B "1-4 family construction"** — residential, generally healthier

And from noncurrent data, 75% of stress is in **"other nonfarm nonresidential"** (office/life sci).

**The mismatch:** Rate cuts help the categories (multifamily, industrial) where OZK has LESS stress. They don't help the categories (office, life sci) where OZK has 75% of its noncurrent.

### Modeling Three Rate Scenarios

**Assumptions:**
- OZK's 2022 vintage construction book ($13.8B originated) reaches maturity Q1-Q3 2026
- Record RESG paydowns already happening = the healthy loans are already refinancing
- Remaining loans at maturity are the ones that COULDN'T refi yet — biased toward stressed properties
- $7.0B on interest reserves (89.7% of construction) = borrowers not paying from cash flow

**Scenario 1: No cuts (current path — 25bps max by Dec 2026)**

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| Fed funds at mid-2026 | 4.25-4.50% |
| Refi market for office | Frozen (cap rates still rising) |
| Refi market for MF/industrial | Partially open (RESG paydowns continuing) |
| Construction loans converting to nonaccrual | $400-600M additional by Q3 2026 |
| Impact on noncurrent | $341M → $700-900M |
| Impact on EPS | Provision surge → EPS $4.00-4.50 |

**Scenario 2: Moderate cuts (75bps by mid-2026 — market pricing)**

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| Fed funds at mid-2026 | 3.50-3.75% |
| Refi market for office | Still largely frozen. 75bps doesn't close the gap when cap rates are 9%+ and buildings are vacant. |
| Refi market for MF/industrial | Opens further. More RESG paydowns. |
| Benefit to OZK | 15-25% of at-risk construction loans successfully refi that wouldn't have otherwise |
| Construction loans converting to nonaccrual | $300-500M additional |
| Impact on noncurrent | $341M → $600-800M |
| Impact on EPS | EPS $4.50-5.00 |

**Why 75bps doesn't save office:**
- Current office cap rate: 9.10% national
- Typical construction loan rate: SOFR + 300-400bps = 7.50-8.50% currently
- With 75bps cut: 6.75-7.75%
- Permanent loan takeout requires DSCR >1.25x AND LTV <70%
- A vacant office building has $0 NOI → DSCR = 0. No rate makes this work.
- Even a 50% leased office: NOI supports maybe 50% LTV at current cap rates. Still can't refi.
- **The bottleneck is vacancy, not rates.**

**Scenario 3: Aggressive cuts (150bps by mid-2026 — recession scenario)**

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| Fed funds at mid-2026 | 2.75-3.00% |
| Why Fed is cutting 150bps | Recession. Labor market cracking. |
| Refi market for office | Slightly better on math, but recession means LESS tenant demand, not more |
| Paradox | Aggressive cuts = recession = worse fundamentals for CRE tenants |
| Construction loans converting to nonaccrual | $250-450M additional (rate math helps, but recession hurts tenants) |
| Impact on noncurrent | $341M → $550-750M |
| Impact on EPS | Revenue compression from lower rates partially offsets provision relief. EPS $4.00-5.00 |

**The recession paradox:** The scenario where the Fed cuts aggressively enough to meaningfully help CRE borrowing costs is the SAME scenario where tenant demand collapses. You can't have 150bps of emergency cuts without an economic deterioration that makes office/retail/life sci vacancy WORSE. Rate relief and demand destruction arrive together.

## The Decisive Data Point

**75% of OZK's noncurrent is in "other nonfarm nonresidential" — office and life sciences.**

Office cap rates: **9.10% and STILL RISING** (Q4 2025 vs Q4 2024: +20bps national, +50bps in SF and DC).

This means:
1. Even without rate hikes, office cap rates are expanding = values are falling
2. Rate cuts need to overwhelm this structural expansion to reverse it
3. A 25-75bps cut won't reverse structural office vacancy (national office vacancy ~20%+)
4. The properties going nonaccrual at OZK (IQHQ 97% vacant, Bioterra vacant, Boston charged off) have ZERO tenants — no rate makes $0 NOI refinanceable

**Rate relief helps the performing part of OZK's book (where paydowns are already happening). It does NOT help the stressed part (where the noncurrent is concentrated).**

## Rebuttal to the Bull — Publication-Ready Framing

"Rate cuts could soften the maturity wall" is the strongest bull argument. Here's the counter:

1. **The Fed is barely cutting.** March 2026 dot plot: one 25bps cut this year. PCE at 3.1% constrains the Fed. The market prices ~50bps by year-end — not enough to move the needle.

2. **Rate cuts help the wrong categories.** Multifamily and industrial (where demand exists) benefit from rate relief. Office and life sciences (where OZK has 75% of its noncurrent) don't — because the problem is vacancy, not cost of capital. You can't refinance a building with no tenants at any rate.

3. **The recession paradox.** Aggressive cuts (100-150bps) only happen in a recession. But recession means worse tenant demand, more layoffs, more vacancy. The rate relief and demand destruction arrive simultaneously — they don't cancel out, because structural vacancy (office, life sci) isn't cyclical.

4. **The healthy loans are already leaving.** Record RESG paydowns in Q3-Q4 2025 = the performing loans can already refinance. What's LEFT as the maturity wall hits are the loans that couldn't refi even in the current environment. These are the distressed properties — and rate cuts of 25-75bps don't transform distressed into performing.

5. **89.7% interest reserves.** The construction borrowers aren't paying from cash flow — OZK is paying itself. Rate cuts reduce the cost of the new permanent loan, but they don't create the cash flow needed to service it. The gap between "construction loan on interest reserves" and "performing permanent loan" is an occupancy gap, not a rate gap.
