## Summary

OZK is classified in the FDIC Dallas district (HQ in Little Rock, AR). Dallas shows a clean 0.89% nonfarm nonresidential noncurrent rate — 41bps below national. **This is a false signal.** OZK's actual loan book is concentrated in New York (1.57% noncurrent) and Atlanta/FL/GA (1.36%) — the most stressed CRE districts in the country. The Dallas classification masks OZK's true exposure geography.

The PDNA/NCO gap — the spread between total distressed assets and actual charge-offs — reveals which districts are recognizing losses vs deferring them. **Dallas has the largest gap in the country (1.69pts).** This is the geographic version of the ACL melting ice cube: stress is accumulating but losses aren't being taken.

## 2. The PDNA/NCO Gap — Deferred Loss Recognition Signal

The gap between Past Due & Nonaccrual (PDNA) rates and Net Charge-Off (NCO) rates reveals extend-and-pretend behavior. A wide gap means stress is building but losses aren't hitting the P&L. A narrow gap means losses are being recognized.

| District | PDNA | NCO | Gap (PDNA − NCO) | Signal |
|----------|------|-----|-------------------|--------|
| **Dallas (OZK HQ)** | **1.88%** | **0.19%** | **1.69pts** | 🔴 Largest gap in country — maximum deferred loss recognition |
| **New York (OZK loans)** | **1.56%** | **0.40%** | **1.16pts** | 🔴 Second worst |
| National | 1.56% | 0.62% | 0.94pts | Benchmark |
| **Atlanta (OZK loans)** | **1.67%** | **0.82%** | **0.85pts** | 🟡 High PDNA, moderate recognition |
| Kansas City | 1.42% | 0.64% | 0.78pts | — |
| San Francisco (WAL HQ) | 1.67% | 1.13% | 0.54pts | Tightest gap — losses being recognized, not deferred |

### What the Gap Means for OZK

**The PDNA gap is the geographic version of the ACL melting ice cube.**

1. **Dallas (1.69pt gap):** OZK's home district has the widest gap in America. Banks classified here — including OZK — are sitting on distressed assets (PDNA 1.88%) while barely charging off (NCO 0.19%). This is extend-and-pretend at maximum.

2. **New York (1.16pt gap):** OZK's primary origination market has the second-widest gap. Stress is accumulating (PDNA 1.56%) but charge-offs lag (0.40%). The 30-89 day pipeline at 0.41% (highest in country) means more stress is coming.

3. **Atlanta (0.85pt gap):** OZK's FL/GA book. Noncurrent already elevated at 1.36%, and PDNA at 1.67% suggests more in the pipe. NCOs at 0.82% show some recognition — but the gap still implies deferred losses.

4. **Methodological note:** Dallas district stats include OZK, but OZK's actual loans are in NY/Atlanta. The relevant comparison for OZK's loan performance is NY (1.16pt gap) and Atlanta (0.85pt gap), not Dallas. The Dallas gap is evidence of *system-wide* extend-and-pretend, not OZK-specific behavior — though OZK's own ACL/noncurrent collapse (8.86x → 1.39x in two quarters) suggests similar dynamics at the institution level. [KB-OZK-050 audit note]

## 3. Why Dallas Creates a False Clean Signal

OZK is headquartered in Little Rock, AR (FDIC Dallas district). Any screen of FDIC district data shows Dallas at 0.89% nonfarm noncurrent — well below the 1.30% national average. A surface read: OZK's home market is clean.

**This is wrong because:**

1. **OZK barely lends in Dallas.** The RESG book is concentrated in gateway cities: New York, South Florida, Atlanta/Georgia, San Diego, Chicago. These map to the NY, Atlanta, SF, and Chicago FDIC districts — all at or above national stress levels.

2. **Dallas's low noncurrent rate may itself be suppressed.** The 1.69pt PDNA/NCO gap (largest in country) suggests Dallas banks are deferring recognition, not experiencing less stress. The 0.89% may be artificially low.

3. **Community bank data confirms the mask is thin.** Dallas community bank nonfarm noncurrent: 0.86% — only 6bps above community bank national (0.80%). But Dallas community bank 30-89 day pipeline is 0.42% (tied highest with Chicago), suggesting the "clean" reading is about to deteriorate.

**Bottom line:** Using Dallas district data as a proxy for OZK's credit quality is methodologically invalid. The actual loan geography maps to stressed districts.

## 6. The Extend-and-Pretend Matrix

Districts sorted by loss recognition behavior:

| | Recognizing Losses | Deferring Losses |
|---|---|---|
| **High stress** (NC > national) | Kansas City (NC 2.01%, gap 0.78pts) | **New York** (NC 1.57%, gap 1.16pts) |
| | Atlanta (NC 1.36%, gap 0.85pts — partial) | |
| **Low stress** (NC < national) | San Francisco (NC 0.90%, gap 0.54pts) | **Dallas** (NC 0.89%, gap 1.69pts) |
| | | Chicago (NC 1.09% — gap not computed) |

**Reading the matrix:**
- **Top-left (high stress, recognizing):** Kansas City and partially Atlanta. These districts have high noncurrent AND are charging off. Stress is visible in reported metrics.
- **Top-right (high stress, deferring):** New York. High noncurrent but wide PDNA/NCO gap. Stress is partially hidden — more charge-offs coming.
- **Bottom-left (low stress, recognizing):** San Francisco (WAL's district). Low noncurrent, tightest gap. Losses hit the P&L fast. This is why WAL is a "fast transmission" thesis.
- **Bottom-right (low stress, deferring):** Dallas (OZK's district). Low noncurrent, widest gap in country. **Maximum deferred loss recognition.** The "clean" number is most likely to deteriorate.

**OZK sits in the worst quadrant (bottom-right) by classification, but its actual loans are in top-right and top-left.** The bank gets Dallas's clean optics while carrying NY/Atlanta stress.

## 8. WAL Comparison — Paired Trade Context

WAL (Western Alliance) is also in our short book. The FDIC district data reveals fundamentally different loss transmission mechanics:

| Metric | OZK (Dallas district) | WAL (SF district) |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Nonfarm NC rate | 0.89% | 0.90% |
| NCO rate | 0.19% | 1.13% |
| PDNA/NCO gap | 1.69pts (widest) | 0.54pts (tightest) |
| 30-89 pipeline | 0.33% | 0.26% (cleanest) |
| Loss recognition | Maximum deferral | Aggressive recognition |

### Key Differences

1. **OZK = reservoir thesis.** Stress accumulates, masked by extensions and reserve cuts. Maturity wall forces recognition Q1-Q3 2026. Losses appear gradually through the delinquency pipeline (30-89 → noncurrent → charge-off).

2. **WAL = fast transmission thesis.** Losses reach the P&L directly. SF has the highest NCO rate (1.13%) despite low noncurrent (0.90%) — losses appear episodically (Cantor fraud pattern, Oct 2025), not through the standard pipeline.

3. **Pipeline methodology works for OZK, breaks for WAL.** NY's 0.41% 30-89 rate predicts OZK's next wave. But SF's clean 0.26% pipeline doesn't mean WAL is safe — WAL losses bypass the pipeline entirely.

4. **Position timing:** OZK puts (May/Aug) align with maturity wall + IQHQ Aug maturity. WAL puts (Jun/Sep) catch Jefferies contagion + Q1 earnings (~Apr 21).

**These are complementary, not symmetric shorts.** Different mechanisms, different timing, same directional thesis on CRE stress.
