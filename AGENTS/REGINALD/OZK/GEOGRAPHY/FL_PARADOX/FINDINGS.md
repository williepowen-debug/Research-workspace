# FL PARADOX — Cross-Model Findings

**Completed:** 2026-03-24 | **Models:** Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity | **KB rows:** 121–132

---

## Bottom Line

**The FL condo book is genuinely well-structured and is NOT where the OZK thesis breaks.** All four models agree on the structural protections. The bear case rests on bank-level ACL exhaustion from life sciences and office losses, not FL stress. FL becomes relevant only as a potential amplifier if broader credit deterioration forces reserve replenishment from an already-depleted ACL.

One actionable exception: **Biscayne 21 ($105M)** — a named FL loan with impaired collateral.

---

## Finding 1: Deposit Wall Is Real
**Verdict: STRENGTHENS the paradox** | **Confidence: A2** | **KB-125, KB-131**

30–50% staged non-refundable deposits (vs 10–20% pre-2008). Walkaway requires >50% price decline to be economically rational. 2008 Miami was 38–42% peak-to-trough — insufficient to breach current structures.

All-cash buyer share: 78–83% above $2,000/SF (Claude), 43% broader market (Gemini), 59%+ in $10M+ tier at Four Seasons (Perplexity).

**Key nuance (Perplexity):** Forfeited deposits flow to the *developer* for contractor liens and operating costs, NOT directly to OZK as loan paydown. Not a clean 1:1 offset. [KB-131]

**Early stress signal (Perplexity):** Contract re-trades and assignments appearing — buyers trying to exit positions without outright defaulting. Not yet material but the earliest possible canary.

---

## Finding 2: SB 4-D Creates Relative Moat, Absolute Cost Headwind
**Verdict: NET POSITIVE but nuanced** | **Confidence: A2** | **KB-123**

Mandatory SIRS reserve funding (Jan 1, 2026) hammers older buildings with $50K–$200K/unit special assessments across ~900K units. New construction exempt 25–30 years. Market bifurcation confirmed: <25yr sales +2%, >25yr sales -6%.

**Bull:** Regulatory destruction of older stock funnels demand into exactly the projects OZK finances.

**Bear (Perplexity's nuance):** Even new towers face higher structural reserve costs than pre-Surfside — can't waive reserves anymore. Pushes TCO up for all. Also creates headline risk: any post-completion defect surfaces quickly via mandated SIRS. Moat is real but water level rises for everyone.

---

## Finding 3: Insurance Stabilizing But Cumulative Burden Persists
**Verdict: NEUTRAL — crisis abating, costs structural** | **Confidence: B1** | **KB-124**

2025: first stabilization year. Unit-owner premiums +1.3% (lowest tracked). Citizens depopulated 73%. 17 new insurers. Private market first underwriting profit since 2016 (combined ratio 93.1%). Reinsurance -10.7%.

But cumulative: Miami-Dade luxury total insurance $6,800–7,800/yr, HOA >$1,900/mo. New construction premiums 20–40% lower than older buildings (reinforces SB 4-D moat).

**Risk:** One bad hurricane season reverses everything instantly. Catastrophe models predict 10–40% higher losses than prior models.

---

## Finding 4: Biscayne 21 — Only Named FL Loan With Visible Stress
**Verdict: WEAKENS the paradox (single name)** | **Confidence: B1** | **KB-121**

$105M OZK senior mortgage + $45M mezz ($150M total). Two Roads Dev bulk-purchased 192-unit tower for Edition Residences conversion. FL Supreme Court declined review Oct 2025 — cannot retroactively lower termination thresholds. Jan 2026 court order: restore to habitable condition at significant cost. No clear path to luxury redevelopment.

**Only Claude caught this.** Gemini, ChatGPT, and Perplexity all missed it.

Watch for: Q1 non-accrual classification on Apr 16 earnings. If it surfaces, shatters the "FL book is pristine" narrative.

---

## Finding 5: FinCEN Rule Adds Foreign Buyer Friction
**Verdict: MODESTLY WEAKENS (long-term)** | **Confidence: A2** | **KB-122**

FinCEN Residential Real Estate Rule effective March 1, 2026: beneficial ownership reporting on non-financed transfers to legal entities. Foreign buyers = 49% of new construction sales ($4.4B, +42% YoY), 86% from Latin America.

Not a deal-killer but adds friction to the all-cash pipeline that OZK's FL book depends on. Monitor absorption rates post-March for evidence of impact.

---

## Finding 6: Concentration Duality
**Verdict: FRAMEWORK — neither bull nor bear** | **Confidence: B1** | **KB-132**

Perplexity's sharpest insight: the factors that support OZK's thesis (branded ultra-luxury, presale-heavy, wealthy all-cash buyers) also concentrate exposure to a small, sentiment-driven global buyer pool that can step back simultaneously.

This is not a current risk. It's the mechanism by which FL *could* correlate with broader stress if global risk appetite fades, LatAm instability worsens, or FL regulatory burden becomes too onerous.

---

## Finding 7: Project Progress Confirms Construction On Track
**Verdict: STRENGTHENS the paradox** | **Confidence: A2** | **KB-127**

- Waldorf Astoria: 60th floor (Feb '26), facade on 4th of 9 cubes, 90%+ presold
- Baccarat: 40th of 75 floors, curtain wall advancing, 95%+ presold
- South Flagler House: topped out Nov 2025, 28 stories, WPB luxury +30% YoY
- Ritz-Carlton Pompano: 90%+ sold
- PMG announced 90-story Delano tower adjacent to Waldorf (developer confidence signal)

No construction delays, cost overruns, or sponsor distress reported for any named project.

## Finding 8: Bank-Level Metrics — The Real Story
**Verdict: IRRELEVANT TO FL specifically, CRITICAL to thesis** | **KB-126, KB-128**

ACL built from $300M to $632M but still 1.16% ratio (below peer avg ~1.75%). CRE concentration 455% of tangible equity. 49 Q4 extensions. FY NCO rate 1.18% already exceeds ACL ratio.

This is why FL doesn't need to crack: the bank is already absorbing losses faster than reserves can handle, from NON-FL sources.
