```
You are a commercial real estate credit analyst specializing in Florida 
construction lending. I need you to stress-test the "Florida Paradox" — 
the argument that Bank OZK's $7.45 billion Florida RESG book is safe 
despite being its largest geographic concentration.

OZK's Florida book is primarily luxury condo construction loans in 
Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach ($3.96B), Tampa ($1.11B), Cape 
Coral ($499M), and Sarasota ($478M). Management disclosed 14 Miami condo 
loans totaling $2.47B with weighted average LTC 45.9%, LTV 39.1%, and 
11 of 14 have presales covering full loan repayment.

Named projects include: Waldorf Astoria Miami ($668M, PMG/Mohari, 90% 
pre-sold), Four Seasons Coconut Grove ($324M, CMC/Fort Partners, Jan 
2026 origination), Baccarat Residences ($328M, 95%+ pre-sold), South 
Flagler House (~$310M, Related Ross), Ritz-Carlton Pompano ($259M), Vita 
at Grove Isle ($239M), Wynwood Plaza ($215M), Waldorf Pompano ($160M), 
West Palm Beach condo ($475M, 2025 origination).

I need you to investigate:

1. PRE-SALE WALKAWAY RISK: In Florida luxury condo pre-sales, what is 
   the typical deposit structure? What are the legal and economic 
   conditions under which buyers walk away from pre-sale contracts? Has 
   there been any evidence of pre-sale cancellations or contract 
   failures in Miami luxury developments in 2025-2026? What happened to 
   pre-sales during the 2008 cycle — what was the walkaway rate? How 
   does a construction lender like OZK get hurt if pre-sales don't 
   convert to closings?

2. THE SB 4-D / SIRS REGULATORY IMPACT: Florida's post-Surfside 
   legislation (SB 4-D, HB 913) mandates structural integrity reserve 
   studies and milestone inspections. As of January 1, 2026, structural 
   reserve funding is mandatory and cannot be waived. How does this 
   affect the NEW construction luxury condo market specifically? Does it 
   help (making older condos less attractive) or hurt (raising total cost 
   of ownership even for new)? Are new construction projects exempt from 
   SIRS initially, and if so, for how long?

3. INSURANCE CRISIS: Florida property insurance premiums have surged. 
   What is the current insurance cost trajectory for luxury condos? How 
   do insurance costs affect condo closings and buyer willingness to 
   close on pre-sales? Are there cases where insurance costs have caused 
   deal fallthrough?

4. SUPPLY GLUT: How many luxury condo units are currently under 
   construction or in pre-sale in Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach 
   counties? What is the absorption rate? How many months of supply 
   exist at the luxury tier ($2M+)? Is there evidence of oversupply at 
   the ultra-luxury tier?

5. FOREIGN BUYER EXPOSURE: What percentage of Miami luxury pre-sales 
   are to international buyers (particularly LatAm)? Are there any 
   capital controls, sanctions, or currency issues affecting these 
   buyers' ability to close? How did foreign buyer pullback affect Miami 
   condos in 2015-2018 (the last LatAm currency crisis)?

6. SPECIFIC PROJECT RISK: For any of the named OZK projects above, are 
   there reports of construction delays, cost overruns, pre-sale 
   cancellations, or market concern? Has the Waldorf Astoria Miami faced 
   any headwinds? What is the status of Water Street Tampa ($664M)?

7. MACRO INDICATORS: UBS ranked Miami #1 globally for bubble risk in 
   2025 (score 1.73, exceeding 2006 peak). Net domestic migration to FL 
   collapsed 93%. Statewide condo supply is at 13.2 months. High-end 
   inventory is up 40% YoY. Sarasota vacancy hit a 25-year high. How do 
   these macro indicators reconcile with the "pre-sales make it safe" 
   argument?

Be specific. Use primary data where possible. Flag anything you can't 
verify. I'm testing whether a $7.45B construction lending concentration 
in a market ranked #1 for global bubble risk is actually as safe as the 
lender claims.
```

## Preliminary Findings (pre-LLM, from web search)

**UBS Global Real Estate Bubble Index:** Miami #1 globally, score 1.73, exceeds 2006 peak. "Cities with elevated or high bubble risk continued to decouple from fundamentals: over the last five years, inflation-adjusted home prices increased nearly 25% on average, while rents rose about 10% and incomes about 5%."

**SB 4-D / SIRS:** Effective Jan 1, 2026, structural reserve funding is mandatory and cannot be waived by owner vote. Special assessments of $50,000-$200,000 per unit common in older high-rises. This primarily hits EXISTING buildings — new construction exempt for 25-30 years. May actually benefit OZK's new builds by making older condos less competitive.

**Insurance:** Costs surging. Florida requires 25% down for condos that don't meet reserve requirements (vs 10% in every other state). This creates financing barriers for older condos but not new construction.

**Construction lawsuits:** Commercial Observer (Mar 2026) reports lawsuits "abound" over shoddy condo construction — "a combination of deeper-pocketed litigants, a luxury construction boom and fiercer government regulations."

**Demand:** NYT reports billionaires still buying (Zuckerberg, Brin). 65.3% cash transactions. But demand is thin at the top — concentrated in ultra-wealthy, not broad market.
