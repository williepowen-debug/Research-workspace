**Date:** 2026-04-22 (earnings day) | **Author:** REGINALD | **Source primaries:** 1Q26 Mgmt Comments (40pp), 1Q26 Financial Supplement, Q1 2026 earnings call transcript

**Verdict:** VERTICAL-SPECIFIC COMPRESSION, NET-NEUTRAL SHORT-TERM — 3 of 6 CIB verticals (ABLG, Fund Finance, LFG) are compressing on spread/structure, offset by mgmt pushing into CBSF/NRG/EFG; the "+12bp new-vs-legacy spread" is a mix-shift metric not a broad-based lift. ## Section 2 — Reconciling "+12bp new-vs-legacy" with 3/5 compression

Munn's quote (line 85): **"legacy book versus the new deals generated this last quarter, there was actually about, call it a 12 bip uptick on the average spread."**

**This is a mix metric, not an apples-to-apples spread metric.** Three ways it reconciles with 3/6 verticals compressing:

1. **New deals are weighted toward lean-in verticals.** Munn explicitly says they "pull on the levers and push into" CBSF/EFG/NRG. If new originations Q1 were ~70% CBSF+NRG+EFG+FCS and only ~30% ABLG/FF/LFG, the weighted-average new spread can be +12bp vs a legacy book dominated by older (lower-priced) ABLG paper.
2. **Legacy book includes seasoned, low-coupon deals.** OZK legacy ABL and FF loans priced at SOFR + X when SOFR was higher — variable but with floors. New deals at today's indexes + 12bp relative spread is comparing across rate environments.
3. **FCS has no legacy.** The new vertical ships exclusively in the "new deals" bucket at whatever market pricing — likely SOFR+325–400 on sponsor-backed franchisee QSR deals (crowded space, so probably not a big spread premium).

**Hypothesis stands:** +12bp uptick is concentrated in 2–3 lean-in verticals. The compression in ABLG/FF/LFG is real on a per-vertical basis, and OZK's defense is **rotation, not pricing power.**

**Falsification test:** If Q2 new-vs-legacy spread drops below +12bp while CIB keeps growing, it means rotation is running out of runway (lean-in verticals are saturating competitively too). Watch Q2 comment.

## Section 4 — Implication for 4.20% NIM through 2026

**Inputs (Financial Supplement p.7, YoY bridge):**

| Metric | 1Q25 | 1Q26 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loan yield | 7.79% | 7.24% | -55bp |
| Total earning asset yield | 7.28% | 6.82% | -46bp |
| Interest-bearing deposit cost | 3.78% | 3.29% | -49bp |
| Total IB-liability cost | 3.77% | 3.30% | -47bp |
| **NIM-FTE** | **4.31%** | **4.20%** | **-11bp** |

**Path arithmetic to hold 4.20% through 2026:**
- CIB ($6.2B, 18.8%) is predominantly variable-rate (Munn line 83). If CIB grows another $3–4B across 2026 (on pace from +$3.2B YoY) AND compression in ABLG/FF/LFG widens 15–25bp while new verticals keep +12bp delta, **blended CIB spread drifts ~5–10bp lower** over 2026.
- RESG runoff at 7.24%+ yield replaced with CIB at similar or lower yield is a mix-neutral-to-slightly-negative trade.
- Securities build ($2.61B → $3.84B, +$1.2B, p.2) at ~5% blended yield (6% muni tax-equiv + 4.60% MBS, p.7 & call line 31–35) is **dilutive to NIM** (securities yield 5% < loan yield 7.24%) — this is one reason NIM was flat QoQ despite Fed cuts.
- Deposit cost tailwind is largely exhausted: 3.29% down from 3.78% YoY but -18bp QoQ per Cindy Wolfe comment — room for 10–20bp more deposit relief in 2026 if rates flat.
- Variable rate floors: 34% at floor now, rising to 50%+ if rates drop another 50bp (Fig 20, p.18) — asymmetric downside if Fed cuts resume.

**Verdict on 4.20% hold:** Plausible for 2026 if Fed stays flat, but 1–2 quarters of NIM drift to 4.10–4.15% is the more likely path given (a) securities drag, (b) CIB compression in 3/6 verticals, (c) deposit cost compression is slowing. **Risk to downside if FCS or NRG hits competitive compression too** (compression would then be 5/7 verticals, rotation exhausted).
