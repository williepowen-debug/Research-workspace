Executive Summary
Bank OZK (Nasdaq: OZK) is the largest construction lender in the United States, with $40.8 billion in total assets and $32.3 billion in total loans as of December 31, 2025. Its Real Estate Specialties Group (RESG) accounted for 54.4% of total loans (~$17.6B) at year-end 2025, down from an all-time peak of 70% but still far above any peer. The bank's CRE loan-to-capital ratio of ~398% at mid-2024 was approximately double the regulatory peer average of ~202%.[1][2][3][4]
Geographic disclosure is a critical gap: 
OZK does NOT disclose specific dollar amounts per metro/state in its public SEC filings.
 Quarterly investor presentations show a bar chart ranking of RESG markets, but the Y-axis amounts are not labeled. Management's most granular disclosed statement on geography was in the Q4 2024 earnings call: 
"Our highest geographic concentration continues to be in Miami."
 This report synthesizes all available primary sources — earnings call transcripts, management commentary, individual loan origination disclosures, investor presentations, and transaction records — to construct the most complete metro-level map possible.[5]
Critical credit developments as of March 2026:
Non-performing loans rose from $131.5M (Q2 2025) to $341.2M (Q4 2025)[6]
Q4 2025 net charge-offs: $98.3M — highest since 2010[2]
ACL: >$600M at year-end 2025, built over 14 consecutive quarters[2]
Life science loan portfolio: ~$3.2B (~10% of RESG); majority facing leasing stress[7][8]
RESG target: Management guiding to reduce RESG below 50% of total loans by late 2026[9]
Section 1: FFIEC Call Report — Geographic Data Limitations
Finding: No state-level loan geographic distribution is publicly available from OZK's FFIEC filings.
Bank OZK files the FFIEC 031 form (large bank, domestic offices) as a state-chartered bank supervised by the Arkansas State Bank Department and the FDIC. The standard Schedule RC-C, Part I (Loans and Leases) requires reporting of loan categories by type (construction, multifamily, nonfarm nonresidential, etc.) but does 
not
 require banks to disclose geographic distribution of loans at the state or metro level. This is a fundamental limitation of the Call Report schema for construction/CRE lenders: the collateral location is not a mandated data field in public reports.[10][11][12]

Significance for the short thesis:
 FDIC Dallas examiners — who evaluate OZK's CRE underwriting — have primary expertise in Texas and Arkansas commercial real estate. They rely on field examination support from regional offices in gateway cities, but OZK's largest concentrations (South Florida, New York, San Diego, Boston, SF Bay Area) involve market-specific valuation complexities (life science lab buildouts, luxury condo absorption, office conversion risk) that are most familiar to FDIC New York, SF, and Chicago examiners. This creates potential for examination lag and may mean that collateral appraisal adequacy is reviewed less rigorously than at a bank chartered in those states.

CEO Gleason has stated: "The longer this challenging cycle drags on for our sponsors, the more likely it would be that individual sponsors on some individual projects would run into trouble."[2]
Section 8: Key Risks for the Short Thesis
IQHQ RaDD maturity:
 The $915M loan was extended to ~Q3 2028. At current leasing pace (one 50K SF tenant of 1.7M SF), the loan will still be substantially vacant at maturity. A refinancing at 2028 values would likely require significant paydown. If IQHQ's capital base (including $270M from cannabis REIT investor) runs out before leasing, OZK faces a loss on its largest individual credit.[52][25]
Bioterra San Diego ($202.6M):
 Co-originated with Square Mile Capital; 100% vacant as of Q3 2025 broker open house; in distress. OZK's pro-rata share of the $202.6M is not precisely disclosed (Square Mile originated the senior, OZK was co-lender; structure unclear from public sources).
Sullivan Courthouse Boston:
 $156.4M remaining balance; equity withdrawn; dual-tracking title acquisition. Likely to become REO in 2026.[2]
Seattle "The Jack":
 LTV 111% is a first for this cycle — means at current appraised value, OZK would take a loss of ~$6M+ if liquidated today, plus any further depreciation.[2]
$2B+ of leases-not-yet-signed pipeline:
 Management's own disclosure that >$2B of RESG loans are entering their final year substantially unleased creates a pipeline of future NPL migrations. These are likely concentrated in Boston (Boynton Yards), San Francisco (Southline), and residual San Diego.[50]
Supervisory jurisdiction mismatch:
 The FDIC Dallas Region's examination of a portfolio 80%+ concentrated in NY, CA, FL, IL, MA, and WA creates structural lag in collateral re-appraisal and classification. Dallas examiners are less likely to have market-specific expertise in gateway CRE valuations.[15][14]
Regulatory capital threshold:
 CRE/capital ratio of ~398% (Q2 2024) is nearly double the 300% enhanced scrutiny threshold. As charge-offs erode capital and loan growth continues, this ratio's trajectory warrants monitoring.[1]
