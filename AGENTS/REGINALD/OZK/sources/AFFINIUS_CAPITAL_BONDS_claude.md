# Affinius Capital has no public debt — but its $64B platform shows more ambition than distress

**Affinius Capital (formerly USAA Real Estate Company) does not issue publicly traded bonds, notes, or any SEC-registered debt instruments.** The firm is a private, SEC-registered investment adviser — a capital *provider*, not a public-market borrower — meaning no FINRA TRACE data, EMMA filings, or corporate bond prices exist under either its current or legacy name. With **$64 billion in gross AUM** and **$3.76 billion in CRE loans originated in 2025 alone**, the firm appears operationally aggressive rather than distressed, though one legacy office foreclosure and heavy execution risk from a $3.4 billion REIT take-private warrant close monitoring.

The confusion is understandable: several USAA-affiliated entities *do* issue public bonds — USAA Capital Corp (rated Aa1 by Moody's) and USAA Federal Savings Bank among them — but these are entirely separate legal entities from Affinius Capital, which spun off from USAA via a management-led buyout in April 2020 and formally rebranded in March 2023.

---

## Why no bonds, ratings, or TRACE data exist for this entity

Affinius Capital LLC operates as a **registered investment adviser** (SEC CRD# 316030), not a securities issuer. Its business model is to raise equity capital from institutional limited partners — pension funds, insurance companies, sovereign wealth — through private fund vehicles (such as Affinius Capital Partners VII), then deploy that capital as commercial real estate equity investments and originating loans *to* borrowers. Fund-level borrowing may occur through private bank subscription lines and warehouse facilities, but these are bilateral credit arrangements that never appear in public bond databases.

Exhaustive searches across SEC EDGAR, EMMA/MSRB, FINRA Bond Center, Cbonds, and Bloomberg confirmed **zero outstanding public debt instruments** under either "Affinius Capital" or "USAA Real Estate Company." No CUSIPs, no prospectuses, no registration statements for debt offerings. The firm carries **no public credit ratings** from Moody's, S&P, Fitch, or KBRA — again, because it has no rated public debt. Any credit analysis of this entity would require access to private financial statements shared under NDA with LPs or lender counterparties.

**No debt maturing in October 2026 or Q3-Q4 2026 was identified** in public markets. If Affinius has private credit facility maturities approaching, these would not be visible in any public database.

## The Columbus Center foreclosure is real but isolated

The most significant distress signal uncovered is a **$69 million foreclosure suit** filed in October 2024 against Affinius Capital on **Columbus Center**, a 14-story, 262,000-square-foot office building at 1 Alhambra Plaza in Coral Gables, Florida — a legacy USAA Real Estate development.

The property's performance deteriorated sharply: occupancy dropped from 69% to **63%** between 2021 and mid-2024, while the debt service coverage ratio collapsed from 1.98 to **0.59** as floating-rate interest costs surged. Lender Värde Partners (through special servicer Trimont) accelerated the maturity in March 2024 after Affinius failed to execute a required interest rate cap agreement. The firm was offered reinstatement for $2.4 million plus a new rate cap and $2.5 million interest reserve — and **declined**. Default interest accrues at roughly **$19,000 per day**, with total recovery sought exceeding $74 million.

This appears to be a deliberate decision to walk away from an underperforming office asset rather than inject more capital — a rational triage move given the broader office sector's distress. Affinius agreed to a receiver appointment. Critically, the firm's new lending activity overwhelmingly avoids the office sector, concentrating instead on multifamily, industrial, data centers, and self-storage.

## A $3.4 billion REIT acquisition signals firepower, not fragility

Far from showing signs of systemic stress, Affinius Capital announced in **February 2026** the **$3.4 billion take-private acquisition of Veris Residential (NYSE: VRE)**, a Class A multifamily REIT. The all-cash deal at $19.00 per share — a **23.2% premium** to the unaffected price — is being executed alongside Vista Hill Partners (co-led by Jonathan Kushner and Bradford Klatt). Financing includes a **$2.08 billion committed senior secured bridge loan facility**.

This is a landmark transaction. Taking a public REIT private at this scale requires institutional credibility, deep capital relationships, and the ability to secure multi-billion-dollar committed financing on short notice. The deal is expected to close in Q2 2026 and would make Affinius one of the largest private multifamily owners in the Northeast.

The firm's 2025-2026 lending pipeline reinforces this picture of aggressive deployment:

- **$340 million** refinancing for a Gowanus, Brooklyn mixed-use project (July 2025)
- **$250 million** construction loan for Brickell Starlite in Miami — Florida's second-largest multifamily construction loan of 2025
- **$200 million** construction loan for a 21-story Gowanus multifamily tower (December 2025)
- **$300 million** refinancing for a Downtown Brooklyn high-rise (March 2026)
- **£80 million** debut European loan for UK student housing — the first deal on a platform targeting **$2 billion in European lending**

Total 2025 lending volume reached approximately **$3.76 billion across 30 deals**, with roughly half in new construction and half in refinancings.

## The Bank OZK co-lending partnership remains active and concentrated in stronger sectors

Affinius and Bank OZK maintain a **systematic, repeat co-lending partnership** with at least **eight documented transactions totaling over $837 million**. The structure is consistent: Bank OZK provides the **senior secured tranche** while Affinius takes the **subordinate/mezzanine position**, creating a full-stack construction financing solution for institutional-grade sponsors.

Key joint deals include the **$135 million Perris Gateway** industrial project in California's Inland Empire (September 2024), the **$120 million Edenvale Business Park** in Silicon Valley (July 2024), the **$182 million Hillcrest Apartments** in Thousand Oaks (2025), and the **$141 million Piazza at Ardmore** in suburban Philadelphia (January 2026). The deals concentrate in **industrial/logistics and multifamily** — sectors with meaningfully stronger fundamentals than the office and life sciences segments where Bank OZK's portfolio stress has materialized.

**No defaults or credit problems have been reported in any Affinius-Bank OZK co-originated loan.** Bank OZK's internal assessment reportedly classifies the Affinius relationship as "Stable." This stands in contrast to Bank OZK's broader RESG portfolio challenges, which include a **$915 million IQHQ life sciences loan** in San Diego maturing August 2026, a **$72.4 million Boston office charge-off**, and the distressed sale of a $265 million life sciences construction loan to Strategic Value Partners in January 2026.

Bank OZK itself is undergoing a deliberate diversification, capping new loans at **$500 million** and reducing RESG's share from a peak of 70% to a target of ~50% of total loans (currently **54.4%**). The bank's allowance for credit losses has grown to **$537 million**, and short interest sits around 14%. Management expects elevated RESG loan repayments through 2026 with meaningful growth resuming in 2027.

## The real risks to monitor are execution and concentration

Affinius Capital shows **no signs of systemic financial distress or liquidity impairment**. The Columbus Center foreclosure is an isolated legacy office casualty, and the firm's deal pipeline, fundraising capacity, and strategic expansions all point to a platform in aggressive growth mode. However, several risk factors deserve ongoing attention:

**Execution concentration** is the primary concern. The firm is simultaneously managing a $3.4 billion REIT take-private (with $2.08 billion in bridge financing to eventually refinance), an active $3+ billion annual construction lending pipeline, a European lending platform launch targeting $2 billion, and a $25 billion data center development pipeline through Corscale. Each of these initiatives individually would strain a mid-sized firm's capacity.

**Coastal multifamily exposure** is notable — roughly 70% of 2025 multifamily lending volume targeted New York, California, and Florida markets. This is a concentrated bet on 2026-2027 rent stabilization in supply-heavy metros. **Subordinate position risk** in the Bank OZK co-lending structure means Affinius absorbs losses first if any of those construction projects encounter cost overruns, lease-up delays, or value declines.

Finally, the firm's private structure means **balance sheet health is opaque**. With no public debt, no public ratings, and no SEC financial reporting obligations beyond Form ADV, outside observers cannot independently verify liquidity, leverage, or fund-level performance. Any assessment of Affinius's ability to continue funding CRE construction loans ultimately depends on institutional LP confidence, private credit facility availability, and the performance of its existing portfolio — none of which are publicly visible. The available evidence suggests the firm retains strong institutional backing, but the absence of public transparency itself constitutes a risk factor for counterparties and co-lenders.
