# Bank OZK's NDFI lending: confirmed counterparties and hidden exposures

Bank OZK's **$2.74 billion NDFI portfolio** is split between two distinct operations — its CIB Lender Finance group and its RESG "loan-to-lender" program — but the specific debt fund borrowers remain largely undisclosed. This research identified **two confirmed direct lending relationships** with NDFIs (Prospect Floating Rate Fund and Regents Capital), **at least nine repeat co-lending partnerships** with CRE debt funds and mezzanine lenders, and important structural insights about OZK's exposure. Critically, none of the major mortgage REITs or large BDCs on the target list (Claros, Ready Capital, ARCC, BXMT, KREF, OBDC) were found as Bank OZK counterparties in any public filing or disclosure.

---

## Two confirmed direct NDFI lending relationships

The most concrete finding is a verified SEC filing establishing Bank OZK as a direct fund lender. **Prospect Floating Rate and Alternative Income Fund, Inc.** — a non-traded BDC managed by Prospect Capital Management — disclosed in an 8-K filed August 13, 2025, that its subsidiary Prospect Flexible Funding, LLC entered into a **$75 million senior secured revolving credit facility with Bank OZK as facility agent**. The facility carries interest at one-month SOFR + 250 basis points and matures August 10, 2029. It replaced a prior $75 million facility with Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation that had $33.8 million drawn at termination. This is the only confirmed SEC filing where Bank OZK appears as a credit facility provider to a BDC or similar fund entity.

The second confirmed relationship is with **Regents Capital Corporation**, a private equipment leasing and financing company based in Costa Mesa, California. Bank OZK serves as sole lender on a **$150 million revolving credit facility**, initially closed at $100 million and later extended and upsized in November 2025. Brean Capital advised on the transaction. While Regents is a non-depository specialty finance company rather than a CRE debt fund, this confirms OZK's active presence in the lender finance space.

No other BDC, private credit fund, or CRE mortgage REIT was found listing Bank OZK among its credit facility lenders in any reviewed SEC filing. The major mortgage REITs and BDCs (BXMT, STWD, ARCC, KREF, LADR, TRTX, GBDC, OBDC) universally rely on money-center banks — JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank — for their warehouse lines, repurchase facilities, and revolving credit. Bank OZK does not appear in any of these lender groups.

## Nine confirmed co-lending partnerships reveal OZK's NDFI orbit

While direct lender-to-fund relationships proved scarce in public records, a rich pattern of **co-lending relationships** emerged where Bank OZK provides senior construction debt and a private credit fund provides subordinate/mezzanine financing on the same deal. These partnerships are the most likely pipeline through which Gleason's "loan-to-lender" relationships develop — the same funds that co-lend alongside OZK on some deals may borrow from OZK to leverage whole loans on other deals.

**Square Mile Capital Management** is Bank OZK's most prolific co-lending partner, with at least **five confirmed joint transactions totaling over $1.5 billion** in combined financing between 2019 and 2023. Square Mile publicly described Bank OZK as "a trusted and reliable partner in the construction lending space." Their deals include One Chicago Square ($735M, March 2019), 100 Altair in Sunnyvale ($114M, January 2021), 2150 Kittredge in Berkeley ($91M, November 2021), Bioterra life sciences campus in San Diego ($203M, December 2022), and a San Francisco life sciences project ($373M, January 2023). The Bioterra loan is notable because the project is now reportedly vacant and facing distress.

**Starwood Property Trust (STWD)** has co-lent with Bank OZK on multiple Philadelphia developments for Post Brothers, including a **$400 million construction package** at 1001 South Broad Street (January 2022), where OZK provided $250M senior and Starwood provided $110M mezzanine. Press reports noted the two "have previously provided construction financing within Post Brothers' portfolio" for The Atlantic, Presidential City, and Piazza Alta — indicating a multi-year relationship.

## Inside OZK's NDFI lending machine: three distinct channels

Bank OZK's Q3 2025 earnings call (October 17, 2025) provided the most detailed public disclosure of the bank's NDFI lending architecture. The exposure flows through three distinct channels, each with different risk profiles.

**Channel 1: CIB Lender Finance Group.** Led by Managing Director Jim Lyons (hired October 2021), this team — formerly called Equipment Finance & Capital Solutions (EFCS) — provides leverage to BDCs and other non-bank lenders. Jake Munn, CIB President, described the approach: "We really focus on single-lender opportunities and to a degree, 2-bank club deals within that niche more than the broader syndications... We do a bottoms-up risking, which is very different than other institutions." The team reviews each underlying portfolio company investment and rates it against OZK's internal methodology. They use third-party field examinations and appraisals on collateral. The **pull-through rate is sub-15%**, meaning over 85% of deals are declined. Industries OZK avoids in this book include consumer lending, auto, healthcare, venture capital, and technology — sectors where BDC structures are most stretched.

**Channel 2: RESG "Loan-to-Lender" Program.** This is the component CEO George Gleason emphasized. When CRE debt funds win a unitranche deal, they sometimes bifurcate it with Bank OZK as senior lender and the fund holding the mezzanine. Other times, the fund holds the whole loan but leverages it with a senior loan from OZK. Gleason stated these are underwritten "exactly as if we were the senior lender" — with identical servicing, management, and risk rating. This structure means a significant portion of the $2.74B NDFI Call Report total represents what OZK considers **de facto senior CRE construction lending**, not traditional fund finance exposure.

**Channel 3: CIB Fund Finance.** Led by Senior Managing Director Parul Abella (25+ years experience; new hire Henry Newton joined from Truist Securities in Houston), this team provides subscription facilities, capital call facilities, and bridge loans to investment funds. Bank President Brannon Hamblen disclosed that this legacy book is being **actively optimized** — "we've shed some legacy borrowers who, in some cases, weren't even utilizing their facilities. We weren't getting the fees that we wanted out of those opportunities." Yahoo Finance's corporate description for OZK explicitly lists "subscription financing services" as a product offering.

## Target entities: Claros and Ready Capital show no OZK connection

**Claros Mortgage Trust (CMTG)**, despite severe financial distress ($489 million net loss in FY2025, 31% non-accrual rate, $747M in REO assets), does **not appear to be a Bank OZK counterparty**. CMTG's five repurchase agreement lenders are JPMorgan Chase, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo, Goldman Sachs, and Barclays. In February 2026, CMTG obtained a $500M distressed-terms secured loan from HPS Investment Partners, issuing warrants at $4.00/share. Bank OZK appears nowhere in these arrangements.

**Ready Capital Corporation (RC)**, also under significant stress (net loss of $0.31/share in Q2 2025), maintains **15 warehouse counterparties** according to supplemental filings, but specific lender names are not publicly listed. No evidence of Bank OZK among them was found. Ready Capital's financing relies primarily on CLO securitizations, warehouse lines from institutional banks, and corporate secured notes.

Comprehensive searches across SEC filings, news sources, and trade publications produced **no evidence of Bank OZK lending relationships** with Arbor Realty Trust (ABR), KKR Real Estate Finance Trust (KREF), Blue Owl/OBDC/OBDE, Ares Capital (ARCC), Apollo/Athene, Golub Capital (GBDC), Ladder Capital (LADR), TPG RE Finance Trust (TRTX), Blackstone Mortgage Trust (BXMT), BrightSpire Capital, Granite Point, or any of the smaller CRE bridge lenders searched (Thorofare, Trez, Churchill, Parkview, Dwight, etc.).

## UCC filings, court records, and data gaps

**UCC filing searches were inconclusive.** Both Delaware and Arkansas require authorized-searcher access or per-debtor paid searches to query by secured party name. These databases are not publicly searchable online by secured party. A comprehensive UCC search would require engaging a service provider (CT Corporation, Wolters Kluwer, or CSC) to run "Bank OZK" secured party searches across Delaware, New York, Arkansas, and other key jurisdictions. Given OZK's confirmed NDFI lending, UCC-1 filings almost certainly exist but cannot be identified without paid access.

**Court records revealed no fund-related litigation.** The only significant Bank OZK litigation found was the 2018 Strathclyde Pension Fund securities class action ($45M settlement in 2022), which concerned RESG loan quality disclosures rather than NDFI counterparty disputes. No lawsuits involving Bank OZK versus any BDC, debt fund, or bridge lender were identified through CourtListener, Justia, or general web searches.

**EDGAR full-text search** (EFTS) could not be accessed directly through automated tools due to API access restrictions. A manual browser-based search of EDGAR for "Bank OZK" filtered to 10-K and 10-Q filings from 2024–2026 would be the single most productive next step for identifying additional fund counterparties that disclose Bank OZK as a lender.

The **Benefit Street Partners connection** referenced in earlier research (a 2020 8-K filing, CIK 0001562528) could not be verified — the filing was inaccessible, and no corroborating evidence emerged. Benefit Street Partners Realty Trust (now Franklin BSP Realty Trust, NYSE: FBRT) lists JPMorgan, Barclays, Wells Fargo, and Credit Suisse as its lenders in known filings.

## Stress indicators and risk assessment

Among the confirmed co-lending relationships, one project shows clear distress: the **Bioterra life sciences campus** in San Diego ($203M co-originated with Square Mile Capital, December 2022) is reportedly a vacant 316,000 SF property. This sits alongside OZK's broader RESG stress — the adjacent IQHQ RaDD campus ($915M commitment, ~$555M funded) remains largely unleased with an August 2026 maturity, and the nearby Pacific Center ($265M) was sold to distressed-debt specialist Strategic Value Partners in January 2026.

OZK's overall credit trajectory is deteriorating: **net charge-offs hit $160 million in FY2025** (up from $57.4M in 2024), with Q4 alone producing $98.3M in charge-offs. The allowance for credit losses stands at $632 million (1.16% of loans), now **below the annualized charge-off rate** for the first time. CRE concentration remains at approximately **455% of tangible equity**, well above the 300% regulatory guidance threshold. The 2022-vintage construction loans — OZK's record $13.8 billion origination year — begin hitting hard maturity deadlines through 2026.

For the NDFI portfolio specifically, management has not disclosed any losses or stress. The sub-15% pull-through rate, bottoms-up underwriting approach, and avoidance of consumer/auto/healthcare/VC-focused BDCs suggest deliberate risk management. However, the opacity of the counterparty list means market participants cannot independently assess concentration risk within the $2.74 billion NDFI book.

---

## Conclusion

Bank OZK's NDFI exposure is real and growing, but it is structurally different from what the headline Call Report number suggests. A substantial portion represents RESG senior construction lending packaged as "loans to lenders" — economically equivalent to the bank's core business. The confirmed direct fund lending relationships (Prospect Floating Rate, Regents Capital) are modest in scale. The co-lending partnerships with Square Mile Capital, Starwood, Cain International, JVP, and Related Fund Management reveal OZK's network of CRE debt fund relationships — precisely the firms most likely to also borrow from OZK's loan-to-lender program. The most productive avenues for identifying additional counterparties would be a paid UCC secured-party search in Delaware, a manual EDGAR full-text search for "Bank OZK" across all 10-K/10-Q filings, and review of proprietary syndicated loan databases (Refinitiv LPC, Bloomberg). The absence of any identified relationship with Claros, Ready Capital, or the major institutional BDCs suggests OZK's NDFI exposure tilts toward mid-market CRE-focused funds rather than the largest alternative asset managers.
