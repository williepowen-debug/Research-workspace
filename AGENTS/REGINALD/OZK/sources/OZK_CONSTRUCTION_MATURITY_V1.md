Bank OZK (OZK): Construction Loan Maturity Wall — 2026 Analysis
Executive Summary
Bank OZK does not publish a quarterly breakdown (Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4) of construction loan maturities in its regulatory filings. The 10-K and investor presentations disclose maturity schedules only in annual buckets (by calendar year for unfunded commitments) or in duration bands (within one year / one-to-five years / after five years for the loan portfolio). However, combining these disclosures with management commentary from Q3 and Q4 2025 earnings calls allows for a reasonably informed view of the shape and severity of the 2026 maturity wall.

What the 2024 10-K Actually Shows
Contractual Maturities of Unfunded Loan Commitments (Dec 31, 2024)
This table — from page 108 of the 2024 annual report — covers the $19.08 billion in outstanding unfunded commitments (the "draw-down pipeline"), not the funded balance. It shows calendar-year contractual maturities:[1]

Key insight: 2026 is the single largest maturity year for unfunded commitments — $6.19B, or roughly 32% of the total pipeline — reflecting that 2022 vintage loans with 3–4 year terms are reaching contractual maturity dates. 2025 and 2027 bracket it with $3.97B and $5.16B respectively.
The 2023 10-K for Comparison
At year-end 2023, the unfunded commitment maturity table showed $9.85B maturing in 2026 (the dominant year), versus $2.34B in 2024 and $5.04B in 2025. As loans have funded and/or been repaid through 2024–25, that $9.85B has drawn down to $6.19B — confirming that significant payoff activity has already occurred.[2]

The RESG Vintage Cadence Chart (Management Comments, Fig. 13)
The Q3 2025 investor presentation provides the definitive picture of remaining commitment by origination vintage as of September 30, 2025:[3]

The 2022 vintage ($13.82B originated) still had $9.94B in remaining commitment ($7.82B funded, $2.12B unfunded) as of September 30, 2025 — making it by far the largest single cohort driving 2026 maturities.[3]
Q3 2025 alone saw $1.25B of 2022 vintage commitments reduced (paid off or reduced), plus $0.63B of 2021 vintage and $0.49B of 2019 vintage. The pace is accelerating.[3]

Management Commentary: The Shape of the Wave
Q3 2025 Earnings Call (October 17, 2025) — Key Quotes
CEO George Gleason was direct about the 2022 vintage timeline:[4]
"We originated $13.8 billion in 2022… most of these loans have a three- to four-year life cycle. It's no surprise to anyone that we would have an exceptionally high level of RESG payoffs in 2025, probably late 2025, and in 2026 as those loans reach that three, four-year timeframe… Next year is going to be the big payoff wave if the normal expected cadence holds true."
On the overall shape of 2026 loan growth:
"Our mid-single-digit loan growth guidance for the year is probably going to be more loaded into the final three quarters than the first quarter. We've got a lot of payoffs we are expecting in Q1."
— CFO Tim Hicks confirmed: "Q1 I think is still a positive quarter of growth but you could have a payoff or two that comes in or pushes out… second quarter, third quarter, fourth quarter will be stronger than first quarter."[4]
Q4 2025 Earnings Call (January 21, 2026)
Management confirmed 2026 guidance mirrors 2025 in structure — mid-single-digit loan growth with RESG repayments elevated throughout the year:[5][6]
Q4 2025 set a second consecutive record for RESG loan repayments at $3.00 billion for the quarter, bringing full-year 2025 RESG repayments to a record $7.24 billion.[7]
RESG total commitments declined $5.7 billion from peak ($34.5B at March 2024) to $28.8B at December 2025, and management expects "this decreasing trend in RESG total commitments is likely to continue through 2026 and possibly into 2027."[7]
CEO Gleason: "We think 2026 is another year like 2025 where we're just working through the environment with our sponsors."

Quarterly Shape: Cliff or Slope?
The Answer: A Rolling Slope, Not a Single Cliff — With Q1 2026 as the Most Intense Point
Based on all available data, the maturity wall in 2026 is best characterized as follows:

The 2022 origination cadence — Q1: $3.14B, Q2: $3.53B, Q3: $4.35B, Q4: $2.81B — creates a naturally middle-weighted wave within 2026, assuming standard 36-42 month terms. However, Q1 2026 pulls forward because many 2022 Q1 loans have reached or exceeded their contractual maturity, and management specifically called out Q1 2026 as the quarter where payoff velocity would be highest.
Critical caveat: Construction loans rarely pay off at contractual maturity on the dot. Extensions, modifications, and partial paydowns are the norm — OZK extended/modified 41 loans in Q3 2025 alone and collected $70M in reserve deposits from doing so. Of the 541 total modifications/extensions since 2022, sponsors contributed $1.3B in additional equity contributions. This means the "wall" is being dismantled loan-by-loan through the extension machinery, not hitting all at once.[4]

The IQHQ Wildcard
The single largest loan in the RESG portfolio — the $915M commitment to IQHQ for the Research and Development District in San Diego — has ~$555M funded and matures in August 2026. The project is substantially complete but has signed no major life sciences tenants. Management's Q4 2025 call confirmed discussions with IQHQ leadership were "constructive" but offered no resolution. A $300M write-down scenario on this single loan would consume ~47% of the remaining ACL ($632M).[8]

Unfortunately, the page-level PDFs of the eproxymaterials.com interactive annual report required JavaScript rendering and could not be directly scraped. The table does exist in the 10-K but the precise dollar breakdowns for construction/land development by maturity band were not retrievable in text form. Based on OZK's loan structure (86-93% variable rate with SOFR/Prime floors, standard 3-5 year terms), the overwhelming majority of construction loans fall in the 1–5 year band, and based on the 2026 unfunded commitment concentration, the "within one year" bucket at year-end 2024 would have been modest relative to the 2026 surge.

Investment Thesis Implications

Bottom line on your core question: The maturity wall is most intense in Q1–Q3 2026, with Q1 carrying the highest single-quarter risk per management's own guidance. It is not a single cliff — the extension machinery, sponsor support statistics, and already-record repayment pace suggest it functions more like a steep slope. However, the August 2026 IQHQ maturity and the front-loading of payoffs into Q1 2026 (explicitly guided) mean that April–September 2026 is the highest-risk window for surprise credit events. Management's guidance of "mid-single-digit loan growth" for 2026 vs. the current pace of growth implies RESG repayments will likely run $6–8B again in 2026, consistent with a continuing roll-off rather than an abrupt wall.
