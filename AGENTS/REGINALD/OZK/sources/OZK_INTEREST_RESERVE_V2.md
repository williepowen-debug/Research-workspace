# Bank OZK Construction Loan Reserve Analysis

**1. Reserve Duration for a mid-2022 loan.** Construction loans are typically short-term (roughly 12–24 months)【27†L109-L112】. An 18‑month interest reserve added at mid-2022 origination would therefore be exhausted about 18 months later – roughly by late 2023 or very early 2024.  In practice, once the 18‑month window passes, there is no more reserve funding for interest, and the borrower must begin paying interest in cash.  (By contrast, OZK’s CEO has noted that full stabilization deadlines for 2022‐vintage loans stretch into 2025–2026【28†L156-L164】, but the interest reserves typically expire much earlier.)  

**2. Share of construction book with depleted reserves.**  Since OZK’s construction lending exploded in 2021–2022 (around $13.8 billion originated in 2022【28†L156-L164】) and most interest reserves cover only ~12–18 months【27†L109-L112】, the vast majority of those earlier vintages would have exhausted their reserves by now.  In other words, nearly all 2021 and early‐2022 originations (and even many late-2022 loans) have long since used up their funded interest.  By comparison, any loans funded in 2023–24 would be the only ones still in a reserve period.  Altogether, this implies **a very large fraction (likely well above 50–60%)** of OZK’s $7.8B construction portfolio has already run through its reserve, with only the most recent originations still drawing on theirs【27†L109-L112】【28†L156-L164】.  

**3. Mechanics when reserves run out.**  Per banking guidance, an interest reserve is meant to cover all projected interest through construction and lease-up【94†L820-L829】. Once it’s depleted, the borrower must begin paying interest out-of-pocket (“cash-pay”).  In practical terms, OZK would stop capitalizing unpaid interest once the reserve is gone. If the developer cannot meet interest payments, the loan typically must be restructured or reclassified as non‐performing.  As the OCC notes, depleting an interest reserve early is a red flag: the bank will then require actual interest payments from the sponsor or additional equity deposits; if the sponsor cannot pay, the loan can no longer accrue and may become nonaccrual【94†L824-L832】【94†L838-L846】.  In short, reserve exhaustion forces the loan into “cash-pay” mode; inability to pay cash interest leads to workout or nonaccrual status.  

**4. Implication of $108.6M/quarter capitalized interest.**  OZK’s Q4’25 quarterly statement shows $108.6M of interest taken from reserves and added to loan balances.  If the reserve balances were still largely intact, one would expect a higher draw: for example, in Q2’24 OZK recognized about $137M from interest reserves【75†L2819-L2827】, and $7B at ~8–9% annual rate implies ~$140–160M per quarter if fully drawn.  The current $108.6M is noticeably below those levels, suggesting that less interest is being funded from reserves now.  This pattern indicates the reserve pool is shrinking (i.e. reserves are being depleted) rather than remaining “healthy.” In other words, OZK is drawing down existing reserves at a significant rate, so the relatively modest $108M in Q4’25 implies that many reserves have already run dry.
