# Life sciences vacancy keeps climbing, and Bank OZK's $915M bet sits 97% empty

**The U.S. life sciences real estate market shows its first tentative signs of bottoming — national vacancy dipped to 23.0% in Q4 2025, the first decline since mid-2022 — but San Diego remains one of the most distressed markets in the country.** The IQHQ RaDD project, Bank OZK's single largest loan exposure at $915 million, has leased just 50,000 of its 1.5 million square feet of lab/office space since completing construction in 2024. With the loan's original maturity hitting in August 2026 and downtown San Diego life sciences vacancy at **93.7%**, the gap between national stabilization narratives and project-level reality could not be wider. This analysis examines vacancy data across the four major U.S. life sciences clusters, demand trajectory, and the specific credit risk implications for Bank OZK.

---

## San Diego vacancy has tripled in two years and Sorrento Mesa leads the distress

San Diego's overall life sciences vacancy rate stands between **26% and 31%** as of Q4 2025, depending on the brokerage and methodology — roughly triple the sub-10% rates that prevailed in 2022. JLL's core cluster metric recorded 31.2% in Q3 2025 before improving to approximately 27.5% in Q4, buoyed by Bristol Myers Squibb's occupancy of a 427,000 SF build-to-suit at 10300 Campus Point Drive. Newmark pegged core vacancy at **26.1%** in Q4, a marginal improvement from 26.4% in Q3. Savills reported **28.6%** — up 61.6% year-over-year — noting that more than 3 million square feet of new space was delivered in 2025 alone. Cushman & Wakefield's Q3 figure was 26.5%, up from 18.7% a year earlier.

The **Sorrento Mesa submarket is substantially worse**. JLL recorded **38.2% vacancy** in Q3 2025, well above the county-wide average and up sharply from the 30%+ levels observed at mid-year. Sorrento Mesa was the epicenter of speculative office-to-lab conversions in 2022–2024 — roughly half of its office inventory was converted to wet lab — and new ground-up projects like Sterling Bay's Pacific Center (690,000 SF Phase 1) and Longfellow's Bioterra (323,000 SF) delivered into a market with almost no tenant demand. Sorrento Mesa now holds **48% of all sublease space** in San Diego's life sciences market, with approximately 650,000 SF across 30 subleases ranging from 3,000 to 60,000 SF. Torrey Pines, the other premium submarket, faces 29% availability that could spike to **45%** if BioMed Realty's repositioning of the former 630,000 SF Pfizer campus adds to supply.

**Rental rates are in their longest sustained decline on record.** JLL reports Class A lab rents fell to **$5.86 per SF per month NNN** in Q4 2025 — the 14th consecutive quarter of decline. Newmark's core submarket figure was even lower at **$5.37/SF/month** in Q3, representing a 6.7% year-over-year drop. These headline numbers understate the real decline: Hughes Marino estimates effective rents are **20–25% below peak**, with landlords routinely offering a month of free rent per year of lease term and "whatever is required" in tenant improvement packages. One Q4 2025 deal reportedly included a full year of free rent. JLL Vice Chairman Grant Schoneman characterized the landscape as "tenant-favorable" and "likely to continue into the first half of [2026]."

The single bright spot: the construction pipeline has completely cleared. **Zero life sciences space is under construction** in San Diego's core submarkets as of Q4 2025, aside from Novartis's 467,000 SF build-to-suit at Campus Point (not delivering until 2028). This supply-side correction is necessary but insufficient — JLL estimates 5–7 years of available supply exist at current absorption rates to return to equilibrium vacancy of 9–10%.

## Bay Area shows the first real signs of recovery while Boston hits new highs

The **San Francisco Bay Area** offers both the most encouraging and most sobering comparison. CBRE reported Bay Area life sciences vacancy fell to **30.2% in Q4 2025**, down from 32.8% in Q3 — the first quarterly decline in the cycle. For the first time in two years, more life sciences space was leased than came onto the market, with Q4 leasing hitting **1.2 million SF** (87% on the Peninsula). South San Francisco specifically remains around **27% vacancy**, weighed down by spec deliveries like IQHQ's Spur Phase I (340,000 SF, delivered with zero preleasing) and Kilroy's Oyster Point Phase 2. The UCSF Health 280,000 SF lease at Oyster Point in January 2026 was the market's most significant demand signal. However, Bay Area sublease availability **nearly doubled** through 2025, reaching 2.7 million SF — now 20% of total availability, the highest sublease share of any major U.S. life sciences market. Asking rents hover at **$5.86–$5.89/SF/month NNN**, down roughly 5–8% year-over-year, with substantial concessions on top.

**Boston/Cambridge hit a new all-time high vacancy of 28.0%** in Q4 2025, per CBRE, with full-year negative absorption of 929,000 SF. The Seaport submarket reached a staggering **34.8% vacancy**. Colliers places broader availability at approximately 34% heading into 2026. However, the market is sharply bifurcated: **East Cambridge/Kendall Square** — the global gold standard for life sciences real estate — maintained a relatively tight **9.3% direct vacancy** (Lincoln Property Company data), though even Kendall saw its largest quarterly jump since early 2024. Cambridge asking rents have fallen to **$93/SF annually**, down from $113/SF in Q3 2021 when vacancy was 0.3%. Greater Boston asking rents declined to **$83.56/SF NNN** per CBRE, down 620 basis points from year-end 2024. Sublease availability stands at 6.7% of metro inventory. Biogen's 580,000 SF Kendall Square lease was the market's largest deal of 2025, while Takeda listed 449,000–630,000 SF for sublease as it consolidates into a new campus.

## National demand is stabilizing but not recovering — and headwinds are mounting

The national picture offers cautious hope. CBRE's Q4 2025 data showed the **first decline in U.S. lab vacancy since Q2 2022**, with two consecutive quarters of positive net absorption (938,000 SF in Q3, 156,000 SF in Q4). Cushman & Wakefield confirmed net absorption turned positive for the first time in seven quarters at approximately 555,000 SF. CBRE projects vacancy will stabilize at a **cyclical peak of 23.3%** in 2026 before beginning a slow recovery. The construction pipeline collapsed to **4.5 million SF nationally** — the lowest since mid-2017 — down from a peak of 15.4 million SF of starts in 2022.

But the term "stabilization" obscures a brutal underlying math. JLL calculates the market needs **20–25 million SF of net absorption or supply reduction** to reach equilibrium. At current quarterly absorption rates of 150,000–950,000 SF, organic demand alone would take many years. JLL projects that **18.7 million SF** — roughly 30% of current available space — will convert to other uses or face distress by 2030, making supply-side attrition the primary rebalancing mechanism rather than demand growth.

Several structural headwinds compound the challenge. **Biotech's share of U.S. venture capital has fallen from 15% to roughly 7%** as AI investment crowds out life sciences. AI-native biotech firms, which account for one-sixth of all biotech VC deals, use approximately **one-third less space per employee** than traditional biotechs. NIH scaled back new grant awards by roughly $2.3 billion in 2025, with over 7,800 grants cancelled or suspended. San Diego venture funding declined **44.8%** to just $2.3 billion in 2025. The biotech IPO market produced only about 10–11 offerings in 2025, though early 2026 shows improvement with $1.4 billion in biotech stock offerings in February alone.

On the demand side, large pharma companies remain the primary source of leasing activity, driven by the **patent cliff** (over 200 drugs losing protection, 69 blockbusters with $300+ billion in cumulative revenue at risk) and the GLP-1/obesity gold rush (120+ metabolic assets in development, a $100 billion market opportunity by 2030). Novartis's 466,598 SF lease in San Diego and Biogen's 580,000 SF deal in Cambridge demonstrate that well-capitalized pharma will transact — but their deal volume cannot offset the retreat of hundreds of capital-constrained startups.

## The IQHQ RaDD project is a $1.9 billion campus with one tenant

The Research and Development District — IQHQ's flagship 1.7 million SF, $1.9 billion waterfront development on the former Navy Broadway Complex — completed construction by mid-2024. Five buildings across eight acres are finished. Progress Park, a 1.5-acre adjacent public amenity, opened in October 2025. Retail space is roughly 50% leased to tenants including Equinox, Rivian, and Javier's. The problem is the other **1.5 million SF of lab and office space, which is approximately 97% vacant.**

The sole life sciences tenant is the **J. Craig Venter Institute**, which signed a 50,000 SF lease in May 2025 and expects to move in by fall 2026. That represents just **3.3% of available lab/office space**. IQHQ has told lenders it is in "active dialogue with three life science users and three office users," but no additional leases have been announced. The project sits in a downtown San Diego submarket where CBRE measured life sciences vacancy at **93.7%** — a near-total absence of demand for lab space in this location. San Diego's life sciences cluster is concentrated 12–15 miles north in the Torrey Pines/Sorrento Mesa/UTC corridor, and convincing tenants to pioneer a downtown location amid the worst oversupply cycle in the sector's history has proven extraordinarily difficult.

Bank OZK's $915 million loan commitment on RaDD — its single largest exposure — has approximately **$555 million funded**, with the remaining $360 million designated for tenant improvements and leasing commissions. The original maturity is **August 2026**, though Bank OZK disclosed in mid-2024 that it negotiated a two-year extension option, potentially pushing maturity to August 2028. IQHQ has invested approximately $950 million of its own equity and contributed an additional $87 million to loan reserves. The developer has remained current on all payments through Q4 2025. No charge-off, write-down, or specific reserve has been taken on the loan.

However, signals of financial strain at IQHQ are unmistakable. **Aimco, a multifamily REIT that invested $50 million in IQHQ in 2020, wrote its position down to $3 million — a 94% loss** — citing "adverse capital market conditions" and IQHQ's "financial condition." IQHQ raised $900 million in early 2025 from six firms, then secured $270 million from Innovative Industrial Properties in August 2025 at a **weighted average return exceeding 14%** — pricing that signals severely constrained access to cheaper capital. An additional $310 million financing round closed in October 2025.

## Bank OZK's broader San Diego book is shedding risk but absorbing losses

Beyond RaDD, Bank OZK held approximately **$1.4–1.6 billion** in San Diego life sciences exposure. The most notable recent development: Bank OZK **sold its $265 million Pacific Center loan** (Sterling Bay's vacant Sorrento Mesa project) on January 5, 2026, to Strategic Value Partners, a distressed debt firm. Bank OZK claimed par recovery on the $100 million of outstanding principal, though the involvement of a distressed debt buyer raises questions about the price paid for future unfunded commitments.

The bank's **$475 million Aperture Del Mar loan** is the portfolio's bright spot — the project is pre-leased to Neurocrine Biosciences through 2036, making it the only clearly performing San Diego life sciences exposure. The **$202 million Bioterra loan** (Longfellow's Sorrento Mesa project, co-originated with Square Mile Capital) faces distress, as the all-electric lab building sits vacant in a 38% vacancy submarket.

Bank OZK's Q4 2025 earnings revealed **$98.3 million in net charge-offs** — nearly eight times Q4 2024's figure and the highest annualized charge-off rate (1.18%) in 15 years. Full-year 2025 charge-offs reached $160 million, up from $57.4 million in 2024. The allowance for credit losses stood at approximately **$632 million**. A $72.4 million charge-off on a Boston office development and $9 million on a Chicago life sciences project (Lincoln Yards) contributed to Q4 losses. Total bank-wide life sciences exposure was approximately **$3.2 billion** as of Q3 2024, representing roughly 10% of RESG loans.

Analyst coverage is polarized. Citigroup downgraded Bank OZK to "Sell" in May 2024, cutting its price target to $37 and flagging "newfound but substantial concerns" about RaDD, estimating 40% valuation compression if the space leases as office rather than lab. Temple 8 Capital published a short seller report on March 10, 2026, calling Bank OZK "The Next SVB?" and targeting a catalyst window of April–June 2026 around the original loan maturity. A more balanced analysis from Red Beryl argued that Bank OZK's risk to actual principal loss is moderated by IQHQ's $950 million equity cushion — the property value would need to decline approximately 75% from its 2022 appraisal for the bank to face impairment on funded amounts.

## Conclusion: A market bottoming nationally but years from equilibrium in San Diego

The national life sciences market is forming a cyclical bottom. Vacancy declined for the first time in three years, construction has collapsed to 2017 levels, and net absorption has turned positive. But the gap between "stabilization" and "recovery" is measured in years, not quarters — **JLL estimates 18.7 million SF must exit the market through conversions and distress before equilibrium returns**, a process extending through 2030. San Diego faces a particularly acute challenge: vacancy roughly triple pre-pandemic norms, venture funding down nearly 45%, and zero construction offering no near-term relief because the existing oversupply is so severe.

For Bank OZK's credit exposure, three dynamics matter most. First, the IQHQ RaDD project's fundamental challenge is locational — it built a life sciences campus in a submarket with essentially zero life sciences tenant demand, and no amount of concessions has overcome this in two years of marketing. The August 2026 original maturity date will force a decision on the extension option, likely drawing intense analyst and regulatory scrutiny. Second, IQHQ's equity cushion and continued capital raises ($1.4+ billion raised in 2025 alone) provide meaningful downside protection on the bank's $555 million of funded exposure — but the 14%+ cost of new capital signals a borrower under severe financial pressure. Third, the broader San Diego portfolio is de-risking (Pacific Center sold, Aperture performing), but the RaDD concentration — representing roughly **21% of Bank OZK's tangible equity** — means this single asset remains the defining credit question for the institution. The most likely scenario is an extend-and-amend resolution with gradually improving but still distressed leasing conditions; the tail risk is a forced sale or restructuring in a market where comparable life sciences assets have traded at discounts of 22% or more from prior valuations.
