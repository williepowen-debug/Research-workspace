# Bank OZK geographic loan exposure: a complete risk map

**Bank OZK's $32.7 billion RESG loan book is overwhelmingly concentrated outside its Dallas FDIC supervisory district, with ~90% of commitments spread across markets overseen by five other FDIC regional offices.** The portfolio's top two MSAs — Miami ($4.0B) and New York ($3.5B) — together represent 23% of all RESG commitments. Florida alone accounts for nearly 23% of the book, while California totals ~16%. Active credit deterioration is centered in life sciences (San Diego, Boston, Chicago) and office (Seattle, Los Angeles), with **$98.3M in Q4 2025 charge-offs**, a 15-year-high annualized charge-off rate of **1.18%**, and at least $285M in non-accrual loans. The single largest risk event is the **$915M IQHQ RaDD loan maturing August 2026** — representing ~21% of OZK's tangible equity — in a San Diego life science market running at 30%+ vacancy. This report maps every disclosed geographic exposure, project-level distress signal, and regulatory mismatch using primary sources.

## Project-level distress map: the problem loans by metro

### San Diego — life science crisis ($1.94B MSA exposure)

San Diego is ground zero for OZK's life science distress. County-wide lab vacancy hit **29.7–31.2%** in Q3–Q4 2025 (JLL/Savills/Newmark records), with the Sorrento Mesa submarket at **35–38%** vacancy and downtown at **>90%** for direct lab space. Asking rents have declined for **13 consecutive quarters**.

**IQHQ RaDD ($915M commitment, ~$555M funded):** The single largest risk in OZK's portfolio. This 1.7M SF waterfront life science campus is construction-complete but remains **overwhelmingly vacant**. The J. Craig Venter Institute signed a lease in May 2025 (the first life science tenant), and retail space is ~50% leased (Equinox, Javier's, Rivian showroom), but the core lab space is essentially unleased. The loan matures **August 2026** with a negotiated two-year extension option. Citigroup's May 2024 double-downgrade of OZK to "Sell" cited RaDD specifically, triggering a 14%+ single-day stock decline. Finance professor Rebel Cole estimated the project could be worth as little as **$500M** against $555M in funded debt. IQHQ raised $900M in new equity from existing investors in 2024 and contributed an additional $87M to the project. OZK management expressed confidence and noted the sponsor continues supporting the project. [**Confidence: HIGH** on loan amount and project status — extensively documented in Bisnow, SD Union-Tribune, Citi research. **MEDIUM** on valuation estimates, which are third-party opinions.]

**Bioterra ($202.6M):** A 316,000 SF life science campus at 5889 Oberlin Drive, Sorrento Mesa, developed by Longfellow Real Estate Partners and co-originated with Square Mile Capital in December 2022. Construction completed ~2024 but the building is **vacant** and "facing loan distress" per AtriumData.ai (cited in Bisnow, January 2026). Located in the same Sorrento Mesa submarket as Pacific Center. [**Confidence: HIGH** on loan amount; **HIGH** on vacancy status.]

**Pacific Center / Sterling Bay ($265M — DISPOSED):** A 500,000 SF life science campus in Sorrento Mesa developed by Sterling Bay and Harrison Street. Phase 1 completed May 2025 but sits largely vacant. OZK **sold this nonperforming loan to Strategic Value Partners (SVP)** in a transaction recorded December 18, 2025, and reported January 5, 2026. OZK claimed collection of "all outstanding principal, all accrued interest," but Bisnow noted it was "unlikely SVP paid [full $265M]" for the nonperforming loan. Actual sale price undisclosed. [**Confidence: HIGH** on disposition; **FLAG**: OZK's "at par" claim is disputed by market observers and unverifiable.]

**Combined San Diego project-level exposure in these three deals alone: ~$1.38B.** The MSA total of $1.94B implies an additional ~$560M in other San Diego projects (e.g., Hines-backed Riverwalk, Neurocrine Biosciences campus).

### Chicago — Sterling Bay collapse ($1.13B MSA exposure)

**Lincoln Yards Northern Land ($128M original → $84M foreclosed value):** Sterling Bay surrendered 27 acres of the former Finkl steel plant site via deed-in-lieu of foreclosure in **March 2025**. OZK wrote the loan down twice (from $128M to ~$88M), booking ~$44M in total impairments. JDL Development and Kayne Anderson Real Estate purchased the land for ~$84M in mid-2025, rebranding it "Foundry Park." OZK reported "substantially no gain or loss" on the sale relative to carrying value. [**Confidence: HIGH** — Crain's, CoStar, Bisnow, Block Club Chicago.]

**1229 West Concord Place ($125M commitment, $65M outstanding → $50M carrying value):** Sterling Bay's 284,000 SF life science building, completed early 2023, has been **100% vacant since delivery**. After $14.1M in cumulative charge-offs (Q3–Q4 2025), OZK **seized the property via deed-in-lieu of foreclosure in March 2026**. Carrying value is $50M as of December 31, 2025 (68% of May 2025 appraised value). OZK indicated it may pivot marketing from life sciences to traditional office. [**Confidence: HIGH** — The Real Deal and Bisnow reported the seizure on March 20, 2026.]

**One Chicago Square ($475M OZK senior loan, $735M total package):** Developed by JDL Development and Wanxiang America, this twin-tower mixed-use project (77-story and ~50-story, 812 residential units, 188K SF retail including Whole Foods and Life Time Athletic) was **completed in 2022**. No reports of defaults or distress found. Given the project's 2022 completion and typical construction loan terms, the OZK loan has likely been repaid or refinanced, but **this could not be confirmed from available sources**. [**Confidence: HIGH** on original loan amount; **LOW** on current status — unverified whether loan remains outstanding.]

**Other Chicago Sterling Bay loans:** 300 N. Michigan Ave ($150M, maturity extended to July 2025) and 345 N. Morgan ($~70M, maturity deferred to August 2026) remain on extended terms. [**Confidence: MEDIUM** — limited public detail on current status.]

## FFIEC Call Reports do not contain state-level loan data

A critical finding for this analysis: **the standard FFIEC Call Report (Schedule RC-C Part I) does not include a state-by-state geographic breakdown of loans.** The form collects loan data by type (construction, residential, CRE, C&I, consumer) but not by geography. The FFIEC 041 form (which Bank OZK files, as it has only domestic offices) provides no geographic loan distribution. Geographic lending data exists only through CRA disclosure data (small business/farm loans by census tract), HMDA data (mortgage loans), and the bank's own voluntary disclosures in investor presentations and Management Comments. OZK's Management Comments provide far more granular geographic data (58 MSAs) than any regulatory filing would require. [**Confidence: HIGH** — structural limitation of Call Report forms, confirmed via FFIEC documentation.]

OZK's Call Report does confirm the aggregate loan composition: $9.52B construction/land development (31.8%), $7.84B non-farm non-residential CRE (26.2%), $3.27B multifamily (10.9%), $1.73B C&I (5.8%), and $3.66B consumer (12.2%) as of Q4 2024.

## Conclusion: what the geographic map reveals

Three findings stand out from this analysis. First, **OZK's geographic risk is concentrated in exactly the asset types and markets experiencing the most acute stress** — life science in San Diego/Sorrento Mesa, office in Boston/Seattle/Chicago, and speculative development in markets with collapsing demand. The $1.1B+ on the critical watch list is dominated by life science projects that may never lease at underwritten rates. Second, the **FDIC supervisory mismatch is not theoretical** — 84% of RESG credit exposure sits outside the Dallas district's jurisdiction, meaning the examiners closest to OZK's actual risk are not its primary regulators.
