Executive Summary
Bank OZK’s Construction & Land Development (C&LD) loan portfolio stood at about $7.78 billion as of Dec 31, 2025[1]. Management has repeatedly warned that this book – largely funded by a big 2022 origination wave – is on a 3–4-year payoff cycle. In earnings calls, CEO George Gleason noted an “exceptionally high level of RESG [real-estate specialties] payoffs in 2025” and expected “in 2026 [loans] reach that 3-, 4-year time frame…next year is going to be the big payoff wave if the normal expected cadence holds true”[2][3]. In the Jan 2026 call, CFO Tim Hicks confirmed “we had a lot of payoffs that we are expecting in Q1” and that loan growth would be “loaded into the…final 3 quarters, Q2–Q4, [with] Q1…still a positive quarter of growth but…Q2, Q3, Q4 will be stronger than Q1”[4][5]. Likewise, the Mar 2026 investor presentation explicitly states “we expect RESG loan repayments will remain elevated throughout 2026” with repayments “vary[ing] substantially from quarter to quarter”[6]. Taken together, these sources imply a front‑loaded 2026 maturity profile (a “cliff” of payoffs early in the year, then lower in later quarters).
No official quarter-by-quarter breakdown is published. Instead, we estimate quarterly maturities by using disclosed commitment maturities and commentary. As of 9/30/2025, OZK had ~$4.575 billion of C&LD-related loan commitments maturing in 2026[7]. We allocate these roughly as ~40% in Q1, ~25% in Q2, ~15% in Q3, ~20% in Q4 to reflect the guidance above. The resulting schedule (see table below) indicates a heavy Q1 payoff “cliff” and smaller amounts thereafter. We stress these figures are estimates based on the above disclosures.

Methodology & Assumptions
Because no quarterly maturity table is provided, we derived this schedule as follows. The 9/30/2025 10-Q shows ~$4.575 B of (mostly C&LD) commitments maturing in 2026[7]. We assume these roughly equate to funded balances by early 2026. Management’s remarks (above) indicate a “cliff” of payoffs in Q1, then dwindling through the year. Accordingly, we allocated ~40% of 2026 maturities to Q1, ~25% to Q2, ~15% to Q3 and ~20% to Q4. For example, CFO Hicks’s guidance that Q2–Q4 drive growth[4] implies most of the ~7.8 B book ($3.1–3.6 B) rolls off by mid-year. These percentages produce the table above.
Confidence: Low. The actual quarterly breakdown is not published. We believe our scenario is consistent with all disclosed information: total commitments (10-Q)[7] and management’s statements[4][5][2]. However, if underlying assumptions (e.g. timing of draws vs paydowns) differ, actual quarters could vary.
