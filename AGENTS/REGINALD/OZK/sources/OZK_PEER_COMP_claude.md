# CRE-heavy regional banks: a Q4 2025 peer comparison

**Bank OZK remains a dramatic outlier among CRE-concentrated regional banks, with estimated CRE-to-capital ratios roughly double the interagency guidance thresholds — and deteriorating credit quality to match.** Its Q4 2025 annualized net charge-off rate of **1.18%** was 4–24 times that of peers, driven by write-downs on troubled construction projects. Columbia Banking System (COLB), freshly combined with Pacific Premier, may also breach the 300% CRE/capital threshold. The remaining six banks cluster well below regulatory concentration guidelines, though credit quality varies significantly — from East West Bancorp's pristine **0.08%** NCO rate to Truist's consumer-driven **0.57%**.

## Bank OZK stands alone as a concentration outlier

Bank OZK's CRE exposure dwarfs every peer in this group. KBRA reported OZK's CRE concentration at **358% of total risk-based capital** and construction & development at **197%** as of Q2 2024 — the most recent confirmed regulatory data available. Both figures massively exceed the interagency guidance thresholds of 300% and 100%, respectively. Moody's separately calculated CRE at **455% of tangible common equity** with unfunded commitments nearly doubling that figure.

By year-end 2025, OZK's Real Estate Specialties Group (RESG) had declined from roughly 64% to **54.4% of total loans**, suggesting modest deleveraging. However, total loans grew 7.8% to approximately **$32.4 billion**, meaning absolute CRE dollar exposure likely remained elevated. The Q4 2025 Call Report — which would contain the precise regulatory ratios — is not yet publicly accessible, but directional evidence suggests OZK's CRE/capital ratio remains in the **350–380% range**, roughly 2× the peer median of ~186%.

The credit consequences became visible in Q4. Net charge-offs surged to **$98.3 million** (annualized 1.18%), nearly 8× the year-ago quarter's $12.4 million. A **$72.4 million** write-down on a Boston office development and a $9 million charge-off on a Chicago life sciences project drove the spike. Nonperforming loans reached 1.06% of total loans, up from 0.44% a year earlier — a 2.4× increase. Management characterized this as the "late stages" of the CRE credit cycle, though the trajectory remains concerning. OZK's ACL of **$632 million** (1.95% of loans) provides a buffer, but the coverage ratio of ~184% against noncurrent loans is actually below the peer median of ~231%, meaning OZK is less well-reserved per dollar of problem loans than most peers despite its higher absolute reserve ratio.

## Columbia Banking's post-merger CRE spike and the rest of the pack

Columbia Banking System (COLB) emerges as the second bank potentially breaching the 300% CRE/capital threshold, with an estimated regulatory CRE concentration of **~316%**. This spike is a direct consequence of the Pacific Premier Bancorp acquisition that closed August 31, 2025, which loaded approximately **$10.3 billion in multifamily loans** (22% of combined loans) onto Columbia's balance sheet. On a broad definition including all CRE subcategories, the ratio reaches ~428%. The bank's construction and development exposure, however, remains modest at ~$2.1 billion or roughly 32% of capital — well below the 100% threshold. COLB's credit quality metrics look healthy: a 0.25% NCO rate, 0.41% noncurrent ratio, and strong 245% coverage ratio suggest the CRE book is performing despite its size.

**East West Bancorp** occupies an interesting middle ground with estimated CRE/capital of ~227% — meaningfully below the 300% threshold but above the peer median. What distinguishes EWBC is its **exceptionally low construction exposure** (approximately 6% of capital) and industry-leading credit quality. Its Q4 2025 NCO rate of just **0.08%** was the lowest in the peer group, and its ACL coverage ratio of ~405% was the highest alongside FNB. The bank's **15.1% CET1 ratio** provides the strongest capital cushion in the group.

**FNB Corp's** CEO disclosed a CRE concentration of 197% on the Q4 earnings call, noting the bank had "strategically decreased" this ratio over recent years. FNB's credit quality metrics are similarly strong: a 0.19% NCO rate, the lowest noncurrent loan ratio in the group at ~0.31%, and a commanding ~408% ACL coverage ratio. **Western Alliance** runs at ~174% CRE/capital with notable C&D exposure at ~44% of capital — the second-highest after OZK — reflecting its active construction lending in Western U.S. markets. WAL's noncurrent ratio of ~0.96% is elevated, and its ACL coverage of ~90% is the weakest in the peer group, warranting monitoring.

**Zions Bancorporation** posted the single best quarterly NCO figure — just **$7 million** or 0.05% annualized — a dramatic improvement from 0.37% in Q3 2025. Its CRE concentration of ~140% is moderate, and the 215% coverage ratio provides adequate cushion.

## The super-regionals bring diversification but different risks

**Huntington Bancshares** and **Truist Financial** serve as diversified benchmarks. Their CRE concentrations — **~64%** and **~48%** of capital, respectively — are fractions of the smaller banks' levels, reflecting loan books dominated by C&I, consumer, and residential mortgage lending.

Huntington's profile shifted in Q4 2025 with the **Veritex Community Credit Union acquisition** (closed October 20, 2025), which added ~$4.2 billion of CRE and pushed CRE from 7.8% to **10.2% of total loans**. Management acknowledged that criticized assets rose to 4.2% "primarily from Veritex commercial real estate loans." Despite this, HBAN's NCO rate held at a moderate 0.24%, and its ACL of **$2.74 billion** (1.83% of loans) — the second-highest reserve ratio in the group — provides substantial loss absorption capacity.

Truist carries the highest absolute NCO rate among the diversified peers at **0.57%**, driven not by CRE stress but by its large consumer lending book (indirect auto, credit card). Its ALLL-to-NPL ratio of **3.2×** is the strongest single coverage metric in the group. Truist's CRE book, at roughly $30 billion, is large in absolute terms but represents less than 10% of its $328.6 billion loan portfolio — a fundamentally different risk profile than concentrated lenders like OZK.

## Where OZK diverges: a statistical portrait

To quantify how far OZK deviates from its peers, consider these multiples of the peer group median:

- **CRE/Capital:** OZK at ~358% is **1.9× the peer median** of ~186% and the only bank more than 1× above the 300% regulatory threshold
- **C&D/Capital:** OZK at ~197% is **7× the peer median** of ~28% and the only bank exceeding the 100% threshold
- **Q4 NCO rate:** OZK at 1.18% is **5.4× the peer median** of 0.22%
- **Noncurrent ratio:** OZK at 1.06% is **1.7× the peer median** of 0.61%
- **ACL/Noncurrent coverage:** OZK at ~184% is **below the peer median** of ~231%, indicating it is actually *less* reserved per dollar of problem loans despite carrying the highest absolute reserve ratio

The combination of extreme concentration, rising charge-offs, and below-median coverage against noncurrent loans makes OZK a clear statistical outlier on virtually every risk dimension measured here.

## Conclusion

This peer group divides into three tiers of CRE risk. **OZK occupies a tier of its own**, with concentration ratios roughly double the regulatory thresholds and rapidly deteriorating credit metrics that consumed $98 million in charge-offs in a single quarter. **COLB joins the elevated-risk tier** at ~316% CRE/capital, though its credit quality remains healthy and the concentration is a byproduct of a recent acquisition rather than organic strategy. **The remaining six banks** cluster well below concentration thresholds, with EWBC, FNB, and ZION showing the cleanest credit profiles. The most underappreciated risk in the group may be **Western Alliance's thin ACL coverage** (~90% of noncurrent loans) — the lowest in the peer set — which leaves limited margin for error if its construction book encounters stress. For investors evaluating CRE tail risk in regional banking, OZK's trajectory from Q3 to Q4 2025 — rising NPLs, surging charge-offs, and declining reserves — deserves the closest scrutiny heading into 2026.
