# Metropolitan Commercial Bank did not fail — but its CRE metrics tell a story

**No bank named Metropolitan Commercial Bank failed on January 30, 2026.** The institution that failed on that date was **Metropolitan Capital Bank & Trust** (Chicago, IL, FDIC Cert #57488) — a completely different, much smaller bank. Metropolitan Commercial Bank (New York, FDIC Cert #34699) remains an active, well-capitalized institution with **$8.3 billion in assets** and a "well capitalized" regulatory designation as of Q4 2025. This report corrects the premise, provides the failure details for Metropolitan Capital Bank & Trust, and delivers the requested CRE concentration comparison between Metropolitan Commercial Bank and Bank OZK — both of which carry CRE ratios well above the **300% regulatory guidance threshold**.

---

## The bank that actually failed on January 30, 2026

**Metropolitan Capital Bank & Trust**, a single-branch community bank at 9 East Ontario Street in Chicago's River North neighborhood, was closed by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR) and placed into FDIC receivership on January 30, 2026. It was the first U.S. bank failure of 2026.

| Data point | Detail |
|---|---|
| FDIC Certificate # | **57488** |
| Total assets (Sept. 30, 2025) | **$261.1 million** |
| Total deposits | **$212.1 million** |
| Estimated DIF loss | **$19.7 million** |
| Acquirer | First Independence Bank, Detroit, MI |
| Cause of failure | "Unsafe and unsound conditions and an impaired capital position" |

The failure stemmed from a **2019 consent order** citing unsafe conditions, impaired capital, and substandard lending practices. Investigative reporting linked the bank's distress to a problematic commercial real estate loan tied to the largest default in the federal government's skilled nursing facility lending program — a $4.5 million exposure to developer Zvi Feiner's Rosewood care facility. By Q3 2025, the bank's net equity capital ratio had deteriorated to just **1.62%**, and it charged off $13.9 million in C&I loans in that quarter alone, producing a staggering **27.34%** net charge-off ratio.

First Independence Bank assumed substantially all deposits and purchased approximately $251 million of assets. The branch reopened as a First Independence location on February 2, 2026. **No FDIC OIG Material Loss Review has been published yet** as of April 2026. Given the $19.7 million DIF cost, it will likely receive a Failed Bank Review rather than a full MLR — the most recent OIG report is an In-Depth Review of Pulaski Savings Bank (EVAL-26-01, dated March 2, 2026).

## Metropolitan Commercial Bank's CRE-heavy profile at Q4 2025

Metropolitan Commercial Bank (NYSE: MCB, FDIC Cert **#34699**) is headquartered at 99 Park Avenue in Manhattan, with 7–8 banking offices. Its parent, Metropolitan Bank Holding Corp., is publicly traded. The bank is notable for an extraordinarily high CRE concentration that has been climbing steadily through 2025 — rising from **346.1%** of total risk-based capital at year-end 2024 to **376.5%** at year-end 2025, well above the 300% enhanced scrutiny threshold in the 2006 Joint CRE Guidance.

**Key Q4 2025 metrics (December 31, 2025):**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total assets | **$8.256 billion** |
| Total loans (net of deferred fees) | **$6.810 billion** |
| CRE concentration (non-owner-occupied CRE / total risk-based capital) | **376.5%** |
| Bank-level total risk-based capital ratio | **11.7%** |
| ACL (Allowance for Credit Losses) / total loans | **1.43%** ($97.1M / $6.81B) |
| Noncurrent (non-performing) loans / total loans | **1.28%** ($86.9M) |
| Schedule RC-C Memo Item 3 / C&I ratio | **39.6%** (user-provided; not independently verified from Call Report) |
| Stockholders' equity | $743.1 million |

The CRE concentration increase through 2025 was driven by **$884.1 million in CRE loan growth** while C&I loans actually declined $174.5 million year-over-year. The bank's investor presentations explicitly note that "CRE owner-occupied is a segment of our C&I Lending platform," meaning a significant share of loans secured by real estate are classified as C&I — consistent with the user's cited 39.6% Memo Item 3/C&I ratio. The NPL spike from 0.54% to 1.28% was driven primarily by a single out-of-market CRE multi-family loan relationship (~$34 million across three loans) that went non-performing in Q3 2025, plus two additional multi-family loans totaling $5.3 million in Q4. Notably, MCB recorded **zero net charge-offs** in Q4 2025 and just 0.06% for the full year.

## Bank OZK carries even larger CRE exposure at $40.8 billion in assets

Bank OZK (FDIC Cert **#4214**), headquartered in Little Rock, Arkansas, operates 265 offices across nine states. Its Real Estate Specialties Group (RESG) — which originates large, complex CRE construction and development loans nationwide — has historically dominated the loan book, though the bank has been actively diversifying. RESG's share of total loans fell from an all-time high of ~70% to **54.4%** at year-end 2025, with the Corporate & Institutional Banking (CIB) division growing to 16.3%.

**Key Q4 2025 metrics (December 31, 2025):**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Total assets | **$40.8 billion** |
| Total loans | **~$32.3 billion** |
| CRE / total risk-based capital | **~358%** (KBRA, last reported at Q2 2024; precise Q4 2025 figure not publicly disclosed in earnings release) |
| CRE / tangible common equity | **~455%** (Moody's and Bank Director, confirmed by multiple analysts) |
| ACL / total loans (ALL portion only) | **~1.5%** (estimated; ALL was $532.3M or 1.62% at Q3 2025, declining in Q4 after $98.3M in charge-offs) |
| ACL / (total loans + unfunded commitments) | **~1.16%** (total ACL ~$632M including unfunded reserve) |
| Noncurrent loans / total loans | **1.06%** (up from 0.44% at Dec 2024 and 0.23% at Dec 2023) |
| Schedule RC-C Memo Item 3 / C&I ratio | **37.6%** (user-provided; not independently verified from Call Report) |

A critical distinction: Bank OZK's widely cited **455% figure uses tangible common equity** as the denominator, while the regulatory 300% threshold and MCB's reported 376.5% use **total risk-based capital**. These are different measures. Bank OZK's CRE/total risk-based capital ratio was **358%** at Q2 2024 per KBRA; with the RESG share declining meaningfully since then, it may have moderated slightly by Q4 2025, but almost certainly remains well above 300%.

The sharpest contrast between the two banks lies in credit losses. Bank OZK recorded **$98.3 million in net charge-offs in Q4 2025 alone** (annualized rate of 1.18%), driven by a $72.4 million charge-off on a Boston office development, a $9 million charge-off on a Chicago life sciences project, and a foreclosure on a Los Angeles land parcel valued at $54.45 million. Full-year 2025 net charge-offs reached **$160 million** (0.50%), the highest rate in 15 years — compared to MCB's near-zero charge-offs.

## Side-by-side comparison reveals different CRE risk profiles

| Metric | MCB (Q4 2025) | Bank OZK (Q4 2025) | Regulatory context |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total assets** | $8.3B | $40.8B | — |
| **CRE / risk-based capital** | **376.5%** | **~358%** (est.) | 300% triggers enhanced scrutiny |
| **ACL / total loans** | **1.43%** | **~1.5%** (est.) | Industry median ~1.2–1.4% |
| **NPL / total loans** | **1.28%** | **1.06%** | — |
| **Memo Item 3 / C&I** | 39.6% (user) | 37.6% (user) | No regulatory threshold |
| **Net charge-off rate (2025)** | **0.06%** | **0.50%** | — |
| **Q4 2025 NCO rate (ann.)** | 0.00% | 1.18% | — |

Both banks exceed the 300% CRE concentration threshold significantly, and the similar **Memo Item 3/C&I ratios** (39.6% vs. 37.6%) suggest both classify a substantial share of real-estate-secured loans as C&I — a practice that can mask true real estate exposure in standard Call Report categories. The key difference is in realized losses: **Bank OZK's charge-off rate is dramatically higher**, reflecting the riskier profile of its large, complex CRE construction loans (office conversions, life sciences campuses) compared to MCB's more conventional multifamily-heavy CRE book. However, MCB's rapid NPL increase — from 0.54% to 1.28% in just two quarters — warrants monitoring.

## Conclusion

The January 30, 2026 bank failure involved Metropolitan Capital Bank & Trust (Chicago), not Metropolitan Commercial Bank (New York). MCB remains an active institution, though its **376.5% CRE concentration ratio** ranks among the highest for any U.S. bank of its size. Both MCB and Bank OZK sit well above the 300% regulatory threshold, and both show elevated Memo Item 3/C&I ratios near 38–40%, indicating that standard C&I loan classifications at each bank contain significant real-estate-secured exposure. The divergence in actual credit losses — MCB's near-zero versus Bank OZK's 15-year-high 0.50% — reflects fundamentally different CRE strategies: MCB's New York metro multifamily focus versus OZK's nationwide construction lending to complex, speculative developments. Neither bank's CRE concentration has triggered public enforcement action, but both remain on the short list of institutions regulators watch most closely under the 2006 Joint CRE Guidance.
