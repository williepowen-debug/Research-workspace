# IQHQ's $1.9B waterfront gamble faces a reckoning

Bank OZK's **$915M construction loan** on IQHQ's Research and Development District (RaDD) — a fully built, 1.7 million square foot life sciences campus on San Diego's waterfront — sits at the nexus of a historic lab-space glut, a developer showing multiple signs of financial distress, and a lender whose credit deterioration is accelerating. The five-building campus has been complete since May 2024, yet **only 3.3% of its lab/office space is leased** to a single tenant. With the original loan maturity of August 2026 now extended to approximately August 2028, the key question is whether IQHQ can generate enough leasing momentum to avoid a restructuring event — and the data suggests the odds are against it.

---

## A near-empty campus in a market drowning in vacancy

RaDD's leasing picture is stark. Of **1.5 million SF of lab/office space**, the sole signed tenant is the **J. Craig Venter Institute (JCVI)**, a genomics research nonprofit that took ~50,000 SF in The Core building in May 2025, with occupancy targeted for fall 2026. Even JCVI's own founder acknowledged the risk, stating "I feel that I'm taking a fairly sizable risk in doing this now." Brokers privately note IQHQ offered JCVI a "sweetheart deal" to secure the marquee name.

Retail leasing tells a marginally better story: roughly **50% of the ~200,000 SF retail component is leased**, including Equinox, Rivian (showroom), Javier's, The Shade Store, and Eve (an event venue managed by the San Diego Padres). But retail represents only 12% of total project area.

The leasing pipeline offers limited comfort. As of December 2025, Cushman & Wakefield Vice Chairman Brett Ward — RaDD's exclusive lab/office broker — confirmed IQHQ is "negotiating leases with five or six other tenants in the professional services and financial sectors." The telling detail: **these are not life sciences tenants**. IQHQ is also building out speculative move-in-ready suites ranging from 10,000 to 40,000 SF, a clear pivot from the original large 50,000 SF floor plates designed for biotech companies. CBRE's Matt Carlson offered a candid assessment: "So far, the San Diego life sciences market has not said 'we need to be downtown, on the waterfront, near to the airport.'"

Average asking rents for San Diego lab space have fallen for **14 consecutive quarters**, dropping to roughly $5.37–$5.95/SF/month NNN by late 2025 (approximately $64–$72/SF annually), with effective rents running **20–25% below asking** after concessions. RaDD lists rents as simply "negotiable."

---

## Five completed buildings, zero construction risk — but a $1.9B price tag

Construction is no longer the concern. All five buildings were delivered by May 2024, roughly six months behind the original Phase 1 target — a modest delay for a project of this scale. Turner Construction, the nation's largest general contractor, served as GC and won an AGC of California award for the work.

| Building | Floors | Size (SF) | Status |
|----------|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Rise** | 15 | 729,903 | Complete — the tallest building on campus |
| **Vida** | Mid-rise | 396,154 | Complete |
| **Core** | 7 | 269,341 | Complete — houses JCVI's 50K SF lease |
| **Alley** | 4 | 132,068 | Complete — primary retail frontage |
| **Edge** | 4 | 104,527 | Complete |

The total development cost has ballooned to **$1.9 billion**, up from an original estimate of $1.5B, reflecting pandemic-era construction cost escalation. The campus includes 5+ acres of green space, a new 1.5-acre public park (Progress Park, opened November 2025), and LEED Gold certification. The physical product is Class A+ by any measure — the challenge is purely demand.

## IQHQ's capital structure reveals deep stress

Multiple data points suggest IQHQ is under severe financial pressure, despite management's insistence that the company has "plenty of runway."

**Rescue-priced capital raises tell the story.** IQHQ raised **$900M in December 2024** from existing institutional investors (including Morgan Stanley, CenterSquare, and NexPoint), hiring restructuring advisory firm PJT Partners to facilitate the raise. Then in August 2025, **Innovative Industrial Properties** (a cannabis REIT where IQHQ co-founder Alan Gold serves as Executive Chairman) committed **$270M** — a $100M revolving credit facility plus $170M in preferred stock at what analysts estimate is a **16.5% coupon**, senior to all common equity. One analyst noted: "When your rescue lender is a cannabis REIT and the coupon is 16.5%, the equity is under severe pressure."

**Investor markdowns are cascading.** Six registered funds holding $707M in IQHQ marked down their positions in H2 2025. The most dramatic: **Aimco wrote its $50M investment down to $3M** — a 94% loss — citing "adverse capital market conditions, increased real estate development costs, and IQHQ's financial condition." Bluerock marked down 4.4% to $6.28/share, Highland/NexPoint dropped 23% to $7.72, T. Rowe Price valued common equity at $4.92/unit, and Altegris marked at just $2.26/unit.

**Bluerock, IQHQ's largest shareholder**, holds exposure exceeding $700M and has extended two PIK loans — $160M at 13.5% and $86M at 14% — on which **IQHQ is not paying current interest**, accruing roughly $8–9M per quarter. Bluerock's own fund listed on the NYSE in December 2025 and promptly lost ~40% of its value on the first trading day.

**Other distress signals across IQHQ's portfolio:**

- **Fenway Center** ($1B, 960K SF, Boston): Vertical construction **paused** in November 2025. Co-CEO Tracy Murphy told the Boston Globe: "We have no plans to go vertical at this point."
- **Brighton development sites** (Boston): Plans for 646K SF of labs scrapped; 155 N. Beacon St. sold for **$35M — roughly two-thirds of its 2022 purchase price**
- **109 Brookline Ave** (285K SF, Boston): Completed in 2024, sits entirely **vacant**
- **J.F. White Contracting** sued IQHQ for over **$27M** owed on Fenway Center work
- Co-founder **Alan Gold departed** as Executive Chairman in December 2024

A positive note: IQHQ signed a **225K SF lease with the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative** at its Elco Yards project in Redwood City (February 2025) and a **244K SF lease with Lila Sciences** at Alewife Park in Cambridge (October 2025). But these wins at other properties do nothing to fill RaDD's 1.45 million SF of vacant lab/office space.

## San Diego's lab market is in its worst downturn in decades

The macro environment for RaDD could hardly be worse. San Diego life sciences vacancy has surged from **4% at the end of 2021** to **28.6–31.2%** by late 2025, depending on the source. JLL's core cluster vacancy hit a record **31.2%** in Q3 2025. Downtown San Diego — where RaDD sits — has an effective life sciences vacancy rate exceeding **90%**, reflecting RaDD's near-total emptiness plus the adjacent **Campus at Horton** (Stockdale Capital, 770K SF), which has zero tenants.

The numbers that matter for absorption analysis are sobering. Approximately **7–8 million SF** of lab space sits vacant across San Diego. Net absorption was **negative 546K SF in 2024** and **negative 497K SF through Q3 2025**. Full-year 2024 leasing totaled just 2.4M SF — mostly renewals, not new demand — and H1 2025 saw only ~500K SF of activity. Industry veteran Mike Krenn estimated in December 2025: "It will be a long road. I heard it will be around nine years until we are at 70-80% occupancy again." JLL projected **3–5 years** before single-digit vacancy returns.

The demand base has shifted structurally. Early-stage biotech startup demand — historically a major driver of lab leasing — has collapsed alongside VC pullback. Biotech's share of venture capital has fallen from ~15% to ~7%. Average lease sizes have shrunk to approximately **12,000 SF** (per JLL, January 2026), making RaDD's massive floor plates a mismatch for current demand. The only active large tenants are big pharma: Novartis leased 700K+ SF in University City in Q3 2025, and Pfizer relocated 230K SF to Del Mar Heights. But these companies chose established biotech clusters, not downtown.

One genuine positive: **new construction has essentially stopped**. Savills reported zero life sciences development under construction in San Diego as of Q4 2025. This supply shutoff will eventually help, but absorption of the existing overhang will take years.

## Bank OZK faces a slow-motion stress test

Bank OZK's exposure to RaDD is the bank's single largest loan commitment. Key loan parameters as of the most recent public disclosures:

- **Committed amount:** $915M
- **Funded amount:** $555M (as of May 2024), with ~$360M designated for tenant improvements and leasing commissions
- **Original maturity:** August 2026; **extended to ~August 2028**
- **Original appraised value:** $2.35B (July 2022 appraisal — now widely viewed as stale)
- **IQHQ additional contribution:** $87M to reserves (disclosed Q2 2024)

**The Citigroup downgrade was the watershed moment.** On May 29, 2024, Citi analyst Benjamin Gerlinger double-downgraded Bank OZK from Buy to Sell — an extremely rare move — citing "substantial concerns with life science construction lending in general" and noting RaDD was "0% leased." He estimated a ~40% valuation compression if the project were refinanced as office rather than lab space. **OZK shares fell 14–17% that day.**

CEO George Gleason has been publicly defiant. On the Q2 2024 earnings call: "If it takes two years to lease or three years to lease or five years to lease, those guys will get to the finish line on it, I'm confident." He subsequently **capped new loan sizes at $500M** to avoid similar attention, calling the media scrutiny "crap" he didn't want to deal with again.

**But the bank's broader credit metrics are deteriorating rapidly.** Q4 2025 net charge-offs surged to **$98.3M — an 8x increase** from $12.4M a year earlier. Full-year 2025 charge-offs hit $160M versus $57.4M in 2024. The annualized Q4 charge-off rate of **1.18%** is the highest in 15 years and now exceeds the bank's allowance-for-credit-losses ratio of 1.16% — meaning **reserves are being consumed faster than they're being built**. Specific CRE losses include a $72.4M charge-off on a Cambridge courthouse redevelopment, a $9M hit on Sterling Bay's Lincoln Yards life sciences project in Chicago, and a deed-in-lieu-of-foreclosure on another Sterling Bay asset.

Notably, Bank OZK **sold its $265M Pacific Center loan** — another San Diego life sciences project (Sterling Bay developer) — to distressed debt specialist Strategic Value Partners in January 2026, claiming par recovery. This may signal the bank is actively pruning its most problematic life sciences exposures.

**Regulatory scrutiny is elevated.** Bank OZK's CRE loans stand at **455% of tangible common equity**, far above the 300% threshold for enhanced regulatory scrutiny. Construction and development concentration sits at 197% versus a 100% guideline. Both KBRA and Moody's have assigned **Negative outlooks** to OZK's ratings. On the Q4 2025 call, Gleason acknowledged: "The longer this challenging cycle drags on for our sponsors, the more likely it would be that individual sponsors on some individual projects would run into trouble."

A March 10, 2026 short seller report from Temple 8 Capital, titled "Bank OZK: The Next SVB?", estimated RaDD's distressed value at as little as **$500M** against $555M in funded debt, and flagged a "2022 vintage maturity wall" hitting in Q1–Q3 2026. While the SVB comparison is hyperbolic and the author has a financial interest in the stock declining, the data points raised merit attention.

---

## Conclusion: the math does not work without a fundamental shift

The core due diligence finding is a profound **mismatch between IQHQ's capital needs and the market's ability to generate leasing demand**. At current absorption rates, filling RaDD's ~1.45M SF of vacant lab/office space would require capturing an implausible share of a shrinking market. IQHQ's own internal projection — that it will capture 33% of new San Diego life science demand once legacy submarket supply is exhausted — implicitly acknowledges that meaningful occupancy is years away.

Bank OZK's extended loan maturity of ~August 2028 buys time, but the intervening period is marked by **IQHQ's escalating capital costs** (16.5% preferred from IIP, 13.5–14% PIK loans from Bluerock), continued negative absorption, and a sponsor whose other projects are being paused, sold at losses, or donated. The critical variable is whether IQHQ can pivot RaDD to attract traditional office tenants — professional services, financial firms, tech companies — at rents that support even partial debt coverage. Broker negotiations with 5–6 such tenants are underway but none have signed.

Five key risk factors demand ongoing monitoring: **leasing velocity** (any signed leases beyond JCVI), **IQHQ liquidity** (whether PIK interest accrual and IIP preferred equity tranches create a cash crunch), **Bank OZK's willingness to extend further** if the 2028 maturity arrives with minimal leasing progress, **NIH funding and tariff impacts** on biotech demand, and any sign of a **forced sale or restructuring** process. The $555M of funded loan against what may be a $500–800M distressed valuation represents meaningful loss-given-default risk for Bank OZK if the current trajectory does not change.
