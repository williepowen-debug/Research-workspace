# Metro-Level Exposures (Known Projects)  
We cross-referenced reported projects in key markets to gauge metro exposures: 

- **San Diego, CA:** ~**$1.12B** (RaDD + Bioterra).  OZK is the senior lender on IQHQ’s 1.7M SF RaDD life-science campus ($915M loan)【79†L285-L292】 and on Bioterra (316K SF life-science campus, $203M loan)【81†L119-L127】.  These are all life‐science R&D developments.  San Diego’s life-science vacancy is very high (∼28.6% as of late 2025【79†L274-L282】) and cap rates have jumped (from ~4.4% in 2022 to ~6.6% in 2025【79†L190-L199】).  

- **Chicago, IL:** ~**$0.80B** (Lincoln Yards + One Chicago).  OZK seized Sterling Bay’s vacant 320K SF life-science building at Lincoln Yards via deed-in-lieu after a $65M loan went bad【83†L48-L57】.  Separately, OZK (with partner Square Mile) provided a $735M financing package for the One Chicago Square mixed-use tower (about $475M senior loan from OZK plus $260M mezzanine)【88†L262-L270】.  One Chicago Square will have 735 apartments (77 condos) and retail; it is pre-leased and should benefit from strong residential/retail demand.  In contrast, Chicago’s new life-science development (Lincoln Yards) suffered from severe oversupply and has since been repositioned for office use【83†L48-L57】【83†L95-L99】.  

- **New York, NY:** ~**$0.80B** (50 West 66th Street).  OZK provided an $800M senior construction loan (with $167M mezzanine) to Extell’s 69-story condo tower at 50 W 66th【92†L44-L47】.  This is a luxury residential project (775 ft tall, 134 condos).  NYC’s high-end condo market has softened somewhat, but prime Upper West Side condos generally remain in demand.  (The broader Manhattan office market remains challenged, but this is a for-sale residential project.)  

- **Florida (Miami–FtL, Tampa, Orlando, etc.):** OZK’s top metro is **Miami–Fort Lauderdale–WPB ($4.465B)**【53†L915-L921】, and it also has large exposures in Tampa ($0.752B) and other FL metros.  These loans are heavily concentrated in **multifamily, hospitality, and related construction** (Florida is Ozark’s largest development finance market).  For example, Florida has seen a boom in apartment and condo projects.  Miami office vacancy is relatively low (around 11% in Q4’25)【96†L4-L7】, but South Florida multifamily rents have climbed strongly.  Recent slowing in condo sales and higher rates have started to cool the market, but Florida remains a core construction market for OZK.  

- **Georgia (Atlanta):** **$0.907B** in Atlanta–Sandy Springs【53†L915-L921】, mainly **office** and some commercial/retail.  Metro Atlanta’s office vacancy is about **25%** (Q4’25)【94†L104-L113】 – improving slowly but still elevated.  Buckhead/Perimeter saw recent absorption, but overall demand is moderate.  Multi-family and retail in Atlanta have been stronger, while Downtown/Perimeter offices are under more stress.  

- **Los Angeles, CA:** **$1.045B**【53†L915-L921】.  Exposure is mixed (development and construction loans for office, retail, multifamily, etc.).  LA office vacancy is roughly 20–25% (high sublease + low leasing volume in recent years).  Industrial and apartments in LA are tighter (low vacancy).  (OZK also holds a $265M life-science loan in San Diego – a Sterling Bay project – which it sold to Strategic Value Partners early 2026【83†L95-L99】; this shows its LA-SoCal life-science risk too.)  

- **Boston, MA:** **$1.385B**【53†L915-L921】.  Likely heavy in **life-science and office** loans (OZK has financed major lab projects in Cambridge and Somerville).  Greater Boston’s life-science vacancy has risen (around 9% by late 2025, per CBRE reports) and rents have softened modestly.  Office vacancy in Boston is also elevated (regional office markets ~20%+).  

- **Washington, DC (DMV):** **$0.975B**【53†L915-L921】.  Loans likely include **office and life-science** (e.g. Alexandria biotech properties).  Northern Virginia office vacancy is around 21–23%.  Federal sector support is helping DC office demand, but vacancies remain high (often ~20%).  

- **Philadelphia, PA:** **$0.636B**【53†L915-L921】.  Exposure mainly in **office and residential**.  Philadelphia office vacancy is roughly mid-teens (perhaps ~15–18%), with pockets of strength in Center City and Suburban markets.  Multi-family is strong citywide, while older office projects have struggled.  

_(In general, each metro’s exposure can be **~5–20% of OZK’s RESG book** – e.g. Miami alone was ~12% of RESG as of ’25 – though OZK reports such figures only via presentations.)_  

# FDIC Supervisory Districts  
OZK’s home charter is in Little Rock, AR (Federal Reserve Dallas Fed, FDIC Dallas region)【64†L127-L134】.  In contrast, most of its big loan markets fall under other FDIC regional offices: 

- **San Diego & San Francisco, CA** – FDIC **San Francisco** Region (Fed SF District)  
- **Los Angeles, CA** – FDIC **San Francisco** Region  
- **Chicago, IL** – FDIC **Chicago** Region  
- **New York & Philadelphia (NY/NJ/PA)** – FDIC **New York** Region (Boston FDIC also under NY Region)  
- **Boston, MA** – FDIC **Boston**/New York Region  
- **Washington DC** – FDIC **Atlanta** or **New York** Region (territory overlaps)  
- **Miami, Tampa, Orlando (FL)** – FDIC **Atlanta** Region (also covers GA/FL)【35†L19-L21】  
- **Atlanta, GA** – FDIC **Atlanta** Region  

Thus, OZK’s regulatory reports (Dallas FDIC) do not align with where most loans are booked (SF, Chicago, NY, Atlanta FDIC regions). This geographic mismatch means examiners in multiple regions scrutinize OZK’s RESG credit risks outside its home regulator.
