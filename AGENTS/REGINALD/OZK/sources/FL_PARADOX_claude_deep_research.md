# Bank OZK's Florida condo book: three stress vectors converging

**Bank OZK's $2.47 billion Miami luxury condo construction portfolio faces a convergence of risks that, while structurally better protected than the 2008 cycle, merit serious credit scrutiny.** The bank's Q4 2025 net charge-off rate of **1.18%** has breached its allowance for credit losses ratio of **1.16%** for the first time since 2010 — the loss buffer is mathematically gone. Miami luxury condo inventory has ballooned to **23 months of supply**, UBS ranks Miami **#1 globally for bubble risk** (exceeding its own 2006 peak score), and the January 2026 mandatory reserve funding deadline under SB 4-D is triggering special assessments of $50,000–$200,000+ per unit across nearly 900,000 older Florida condo units. The three risk vectors examined here — pre-sale walkaway risk, post-Surfside regulatory impact, and the insurance cost crisis — each carry distinct transmission mechanisms to OZK's loan book, and their interaction creates compound tail risk that the bank's stated **45.9% weighted average LTC** and **39.1% LTV** may not fully cushion.

---

## The deposit structure wall that separates this cycle from 2008

The single most important structural mitigant in OZK's Miami condo book is the post-GFC transformation of deposit requirements. Today's luxury pre-construction buyers commit **30–50% of the purchase price** in staged, largely non-refundable deposits before closing — a fundamental change from the 10–20% deposits that prevailed before 2008. The typical structure layers a 10–20% reservation deposit, additional payments at contract execution and construction milestones (groundbreaking, mid-rise, top-off), with the remaining 50–70% due at temporary certificate of occupancy. Florida Statute §718.202 mandates the first 10% be held in independent escrow; amounts exceeding 10% go into a separate escrow account but may contractually be released for "actual construction and development."

This deposit architecture serves as a massive walkaway deterrent. A buyer in the Waldorf Astoria Miami who contracted at $5 million has $1.5–$2.5 million at risk before closing. Even in a 20% price decline, walking away destroys more capital than closing. During the GFC, when deposits were 10–20%, the math frequently inverted — buyers rationally walked when prices fell 30–40%, losing a $50,000–$100,000 deposit to avoid a $200,000+ loss at closing.

However, legal walkaway mechanisms remain intact regardless of deposit size. **Florida's Condominium Act (§718.503) grants buyers an irrevocable 15-day rescission right** that cannot be waived. More critically for stress analysis, any "material alteration or modification adverse to the buyer" in developer disclosures reopens a 15-day rescission window. During the GFC, sophisticated buyers' attorneys systematically mined developer amendments for material alteration arguments. The Fourth District Court of Appeal set a broad standard: a change is material if "a reasonable buyer would find it so significant that it would alter the buyer's decision." Budget changes, amenity modifications, timeline delays, and specification revisions all potentially qualify. The *Double AA v. Swire* decision further established that even technical escrow violations — commingling the 10% and excess deposits in one account — render contracts voidable with full refund plus interest.

The GFC precedent is instructive but not perfectly analogous. When Miami's condo market collapsed, **22,000 units sat unsold** and inventory reached **52 months of supply**. Building-level data reveals staggering non-closing rates: Onyx on the Bay saw only **45% of units close**; Wind by Neo managed roughly **11%**; Icon Brickell was **4.9% occupied** by 2009. Downtown prices fell **41.8%**. Today's 23-month inventory is alarming but not yet catastrophic. The buyer profile has shifted decisively — **78–83% of transactions above $2,000/SF are all-cash**, versus the speculative flippers who dominated pre-2008 sales. OZK management's claim that 11 of 14 Miami condo loans have presales covering full loan repayment provides meaningful but unverifiable comfort.

The current cancellation evidence is ambiguous. Nationally, Redfin reported a **16.3% home-purchase contract cancellation rate** in December 2025 — the highest December on record — with Tampa at 19.4% and Jacksonville at 20.6%. No mass pre-sale cancellations have surfaced at OZK's named projects. Baccarat Residences remains **95%+ pre-sold**, and the Waldorf Astoria Miami is **90%+ sold** with construction at the 50th floor. But the transmission mechanism from pre-sale failure to loan loss is delayed: construction loans repay from closing proceeds, typically 2–4 years after pre-sale contracts are signed. The true test comes at delivery, not during construction.

## SB 4-D creates a structural moat for new construction — and a direct credit problem at Biscayne 21

Florida's post-Surfside legislation has created a bifurcated condo market that simultaneously benefits and threatens OZK's portfolio. The Structural Integrity Reserve Study requirements, which became mandatory for reserve funding on **January 1, 2026**, force condo associations to fully fund reserves for eight structural components — roof, load-bearing walls, fire protection, plumbing, electrical, waterproofing, windows/doors, and any item exceeding $25,000 that impacts structural integrity. The era of owners voting to waive reserves is over.

The financial impact on older buildings is severe and measurable. Special assessments of **$50,000 to $200,000+ per unit** are being levied across Florida. At Williams Island in Aventura, one couple faces a **$224,000 individual assessment** from an $11.4 million building-wide obligation. Monthly HOA fees have jumped from $800 to $2,000+ for many older buildings — a **60% increase over five years**. With **nearly 900,000 condo units** in buildings over 30 years old subject to these mandates, and roughly **90% of active listings** in buildings 30+ years old, the law is creating a structural flight to quality.

**For OZK's new construction portfolio, SB 4-D is unambiguously positive.** New buildings are exempt from milestone inspections for 25–30 years. Their initial SIRS will show full useful life across all components and minimal reserve funding needs. The sales data confirms the bifurcation: condos in buildings under 25 years old saw sales **increase 2%** in 2025, while buildings over 25 years saw sales **decline 6%**. Buyers who can afford the $100,000+ special assessments on older units "can just as well afford a newer unit in a building with better amenities," as Bilzin Sumberg partner Joseph Hernandez observed. This regulatory arbitrage directly supports absorption for projects like the Waldorf Astoria, Baccarat, and Four Seasons Coconut Grove.

However, OZK holds a **$105 million senior mortgage** on what may be the single most visible casualty of the post-Surfside legal environment: **Biscayne 21 (2121 N Bayshore Drive, Edgewater)**. Two Roads Development bulk-purchased this 192-unit tower for conversion to Edition Residences, financed with OZK's senior mortgage plus $45 million in mezzanine debt. In October 2025, the Florida Supreme Court declined review, establishing precedent that developers **cannot retroactively lower termination thresholds** from unanimous consent requirements. A January 2026 court order requires Two Roads to restore the building to habitable condition at significant cost — while carrying mounting interest on $150 million in total debt with no clear path to its luxury redevelopment plan. BH Group's CEO noted developers will "become more cautious after this decision." The ruling has effectively frozen condo termination and redevelopment activity statewide, creating what Hernandez calls a "redevelopment stalemate."

The 2025 Florida legislature provided some relief through HB 913 — raising the reserve threshold from $10,000 to $25,000, allowing alternative funding through loans and credit lines, and permitting a two-year pause on contributions during active repairs. SB 328 delayed mandatory enforcement by one year to January 2026. But the core requirements remain intact, and no further delays appear pending. TD Economics forecasts the Florida condo downcycle will last approximately two years from late 2024, with potential recovery beginning by **late 2026** if mortgage rates decline.

## Insurance premiums have stabilized but cumulative damage persists

The Florida property insurance crisis has shifted from acute to chronic. After condo association master policies **doubled** between June 2022 and June 2024, 2025 brought the first meaningful stabilization: unit-owner premiums increased just **1.3%** in the first nine months — the lowest rate since tracking began. Citizens Property Insurance has successfully depopulated from a peak of **1.42 million policies** to roughly **385,000**, a 73% decrease. Seventeen new insurers have entered Florida, and the private market recorded its first collective underwriting profit since 2016 with a combined ratio of 93.1%.

But the cumulative burden remains enormous. A Miami-Dade luxury high-rise condo owner faces total insurance costs of **$6,800–$7,800 per year minimum** — combining the association's master policy allocation (~$4,524/year per unit) with individual HO-6 coverage (~$2,280–$2,570/year), before flood insurance. Miami condo insurance runs **4.7–5.2x the national average**. These costs flow directly into HOA fees, which for Miami-Dade high-rises now exceed **$1,900/month** median — an increase of roughly $500/month from the prior year.

**New construction enjoys a significant insurance advantage**, with premiums **20–40% lower** than comparable older buildings. Structures built to the post-2002 Florida Building Code receive automatic wind mitigation credits, and the FBC roof covering credit can reduce windstorm premiums by up to 86% in extreme cases. KW Property Management reports newer associations "have seen either their property insurance staying the same or going down slightly," while 1980s-era buildings face 15–20% annual increases. This insurance differential reinforces the SB 4-D competitive advantage for OZK's new construction projects.

The insurance-to-closing transmission mechanism is real but concentrated in the mid-market. Redfin agent Aditi Jain documented clients walking away from deals because they "couldn't secure financing" in buildings with inadequate insurance. Banks refuse to issue mortgages for buildings lacking compliant coverage, creating "all-cash only" buildings with severely restricted buyer pools. For OZK's ultra-luxury portfolio, where **70%+ of transactions are all-cash**, insurance-driven financing denials are less relevant. The greater risk is the cumulative impact on total cost of ownership — monthly carrying costs of $1,900+ HOA plus insurance plus reserves create buyer hesitation even among wealthy purchasers, particularly at the **$3–10 million "mid-tier luxury" segment** where inventory is accumulating fastest.

The reinsurance backdrop has improved materially. After surging ~80% in 2022–2023, Florida carriers secured an average **10.7% risk-adjusted reduction** at June 2025 renewals. The quiet 2025 hurricane season reinforced favorable conditions. Citizens is projecting a **2.6% average rate cut** for personal lines starting June 2026 — a stunning reversal from the 91% indicated increase in early 2022. However, a single active hurricane season could reverse these gains instantly. Catastrophe models now predict **10–40% higher losses** for Florida than prior models, and an Andrew-equivalent hurricane could cause losses exceeding **$100 billion**.

## OZK's bank-level metrics signal eroding cushions

The bank-level credit picture has deteriorated faster than the Florida condo portfolio specifically. Q4 2025 net charge-offs of **$98.3 million** represented an **8x increase** from Q4 2024's $12.4 million. The NPL ratio tripled during 2025 from 0.44% to **1.06%**. Full-year charge-offs reached $160 million versus $57.4 million in 2024. Management attributed the acceleration to CRE customers "struggling through the later stages of a multi-year CRE cycle" — particularly in life sciences and office, not residential condo.

The critical metric that short-seller Temple8 Capital has flagged: for the first time since 2010, OZK's annualized Q4 net charge-off rate (**1.18%**) has **exceeded its allowance for credit losses ratio (1.16%)**. The loss absorption buffer is mathematically exhausted. Temple8 argues the bank is under-provisioned by roughly 40% relative to regional peers: Huntington holds a 1.83% ACL ratio against 0.24% NCOs; Truist holds 1.55% against 0.57%; OZK holds 1.16% against 1.18%. The bank's CRE concentration at **~455% of tangible equity** — 1.5x the 300% regulatory scrutiny threshold — amplifies the provisioning concern.

OZK's Miami condo book has not yet been tested. Current distress is concentrated in life sciences (San Diego), office, and a Chicago land deal. The Florida projects are performing: Waldorf Astoria at the 50th floor and rising one story every 10 days; Ritz-Carlton Pompano **90%+ sold**; Baccarat **95%+ pre-sold** with delivery pushed to 2028. PMG's announcement of a new 90-story Delano tower adjacent to the Waldorf signals developer confidence. No construction delays, cost overruns, or financial distress have been reported for any named OZK Florida project.

But the **2022 vintage maturity wall** looms. OZK originated $13.8 billion in construction loans in 2022 at the peak of the rate cycle. These 3–4 year construction loans are hitting hard maturities in H1 2026. Management has already been actively extending — 49 loan extensions in Q4 2025 alone, collecting $56.7 million in reserve deposits and $7.6 million in modification fees. The bank capped new loan size at **$500 million** in October 2024, launched a loan syndication desk, and is actively reducing RESG concentration from a peak of 70% to **54.4%** at year-end 2025 (targeting 50%). These are prudent defensive moves, but they also signal management's own recognition of concentration risk. Citigroup analyst Benjamin Gerlinger, who maintains a Sell rating with a $40 target, has flagged that "200 loans make up more than 50% of the loan book" — extraordinary single-name concentration.

## The compound risk scenario and what the numbers don't capture

The three stress vectors examined here interact in ways that simple LTV and LTC metrics cannot capture. Consider the compound scenario: insurance costs raise total carrying costs → SB 4-D reserve requirements further increase monthly obligations → rising inventory and carrying costs suppress resale prices → appraisals decline → pre-sale buyers facing material changes in projected HOA budgets explore legal rescission options → a subset of walkaways forces developers to re-sell into a weakened market → closing proceeds fall short of construction loan payoff → OZK takes losses against an already-depleted loss reserve.

The probability of this cascade hitting OZK's best-protected loans — the Waldorf Astoria at 90%+ presold with $668 million exposure, or Baccarat at 95%+ presold — appears low. These projects have elite sponsors (PMG, Related Group), deep deposit pools, and predominantly cash buyers. The vulnerability lies in the tail: the 3 of 14 Miami loans where presales do **not** cover full loan repayment, the $475 million West Palm Beach project originated in 2025 whose presale coverage is undisclosed, and the $105 million Biscayne 21 loan where legal developments have materially impaired the collateral strategy.

Several facts should weigh on any credit assessment:

- OZK originated **26.5% of all construction debt** fueling Miami's condo market since 2013 — the bank's fortunes are inextricable from this market's health
- UBS ranks Miami **#1 globally for bubble risk** with a score of 1.73, exceeding the 2006 peak
- Luxury condo inventory at 23 months and rising, with **411 units at $6M+ entering the market** versus total sales since 2020
- Foreign buyers now account for **49% of new construction sales** ($4.4 billion, up 42% YoY), providing critical demand support but also introducing geopolitical and currency risk — 86% from Latin America, where political instability both drives capital flight and creates counterparty uncertainty
- The FinCEN Residential Real Estate Rule, effective **March 1, 2026**, will require beneficial ownership reporting on non-financed transfers to legal entities, potentially adding friction to the all-cash foreign buyer pipeline that sustains ultra-luxury absorption

## Conclusion

OZK's Florida luxury condo portfolio is **better structured than any comparable book in 2008** — higher deposits, more cash buyers, lower leverage, stronger sponsors. Management's stated metrics (45.9% LTC, 39.1% LTV, 11 of 14 loans with presale coverage) would, if accurate, provide substantial loss absorption even in a significant downturn. The SB 4-D regulatory framework and insurance cost dynamics actually create a competitive moat for new construction that supports demand for OZK-financed projects.

The critical vulnerability is not the condo portfolio in isolation but its interaction with bank-level credit deterioration. With the ACL ratio already breached by the NCO rate, a CRE concentration at 455% of tangible equity, and a 2022-vintage maturity wall approaching, OZK has minimal margin for error. Even a modest increase in condo loan stress — a single $300–500 million loan moving to non-accrual — would have outsized impact on a bank whose loss reserves are already strained by life sciences and office losses. The Biscayne 21 situation ($105M) is an early warning of how legal and regulatory developments can transform performing collateral into impaired assets. The ultimate test arrives when the 2024–2025 vintage condo projects reach closing in 2027–2028, and today's 90%+ presale rates must convert to actual closings in a market where UBS says prices have decoupled from fundamentals.
