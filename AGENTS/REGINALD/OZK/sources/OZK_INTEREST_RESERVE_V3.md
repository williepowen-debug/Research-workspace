# Bank OZK: Interest Reserve Depletion Analysis

## Executive Summary

Every 2021–2022 vintage construction loan on Bank OZK's books has, by mathematical certainty, already exhausted its *original* interest reserve — even loans underwritten with the most generous 24-month buffers. The $108.6M in quarterly capitalized interest OZK reported in Q4 2025 represents roughly **66% of what a fully-reserving book would generate**, implying that approximately **34% of the construction book is no longer capitalizing through original reserves** — those loans are either cash-paying, nonaccrual, or have been extended with sponsor-deposited fresh reserves. The 2022 vintage ($13.8B peak) is simultaneously hitting its hard 36–42 month loan maturities in Q1–Q3 2026, creating a convergence of reserve depletion and maturity pressure that defines the bank's primary credit risk through this year.

***

## Q1: Reserve Depletion Timeline — Mid-2022 Origination

A loan originated in **July 2022** with an 18-month interest reserve had those reserves exhausted by **January 2024** — now more than **27 months ago**. That is not a future risk. It already happened.[^1][^2]

| Reserve Duration | Depletion Date | Status as of March 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months | July 2023 | Depleted ~33 months ago |
| 18 months | **January 2024** | **Depleted ~27 months ago** |
| 24 months | July 2024 | Depleted ~21 months ago |

The loan's maturity tells a similarly uncomfortable story: a 36-month term matures in **July 2025** (already past), and a 42-month term matured **January 2026**. As of March 2026, even the longest standard construction terms on mid-2022 originations have expired. Any loan from this vintage still outstanding has been formally extended — and the question is whether the extension came with adequate new reserves.[^3]

This is not theoretical for OZK. The bank's 2022 vintage constituted **$13.8 billion** in construction originations, making it the largest U.S. construction lender that year. Standard construction terms of 36–42 months mean the full vintage of 2022 loans began hitting hard maturity deadlines in **Q1 through Q3 of 2026**.[^3]

### The Rate Problem Compounds the Timing Problem

Loans originated in 2021–2022 were underwritten when SOFR was near zero. The interest reserve was sized at origination assuming a specific rate — typically derived from the then-current floating rate plus spread. As SOFR surged from ~0% to over 5% in 2022–2023, a reserve budgeted for, say, 4.5% annualized interest suddenly needed to service 8.5%+ rates. A 12-month reserve underwritten at 4.5% covered approximately half the months it needed to at 8.5%+. This compression accelerated depletion by an estimated 30–50% beyond the original schedule for many 2021–2022 originations.[^4]

***

## Q2: What Percentage of the Book Likely Has Depleted Reserves?

The blunt answer: **essentially 100% of the original reserves** from 2021–2022 vintage loans have been exhausted by mathematical necessity. The relevant question is therefore not *whether* reserves depleted, but *what happened after depletion* — and the evidence suggests a three-way split.[^2][^1]

### The Three-Way Split of the $7.0B Reserve Book

**Tier 1: Extended with Sponsor-Funded Fresh Reserves (~35–45% of book)**

OZK disclosed that over 14 quarters (approximately Q2 2022 through Q4 2025), sponsors deposited **$866 million in new reserve funds** as part of loan extensions and modifications. The bank also collected **$76 million in modification fees** in Q4 2025 alone from 49 loan extensions. When a loan is extended at OZK, the bank's stated policy requires sponsors to fund sufficient new reserves to cover the extended period — hence the "no situations where reserves were advanced outside contractual terms" language in the 2024 Annual Report. These loans legitimately show as "on interest reserves" in OZK's disclosure and are currently capitalizing interest. However, at $866M deposited over 14 quarters (~$62M/quarter), new reserve inflows are materially less than the $108.6M/quarter being consumed — meaning the aggregate reserve pool is still net-declining.[^5][^6]

**Tier 2: Cash-Pay Loans (~20–30% of book)**

Some loans where original reserves depleted have sponsors generating enough external cash flow (from pre-leasing, equity injections, or completed-but-not-stabilized assets) to service interest out-of-pocket. These would not show as capitalizing reserves. This segment is operationally performing but not technically "on reserve."

**Tier 3: Nonaccrual / Stressed Loans (~5–10% of book)**

When reserves deplete on a loan where the sponsor cannot cash-pay, and the bank determines repayment is in doubt, the loan moves to nonaccrual — and capitalization stops entirely. OZK's nonaccrual balance rose from $66.7M at year-end 2023 to $131.5M at year-end 2024, with further deterioration expected in 2025.[^6]

### The Capitalization Rate Tells the Story

| Year | Book Size | Annual Capitalized | As % of Book | Expected at 8.5% | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | ~$10B | $332M | 3.3% | 8.5% | 39% |
| 2023 | ~$9.5B | $391M | 4.1% | 8.5% | 48% |
| 2024 | $8.6B | $524M[^6] | 6.1% | 8.5% | 72% |
| 2025 (Q4 ann.) | $7.8B | ~$434M | 5.6% | 8.5% | 66% |

The 2022 figure looks low because loans were ramping draws — early in construction, the outstanding balance (and therefore interest accruing) is smaller. By 2024, loans are fully or near-fully drawn and the capitalization rate climbed. The 2025 *decline* from 72% to 66% coverage is the signal — as loans mature off, go nonaccrual, or shift to cash-pay, the reserve capitalization rate drops even on a shrinking book.

**Bottom line: approximately 34% of the $7.0B book's interest is not being capitalized via reserves as of Q4 2025.** That share will grow as the 2022 maturity wall accelerates through 2026.

## Q3: Mechanics When Reserves Deplete on an Unstabilized Loan

The step-by-step mechanics follow a specific regulatory and contractual path:[^7][^8][^1]

### Step 1: Reserve Account Reaches Zero
The interest reserve escrow balance hits zero. No automatic default — the loan is technically structured to transition to cash-pay at this point.

### Step 2: Lender Issues Cash-Pay Demand
The bank notifies the borrower that interest is now due in cash, typically on the next payment date. The loan remains technically **current** (no past-due event has occurred yet).[^1]

### Step 3: Borrower Cannot Pay → 30/60/90-Day Clock Starts
If the project has not stabilized (no permanent financing available, insufficient operating cash flow), the sponsor faces a binary choice: inject equity to service debt or miss the payment. When a payment is missed, the 90-day clock toward technical delinquency begins.

### Step 4: Regulatory Nonaccrual vs. Economic Reality
Critically, the Federal Reserve's SR 23-5 guidance (June 2023) makes clear that a loan can and **should** be adversely classified *even while technically current* if it is masking repayment risk. The exact language: *"If the development project stalls and management fails to evaluate the collectability of the loan, interest income could continue to be recognized from the interest reserve and capitalized into the loan balance, even though the project is not generating sufficient cash flows to repay the loan... the loan will be contractually current due to the interest payments being funded from the reserve, but the repayment of principal may be in jeopardy."*[^8]

OZK's own policy matches regulatory guidance: nonaccrual is triggered when the bank determines the borrower "may be unable to meet payments as they become due," or when a loan is 90+ days past due — and interest previously accrued is **reversed against income**.[^6]

### Step 5: Nonaccrual → Income Statement Impact
Once nonaccrual is declared:
- All previously accrued but uncollected interest is reversed from income[^6]
- No further interest income is recognized until cash is actually received
- The loan is classified Substandard or worse
- Any excess of loan balance over collateral fair value must be reserved or charged off[^7]
- Return to accrual requires **at least six months of sustained cash payments**[^6]

### Step 6: The Loss Realization Sequence
The practical sequence for an OZK-style construction loan on an unstabilized asset: reserve depletion → cash-pay demand → missed payment → substandard classification → appraisal update to "as-is" value (dropping from "as-stabilized") → collateral shortfall → provision → charge-off. The Boston office loan OZK charged off in Q4 2025 for $72.4M followed exactly this sequence — the shift from "as-stabilized" to "as-is" appraisal methodology alone drove the loss recognition.[^9][^3]

### The Life Sciences Amplifier

OZK carries approximately **$3.2 billion in life sciences loans** within RESG. San Diego's Sorrento Mesa submarket — where OZK has concentrated exposure — had vacancy rates exceeding **35%** in 2025. The $915M IQHQ loan for the Research and Development District in downtown San Diego had funded approximately $555M, is substantially complete, and has **signed no major tenants**, with maturity in August 2026. If this loan fails to refinance, it represents a loss scenario Professor Rebel Cole estimates could involve impairment of $55M–$300M against the remaining ACL of ~$632M.[^3]

## Q4: Is $108.6M/Quarter in Capitalized Interest Consistent with Healthy Reserves?

The short answer: **No — it is consistent with a book where reserves are materially thinning and the reserve capitalization rate is declining faster than the book itself.**

### The Key Data Points

OZK disclosed annual interest income recognized from reserve advances and total reserves advanced:[^6]

| Year | Annual Capitalized | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $332M | — |
| 2023 | $391M | +17.8% |
| 2024 | $524M[^6] | +34.0% |
| 2025 (Q4 annualized) | ~$434M | **-17.1%** |

The 2022–2024 trend was increasing capitalization despite a shrinking book — loans were drawing down fully and all accruing interest was reserve-funded. The 2025 reversal is significant: the book shrank 9% (from $8.6B to $7.8B) but capitalization dropped 17%. That differential — capitalization falling at roughly **2x the rate of the book** — means an accelerating share of loans are not generating reserve advances.

### The Net Burn Rate

At $108.6M/quarter consumed versus ~$62M/quarter in new sponsor reserve deposits ($866M over 14 quarters), the aggregate reserve pool is experiencing **net quarterly depletion of approximately $47M** — roughly $187M per year. Unless new originations (with fresh reserves) materially increase, or payoffs accelerate (removing both the reserve draw and the underlying loan), the total reserve pool balance will continue declining.[^5]

### What $108.6M/Quarter Is NOT

It is **not** evidence of a reserve surplus. A book of $7.0B at 8.5% generates ~$149M in quarterly interest. The fact that only $108.6M is capitalizing (73% of potential) means:

- **~$40M/quarter in interest** is being serviced by cash-paying sponsors (healthy outcome) **or**
- **~$40M/quarter in interest** is simply not being collected on nonaccrual/stressed loans (unhealthy outcome)

OZK does not distinguish between these in public disclosures, but the nonaccrual trend (rising from $66.7M to $131.5M in 2024 alone) suggests the unhealthy share is growing.[^6]

***

## The 2026 Maturity Wall: Convergence Risk

The most actionable concern is the simultaneous convergence of three forces in 2026:[^3]

1. **Reserve depletion**: Essentially all original 2021–2022 reserves are gone; only sponsor-funded extensions or new reserves remain
2. **Maturity deadlines**: The $13.8B 2022 vintage hits contractual 36–42 month terms in Q1–Q3 2026 — extensions require sponsor capital commitment
3. **Frozen refinancing markets**: Life sciences and office permanent lenders remain largely inactive; sponsors cannot exit via refinance

OZK management acknowledged in the Q4 2025 call that *"we are actively tracking more than $2B of loans entering the final year of their terms that are either completely or substantially unleased"*. These loans cannot pay off (no permanent financing), cannot easily cash-pay (no operating income), and must either receive fresh sponsor equity injections or face classification.[^10]

The Q4 2025 charge-off rate of **1.18% annualized** — an eightfold increase from Q4 2024's 0.16% — and the ACL declining for the first time this cycle (from ~$680M to $632M) suggests the reserve inversion is no longer theoretical. The shock absorber is compressing. The bank's coverage ratio of ACL to net charge-offs has fallen below 1.0x, compared to a regional banking norm of 4.5–5.0x.[^3]

| Question | Finding |
|---|---|
| When did 18-month reserve deplete on mid-2022 origination? | **January 2024** — 27 months ago |
| What % of book has depleted original reserves? | **~100%** of 2021-2022 vintage original reserves are gone |
| What % is no longer capitalizing via reserves? | **~34%** of the $7B book (by rate analysis) |
| Is $108.6M/quarter consistent with healthy reserves? | **No** — it implies 66% coverage of full-book interest, declining from 72% in 2024 |
| What happens mechanically at depletion + no stabilization? | Cash-pay demand → missed payment → 90-day clock → nonaccrual → as-is appraisal → charge-off |
| Primary 2026 risk catalyst? | $2B+ of loans in final-year terms, substantially unleased, reserve-depleted, in frozen refinancing markets |
