# Bank OZK's 590 loan modifications reveal a widening classification gap

Bank OZK's own voluntary disclosures expose a striking disconnect at the heart of its credit quality narrative: over 14 quarters through Q4 2025, the bank executed **590 modifications or extensions** on its Real Estate Specialties Group (RESG) construction loans — yet formal ASC 326 disclosures classified only **10 loans ($96.4 million)** as modifications to borrowers experiencing financial difficulty in H1 2025, and Call Report data shows just **$1.525 million** in restructured loans. This means roughly 98% of RESG modifications avoided the regulatory classification that would trigger enhanced disclosure requirements, including re-default rate reporting the bank has never provided. The Q4 2025 charge-off spike to **$98.3 million** (annualized 1.18%, an 8x year-over-year increase and 15-year high) — combined with a reserve ratio that has now fallen below the charge-off run rate — suggests the extend-and-pretend strategy is approaching its structural limits just as a **$13.8 billion maturity wall** from 2022-vintage loans hits in H1 2026.

## The modification machine: 590 extensions on ~300 loans

Bank OZK is the largest construction lender in the United States, with its RESG portfolio peaking at $18.6 billion in funded balances (62% of total loans at Q4 2024, declining to 54.4% by Q4 2025). Since the Federal Reserve began raising rates in mid-2022, the bank has systematically extended or modified nearly its entire RESG book. CEO George Gleason describes a "pay to stay, don't pay, don't stay" framework: sponsors must contribute additional equity, replenish reserves, and pay modification fees to earn extensions. Through Q4 2025, sponsors contributed a cumulative **$1.3 billion** in additional equity — $866 million in reserve deposits and $429 million in unscheduled paydowns. Floor rates were enhanced on **200 loans** and spreads on 11, with only one floor reduction and five spread reductions granted across the entire period.

Management frames these modifications as evidence of portfolio strength. The bank argues it *improves* its position with each extension rather than granting concessions. However, the sheer scale tells a different story: 590 modifications on a portfolio of approximately 290–320 loans means the average RESG loan has been modified roughly twice. The frequency is accelerating — 49 extensions in Q4 2025 alone generated $56.7 million in reserve deposits and $7.6 million in fees.

The formal disclosures tell a radically different story. Bank OZK's Q2 2025 10-Q (filed with the FDIC, not the SEC, as the bank is state-chartered) disclosed only 10 modifications to borrowers experiencing financial difficulty in H1 2025, totaling $96.4 million. The largest was a **$66.1 million construction/land development loan** that received both a term extension and a **261 basis point rate reduction** — bringing its spread down to SOFR + 300 bps. This rate concession directly contradicts management's repeated claim of granting "no concessions." The remaining modifications included a $28.5 million C&I term extension and seven small residential loans. For H1 2024, modifications to borrowers in financial difficulty were described as "not material."

## How OZK avoids the "financial difficulty" classification

Under ASU 2022-02, which eliminated the old TDR designation effective January 2023, banks must disclose modifications to borrowers experiencing financial difficulty — defined as situations where "it is probable the debtor will default on the original loan if the modification is not granted." Bank OZK's classification strategy rests on three pillars. First, sponsors are contributing *more* capital at each extension, which the bank argues demonstrates financial capacity rather than distress. Second, most modifications involve enhanced terms (higher floor rates, fees) rather than concessions. Third, borrowers remain current on interest payments throughout the modification process.

This interpretation is aggressive but not without basis. ASC 326 does not provide a bright-line test for financial difficulty, and reasonable judgment is required. However, the counterargument is substantial: if a construction project reaches its original maturity date without stabilizing, requiring an extension, this is *by definition* a failure of the original business plan. The granting of additional time — regardless of fees collected — is itself a concession because the bank could demand repayment at maturity. The additional equity contributions may signal financial stress (sponsors throwing good money after bad) rather than financial health. Most tellingly, when sponsors eventually stop supporting their loans, the losses are severe and sudden — as Q4 2025 demonstrated.

The **Call Report discrepancy** is equally striking. Schedule RC-C Memoranda shows just $1.525 million in restructured loans in compliance with modified terms for Bank OZK — a figure that is functionally zero relative to the $32 billion loan portfolio and bears no relationship to the 590 modifications disclosed in management commentary.

## Q4 2025 marked an inflection point for credit deterioration

The trajectory of Bank OZK's credit metrics through 2024–2025 reveals accelerating deterioration punctuated by bursts of resolution activity:

| Quarter | NPL Ratio | NCOs (Annualized) | ACL ($M) | ACL Ratio | Key Events |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|----------|-----------|------------|
| Q4 2023 | 0.23% | 0.06% | $501 | ~1.00% | Baseline |
| Q2 2024 | 0.30% | 0.14% | $574 | ~1.07% | Citi downgrade to Sell |
| Q3 2024 | 0.60% | 0.36% | $595 | ~1.19% | Sterling Bay $20.8M write-down |
| Q4 2024 | 0.44% | 0.16% | $619 | 1.26% | Lincoln Yards $16.9M additional charge-off |
| Q2 2025 | 0.18% | 0.10% | $666 | ~1.28% | Foreclosed assets surge to $160M |
| Q3 2025 | 0.46% | 0.41% | **$680** | 1.34% | **ACL peak** |
| Q4 2025 | **1.06%** | **1.18%** | **$632** | ~1.16% | Boston $72.4M charge-off; **reserve inversion** |

The NPL ratio's extreme volatility — swinging from 0.60% to 0.18% to 1.06% within five quarters — is itself a forensic signal. The sharp drop to 0.18% in Q2 2025 coincided with **$101 million in foreclosure transfers** (versus $14 million in H1 2024), meaning nonperforming loans were being resolved not through repayment or recovery but by moving them off the loan book and onto the foreclosed assets line. Foreclosed assets surged from $69 million to $160 million in the first half of 2025.

The Q4 2025 charge-off spike was driven by specific credit failures: a **$72.4 million** write-down on a Boston office development (Cambridge Courthouse, 422,000 SF, fully vacant, equity partners unwilling to continue without a lease), a **$9 million** charge-off on the Chicago Lincoln Yards life science project (modified six times since 2019), and a **$5.7 million** write-down on a Santa Monica creative office (15% leased, sponsor stopped paying). Full-year 2025 net charge-offs reached **$160 million**, nearly triple the $57.4 million in 2024.

## The reserve cushion has been breached

The most concerning quantitative finding is what analysts have termed "reserve inversion." Bank OZK's allowance for credit losses peaked at approximately **$680 million** in Q3 2025 after more than doubling from $300 million over 13 quarters. In Q4 2025, the ACL *declined* for the first time this cycle — falling roughly $48 million to **$632 million** — as charge-offs consumed reserves faster than provisioning could rebuild them. The annualized Q4 charge-off rate of **1.18%** now exceeds the ACL-to-loans ratio of approximately **1.16%**, meaning the bank has less than one year of loss absorption capacity at the current run rate.

Peer comparisons sharpen the concern. Huntington Bancshares maintains an ACL ratio of 1.83% against a charge-off rate of 0.24% (7.5x coverage). Truist holds 1.55% against 0.57% (2.7x coverage). Bank OZK's coverage ratio has fallen below 1.0x — the lowest among comparable institutions. Management stated on the Q4 2025 earnings call that further ACL builds are "probably not my base case" but would be implemented "if warranted," suggesting confidence that credit losses will decelerate. Piper Sandler's Steven Scouten characterized Q4's losses as "a blip, as opposed to an emerging systemic issue."

## CRE concentration and the 2022 maturity wall loom large

Bank OZK's CRE concentration remains at approximately **455% of tangible common equity** — well above the 300% regulatory threshold that triggers enhanced supervisory scrutiny. Construction and development lending alone stands at roughly **197% of TCE**. KBRA assigned a Negative outlook citing these concentrations, and Moody's affirmed its A3 deposit rating with a Negative outlook (later revised to Stable) on similar grounds.

The critical near-term risk is the 2022-vintage maturity wall. Bank OZK originated approximately **$13.8 billion** in construction loans during 2022, with standard 36–42 month terms pointing to hard maturities in **Q1 through Q3 2026**. These loans must either demonstrate project stabilization (sufficient leasing, permanent financing takeout) or be extended, restructured, or classified as nonperforming. Given that 590 modifications have already occurred and many projects remain unstabilized — particularly in life sciences, where San Diego's Sorrento Mesa submarket has seen vacancy rates climb to 35% — additional extensions appear inevitable.

The life sciences portfolio represents an acute concentration risk. Bank OZK holds approximately **$3.2 billion** in life science construction loans, roughly 10% of the RESG book. The flagship exposure is IQHQ's RaDD campus in San Diego — a **$915 million** commitment ($555 million funded) for a 1.7 million-square-foot development that has signed essentially no major tenants despite being under construction for over four years. Sponsors have contributed $187 million in additional capital, and the loan was granted a two-year extension to approximately 2028. Six registered investment funds with $707 million invested in IQHQ marked down their positions in H2 2025, and Aimco took a $47 million impairment on its $50 million IQHQ investment. The bank sold its $265 million Pacific Center loan (another San Diego life science asset) to distressed debt specialist Strategic Value Partners in January 2026.

## What the evidence tells us about extend-and-pretend

The Federal Reserve Bank of New York's Staff Report #1130 (October 2024), titled "Extend-and-Pretend in the U.S. CRE Market," found that banks systematically extended impaired CRE mortgages to avoid capital write-downs, with well-capitalized banks more willing to classify loans as nonperforming than weakly capitalized ones. The report estimated this behavior crowded out **4.8–5.3%** of new CRE mortgage originations since Q1 2022. The American Bankers Association disputed the characterization, arguing that workout practices are prudent "extend to defend" rather than deceptive "extend and pretend."

Bank OZK occupies an unusual middle ground. The evidence supporting extend-and-pretend concerns is substantial:

- **590 modifications** on ~300 loans over 14 quarters, with many loans modified multiple times
- **Sterling Bay/Lincoln Yards** modified six times over five years before ultimate foreclosure — the textbook E&P pattern
- **IQHQ RaDD** granted a two-year extension despite zero major tenants — deferring impairment recognition on the bank's largest single credit
- **NPL volatility** suggesting classification management through foreclosure transfers and timing of loss recognition
- **No re-default rate disclosure** despite ASC 326 requirements for modifications to borrowers in financial difficulty — an omission consistent with avoiding the "financial difficulty" classification
- **The 261 bps rate reduction** on a construction loan disclosed in the Q2 2025 10-Q contradicts management's blanket claim of granting no concessions

The counterarguments are real but narrowing. Management extracted **$1.3 billion** in genuine additional capital from sponsors — these are not phantom concessions. The bank's weighted average loan-to-cost of **51%** and loan-to-value of **44%** provide meaningful equity cushions. The 22-year RESG historical net charge-off rate of only **11 basis points** demonstrates a strong long-term track record. And the bank remains profitable, earning record EPS of **$6.18** in FY2025 with an efficiency ratio of 35%.

## Conclusion

Bank OZK's forensic profile reveals a sophisticated form of structured forbearance rather than crude extend-and-pretend. The bank genuinely extracts economic value from modifications — fees, additional equity, enhanced terms — distinguishing its approach from passive loan rolling. But the classification framework that keeps 98% of modifications outside the "financial difficulty" designation understates the true volume of stressed credit activity and shields investors from disclosure of re-default rates that would quantify tail risk. The Q4 2025 charge-off spike and reserve inversion demonstrate that when sponsors finally reach their limits, losses arrive suddenly and at scale. With $13.8 billion in 2022-vintage loans maturing in the first three quarters of 2026, life sciences exposure of $3.2 billion in a deteriorating market, and CRE concentration at 455% of tangible equity, the gap between Bank OZK's formal disclosures and its actual modification activity will face its most consequential test in the months ahead. Management projects 2026 charge-offs similar to 2025 and expressed optimism about 2027 — but the reserve cushion that supported this optimism has already been consumed.
