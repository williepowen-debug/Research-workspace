# Bank OZK: sell-side consensus at a crossroads

Bank OZK (OZK) carries a **Hold consensus** from 11 sell-side analysts, with the street split almost evenly between bulls and skeptics—**5 Buy, 5 Hold, 1 Sell**. The average price target of roughly **$53–$57** (depending on aggregator) implies 15–23% upside from the current share price near **$46.31**, yet that headline optimism masks a notable bearish drift over the past three months as analysts ratcheted targets lower amid rising credit concerns. Citigroup's Benjamin Gerlinger remains the lone Sell, a rating held continuously since his dramatic double-downgrade in May 2024, while a March 2026 short report from Temple 8 Capital comparing OZK to SVB has added fuel to the CRE-risk debate. The stock's next major catalyst arrives April 21, when Q1 2026 earnings will test whether the bank's **$915 million IQHQ life sciences loan**—maturing in August 2026 with zero tenants signed—is heading toward resolution or toward a charge-off.

## Citi's Sell rating endures as the street's loudest bear

**Citigroup's Benjamin Gerlinger has maintained his Sell rating continuously since May 2024**, when he double-downgraded OZK from Buy directly to Sell and slashed his price target from $57 to $37 (later raised modestly to $40). That action triggered a **14% single-day stock decline**. Gerlinger's thesis centers on OZK's exposure to the **IQHQ RaDD project**—a $915 million construction loan for a 1.7-million-square-foot waterfront life sciences campus in San Diego where, as he wrote, *"we believe 0% of the 1.7 million square feet is leased."* On January 5, 2026, Gerlinger reiterated his Sell and opened a **30-Day Downside negative catalyst watch**, anticipating further deterioration ahead of Q4 earnings.

Citi remains the only major sell-side firm with a Sell rating. However, the sentiment gap between Citi and the rest of the street has narrowed. With UBS at $48 and Wells Fargo at $50, the "neutral" camp's targets are now only $8–$10 above Citi's $40 target, suggesting growing discomfort even among non-bears.

## CRE concentration looms as the dominant risk factor

The analyst debate around OZK revolves almost entirely around **commercial real estate and construction lending concentration**. The numbers are stark. As of December 31, 2025, OZK's Real Estate Specialties Group (RESG) comprised **54.4% of total loans**, down from a peak of 70% but still enormous. More critically, **CRE exposure runs at approximately 486% of tangible equity**, versus the **300% regulatory threshold** that triggers enhanced supervisory scrutiny. Construction and development concentration stands at roughly **315% of risk-based capital**, against a 100% regulatory red-line. No peer comes close—East West Bancorp sits at 161%, Western Alliance at 159%, Zions at 124%.

The Q4 2025 earnings report crystallized credit fears. **Net charge-offs surged to $98.3 million**, nearly 8x the $12.4 million in Q4 2024, pushing the annualized charge-off rate to **1.18%—the highest in 15 years**. Non-performing loans climbed to **1.06% of total loans** from 0.44% a year earlier. Major problem properties included a **$72.4 million charge-off on a Cambridge, MA courthouse redevelopment** (a $300 million office construction loan now 100% vacant), a **$9 million charge-off on a Chicago life sciences property** at Lincoln Yards, and ongoing losses on a Baltimore Peninsula development backed by Goldman Sachs and Sagamore Ventures.

The market's biggest single-name concern remains the **IQHQ RaDD loan ($915 million committed, ~$555 million funded)**, which matures in **August 2026** with no major tenants signed. Finance professor Rebel Cole has estimated the property could be worth as little as $500 million against $555 million in funded debt. San Diego's Sorrento Mesa life sciences submarket—where much of OZK's $3.2 billion life sciences exposure is concentrated—carries a **35% vacancy rate** with over 3 million square feet available. TD Cowen's Janet Lee specifically cited "pressure in the Real Estate Specialties Group portfolio" when cutting her target in January, while Piper Sandler's Stephen Scouten highlighted "elevated non-performing loans and potential negative credit migrations."

CEO George Gleason described asset quality on the January 21 earnings call as a *"mixed bag,"* acknowledging that *"the longer this challenging cycle drags on for our sponsors, the more likely it would be that individual sponsors would run into trouble."* However, he noted that **~95% of RESG loans** still receive good sponsor support, with sponsors contributing **over $1.3 billion in additional equity** since the Fed tightening cycle began.

## A short seller's SVB comparison adds urgency to the debate

In March 2026, **Temple 8 Capital published a short report** drawing explicit parallels between OZK and Silicon Valley Bank. While the comparison is provocative and should be weighed against Temple 8's financial interest in the stock declining, several of their data points are worth noting. They flagged that OZK's **allowance-for-credit-losses-to-net-charge-off coverage ratio has fallen below 1.0x**, compared to a peer average of 4.5–5.1x—meaning the bank's reserve cushion has been effectively depleted. They also highlighted the **2022 vintage maturity wall**: OZK originated **$13.8 billion in construction loans in 2022**, making it the largest construction lender in the U.S., and standard 36–42-month terms place those maturities squarely in **Q1–Q3 2026**.

Credit rating agencies offer a more measured view. KBRA maintains an **A- rating with Negative Outlook**, citing CRE concentrations, while Moody's holds **A3 with a Stable Outlook** (upgraded from Negative in mid-2025), requiring successful CRE reduction for further improvement.

## Conclusion

The sell-side consensus on Bank OZK reflects a market caught between compelling valuation (7.5x P/E, 4.1% yield, record profitability) and existential credit-quality questions. The 5-5-1 Buy-Hold-Sell split and $53–$57 average price target suggest analysts see meaningful upside, but the recent trend is unmistakably bearish: seven target cuts versus just two raises since January, and the most up-to-date aggregator data showing an average as low as $49.44. **Citi's Sell rating with a $40 target has been in place since May 2024 with no sign of reversal**—indeed, Gerlinger opened a negative catalyst watch in January 2026. The critical watchpoint is the **IQHQ RaDD loan maturing August 2026**, which will likely determine whether OZK's bull case (diversification working, sponsors supporting, cycle ending) or bear case (under-provisioned, maturity wall cresting, life sciences frozen) ultimately prevails. The April 21 Q1 2026 earnings call should provide the next significant data point, particularly on reserve levels, new credit migrations, and any leasing progress at IQHQ.
