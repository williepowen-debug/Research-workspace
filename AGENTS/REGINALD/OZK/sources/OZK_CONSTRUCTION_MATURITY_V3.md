# Bank OZK's construction loan maturity schedule: slope, not cliff, but front-loaded in 2026

**Bank OZK does not publish a granular quarterly construction-loan maturity schedule**, making precise maturity-cliff analysis impossible from public filings alone. However, reconstructing the timeline from RESG vintage origination data, management guidance, and repayment velocity produces a clear picture: the bank faces a **sustained "maturity slope" running through all of 2026, front-loaded into Q1**, driven overwhelmingly by the record **$13.82 billion** in 2022-vintage originations now reaching their standard 36-to-42-month lifecycle endpoints. Management explicitly guided that 2026 mid-single-digit loan growth will be "loaded into the final 3 quarters" because Q1 payoffs will be heaviest. Full-year 2025 RESG repayments hit a record **$7.24 billion** (up 19% from $6.06 billion in 2024), with Q4 2025 alone reaching a record **$3.00 billion**, and management expects "significant RESG loan repayments" to continue throughout 2026 before subsiding in 2027.

## The 2022 vintage is the maturity engine

The critical variable in any OZK construction-maturity analysis is the **2022 origination vintage**. RESG originated a record $13.82 billion that year — more than double the $7.22 billion in 2023 and $5.41 billion in 2024. Because Bank OZK's standard construction loan lifecycle runs **36 to 42 months** from origination to stabilization/payoff (confirmed repeatedly by CEO George Gleason on earnings calls), the 2022 vintage's hard maturity deadlines began hitting in late 2025 and will run through **Q1–Q3 2026** for the bulk of the book.

The quarterly origination pattern within 2022 matters for pinpointing when maturities cluster:

| 2022 Quarter | RESG Originations |
|---|---|
| Q1 2022 | $3.14B |
| Q2 2022 | $3.53B |
| Q3 2022 | $4.35B |
| Q4 2022 | $2.81B |
| **Full Year** | **$13.82B** |

Adding 36–42 months to these origination dates yields implied maturity windows of roughly **Q1 2025 through Q3 2026** for Q1 2022 originations, and **Q4 2025 through Q4 2026** for Q4 2022 originations. The heaviest single quarter of originations — **Q3 2022 at $4.35 billion** — implies peak maturity pressure in **Q1–Q3 2026**. This math aligns precisely with management's guidance that RESG repayments will remain elevated "throughout 2026" before subsiding in 2027, when they project loan growth accelerating to **10–11%**.

The vintage-level repayment data confirms the 2022 book is now the dominant payoff source. In Q3 2025, the 2022 vintage contributed **$1.25 billion** in repayments — the largest single vintage. In Q4 2025, this rose to **$1.34 billion**. Combined with 2021-vintage payoffs ($0.72 billion in Q4) and accelerating 2023-vintage payoffs ($1.02 billion in Q4), the total RESG repayment wave has been building steadily.

## What the repayment velocity reveals about 2026

RESG repayments have followed a clear acceleration pattern that, extrapolated forward, suggests **$7–9 billion in total RESG payoffs in 2026**:

| Period | RESG Repayments | Annualized Rate |
|---|---|---|
| FY 2023 | $4.01B | $4.01B |
| FY 2024 | $6.06B | $6.06B |
| Q3 2025 | $2.44B (record) | ~$9.8B |
| Q4 2025 | $3.00B (record) | ~$12.0B |
| **FY 2025** | **$7.24B** (record) | **$7.24B** |

The Q4 2025 run-rate of $3.0 billion per quarter would imply $12 billion annualized, but management has not guided to that level for 2026. Instead, the qualitative guidance suggests 2026 repayments will be "similar to" or "slightly above" 2025's $7.24 billion. CFO Tim Hicks noted on the Q4 2025 call that Q1 2026 would see heavy payoffs but that subsequent quarters should see positive net loan growth. Gleason specifically stated: **"Our mid-single-digit loan growth guidance for the year is probably going to be loaded into the — more in the final 3 quarters than the first quarter of the year. We've got — we had a lot of payoffs that we are expecting in Q1."**

This asymmetry — Q1-heavy payoffs tapering through Q2–Q4 — is the strongest available evidence on the intra-year maturity distribution. It suggests a **front-loaded slope** rather than a uniform maturity wall, with Q1 2026 being the peak quarter and the payoff wave gradually subsiding through the rest of the year.

## The construction book is larger than $7.8 billion — and the headline figure obscures total exposure

The regulatory "construction and land development" line item was **$9.52 billion** at December 31, 2024 (down from $11.65 billion a year earlier) and **$8.49 billion** at September 30, 2025. But this figure significantly understates Bank OZK's total construction-related exposure. The RESG portfolio — which encompasses all of the bank's large-scale CRE construction lending — had a total funded balance of **$18.56 billion** at year-end 2024, declining to roughly **$17.6 billion** at year-end 2025. Total RESG commitments (funded plus unfunded) peaked at **$34.5 billion** in March 2024 and declined to **$28.8 billion** by December 31, 2025.

The discrepancy arises because completed construction projects that receive certificates of occupancy and convert to monthly amortizing structures are reclassified from "construction/land development" to "non-farm/non-residential" on the regulatory call report. As Gleason explained on the Q3 2024 call: **"There are really two criteria for moving out of the construction category. One is the project is complete and has a certificate of occupancy and the loan structure includes a monthly amortizing feature."** These reclassified loans still carry construction-era risk profiles — same borrowers, same collateral, same variable-rate structures — but disappear from the "construction" regulatory line item. At year-end 2025, RESG comprised **54.4%** of total loans, with a management target to drive this below **50%** during 2026.

| Date | Regulatory Construction/Land Dev. | RESG Funded Balance | RESG Total Commitments | RESG % of Loans |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 31, 2023 | $11.65B | ~$16.9B | ~$33.0B | ~66% |
| Dec 31, 2024 | $9.52B | $18.56B | $32.4B | 62% |
| Sept 30, 2025 | $8.49B | ~$18.9B (funded) | $30.8B | 57.7% |
| Dec 31, 2025 | est. ~$7.5–8.0B | ~$17.6B | $28.8B | 54.4% |

## Extensions are frequent, but extraction terms are punitive

Bank OZK's extension and modification activity has been substantial. In Q3 2025, **41 loans** reached maturity and were extended. In Q4 2025, **49 loans** were extended. Over **14 quarters** since the Fed began raising rates, management reports collecting **$1.3 billion in additional equity contributions**, **$866 million in reserve deposits**, and **$429 million in unscheduled principal paydowns** from sponsors seeking extensions.

The bank's approach — summarized internally as "you pay, you stay; you don't pay, you don't stay" — requires sponsors to post additional cash reserves, pay modification fees, make unscheduled principal reductions, and accept enhanced floor rates. No rate or spread concessions have been granted. Management claims roughly **95% of RESG loans** continue to demonstrate good sponsor support, with only **four credits** reaching the point where sponsors were "unable or unwilling" to support.

However, the frequency of extensions — roughly 40–50 per quarter — implies that a meaningful share of the construction book is not paying off at contractual maturity. Across 14 quarters, **414 total modifications/extensions** have been executed on a portfolio that contained roughly 290–320 credits at any given time. This suggests many loans have been extended more than once. The most extreme example is the **Sterling Bay/Lincoln Yards** land loan in Chicago, which was modified **six times** before Bank OZK ultimately took a $20.8 million write-down and sold the asset.

## The whale loan and life sciences exposure are the concentrated risk

The single largest identified maturity risk in 2026 is the **IQHQ RaDD loan** — a **$915 million commitment** (~$555 million funded) for a 1.7 million square foot life sciences campus in San Diego. This loan was originated in 2022 and matures in **August 2026**. The project is substantially complete but has **no major tenants signed**. The sponsor contributed $87 million in additional capital in June 2024 and another $82 million in reserves in January 2025, bringing total reserves held by the bank to **$152.9 million**. The most recent appraisal indicated an LTV of **50.3%** assuming full funding.

Bank OZK's total life sciences exposure is approximately **$3.4 billion** (10.6% of RESG commitments), concentrated in markets with significant vacancy — San Diego's Sorrento Mesa submarket has **35–36% lab space availability**. Other notable life sciences exposures include an $89 million loan and $163 million loan, both with LTVs above 50%, plus the $265 million Pacific Center loan in Sorrento Mesa that was sold at par to distressed-debt buyer Strategic Value Partners in January 2026.

Credit deterioration has accelerated. Full-year 2025 net charge-offs reached **$160 million** (up from $57.4 million in 2024), and Q4 2025 alone saw **$98.3 million** in charge-offs — nearly 8x the Q4 2024 level. The annualized Q4 2025 NCO rate of approximately **1.18%** exceeded the allowance-for-credit-losses ratio of **~1.16%** for what appears to be the first time since 2010.

## CRE concentration remains far above regulatory thresholds

Bank OZK's CRE concentration ratios remain among the highest of any publicly traded U.S. bank and far exceed the 2006 interagency guidance thresholds. KBRA reported CRE concentration at **358% of total risk-based capital** (vs. the 300% threshold) and construction and development concentration at **197%** (vs. 100% threshold), leading to a **Negative outlook** on the bank's credit ratings. Moody's measured CRE loans at **421% of tangible common equity** as of March 2025, though it upgraded its outlook from Negative to **Stable**, citing improving diversification trends and strong profitability (three-year average ROA of 2.1%).

A critical structural consideration: Bank OZK shed its holding company structure around 2018, eliminating SEC and Federal Reserve oversight. The bank is now supervised only by the **FDIC and the Arkansas State Bank Department** — which some observers view as a less rigorous regulatory framework for the country's largest construction lender. The bank's filings are submitted as PDFs to the FDIC rather than in EDGAR's searchable HTML format, making maturity-table extraction notably harder for external analysts.

## Conclusion: a sustained but front-loaded repayment wave with concentrated tail risk

The evidence points to a **"front-loaded maturity slope"** rather than a single-quarter cliff. Q1 2026 will be the heaviest quarter for RESG payoffs, consistent with the 36-month maturity of Q1 2022's $3.14 billion in originations. Payoffs should moderate through Q2–Q4 2026 but remain elevated relative to historical norms. Management's own guidance — mid-single-digit loan growth in 2026 "loaded into the final 3 quarters" — confirms this distribution.

The total RESG repayment volume in 2026 will likely fall in the **$7–9 billion range**, implying roughly $1.5–2.5 billion per quarter with Q1 skewing higher. The key unknown is **extension velocity**: if borrower refinancing markets tighten (e.g., if long-term rates rise or CMBS/bridge markets pull back), more loans will require extension rather than payoff, pushing maturities into 2027 and increasing tail risk. The IQHQ whale loan ($915 million, August 2026 maturity) alone represents a binary event that could swing a full quarter's credit results. With the ACL-to-NCO coverage ratio already below 1x as of Q4 2025 and charge-offs running at 8x the prior year's pace, the margin of safety for absorbing incremental losses has narrowed considerably. The bank's 22-year weighted average RESG NCO ratio of just **12 basis points** — a figure management cites frequently — may prove an unreliable guide for the current cycle, which involves a historically unprecedented concentration of maturities in a challenging CRE environment.
