## C1: Reclassification Has an Innocent Explanation

### The Bull Argument
OZK launched CIB to diversify away from CRE. C&I growth (+153% over 8 quarters) is genuine corporate lending. Memo Item 3 at 37.6% means only 37.6% of C&I is RE-linked — the other 62.4% is real diversification.

### Our Rebuttal
**The bull argument is partially correct but materially overstated.**

C&I (item 4: $3.4B) and NDFI (item 9a: $2.7B) are **separate** line items on RC-C. Combined "non-RE" categories = $6.2B. Of that:
- **NDFI ($2.74B):** 70-90% CRE-linked. CEO Gleason confirmed "a chunk of NDFI loans are actually RESG loans... debt funds that do commercial real estate lending." 19 of 19 named counterparties across 4 deep research models are CRE debt funds.
- **Memo Item 3 ($1.29B):** CRE loans in items 4+9 not secured by RE. Spans both C&I and NDFI.
- **Estimated total CRE in "non-RE" categories: $2.3-2.9B (37-46%)**

Genuine diversification = ~$3.3-3.9B (10-12% of total loans). Real — ABLG and CBSF are legitimate corporate lending. But the headline CIB figure ($5.25B, +77% YoY) is inflated by $2.0-2.5B of CRE through a different pipe.

**Framing:** "OZK IS diversifying — but about $2.3-2.9B of what they call 'diversification' is CRE exposure reclassified into non-CRE categories. Reported CRE concentration ratios understate true exposure by 50-60 percentage points of Tier 1 capital."

**Confidence: HIGH.** Based on Call Report structure (primary), CEO admission (primary), and 23 named counterparties (sourced).

## C2: Rate Cuts Could Soften the Maturity Wall

### The Bull Argument
If the Fed cuts materially (75-150bps by mid-2026), cap rates compress, property values recover, and construction borrowers can refinance. The maturity wall doesn't hit as hard because the refi market thaws.

### Our Rebuttal
**Rate cuts help the wrong categories. The thesis is robust to moderate rate relief.**

1. **The Fed is barely cutting.** March 2026 dot plot: one 25bps cut this year. PCE at 3.1% constrains. Market prices ~50bps by year-end.

2. **Rate cuts help multifamily/industrial, NOT office/life sci.**
   - Multifamily cap rates: 6.10% (flat YoY) → cuts compress further. Demand exists. 🟢
   - Industrial cap rates: 7.20% (-10bps YoY) → already stabilizing. 🟢
   - **Office cap rates: 9.10% (+20bps YoY) → STILL RISING despite flat rates.** 🔴
   - Life sciences: 25-29% SD vacancy is structural, not cyclical. 🔴

3. **75% of OZK's noncurrent ($256.7M of $341M) is in "other nonfarm nonresidential"** — office and life sciences. The category rate cuts help *least*.

4. **The vacancy bottleneck.** You can't refinance a building with no tenants at any rate:
   - IQHQ RaDD: 97% vacant → DSCR = ~0 → no rate makes this refinanceable
   - Bioterra: vacant → same
   - 89.7% of construction book on interest reserves = borrowers aren't paying from cash flow. Rate cuts reduce the cost of the permanent loan but don't create the cash flow to service it.

5. **The recession paradox.** Aggressive cuts (100-150bps) only happen in a recession. But recession = less tenant demand, more layoffs, more vacancy. Rate relief and demand destruction arrive simultaneously.

6. **The healthy loans are already leaving.** Record RESG paydowns Q3-Q4 2025 = performing loans can already refi. What's LEFT at maturity are the ones that couldn't — the distressed properties.

### Impact on Probabilities
- Bear case: 50% → 45-50% (minor reduction)
- EV shift: $38.75 → $39.00-39.50 (**less than $1 — immaterial**)

**Confidence: HIGH.** Based on JPMorgan Q4 2025 cap rate data (primary), FOMC March 2026 dot plot (primary), Call Report interest reserve data (RCONG376), and RC-N noncurrent concentration.

## C3: Capital Buys Time / Thin EV Edge

### The Bull Argument
CET1 at 11.70% gives OZK ~$2.3B in loss absorption before hitting regulatory minimums. The probability-weighted EV of $38.75 implies only 10% downside — thin margin for a 14-15% SI crowded short.

### Our Rebuttal
**Correct on capital. But the thesis is about earnings compression, not bank failure.**

1. **Capital absorption math:**
   - CET1 11.70% vs 6.50% well-capitalized = ~$2,310M buffer
   - At current NCO run rate ($393M annualized), **capital GROWS** — OZK earns $680M/year, pays $172M dividend, retains enough to absorb current losses
   - NCOs must roughly **triple** before capital starts eroding
   - Capital is not the vulnerability

2. **The stock breaks on earnings, not capital:**

   | EPS | P/E 7x (current) | P/E 6x (stressed) |
   |-----|-------------------|--------------------|
   | $6.18 | $43 (today) | $37 |
   | $5.00 | $35 (-19%) | **$30 (-30%)** |
   | $4.00 | $28 (-35%) | **$24 (-44%)** |
   | $3.00 | $21 (-51%) | $18 (-58%) |

   Bear case only needs EPS to compress to $5.00 with 6x multiple → **$30** (-30%).

3. **The 10% stock EV edge IS thin — for a stock short.** But we're buying puts:
   - Aug $45 put EV: **$8.13** on ~$3-4 cost = **2-2.7x expected return**
   - Defined max loss (premium paid) vs 2-3x upside in bear case
   - Asymmetry changes the risk/reward calculus entirely

4. **Squeeze risk is real but survivable:**
   - 14-15% SI, 12-18 days to cover
   - Any positive headline → spike to $50-55 temporarily
   - **Size to survive the squeeze.** Position max 3-5% of account per put.
   - The maturity wall still arrives regardless of a 2-week squeeze

### Key Reframe
Stop implying bank failure anywhere in the thesis. OZK can absorb massive losses before regulatory action. The trade is:
- Noncurrent rises (maturity wall + interest reserve depletion) →
- Management forced to increase provisions →
- Provision surge compresses EPS ($6.18 → $4-5) →
- Market re-rates from "growth bank" (10-12x) to "stressed CRE bank" (6-8x) →
- Stock reprices to $24-35

**Confidence: HIGH.** Arithmetic from FDIC API capital ratios and OZK financials.

### C6: Gleason's GFC Track Record Is Genuinely Strong (ADDRESSED — Mar 24)
Four independent research models confirm OZK's GFC performance was exceptional: NCOs well below industry in virtually every quarter, ROA >1.20% throughout, profitable every quarter of 2008, proactive Q4 2008 capital raise (CRE ratio 394%→286% in one quarter). Peer failures (Corus, Colonial, ANB, WaMu) had losses orders of magnitude worse. This IS the strongest bull argument. **Our rebuttal (now data-backed):**
1. **Different bank.** GFC portfolio was ~$2B community AR CRE. Today: $35B+ national RESG with $500M-$915M single-project exposures. RESG was small during GFC [KB-OZK-067].
2. **Different concentration.** CRE/equity ~381% then vs ~620% now. GFC LTV averaged ~68% vs ~49% today — tighter LTV but massively higher total exposure [KB-OZK-068/070].
3. **FDIC subsidy.** 7 failed bank acquisitions with 80% loss-share. Record 2010-2011 earnings driven substantially by bargain-purchase gains and accretion, not organic credit performance [KB-OZK-066/071].
4. **OCC Formal Agreement June 2011.** "Less than satisfactory" mgmt and liquidity. Required 11% capital minimum, enhanced credit review. Three of four research models missed this entirely [KB-OZK-069].
5. **Corus analog.** Corus Bank ran the *same* gateway-city construction model and failed. Difference was basis (LTV), not strategy [KB-OZK-072].
**Verdict:** Gleason demonstrated genuine discipline, but the GFC stress-tested a fundamentally different institution. The current RESG book is being stress-tested for the first time now. See `raw/llm_outputs/OZK_GFC_TRACK_RECORD_V4.md` for full research.
