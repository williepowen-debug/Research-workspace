# Group A: Florida school-district enrollment, 2026-27 and 2025-26

Researched 2026-09-28. Only two districts have a primary-document headcount comparing the same count point this year and last year: **Osceola** (weekly computer-enrollment PDFs) and **St. Johns** (1st 20-day report). Most other 2026-27 figures come from press reports of 10-day counts where the basis is loose ("vs last year"). The only source that covers all 13 districts on one basis is the state Education Estimating Conference (EEC, 8/11/2026), and it is **FTE, not headcount**, with 2026-27 as a **forecast**. Its bases are explained in the note under Table B.

How to read these figures:
- **District FTE** excludes Family Empowerment Scholarship (FES) FTE. The document does not state whether charter-school FTE is included.
- **2025-26** is the preliminary estimate from FTE data through Survey 3 (February 2026).
- **2026-27** is the appropriation **forecast** set at the 3/6/2026 conference. The August conference left it unchanged, so it is **not a count**.
- FTE is not headcount.

Where the forecast and the actual counts can be compared, the counts come in weaker:
- **St. Johns:** forecast +2.2%, actual 20-day count about flat (-0.10% in district schools).
- **Osceola:** forecast -0.3%, actual -1.1% total and -3.2% district-run.
- **Duval:** forecast +0.2%, yet the traditional count fell below 100,000.
- **Seminole and Volusia:** forecasts of -2.6% and -2.5% are roughly in line with their 10-day and workshop figures.

Statewide EEC points (executive summary, 8/11/2026):
- 2025-26 district FTE came in **6,117 below** the March estimate. EDR says this is "primarily explained by" the ESOL loss: ESOL is down **22,084 FTE year over year**, which EDR ties to immigration effects.
- Private-school enrollment grew only 1.5%. EDR now projects **8,578 fewer private-school vouchers** in 2026-27.
- 2026-27 district FTE is projected at **2,722,531 FTE, down from 2,749,749**. FES is projected at 482,528, up from 434,053.

## Causes the districts themselves cite (per source)

- **Duval:** 2023 universal expansion of FES vouchers. Scholarships rose from about 3,000 (2015) to about 29,000, with about 7,000 new this year. Homeschool is about 10,000, roughly double. Charter growth. (Florida Trib 9/9/26; Action News Jax 2025: "increase in vouchers")
- **Osceola:** parents choosing homeschool or private school through the FES scholarship, and the declining birth rate. The district has run a re-enrollment campaign with Caissa K12 since 3/2026: about 60 verified re-enrollments and 294 "set to re-enroll". (Spectrum 8/26/26)
- **Seminole:** lower birth rates, vouchers, "shifting demographics, population changes". Spectrum's roundup adds immigration policy changes and a lack of housing turnover. (Spectrum 7/27 and 8/27/26; ClickOrlando 8/10/26)
- **Central Florida roundup (OCPS, Osceola, Seminole, Lake):** lower birth rates, immigration policy changes, housing-turnover slowdown and aging neighborhoods, and taxpayer-funded vouchers. (Spectrum 8/5 and 8/27/26). This is a pooled attribution, not a statement from each district.
- **Volusia:** charter enrollment and choice scholarships keep rising. The district keeps 78.5% of its total funding in 2026-27, down from 95% in 2021-22. (Observer 6/23/26)
- **Clay:** declining birth rates plus voucher expansion (spokesperson, Jacksonville Today 8/10/26). Vouchers "diverted $30 million" (Broskie, Florida Trib 7/30/26).
- **St. Johns:** "rapid population growth scaling back" and vouchers blamed for the budget (Jacksonville Today 8/10/26).
- **Leon:** school choice, successful charters, declining birth rates, FES expansion (board member A. Smith, Tallahassee Democrat 1/14/26). The superintendent also points to 8th-to-9th grade losses to GED and career training.
- **Alachua:** homeschool, charter and private enrollment, declining birth rates. East Gainesville underenrollment is attributed to a long-running westward population shift and historical housing policy (Alligator 9/14/26).
- **Escambia:** vouchers, with the district "unfunded… over $3 million" for students who took a voucher but did not go where it was sent; homeschool and private schools; COVID introduced families to homeschool (WEAR 5/14 and 6/2026).
- **Brevard:** declining enrollment, which is cutting funding (FOX 35 1/29/26). The Florida Trib roundup cites FES expansion.
- **Marion:** growth. A new high school opened to relieve overcrowding in the Marion Oaks area. No decline causes cited.
- **Polk:** growth is concentrated in charters and FES (3/10/26 board meeting).
- **Statewide (EDR):** ESOL decline tied to immigration effects; slowing private-school growth.
