## Causes cited by districts
- **Broward (district memo, 2025-08-25):** the expansion of private-school and home-education vouchers, net migration out of South Florida, and falling births in the county. The drop was called "unanticipated and unprecedented."
- **Palm Beach (district memo, 2025-10-17):** a regional trend across large urban districts, wider demographic shifts, the FES expansion, and the rising cost of living. Press has also cited the immigration climate and retirees moving in.
- **Miami-Dade (Supt. Dotres, Aug 2025):** fewer students arriving rather than students leaving, meaning immigration fell (newcomers -5,346). Also high cost of living and low birth rates. The district said a survey found no fear-of-ICE pattern. In 2026 WLRN tied the drop to federal immigration enforcement.
- **Hillsborough:** FES vouchers, homeschooling, possible immigration effects, and "competitive choice environment" (Supt. Ayres, 2026). The 2024-25 baseline is disputed because of the Synergy data failure.
- **Orange (Supt. Vazquez):** immigration and TPS changes (~2,500 immigrant students in 2025-26), low births, VPK declines, vouchers, private and home schooling, housing and aging in place.
- **Pinellas:** births fell from 10,000 to 6,000 a year over 15 years, families left because of housing costs, and incomes stagnated.
- **Pasco:** charter and voucher choice, and Florida's record-low birth rate. Kindergarten entrants now trail departing seniors by 495, and that gap is projected to reach ~1,050.
- **Lee:** vouchers and school choice, migration from abroad down 86%, 2,442 students moved out of FL, and moves to home education and private schools.
- **Sarasota:** low birth rate (6.7 per 1,000 in 2024), vouchers and FES (~4,800 students), competition from charters, private schools and nearby districts, housing costs, and immigration enforcement (WSLR).
- **St. Lucie (Supt. Prince):** the FES and choice options (private, micro-schools, homeschool). Press adds affordability and immigration.
- **Martin:** the voucher expansion.
- **Okaloosa:** vouchers for all students, falling births, economic pressure. The county is split: the south is declining while Crestview in the north is growing.
- **Bay:** families moving out of the area, virtual or home school, and an earlier start date that depressed the opening count.
- **Manatee:** growth; schools are over capacity county-wide (Parrish, Lakewood Ranch). **Santa Rosa:** growth, but only the unverified projection above.
