# Fort Lauderdale / Broward multifamily: testing the @aviancapital anecdote against data
**Prepared:** 2026-10-01 (CORAL research assist) · **Lead:** X post by @aviancapital, 2026-09-29. It is one observer's walk-through (n=1) and is treated here as a LEAD, not as evidence.
**Anecdote claims:** (a) "vacancy everywhere" in Fort Lauderdale apartments; (b) renewals still being raised aggressively; (c) a new building that opened about 7 months ago looks "~75% vacant"; (d) $2,100 asking for a 480 sf studio (= $4.375/sf/mo); (e) "maturity wall is coming".

**Basis rules used here:**
- Geography is labelled on every row: METRO = Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach MSA; BROWARD = Broward County / Fort Lauderdale market area; SUBMARKET = a named Fort Lauderdale submarket; CITY = Fort Lauderdale city limits.
- Each series is kept to ONE compiler. Yardi Matrix (asking rent, properties with 50+ units) and CoStar via Cushman & Wakefield (effective rent, 100+ units) are **never combined**: they differ in rent basis, unit threshold and submarket boundaries.
- ⚠️ **The key basis caveat:** both Yardi's "stabilized properties" occupancy and C&W's stabilized occupancy **leave out properties still in lease-up**. C&W also leaves out anything delivered in the past 18 months. **The building in the anecdote is therefore outside both headline occupancy figures by construction.**

### Lease-up pace benchmarks (is "~25% occupied after 7 months" normal?)

| Benchmark | Figure | As of | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| RealPage, national | The average lease-up community signs **10–11 leases per month** (−10% YoY) | Published 2024-04-22, so a 2024 vintage. No newer public figure was found | [RealPage 2Q24 webcast recap](https://realpage.com/analytics/us-webcast-recap-2q-2024) |
| Census SOMA via NAHB, national | **41%** of units in buildings completed in Q1 2026 were leased within 3 months. This was the 7th straight quarter below 50% and the lowest since Q4 2023 | Published Aug 2026 | [NAHB Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/08/multifamily-absorption-rate-remains-below-50-2/) |
| RealPage, market ranking | Fort Lauderdale, West Palm Beach and Miami rank near the top for lease-up absorption per property per month | Oct 2023 (old) | [RealPage](https://www.realpage.com/analytics/lease-up-absorption-by-market/) |

**Arithmetic (derived; the anecdote's building is unidentified):** 7 months × 10–11 leases ≈ **70–77 leases**. The answer therefore depends on building size:
- **About 300 units:** 25% occupied ≈ 75 leases. That is **in line with RealPage's average pace**, so normal.
- **500+ units** (the size of the towers that delivered in Central FTL in H1 2026, e.g. 502 and 563 units): 25% ≈ 125–140 leases ≈ **18–20 per month, faster than the national average**. Not distressed on pace alone. At that pace, reaching 90% takes about 2 more years, which carry-cost math would flag.
- **Under about 200 units:** 25% means fewer than 7 leases a month. That is **clearly slow (abnormal)**.
- Against Census SOMA (41% leased within 3 months), 25% after 7 months is slow for a typical 5+ unit building. SOMA, however, is dominated by smaller garden and mid-rise projects, not 40-story towers.

**Eyeball caveats:**
- Judging "75% vacant" from the street can't tell an empty unit from one that is leased but not yet moved into, one held off the market by phased floor release, or one whose seasonal or snowbird occupant is away. Late September is the off-season, which biases the read toward "vacant."
- "Opened about 7 months ago" may mean the first phase's certificate of occupancy (TCO), not full delivery.

## Verdict: anecdote vs data

| Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| (a) "Vacancy everywhere" | **CONTRADICTED for stabilized stock; cannot be tested for lease-up stock** | Broward stabilized occupancy is 94.2% (CoStar/C&W, Q2-26) and 94.9% (Yardi, Jul-26); M&M vacancy is 4.8% (Mar-26). All of these exclude lease-ups. The only total-vacancy read that includes lease-ups is old (CoStar 7.6%, Q3-25). Metro Census vacancy of 9.1% is elevated, but the YoY change is inside its margin of error. Pockets exist: FTL–West 91%, Broward Class C 93.0% (−2.0 pp). |
| (b) Renewals raised aggressively | **SUPPORTED (direction)** | Yardi Broward Jul-26: renewal trade-out **+3.1%** vs new-lease trade-out **−1.9%**, a 5-point gap. The renewal rate slipped from 67.1% to 66.5%. (Jun-26: +3.0% vs −2.1%.) Whether +3.1% counts as "aggressive" is a judgement, not data. |
| (c) A building about 7 months old looks about 75% vacant | **CANNOT TEST the building; the benchmark says normal-to-slow depending on size** | At the RealPage average of 10–11 leases a month, 25% after 7 months is normal for a ~300-unit building, above average for a 500+ unit tower, and slow for one under 200 units. Against SOMA (41% leased within 3 months), it is slow. Eyeballing from the street is unreliable in the off-season. |
| (d) $2,100 for 480 sf | **CONSISTENT with market (not an outlier)** | $4.38/sf vs $4.00 for the downtown studio average (RentCafe, Aug-26). The absolute rent is below the downtown studio average. |
| (e) "Maturity wall is coming" | **CANNOT TEST with public data** | No Broward-level maturity data was found. The documented forward pressure is **supply**, not maturities: Yardi projects 7,078 Broward completions in 2026 vs 3,432 trailing absorption, and C&W expects vacancy to approach the 2023 peak. |

**Three-line bottom line:**
1. The data contradicts "vacancy everywhere." Stabilized Broward occupancy is about 94–95% on both Yardi (Jul-26) and CoStar (Q2-26) and is flat to up YoY. The softness is in *pricing*, not occupancy: Class A effective rent is −2.2% YoY (CoStar), concessions run at about 1.1 months free on 7% of units (Yardi), and new-lease rents are −1.9% while renewals are +3.1%. That last pair is the one claim the data supports.
2. The ~75%-vacant new building cannot be tested and is not, by itself, abnormal. Every headline occupancy series excludes lease-ups by construction, and 25% leased after 7 months matches the national average lease-up pace for a ~300-unit building and beats it for a 500+ unit tower.
3. The real forward risk the anecdote gestures at is H2-2026 **supply** (Broward projected 2026 completions about 2× trailing absorption; FTL–Central holds 36% of the county pipeline), not a documented maturity wall. No 2026 operating-property distress turned up in public sources. **Upgrade trigger:** a Q3-26 C&W/CoStar or Yardi print showing Broward stabilized occupancy falling below about 93.5%, or the Class A effective-rent decline widening beyond −3%. These are proposed watch levels, not pre-registered thresholds.
