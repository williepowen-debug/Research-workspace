## Why CORAL cares (the relevance call)

Sargassum is a **recurring, structural, Atlantic-coast-specific coastal-amenity tax that lands on exactly CORAL's worst-already condo geography** — SE FL (Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach) + the ocean-side Keys — while **sparing the Gulf Coast** (Tampa/Naples/Sarasota; Loop Current keeps it offshore). It therefore *compounds* on the same metros already carrying the deepest condo inventory/price stress, rather than diversifying.

**Transmission (slow, second-order, perception-driven):**
`record bloom → SE FL Atlantic beaching (Jun–Aug peak) → beachfront amenity degradation + visitor-cancellation perception (≈1 in 10) → coastal/beachfront condo demand softening → reinforces existing condo price/inventory weakness → (eventually, unconfirmed) collateral values / HOA finances → FL bank CRE`

## Key facts (dated)

**Current (mid-June 2026):**
- USF May 31 bulletin: Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt ≈ **28.9M metric tons**, record monthly highs in nearly every region; spring set successive records (Jan 9.5M → Apr 28.7M). On track to rival/break the all-time record (**37.5M, May 2025**). 2026 currently #2 all-time and climbing; ~3-4× the early-2010s blooms.
- NOAA SIR rates **SE Florida "high"** (per Axios Miami, Jun 16). Heavy beaching Keys → Miami-Dade → Broward → Palm Beach; Treasure/Space Coast heavy; NE FL lighter; **Gulf Coast clear-to-light** (normal).
- Peak season **Jun–Aug**; relief typically **Sep–Oct**.

**Economic (Florida east coast):**
- **$2.7B/yr modeled direct impact** (Jin, Wang & Dalton 2025, *Harmful Algae*) — *modeled annual-expected, not realized*; broader total-impact figure cited up to $3.5B (do not conflate direct vs total).
- Municipal cleanup now a fixed recurring cost: **Miami-Dade ~$3.9M/yr** sargassum-specific (up from $2.8M in 2020, ~$6M peak years; theoretical $35M to fully remove); Key West ~$1M/yr; Fort Lauderdale ~$380K/yr.
- Tourism is exposure/risk, not documented realized loss: **~1 in 10 visitors would cancel/reschedule** a Keys trip (Monroe County eval); a single "severe" Keys event est. >$20M (Keys tourism ~$3.5B/yr, ~24,000 jobs).
- Caribbean analogs (for scale): Mexico Caribbean tourism −35% in 2018; IDB/Schling study found sargassum cut beach-segment nighttime lights 17.5% ≈ 11.6% drop in gross local product, effects persisting up to 12 months.

**Historical / structural:**
- Belt first satellite-detectable 2011, recurs every year since except 2013; now ~8,850 km, largest macroalgal bloom on Earth, ~5× the historical Sargasso Sea baseline.
- Driver is **nutrients (N & P), not heat** — equatorial belt already near/above the 24-28°C optimum, so further warming likely *suppresses* tropical growth. Magnitude modulated by upwelling, N-fixation, internal recycling; AMM/Atlantic Niño. Year-to-year landfall severity is highly variable (winds, Loop Current).

**Leading indicators (what to watch):**
- **USF monthly bulletins** — belt biomass (Atlantic + Caribbean). A belt **>~30M tons by spring** (as 2025 & 2026) signals elevated FL risk.
- **NOAA SIR map** — SE FL band (low/warning/medium/**high**).
- **A June/July USF total >~37.5-38M** = new all-time record; a stall <~25M puts 2026 behind 2022.
- A sustained shift to N/NW winds clearing SE FL = near-term relief tell.

**Caveats (from the briefing itself):** beach conditions change hourly; a large offshore belt does NOT guarantee landfall (2023/2024 lesson); "record 2026" is a forecast not realized; satellite tonnages are lower bounds with real uncertainty; FL realized economic losses are under-studied (best loss evidence is Mexico/Caribbean); SIR "high" ratings cited secondhand.
