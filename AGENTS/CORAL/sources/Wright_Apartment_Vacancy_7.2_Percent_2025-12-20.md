# Wright: Apartment List Vacancy 7.2% — Highest Since 2017

*Source: Melody Wright "Make it Happen" (Dec 20, 2025)*

---

## The Headline

> "Vacancy has increased to **7.2%, the highest that Apartment List has seen since it started its index in 2017**."

**Rent trends:**
- National median rent: -1.0% MoM (November)
- 4th consecutive month of MoM decline
- Rents down -1.1% YoY

---

## Supply Pressure

> "The Covid boom meant everyone went gangbusters building a range of housing options, often luxury... townhomes, Built-for-Rent, spec new homes, tiny homes, multifamily, etc."

> "With multifamily and Built-for-Rent still coming online fast and furious in the single-family rental market, **we will see vacancy march steadily higher, becoming chronic**."

Wright reports followers from Wisconsin and Illinois seeing "unchecked building with no demand in sight."

---

## Smart Money Stress

> "Based on their participation in the last housing cycle, PIMCO should really know better. That they don't is telling."

**PIMCO** reportedly under stress in multifamily investments.

---

## San Francisco Transition

> "What we are seeing in some markets such as San Francisco where rents are more resilient is **transition as motivated sellers opt to rent instead**."

Tech layoffs → workers selling to Nvidia millionaires → some sellers become renters → temporary rent support.

---

## Implications for CORAL

1. **7.2% vacancy = highest on record** (Apartment List, since 2017)
2. **Tampa already at 10.3%** — Well above national average
3. **Supply still coming** — MF + BTR still delivering
4. **Vacancy will become "chronic"** — No demand to absorb
5. **SSB MF exposure at risk** — FL vacancy above national already

**Cross-reference:**
- Tampa MF vacancy: 10.3% (prior Wright article)
- 10,000 more units in Tampa pipeline
- FL migration collapsed (22K vs 300K+ peak)
