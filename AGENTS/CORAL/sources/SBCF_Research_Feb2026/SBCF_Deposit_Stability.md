# **Comprehensive Analysis of Deposit Stability and Funding Architecture: Seacoast Banking Corporation of Florida**

The foundational strength of a regional financial institution is historically and empirically tied to the quality, granularity, and cost-efficiency of its deposit franchise. For Seacoast Banking Corporation of Florida (SBCF), the period spanning from the 2023 regional banking crisis to the strategic expansion phases of late 2025 and early 2026 represents a transformative epoch in its balance sheet management.1 As a pure-play Florida institution, Seacoast has leveraged the unique demographic tailwinds and capital inflows characteristic of its home state to construct what management characterizes as a "fortress balance sheet," primarily funded by relationship-based, low-cost liabilities.3 This report provides an exhaustive technical analysis of the corporation’s deposit base, investigating its uninsured deposit profile, concentration risks, cost trajectories, reliance on wholesale funding markets, and its resilience during the systemic stressors of early 2023\.

## **Macroeconomic Context and the Florida Strategic Advantage**

Seacoast Banking Corporation of Florida operates in one of the most dynamic economic environments in the United States. Its market presence is predominantly concentrated in high-growth coastal and inland communities across Florida, serving a diverse customer base that includes affluent retirees, small-to-mid-sized businesses, and local municipalities.4 The bank’s strategic positioning as a leading community-focused bank in Stuart, Florida, has allowed it to capture a significant portion of the wealth migration into the state, which directly translates into a stable and low-cost deposit base.4

As of late 2025, Seacoast holds the \#15 market share position in Florida, but this figure obscures its dominance in specific key markets.3 The bank is the \#1 Florida-based institution in the Orlando Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA) and holds the top position in Palm Beach, Highlands, and ten other counties.3 Furthermore, it maintains the \#1 overall market share in both the Port St. Lucie MSA and the Wildwood-The Villages MSA.3 This localized dominance is critical for deposit stability, as high-touch, relationship-based community banking typically experiences lower churn and higher price inelasticity compared to larger, national counterparts.4

### **Institutional Growth and Financial Trajectory**

The scale of the institution has expanded dramatically through a combination of robust organic growth and disciplined inorganic execution. By the fourth quarter of 2025, the bank had successfully integrated several major acquisitions, most notably Villages Bancorporation, Inc. (VBI), which significantly augmented its deposit footprint in North Central Florida.1

## **Analysis of the Deposit Base: Segmentation and Granularity**

A bank's vulnerability to liquidity shocks is often inversely proportional to the granularity of its deposit base. Institutions with a high concentration of large, institutional, or "hot money" deposits are more susceptible to rapid outflows during periods of market stress.12 Seacoast’s deposit portfolio is defined by its extreme fragmentation and relationship-driven nature, which mitigates these systemic risks.5

As of December 31, 2025, total deposits reached $16.3 billion, a sequential increase of $3.2 billion from the third quarter of 2025\.5 This growth was predominantly fueled by the acquisition of VBI, which added $3.5 billion in low-cost deposits, partially offset by targeted outflows of non-core, high-cost brokered accounts.3

### **Customer Segmentation and Average Balances**

The deposit base is evenly split between consumer and commercial segments, reflecting the bank's dual focus on retail community banking and small-business treasury management.5 This balance provides a diversified funding stream that responds differently to interest rate cycles and economic shifts.

| Segment | Proportion of Total Deposits | Average Customer Balance | Strategic Significance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Consumer | 50% | $26,000 | Highly stable, lower price sensitivity 5 |
| Commercial | 50% | $116,000 | Operational funding, payroll, and treasury 5 |

The average consumer balance of $26,000 is exceptionally low, ensuring that the vast majority of these accounts are fully covered by FDIC insurance, which inherently reduces the risk of withdrawal during periods of broader banking sector panic.5 In the commercial segment, the average balance of $116,000 indicates a focus on local enterprises and professionals rather than large, yield-seeking corporate treasuries.5

### **Transaction Account Mix and Core Funding**

The quality of the funding base is further evidenced by the high proportion of transaction accounts. Customer transaction accounts represented 48% of total deposits at the end of 2025\.5 Non-interest-bearing demand deposits (DDA) are the most valuable component of this mix, as they represent essentially zero-cost funding for the bank. In the fourth quarter of 2025, average non-interest-bearing demand deposits totaled $4.1 billion, a 15% increase from the prior quarter and a 20% increase year-over-year.5

This growth in NIB DDA, particularly during a period of high interest rates, is a testament to the "stickiness" of the Seacoast franchise. It suggests that customers utilize the bank for its operational utility—such as treasury management and bill pay services—rather than solely as a vehicle for interest income.1

## **Uninsured Deposits and the Florida Public Depository Framework**

The March 2023 crisis brought the level of uninsured deposits into sharp focus for regulators and investors alike.12 Seacoast’s management of its uninsured profile is distinctive due to its participation in specific state-level protection programs that provide a more nuanced picture of its true liquidity risk.16

### **Quantitative Profile of Uninsured Deposits**

As of December 31, 2024, the bank estimated total uninsured deposits at $4.4 billion, which represented approximately 36% of its overall deposit accounts.16 While 36% is a manageable level for a regional bank, the effective risk is even lower when considering collateralization.16

A significant portion of Seacoast's deposits consists of public funds under the Florida Qualified Public Depository (QPD) program.16 This program provides a layer of loss protection for public entities—such as local municipalities and county governments—beyond the standard FDIC insurance limits by requiring banks to pledge collateral against these funds.16

| Metric (YE 2024\) | Percentage of Total Deposits | Significance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Total Estimated Uninsured | 36% | Gross regulatory reporting figure 16 |
| Uninsured and Uncollateralized | 30% | True "run risk" exposure 16 |

By early 2026, with the inclusion of the VBI deposit base, which is characterized as "high-quality" and "low-cost," the bank’s management continued to highlight its "fortress balance sheet" positioning, suggesting that the uninsured and uncollateralized ratio remained among the more conservative in the industry.5

### **Liquidity Coverage of Uninsured Balances**

Liquidity coverage measures the ability of a bank to meet immediate withdrawal demands from its uninsured depositors using cash and readily available borrowing capacity.18 Seacoast maintains a substantial surplus in this regard. As of the end of 2024, the bank’s total liquidity sources—consisting of cash and lines of credit with the Federal Reserve and the Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB)—represented 138% of all uninsured deposits and 167% of uninsured and uncollateralized deposits.16

This coverage ratio reached as high as 184% in mid-2023, reflecting a deliberate strategy to bolster liquid reserves during the period of national banking turmoil.20 Such high coverage ensure that the bank is never forced into a "fire sale" of its securities portfolio to meet liquidity needs, a failure of which was the primary catalyst for several large-scale bank failures in 2023\.12

## **Deposit Concentration and Large Relationship Risks**

A hidden risk in many banking models is the concentration of deposits within a small number of large clients. If a few significant depositors withdraw their funds simultaneously, even a well-capitalized bank can face a liquidity crunch.12 Seacoast’s deposit base is remarkably granular, characterized by a lack of large-depositor concentration.13

### **Top Depositor Analysis**

The top ten depositors at Seacoast represent approximately 3% of total deposits.5 This is an exceptionally low concentration level for a bank with $16.3 billion in deposits and $20.8 billion in assets.5 For comparison, many of the banks that struggled in 2023 had significant concentrations in the venture capital, cryptocurrency, or specialized technology sectors, where a few dozen entities could control a large percentage of the funding.12

Seacoast’s lack of concentration is a direct result of its community-centric business model. By focusing on 270,000+ individual and small-business relationships across various Florida counties, the bank avoids the "all eggs in one basket" risk.13 Its largest commercial relationships are typically linked to local economic activity—such as healthcare, real estate, and professional services—rather than volatile global capital markets.4

## **Deposit Cost Trends and Beta Management**

In the cycle of monetary policy tightening that began in 2022, the "deposit beta"—the sensitivity of a bank’s deposit rates to changes in the Federal Funds Rate—has been a primary driver of net interest margin performance.23 Seacoast has demonstrated a sophisticated ability to manage these costs, even achieving a downward trend in deposit pricing in late 2025\.1

### **Trajectory of Deposit Costs**

The cost of deposits at Seacoast reached a peak in the third quarter of 2024 at 2.34%.18 Since then, the bank has executed a deliberate strategy to drive these costs down through a combination of proactive repricing and high-value acquisitions.4

| Reporting Period | Cost of Deposits | Cost of Funds (Total) | Net Interest Margin (NIM) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Q3 2024 | 2.34% | \- | 3.17% 18 |
| Q4 2024 | 2.08% | \- | 3.39% 10 |
| Q1 2025 | 1.93% | \- | 3.48% 4 |
| Q2 2025 | 1.80% | 1.93% | 3.58% 21 |
| Q3 2025 | 1.81% | 1.96% | 3.57% 14 |
| Q4 2025 | 1.67% | 1.80% | 3.66% 1 |

The reduction of 14 basis points in the fourth quarter of 2025 (to 1.67%) is particularly notable.1 This was achieved despite the bank continuing to experience robust organic loan growth of 15% annualized.3 The acquisition of VBI played a critical role here, as it provided $3.5 billion in "high-quality, low-cost" deposits which immediately lowered the weighted average cost of the total deposit base.2

### **Beta Management Strategies**

The bank's "cumulative beta"—the total change in deposit rates relative to the change in the federal funds rate—has been managed through a relationship-focused model.4 By prioritizing treasury management and operating accounts, the bank has created a "sticky" funding base that is less sensitive to rate hikes than the "hot money" CD market.1

In Q4 2025, the bank chose not to compete for higher-priced CD options, which resulted in some targeted outflows in that category but protected the overall margin.5 This strategic decision highlights the bank’s strong liquidity position; because it does not *need* to attract new deposits to fund its loans, it can afford to be price-disciplined.1

Furthermore, the "back book" repricing of assets continues to outpace the repricing of deposits. Seacoast is seeing a "back-book dynamic" where new add-on rates for loans are 50 to 100 basis points higher than the portfolio average, while deposit costs are stabilizing or declining.11 This spread is the primary driver of the bank's projected core NIM expansion.1

## **Reliance on Wholesale Funding: FHLB and Brokered Deposits**

While core customer deposits are the preferred source of funding, regional banks utilize wholesale sources to manage interest rate risk and balance sheet liquidity.27 Seacoast’s reliance on these sources has trended downward as its core deposit franchise has grown.5

### **Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB) Borrowings**

FHLB advances serve as a flexible liquidity tool. At Seacoast, these borrowings are used strategically to bridge gaps in organic deposit growth or to manage seasonal fluctuations.5

* **Average Balance:** In the fourth quarter of 2025, FHLB borrowings averaged $623.8 million at a cost of 4.27%.5 This represented a decrease from the third quarter average of $637.8 million.5  
* **Borrowing Capacity:** The bank maintains a massive unused capacity at the FHLB, which forms a core part of its contingency funding plan. As of late 2024, it had $4.0 billion in available collateralized lines of credit, primarily through the FHLB.18  
* **Historical Comparison:** In 2023, average FHLB borrowings were $175.2 million, but they spiked during the banking crisis as the institution (and the industry) sought to build "fortress" liquidity buffers.16 The subsequent reduction in these balances in late 2025 reflects the bank’s return to a deposit-funded growth model.5

### **Brokered Deposits**

Brokered deposits are typically considered more volatile and expensive than core relationship deposits.30 Seacoast has actively worked to reduce its exposure to these instruments.5

In the fourth quarter of 2025, brokered deposits declined by ![][image1] million.5 This outflow was a "targeted strategy" by management to lower the overall cost of funds.5 Earlier in the year, the bank noted that its robust 7% annualized organic deposit growth provided the excess liquidity needed to proactively pay down brokered deposits and FHLB borrowings.14

By moving away from brokered funding and towards relationship-based deposits (like those from the VBI and Heartland acquisitions), Seacoast has significantly improved its "net interest income quality," as customer-based funding is generally more dependable and less price-sensitive.2

## **Retrospective: Behavior During the March 2023 Banking Stress**

The regional banking crisis of March 2023, triggered by the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank (SVB), was a pivotal test for the U.S. banking system.12 While many institutions saw significant "flights to safety" toward G-SIBs (Global Systemically Important Banks), Seacoast’s performance was characterized by stability and even opportunistic growth.19

### **Deposit Inflows and Merger Integration**

Unlike the banks that experienced severe runs, Seacoast reported an increase in total deposits during the first quarter of 2023\.19 Total deposits rose from approximately ![][image2] billion on December 31, 2022, to ![][image3] billion on March 31, 2023\.19 This growth was primarily due to the acquisition of Professional Holding Corp., which closed on January 31, 2023, just before the crisis began.19

However, the bank’s ability to *retain* these acquired deposits during the height of the SVB panic is significant. Management noted that the funding base remained "broadly diversified and stable" throughout the quarter.19 Seacoast actually added "additional excess liquidity" to its balance sheet late in the quarter as a precautionary measure, which contributed to a strong loan-to-deposit ratio of 82% at March 31, 2023\.19

### **Comparative Resilience Factors**

The reasons for Seacoast’s stability during the 2023 crisis, compared to the industry "super-regionals" (banks between $50B and $250B) who were most affected, include its geographic and sector focus.23

| Characteristic | Most-Affected Banks (2023) | Seacoast Banking Corp (2023) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Deposit Speed** | 20-30% outflows per day 32 | Stable to slightly growing 19 |
| **Business Model** | Specialty (Crypto/VC/Tech) 12 | Community Banking (SME/Retail) 4 |
| **Average Balance** | High (Institutional) 12 | Low ($26k Consumer / $116k Biz) 5 |
| **Liquidity Position** | Asset-liability mismatch (Unrealized losses) 12 | High liquidity coverage (163% of uninsured) 19 |

The institution’s pure-play Florida model was also a psychological stabilizer for its depositors. While institutional depositors in California or New York were "rushing to judgment" based on social media trends, Seacoast’s customer base of local Florida businesses and retirees remained anchored by their high-touch relationships with the bank.4

## **Strategic M\&A and Inorganic Liquidity: The VBI Catalyst**

The most significant event for Seacoast’s funding architecture in recent years was the acquisition of Villages Bancorporation, Inc. (VBI), the parent company of Citizens First Bank, which closed on October 1, 2025\.1 This transaction was strategically designed to bolster the bank’s liquidity profile and lower its cost of capital.

### **The Villages Demographic Advantage**

VBI operated 19 branches in "The Villages," a unique, affluent, and rapidly growing 55+ residential community in Central Florida.9 This community is known for its high concentration of liquid assets and its preference for traditional banking services.22

The acquisition added approximately:

* **$3.5 Billion in Deposits:** These were characterized as "high-quality" and "low-cost".2  
* **$1.2 Billion in Loans:** This resulted in a very low loan-to-deposit ratio (LDR) for the acquired franchise, estimated at 38% at the time of the deal announcement.1  
* **Liquidity Infusion:** The massive surplus of deposits over loans at VBI immediately dropped Seacoast’s consolidated LDR from approximately 84% to 77.78%.3

### **Synergy and Capital Outperformance**

The VBI transaction also closed with "materially higher tangible equity" than initially projected.11 This outperformance was driven by lower-than-expected marks on the securities and credit portfolios.33 This extra capital gave Seacoast the flexibility to engage in the January 2026 securities portfolio repositioning, where it sold off $277 million in low-yielding bonds to buy higher-yielding agency MBS.5

By utilizing the excess liquidity from the VBI deposits, Seacoast was able to "sacrifice immediate value" (the $39.5 million pre-tax loss on the bond sale) for "potentially higher ongoing interest income".34 This proactive management of the balance sheet is a direct result of the strong funding position established through the merger.11

## **Liquidity Coverage and Capital Resilience Analysis**

The ultimate measure of a bank's stability is its ability to withstand severe asset-side losses or liability-side withdrawals without compromising its solvency. Seacoast maintains capital and liquidity levels that are among the highest in its peer group.8

### **Capital Ratios and "Well-Capitalized" Status**

The bank is classified as "well-capitalized" based on all U.S. regulatory standards.18

| Capital Ratio | Q4 2025 (Estimated) | Regulatory Minimum (Well-Cap) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Tier 1 Capital Ratio** | 14.4% 1 | 8.0% 33 |
| **Total Risk-Based Capital** | 15.8% 33 | 10.0% 33 |
| **CET1 Ratio** | 11.5% 33 | 6.5% |
| **TCE / TA Ratio** | 9.3% 1 | \- |

The Tangible Common Equity to Tangible Assets (TCE/TA) ratio of 9.3% is particularly important, as it measures the "real" liquid net worth of the bank excluding intangibles like goodwill.2 Management has explicitly stated that even if all held-to-maturity (HTM) securities were adjusted to fair value—fully accounting for the unrealized losses caused by high interest rates—the TCE/TA ratio would still exceed 9%.4 This is a crucial point of differentiation from the banks that failed in 2023, whose tangible equity was virtually wiped out by their HTM marks.12

### **Contingency Funding Capacity**

In addition to its on-balance-sheet cash ($306 million at Sept 30, 2025), Seacoast has built a robust multi-layered liquidity backstop.21

1. **FHLB Capacity:** \~$4.0 billion in available collateralized lines of credit.18  
2. **Unpledged Securities:** $1.3 billion of debt securities available to be pledged for additional borrowing.18  
3. **Federal Reserve Capacity:** Access to the Discount Window and other FRB facilities.16  
4. **Unsecured Lines:** Additional access to wholesale funding through private market lines.18

These sources, in aggregate, provide the bank with the ability to cover more than 167% of its total uninsured and uncollateralized deposit base, effectively making a "run" on the bank mathematically unfeasible without a complete collapse of the U.S. treasury and agency markets.17

## **Future Outlook: Funding Growth in 2026 and Beyond**

As Seacoast moves into the 2026 fiscal year, its focus has shifted from liquidity *preservation* to liquidity *deployment*.1 The high-level targets set by management reflect a belief that the bank’s funding base can support significant asset-side expansion without compromising its cost structure.1

### **2026 Projections and Guidance**

The bank anticipates:

* **Organic Loan Growth:** High single digits.1  
* **Organic Deposit Growth:** Low to mid single digits.1  
* **Net Interest Margin:** Continued expansion, aided by the full-year impact of VBI and the securities repositioning.4  
* **Efficiency Ratio:** An adjusted target between 53-55%, down from over 60% in previous years, as the bank scales its operations over its growing deposit base.1

The "VBI technology conversion," scheduled for July 2026, is the next major operational milestone.1 Once the systems are fully integrated, management expects to fully realize the cost synergies and treasury management revenue opportunities inherent in the Villages community.1

### **Risk Factors and Mitigants**

While the outlook is positive, the bank remains mindful of potential risks to its deposit stability. These include:

* **Interest Rate Volatility:** Changes in Federal Reserve policy could impact the "seasonal outflows" typical in the second quarter or the appetite for higher-yielding CDs.4  
* **Economic Slowdown:** Florida’s economy, while currently robust, is not immune to national trends in inflation or unemployment.3  
* **Competition:** The presence of large national banks in the Florida market ensures continued competition for low-cost deposits.4

Seacoast’s primary mitigant for these risks is its "pure-play Florida" model. By reinvesting in local banker talent and maintaining a customer-focused culture, the bank aims to maintain its 7% customer deposit growth rate and its \#1 market positions in Florida’s most attractive MSAs.3

## **Conclusion**

The analysis of Seacoast Banking Corporation of Florida’s deposit base reveals a highly resilient and strategically optimized funding architecture. The institution has successfully navigated the most significant banking stress since the 2008 financial crisis, emerging with a strengthened liquidity profile and a lower cost of capital.

Key pillars of this stability include the extreme granularity of the depositor base—where the top ten clients represent only 3% of total funding—and a conservative uninsured deposit profile that is further bolstered by the Florida Qualified Public Depository program. The bank’s proactive reduction of wholesale funding reliance and its "fortress" liquidity coverage of 167% against uninsured/uncollateralized balances provide a definitive shield against systemic shocks.

Furthermore, the transformative acquisition of Villages Bancorporation has provided a long-term, scalable platform in one of the nation's most affluent demographic enclaves. With a loan-to-deposit ratio of 77.78% and a Tier 1 capital ratio of 14.4%, Seacoast is not only well-insulated against potential market volatility but is actively positioned to capture high-margin growth opportunities in the Florida commercial and consumer markets through 2026 and beyond. The institution stands as a benchmark for deposit stability and strategic funding management in the regional banking sector.
