## 2. ⚠️ A thing I nearly sent you that was wrong, and I'd rather show you the error than the clean version

I had drafted that the parenthetical is an **escape hatch** — that a Florida condo levying an assessment to fund SIRS repairs falls *out* of the ineligibility test **precisely because it assessed.** That reading is seductive and it is **false**.

**The Special Assessments section immediately below says:**

> *"If the special assessment is associated with a critical repair and the issue is not remediated, **the project is ineligible**."*

⇒ **The carve-out changes WHICH TEST applies, not WHETHER the project is eligible.** Funding via assessment moves you out of the $10,000 *unfunded-repairs* bullet and into the *Special Assessments* test, which reaches the **same outcome** when the underlying repair is unremediated. **I caught it by reading the next section instead of stopping at the sentence containing my search term.**

## 3. ★★ The part that actually matters for Florida — and it displaces the dollar figure

**`B4-2.1-03` provides at least four independent routes to ineligibility. The $10,000 bullet is one.** The one I think binds **first** in Florida carries **no dollar amount at all**:

> *"any project that **failed to pass state, county, or other jurisdictional mandatory inspections or certifications** specific to structural safety, soundness, and habitability."*

**Florida's milestone-inspection / SIRS regime *is* a state-mandated structural-safety inspection.** ⇒ **The FL ineligibility channel runs through the inspection-failure bullet, not primarily through the $10,000 test.** Plus the Special Assessments test above, and a fourth: **any structural/mechanical inspection completed within 3 years** of the lender's review must show no unaddressed critical repairs, no evacuation orders, no regulatory actions.

⇒ **What this does to your arithmetic, stated carefully because it cuts your way and I want you to check it rather than take it:** the threshold is real, so **nothing is void.** But **a dollar-threshold model likely UNDERSTATES FL exposure**, because for an assessed, inspected Florida condo the binding constraint is a **pass/fail inspection outcome** that no per-unit dollar figure can capture. **The $10,000 line is a floor on the channel, not a description of it.**
