## 6. What this does and does not do to the Coral Bleaching thesis (no regrade)

The cost shift is a **fiscal amplifier on Florida's cost stack, not a new leg of the bank bridge.** It does not touch either insurer that the thesis watches most closely: FEMA pays no insured losses, so Citizens ($9.9B claims-paying vs a $5.0B 1-in-100 PML) and FHCF ($21.43B vs $17B; $23.09B for 2027-28 absent a covered event) are unchanged by it, and Isaias sits inside Citizens' own retention and below FHCF's $11.93B industry retention. What it does is move the **uninsured** part of a storm — debris, emergency work, public infrastructure, and household aid for minor damage — from Washington toward Tallahassee and the counties. Tallahassee can absorb it ($16.2B reserves; a 2024-type season's added state+local share is $0.65–1.5B), but the first-dollar account holds only $163.1M, and the counties carrying the local match are the same local governments Amendment 3 would cut by ~$4.9B in FY27-28 if it passes on 11/3. So the channel this strengthens is pillar 9 (state and municipal fiscal) and the household cost stack that feeds CARL; it does nothing to the bank rail (still NOT met, NOT armed), nothing to the six falsify criteria (criterion 6 resolves on the 11/3 ballot regardless), and nothing to the MSI leg. The one place it could become thesis-relevant is a **Panhandle county that cannot fund its match** after a denied declaration — that would be the first municipal-credit observation on CORAL's surface, and the Isaias decision is the dated test.
