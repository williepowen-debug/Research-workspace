**Three channels, named:**
1. **General funds pay the response, not the drainage utility.** Rescues (≈1,400), road rebuilding, debris and the local share of any FEMA public assistance come from general funds and road funds, which is where the property-tax cut lands. In Palm Bay that cut is about 40–45% of the base (corrected 21:4x from "two-thirds").
2. **Regional flood control is property-tax funded.** The two water management districts covering the flood counties lose $64.1M in FY27-28 and $158.4M a year at full effect, against SFWMD's own $3B backlog.
3. **The replacement route runs through the drainage fee.** Stormwater fees and assessments are the obvious way to refill budgets, and the Governor's proposed "taxpayer bill of rights" would block new fees and assessments. If enacted as described, it would cap the one revenue line that funds drainage in the epicentre. **Proposed, not law; no text; no date.**

**What is NOT established:** (a) any FY27-28 drainage or resilience budget cut (final FY27-28 spending choices not established); (b) whether the Palm Bay canals belong to the city, the county or a separate water-control district (not checked); (c) whether Amendment 3 changes the federal or state share of disaster costs (it does not, by its text; the local share is a budget question).
