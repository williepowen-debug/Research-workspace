**No condominium example could be established from free records tonight. The best-supported association case is a single-family HOA: Hamilton Bay, a gated community of about 164 homes in western West Palm Beach (Palm Beach County), inside the Hamal Community Development District (CDD).** Its streets and lakes flooded from the 11/01–02 record rain and stood for more than a week (CONFIRMED, CBS12 and the CDD's own 11/11 statement). The sampled address sits in FEMA Zone X, minimal flood hazard, outside the mapped flood zone (CONFIRMED for that point, PROXY for the community), so no lender required flood cover there. The cost sits with the CDD (pumps, repairs, engineer's review), which recovers costs through assessments on every platted lot. Its only debt is $3,979,000 of Series 2021 special-assessment refunding bonds (CONFIRMED, FY2025 audit). Who holds those bonds is UNKNOWN: EMMA refuses a plain client, and the audit names no purchaser. Whether any house took water inside, and whether owners carry flood cover, is UNKNOWN. Because it is not a condominium, the SIRS and milestone laws do not apply to it: this case shows the HOA/CDD channel, not the condo stack.

## What it shows, and what it does not

1. **A second public layer between the household and the bank:** in a CDD community the drainage system belongs to a special district that borrows against assessments on every lot. A flood that overwhelms it becomes a CDD cost recovered from all lots, flooded or not. That debt is small here ($3.98M over ~164 lots plus any other lands in the district; the district's full lot count was not read), senior to the homeowner through the assessment lien, and on this record not at risk.
2. **It is not the condo case.** No SIRS, no milestone inspection, no agency condo review. The combination Will asked about (inspection and reserve cost plus flood damage in the same building) has **no named example** in free records tonight.
