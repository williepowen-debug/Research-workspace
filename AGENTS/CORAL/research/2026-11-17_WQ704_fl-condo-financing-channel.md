# Is financed condo demand in Florida breaking away from single-family? (WQ-704)

**Desk:** CORAL · **Written:** Tue 2026-11-17, 08:4x–08:46 ET · **Commission:** PROME packet `inbox/2026-11-16_from-PROME_research-commission-WQ-704.md` (Will "yes", 2026-11-16 23:20 ET) · **Cost:** $0 · **Data file:** `research/2026-11-17_WQ704_financed-series.tsv` (37 months, every figure below traces to it)

**Primary source for every housing figure:** Florida Realtors statewide Monthly Market Detail PDFs, condo-townhouse and single-family: October 2026 (released Mon 2026-11-16, retrieved by CORAL 2026-11-17 08:37 ET; the files were not at their address at 12:46 ET 11/16), October 2025 and October 2024 editions (same publisher, same naming, retrieved 2026-11-17). "Financed" means closed sales minus sales paid fully in cash, in units. It counts every non-cash closing, including non-agency loans (non-QM, portfolio, seller financing), not just Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans.

---

## The answer

**No. Financed condo demand is not breaking away from single-family, and the Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac review change of 2026-08-03 has not shown up in the count of financed closings.** In the three months since that change (Aug–Oct 2026), Florida recorded 23.3 financed condo closings for every 100 financed single-family closings, the same as in Aug–Oct 2025 (23.3). Over the last 12 months financed condo closings rose 2.1%, exactly the same as financed single-family closings.

**The break happened earlier, and it was not specific to financing.** Financed condo closings fell from about 26 per 100 single-family closings (late 2023 to mid-2024) to about 23 per 100 by spring 2025, and have stayed there since. In the year in which that step happened (Nov 2024 to Oct 2025), condo **cash** closings fell 8.4% and condo **financed** closings fell 7.5%, almost the same, while single-family barely moved. A financing-specific squeeze would have cut financed buyers and left cash buyers standing. Both left together, which is what a condo-wide cost shock looks like (special assessments, insurance, association dues hit cash buyers too), and also what a coincident retreat of cash buyers would look like (see rival 1 below).

**What this does not show.** It does not show the new rules are harmless. Losing agency eligibility pushes a buyer to costlier non-agency loans, which this statistic still counts as "financed". Any effect on loan terms, prices or time on market in the buildings that failed review is invisible at statewide level. That would need building-level data, which no free source CORAL has found provides.

**CORAL-02, graded at the first print: FALSE.** October financed condo closings fell 8.3% year on year against 12.4% for single-family; the condo decline was smaller by 4.1 percentage points. ⚠️ Part of that margin is likely a base effect: October 2025 financed single-family closings were strong (+12.2% year on year, against +3.9% for condo), most likely the rebound from an October 2024 depressed by Hurricane Milton (an inference; the PDFs do not say why), so the October comparison flatters condo. Without October, August and September 2026 were mildly below 2025 (22.7 vs 23.4, 23.1 vs 23.5 per 100), within this series' normal monthly swing.

## How much is the financing channel rather than price or rates?

| Candidate driver | What it predicts | What the data show | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agency eligibility (review change 8/03, the $50K deductible cap 7/01, failed-inspection rule)** | Financed condo falls against SF; cash condo holds or rises (buyers switch) | Ratio flat on a year earlier over Aug–Oct; cash surge began Aug 2025, a year before the change | Not visible in counts. Effect on terms or price not measurable here |
| **Special assessments, insurance, dues (condo-wide cost)** | Condo cash and financed fall together against SF | Exactly that in Nov 2024–Oct 2025 (−8.4% and −7.5%) | Fits the 2024–25 step |
| **Mortgage rates** | Financed falls against cash in both types; condo may fall harder (dues count in debt-to-income) | Financed fell faster than cash in both; SF by more | Common effect; no extra condo hit in October |
| **Price** | Lower condo prices draw buyers, especially cash | Condo median −3.3%, SF +2.2%; sub-$200K condo closings +5.1%, $300K–$500K −11% to −14%; condo cash +9.7% on the year | Price is clearing stock to cash buyers; supports the supply-side leg, not the financing leg |

**Inference audit (charter step 13a), the claim "the 2024–25 step was condo-wide, not financing-specific":**
1. **Rival 1:** financing did bite financed buyers, and cash buyers left at the same time for a separate reason, for example the Canadian retreat (MARCO: Canadian return trips from the US 2-year −26.88% in October 2026, 12th month at or below −25%; national, not Florida-specific). This moves both legs the same way, so the equal declines **do not exclude** a financing effect then; they only fail to show one. The claim is therefore stated as "not shown to be financing-specific", not "not financing".
2. **What would refute "no break since 8/03":** the ratio falling below its year-earlier level by more than its 2026 range (worse than −1.5 per 100) for two months running, with condo cash holding.
3. **Confounds by sign:** two flatter the "no break" reading: the Milton base in October 2025 single-family (raises the Oct-2026 ratio), and the counting of non-agency loans as financed (hides switching). One works the other way: higher rates bite condo buyers harder through dues in debt-to-income, which would have produced a break and did not.
4. **Four legs for condo October:** price level −3.3% (median, a mix statistic) · volume −5.0% · realization firmer (93.3% vs 92.3%) · velocity faster (64 vs 76 days). Firmer realization and faster sales point to stock clearing near the ask, not to sellers cutting until it cleared; the lower median is a mix shift toward cheaper units (sub-$200K +5.1%).
5. **Shared-source check:** no peer figure is used for the conclusion. Canadian figure is MARCO's (its 11/16 packet), used only as a rival.
