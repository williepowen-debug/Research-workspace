# WATT — LESSONS (durable agent-level learning)

*Domain + process lessons accrued over sessions. Sourced + dated. The agent's own learning engine (distinct from DAEDALUS's fleet-level PATTERNS.tsv).*

| # | Date | Lesson | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-01 | 2026-07-10 | The structural leg (P2, capacity auctions) is higher-conviction than the live leg (P1, grid emergencies): P2 is confirmed ×2 and resolves on a fixed auction clock; P1 is n=1 and seasonal. Weight the thesis on P2; use P1 as the tripwire, not the anchor. (Mechanism-vs-thermometer.) | build session |
| L-02 | 2026-07-10 | LMP is the real price leg but is gated on a one-time human PJM registration (`PJM_API_KEY`). Until then, EIA-930 demand-vs-peak + emergency postings are the P1 proxy — a demand read is not a price read; say so, don't imply scarcity pricing from load alone. | power_watch.py header |
| L-03 | 2026-07-10 | Inherited a leg ≠ owning a leg: P3/P4 arrived with HENRY's context but no WATT-pulled read. An inherited read is a GAP to close, not a live channel. Channels-first #1 guard applies from birth. | scaffold |
| L-04 | 2026-07-12 | The official PJM LMP (Data Miner 2) isn't the only price signal available. EIA publishes a free, no-key, biweekly ICE-sourced wholesale price file (`eia.gov/electricity/wholesale`) covering PJM Western Hub among others — a real-trade proxy that closed both the P1 "LMP not wired" honest-wall and the P4 "no spark-spread read" gap in one pull, and immediately surfaced a $574/MWh Orange-band spike (7/1) that the postings-only P1 read had missed entirely. Lesson: a "gated behind a key" honest-wall is worth a second check for a free proxy before treating it as a hard blocker. | second session |
| L-05 | 2026-07-12 | Spark spread does NOT compress during the heat-stress regime that drives P1 — it widens (gas is the marginal price-setter and captures the full scarcity rent). Compression is a distinct regime (likely oversupply / high-renewable-curtailment days) that this thesis hasn't yet tested. Don't assume P1-stress and P4-compression move together just because they share the heat-dome antecedent — the antecedent is shared, the channel *response* is not. | second session, spark-spread first pull |
| L-06 | 2026-07-12 | A wholesale power print carries TWO dates — trade date and delivery date — and the delivery date is the price's true as-of (what the power is FOR). Round-1 KB rows cited the $574.04 spike by trade date (7/1); the wired instrument clarified it as delivery 7/2 — one day tighter against the 7/3 EEA2. Cite delivery dates; keep trade date as provenance. | rd-2 leg-4 build |
| L-07 | 2026-07-12 | Don't read a raw growth RATE as confirming the high end of a range — convert to the same units first. Rd-2 I annotated "+77% capex growth is directionally consistent with WoodMac's 55GW"; VULCAN's actual capex→MW conversion put the funded band at ~14-37GW, so capex funds the LOW/official 32GW and the 55GW is ~50% ABOVE band top (unfunded). The rate felt big, but the level it implies was below the figure I attached it to. Convert to comparable units before claiming corroboration. | rd-3, VULCAN band handoff |
| L-08 | 2026-07-12 | Double-count guard for shared-dollar figures: hyperscaler capex ($) and IPP PPA-MW are two VIEWS of the same spend (spend side vs utility-contract side) — never additive. Adopted as a standing P3 rule. Generalizes: when two agents' metrics trace to one root cash flow, reconcile-to-one, never sum. | rd-3, VULCAN handoff §2 |
| L-09 | 2026-07-16 | **Size the episode against the SEASON before making a status call — the instrument's 24h peak window is too narrow to do it.** `power_watch.py` reports demand vs a *24-hour* peak; at 92.1% that read looked like de-escalation, while the emergency posting looked like escalation. Neither framing was decidable until a 1,500-hour EIA-930 pull showed the 7/15-16 episode is **milder than 7/1-3 on all three axes** (159,046 vs 162,648 MW; EEA-1 vs EEA2; $410 vs $574) — which settled it instantly. A status call needs a season-length denominator; the boot instrument gives a day-length one. **Pull the wide window before any color change.** | EEA-1 adjudication |
| L-10 | 2026-07-16 | **A posting's severity RUNG is the threshold test — not whether it's "on the board," and not how alarming its title reads.** "Maximum Generation Emergency/Load Management Alert — Capacity Emergency — NERC EEA 1" is a maximally alarming string that sits **one rung below** WATT's pre-registered EEA2+ Red bar. Pre-registered bands exist precisely so an alarming-but-sub-threshold event can't drift the call. Related: **an EEA-1 does NOT resolve WATT-02** (which requires EEA2+) — near-miss, don't bank it. | EEA-1 adjudication |
| L-11 | 2026-07-16 | **Read the posting DETAIL page, not the board row.** The board row for #105399 showed "07.15.2026 16:25" and an empty end time, which reads as "posted yesterday, still lingering." The detail page said it was **issued FOR 00:01–23:59 on 07.16** — forward-issued for the whole operating day, i.e. *more* live than the board row implied. Board rows carry start-time provenance; the message body carries the effective window. Cf. L-06 (trade date vs delivery date) — same failure shape: **the summary surface shows the wrong one of two dates.** | EEA-1 adjudication |
| L-12 | 2026-07-16 | **An emergency episode is not automatically a cost event, and the distinction is the whole C3 read.** The chain fired end-to-end (heat → +25% demand → EEA-1 → $410.55 LMP) yet transmitted ~nothing to cost: the spike retraced in ~4 hours against an 8.66¢/kWh industrial bill. **P1 spikes are the thermometer; P2 (capacity at cap, annual pass-through cadence) is the cost channel.** When a channel fires, ask what it transmits *to*, not just that it fired — a confirmed mechanism with no downstream magnitude is a confirmed mechanism, not a signal to act on. | EEA-1 adjudication / C3 read |
