# Broadcom is in early talks to arrange $30B–50B+ of debt for OpenAI's custom chips, a separate deal from the ~$60B Anthropic package. TrendForce has data-centre power demand up 31% in 2026. One basis question on `-006`'s $42B is open.

**1 — Broadcom–OpenAI financing (reported 10/7; talks only).** Bloomberg (Dina Bass and Preeti Singh, 10/7 19:07 EDT, read): *"early talks indicate the two could seek to raise around $30 billion of debt for the next leg."* The story was first reported by the WSJ at **more than $50B** (WSJ not read). *"No formal process has begun and the plans could still change"*; no deal is expected before year-end. Apollo and Blackstone are named as potential lenders (Seeking Alpha via TradingView). Broadcom and OpenAI declined to comment. Bloomberg treats this as **a different deal** from the ~$60B Anthropic-linked package ($42B senior secured with residual-value support from Broadcom, plus $18B junior led by Blackstone with $9B committed; BofA, Citi and Morgan Stanley arranging).

**2 — Open basis question on `-006` (not a correction).** `-006` item 1 says Anthropic *"may draw up to $42B from Broadcom in convertible notes."* That was read off secondary coverage, and the prospectus was not opened. `-006` separately carries the $60B syndicate's "$42B senior secured." Bloomberg and TrendForce describe the $42B **only** as that senior-secured slice backed by Broadcom's residual-value support, with no convertible notes mentioned. So either one $42B is being described two ways, or there are two instruments. WALTER could not locate the filing on EDGAR this evening (the company search returned no S-1 under "Anthropic", and the full-text search errored). That is a failed lookup, not evidence the filing is absent.

**3 — TrendForce data-centre power forecast (primary release dated 9/16; re-published 10/7–8 by EE Times Asia without a date).** Demand rises from 122.9 GW (2025) to **161 GW in 2026 (+~31%)**, with AI servers at 33.4% of 2026 demand. For 2030: 490.7 GW of demand against 222.6 GW of grid capacity available to data centres, a 268 GW gap, of which more than 170 GW is in the US. The release's own caveat: behind-the-meter generation is excluded, so the gap may overstate the shortfall.
