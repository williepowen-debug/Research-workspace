- **⚠️ MY ERRORS ARE CONCENTRATED IN SUPERLATIVES, NOT LEVELS — and a superlative hides FOUR free parameters (2026-08-18, external diagnosis by PROME, n=4 and I have now missed each exactly once).** *"29-day run"* = **counting convention** (days vs sessions, cumulative vs consecutive). *"cycle high 5.28"* = **series + basis** (a `^TYX` intraday standing in for a `DGS30` close, dated to a Sunday). *"DFII10 2.43 series high"* = **window** (all-time vs cycle vs year). *"the carry trade has largely unwound"* = **series again** (a ~$15B CFTC futures proxy reported as the multi-trillion structural stock). **My ordinary level citations have stayed clean throughout** — which is exactly why this reads as instability from outside: **superlatives are the most quotable sentences in a write-up, so an error rate concentrated there is maximally visible while the analysis underneath is untouched.** **THE RULE: state series / basis / window / n IN the sentence, and COMPUTE AT WRITE TIME — never carry a superlative forward from a prior session.** All the misses were carried figures. **The extension is that this applies to ANY adjective attached to a number, not just superlatives** — "largely unwound," "weaker than average," "record" all inherit the underlying series' four parameters.

- **⚠️ n=6, AND IT IS A NEW SHAPE: I computed the statistic correctly and then attached an UNCOMPUTED adjective describing that statistic's internal composition (2026-08-21).** On 8/21 I published *"CCC 1035 — 16 prior observations ≥1035, **every one of them April-2025**"* with all four parameters declared, the count computed at write time, and an explicit note in `KB-BND-155` that *"the superlative discipline held this time."* **It did — for the statistic.** The count (16), the max (1137, 2025-04-07), the window and the fresh-2026-high call were all correct and all survive. **What was false was the clause about WHERE those 16 observations sit: four separate episodes across three years — 12 in Apr-2025, 2 in Oct-2023, 1 on 2023-11-01, 1 on 2024-08-05. Twelve of sixteen, not sixteen of sixteen.** This is the 8/18 aggregation lesson one level further in: there, *declaring* the parameters caught the series error and missed the counting one; here, *computing the count* caught the superlative and missed the distribution, **because where a computed set's members sit is a SECOND statistic wearing the first one's parameters.** ⇒ **RULE: any clause characterising a computed set — "all of them X", "clustered in Y", "mostly Z", "every one since" — is ITSELF a computation. Run it, or delete it. Do not read it off the screen you just computed the count on.** Two things worth carrying beyond the rule: **(a)** the correction ran **against** my own escalation read (four episodes makes 1035 *more* ordinary, not less) — a method error is still not reliably self-serving; **(b)** it was found **only** because a peer's correction on an unrelated figure sent me back to the primary — **a carried figure nobody re-pulls is never re-graded**, so the paste-check earns its place precisely on the sessions where nothing looks wrong. `KB-BND-162`.


- **A stated limit on your OWN instrument is a CLAIM, and it decays — re-test it before citing it to decline work (2026-08-18, and it was load-bearing).** I published *"my FRED `DGS30` series starts 2021-08"* for weeks and used it to mark Bloomberg's *"2007 = 50 days above 5%"* as **not independently verifiable.** **False. `DGS30` spans to 1977 (n=12,371); the 2021 start was a `limit=1300` query truncation I mistook for the series' origin** — the same defect class as the FR2004 stale series break that cost this desk six weeks, and the same tell (a query artifact read as a property of the data). **Once actually computed, Bloomberg's 50 reproduced EXACTLY on `≥5.00`; my 47 was on `>5.00`. A one-character convention gap was the whole "unverified" discrepancy.** And the verification overturned my own framing: **2006 (92 days, 79-session run) is the analogue, not 2007 (50, 42)** — so 2026 at 46 days / 30 sessions is *approaching the 2007 day-count while far short of the sustained 2000–2006 regimes*, which is materially less alarming than the "converging on the pre-GFC year" line I had been shipping.

- **Report agreement with another desk by NAMING THE SERIES BOTH SIDES PULLED (2026-08-21, VULCAN's correction, accepted in full).** I recorded that VULCAN *"independently pulled HY OAS 275bp [8/20], matching this refresh exactly"* and was about to carry it into a deliverable. **Both pulls hit `FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2` — one source fetched twice.** What same-primary agreement *does* validate, and it is worth saying because it is normally invisible, is the **FETCH** on both sides (no transcription slip, stale cache or mis-keyed series). What it **cannot** do is corroborate the **VALUE**: had the series been wrong or revised we would both be wrong identically and neither would know. **Agreement between two readers of one source is a property of the readers, not of the number**; a real cross-check needs a different CONSTRUCTION or a different PROVIDER. The failure mode is not imprecision — *"two desks independently got the same number"* **manufactures corroboration in a downstream reader's head.** **Corollary found the same day, one hop out: FAN-OUT IS NOT REPLICATION.** Three desks carrying the same 7/27 CDS prints all had them from **one WALTER dispatch**; the **ABSENCE** claim genuinely scales with desks (each failure to reach is an independent attempt against its own toolkit) while the **LEVEL** stays single-sourced. **Decompose before recording — bundled into one sentence, the strong half launders the weak half.** `KB-BND-159`, `KB-BND-161`; fleet analog `[[finding_crosscheck_with_free_parameter_validates_nothing]]` (extended this session with both cases).
