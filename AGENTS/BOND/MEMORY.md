- **A RUN and a COUNT are two different statistics, and publishing one under the other's name is invisible for weeks (2026-08-15).** STATUS carried *"30Y in a 29-day run above 5%"* from 7/28. The 29 was neither — it conflated calendar days with sessions and/or the cumulative count with the run. **Nobody catches this by re-reading, because every version of the sentence is plausible; you catch it only by recomputing.** Two operational rules: (a) whenever a "streak" or "run" claim goes on a surface, **write both numbers** — consecutive AND cumulative — because the interesting one is usually the cumulative and the impressive-sounding one is usually the run; (b) **compute streak/extremum statistics off FRED constant-maturity closes, never off yfinance bars** — WALTER `SIG-W-20260813-002` documents intermittent NULL bars there, and a null **silently bridges a streak and truncates a max**, i.e. it corrupts exactly these two statistics and nothing else. - **⚠️ MY ERRORS ARE CONCENTRATED IN SUPERLATIVES, NOT LEVELS — and a superlative hides FOUR free parameters (2026-08-18, external diagnosis by PROME, n=4 and I have now missed each exactly once).** *"29-day run"* = **counting convention** (days vs sessions, cumulative vs consecutive). *"cycle high 5.28"* = **series + basis** (a `^TYX` intraday standing in for a `DGS30` close, dated to a Sunday). *"DFII10 2.43 series high"* = **window** (all-time vs cycle vs year). *"the carry trade has largely unwound"* = **series again** (a ~$15B CFTC futures proxy reported as the multi-trillion structural stock). **My ordinary level citations have stayed clean throughout** — which is exactly why this reads as instability from outside: **superlatives are the most quotable sentences in a write-up, so an error rate concentrated there is maximally visible while the analysis underneath is untouched.** **THE RULE: state series / basis / window / n IN the sentence, and COMPUTE AT WRITE TIME — never carry a superlative forward from a prior session.** All the misses were carried figures. **The extension is that this applies to ANY adjective attached to a number, not just superlatives** — "largely unwound," "weaker than average," "record" all inherit the underlying series' four parameters.
- **⚠️ STATING THE FOUR PARAMETERS IS NOT ENOUGH — they attach to the COMPUTATION, not just to the data (2026-08-18, third occurrence in one day, inside the correction that diagnosed the first two).** I declared *series / basis / window / n* for `DGS30`, verified the day-counts, and then published run-lengths computed **per-year on `>5.00`** while the day-counts beside them used **`≥5.00` whole-series** — **two counting conventions inside one table.** Per-year silently truncates any run crossing a year boundary: my "458" was really **721**, my "79" was **92**, my "42" was **44**. **Declaring the parameters caught the SERIES error and missed the COUNTING one, because *per-year vs whole-series* is an aggregation choice I never declared to myself.** ⇒ **For any DERIVED statistic (run, streak, max, rate, percentile), name the aggregation method in the sentence — "maximal run, ≥5.00, session closes, whole-series scan" — not merely the series it came from.** The discipline is only real when it is mechanical; mine wasn't, three hours after I wrote it down. *(And the correction ran in my thesis's favour — the true post-2007 comparison is **11 sessions**, so the current 30 is ~2.7× anything in 19 years and "longest since 2007" **undersold** it. A method error is not reliably self-serving, which is why you cannot audit by checking whether a number flatters you.)*

A one-character convention gap was the whole "unverified" discrepancy.** And the verification overturned my own framing: **2006 (92 days, 79-session run) is the analogue, not 2007 (50, 42)** — so 2026 at 46 days / 30 sessions is *approaching the 2007 day-count while far short of the sustained 2000–2006 regimes*, which is materially less alarming than the "converging on the pre-GFC year" line I had been shipping.

- **⚠️ n=6, AND IT IS A NEW SHAPE: I computed the statistic correctly and then attached an UNCOMPUTED adjective describing that statistic's internal composition (2026-08-21).** On 8/21 I published *"CCC 1035 — 16 prior observations ≥1035, **every one of them April-2025**"* with all four parameters declared, the count computed at write time, and an explicit note in `KB-BND-155` that *"the superlative discipline held this time."* **It did — for the statistic.** The count (16), the max (1137, 2025-04-07), the window and the fresh-2026-high call were all correct and all survive. **What was false was the clause about WHERE those 16 observations sit: four separate episodes across three years — 12 in Apr-2025, 2 in Oct-2023, 1 on 2023-11-01, 1 on 2024-08-05. Twelve of sixteen, not sixteen of sixteen.** This is the 8/18 aggregation lesson one level further in: there, *declaring* the parameters caught the series error and missed the counting one; here, *computing the count* caught the superlative and missed the distribution, **because where a computed set's members sit is a SECOND statistic wearing the first one's parameters.** ⇒ **RULE: any clause characterising a computed set — "all of them X", "clustered in Y", "mostly Z", "every one since" — is ITSELF a computation. Run it, or delete it. Do not read it off the screen you just computed the count on.** Two things worth carrying beyond the rule: **(a)** the correction ran **against** my own escalation read (four episodes makes 1035 *more* ordinary, not less) — a method error is still not reliably self-serving; **(b)** it was found **only** because a peer's correction on an unrelated figure sent me back to the primary — **a carried figure nobody re-pulls is never re-graded**, so the paste-check earns its place precisely on the sessions where nothing looks wrong. `KB-BND-162`.

- **Report agreement with another desk by NAMING THE SERIES BOTH SIDES PULLED (2026-08-21, VULCAN's correction, accepted in full).** I recorded that VULCAN *"independently pulled HY OAS 275bp [8/20], matching this refresh exactly"* and was about to carry it into a deliverable. **Both pulls hit `FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2` — one source fetched twice.** What same-primary agreement *does* validate, and it is worth saying because it is normally invisible, is the **FETCH** on both sides (no transcription slip, stale cache or mis-keyed series). What it **cannot** do is corroborate the **VALUE**: had the series been wrong or revised we would both be wrong identically and neither would know. **Agreement between two readers of one source is a property of the readers, not of the number**; a real cross-check needs a different CONSTRUCTION or a different PROVIDER. The failure mode is not imprecision — *"two desks independently got the same number"* **manufactures corroboration in a downstream reader's head.** **Corollary found the same day, one hop out: FAN-OUT IS NOT REPLICATION.** Three desks carrying the same 7/27 CDS prints all had them from **one WALTER dispatch**; the **ABSENCE** claim genuinely scales with desks (each failure to reach is an independent attempt against its own toolkit) while the **LEVEL** stays single-sourced. **Decompose before recording — bundled into one sentence, the strong half launders the weak half.** `KB-BND-159`, `KB-BND-161`; fleet analog `[[finding_crosscheck_with_free_parameter_validates_nothing]]` (extended this session with both cases).

- **A percentile threshold is ONE rule with a DIFFERENT effective strictness in every stratum — measure the bite per stratum before adopting it, and never base-rate it pooled (2026-08-27, found by computing the adoption's own counterfactual).** MATRIX_V2 §3c replaced a trailing-12 **min**-based indirect bar with a **15th-percentile** bar, the point being a looser, better-calibrated trigger. Measured at adoption, the new bar sits above the old min by **+4.84pp at the 2Y, +0.82pp at the 7Y and +0.24pp at the 5Y** — at the 5Y it is barely a change at all, because that tenor's trailing-12 indirect distribution is bunched hard against its own bottom. **A percentile is a statement about a distribution's SHAPE, so a single percentile rule applied across strata with different shapes is several different rules wearing one name.** ⇒ **The Will-ruled base-rating must report hit-rate and separation PER TENOR; a pooled figure would average three rules and describe none of them.** Two transferable halves: **(a)** the uneven bite was invisible in the spec and appeared only on computing the numbers — *adopting a recalibration without measuring its bite per stratum is adopting an unknown*; **(b)** it surfaced because the adoption's **counterfactual** was computed rather than asserted (*"would this have fired on the prints I missed?"*) — and that same check caught a figure I had already written into the draft from memory. **Compute the counterfactual; it audits the rule and the author at once.** `KB-BND-173`, `KB-BND-174`.

- **A conjunctive test that fails by a rounding-scale margin is evidence the CONJUNCTION is brittle — not evidence the thing it tests turned (2026-08-27, `BND-19`).** The row required indirect ≥ its own trailing-12 median at **all three** new-issue tenors. It resolved **FALSE** on a single leg by **0.24pp** (5Y 61.51 vs 61.75), with the 2Y clearing by **+8.36pp**. **Scored FALSE as written — a registered conjunction is not re-specified after the fact, and the direction of the miss does not license a re-read.** But the REPORTING must carry the margin, because *"the stronger form of the thesis failed"* and *"demand was normal-or-better at two of three tenors and a quarter-point short at the third"* are the same verdict and radically different evidence. ⚠️ **The asymmetry is the trap: a conjunction's FALSE is cheap to produce (any one leg, any margin) while its TRUE requires every leg — so a conjunction's failures are systematically less informative than its successes, and a book scored only on the binary will read as more decisive than the evidence.** Log the spec observation for the base-rating; never fix the spec on the session it costs you. Related: `[[finding_broken_conjunction_leaves_its_other_legs_unrecorded]]` (record every leg — done here: the 7Y leg is graded even though the row was already FALSE).

- **When your evidence for a peer's error is a RELAY, write the correction as a CONDITIONAL — the mechanical form, adopted 2026-08-27 (PROME's suggestion, after it saved me from a false correction).** A doorbell told me a peer's board carried a "leg satisfied" marker that would have been wrong for the prediction it appeared beside. I wrote *"**IF** your board carries a 'leg satisfied' marker on 8/21, it should come off"* rather than *"your board is wrong."* **It didn't.** The annotation was TRUE and correctly scoped on their artifact to a different desk's frozen leg; **their MESSAGE had transplanted it into my prediction's path sentence.** The defect was in the relay, and the artifact was clean.
  **⇒ A relay's defect reads as the source's defect** — a message is not the surface it describes, and the transplant is invisible because both halves are individually true. **The fleet holds the OUTWARD half already** (`[[finding_asymmetric_rigor_counterparty_claims]]` — name your check's REFERENT before publishing a correction of another desk's figure). **This is the operational form of it: when the only thing you have read is a message ABOUT an artifact, the conditional is not politeness, it is the accurate epistemic state**, and it costs nothing — a peer whose surface IS wrong acts on it identically. **Reserve the flat assertion for when you have opened the artifact.**
  ⚠️ **Recorded as luck-adjacent, not as a good call:** I hedged out of habit, not because I had reasoned about relay-vs-artifact. The habit is what made it survive; **the reasoning is what makes it repeatable, which is why it is written down.** Same session, the mirror case: MIDAS caught a fused figure of mine **because they read the ARTIFACT and tried to reproduce the number rather than reading my summary of it.** Both directions of the same rule in one day.
