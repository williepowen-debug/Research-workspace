## Durable Learnings (BOND-local; fleet-wide lessons live in auto-memory, referenced not restated)
- **"Expensive, not broken" (BOND-core):** the long end clearing demand *at price* is categorically different from a *demand hole* (mechanical failure → dealer warehousing → funding stress). Keeping these distinct IS the thesis. Held **eleven straight** benign resolutions through 2026-07-27 (BND-05 F · 06 T · 07 T · 08 F · 09 F · 6/23–25 cluster C+ · **BND-11 7/9 NOT FIRED** · 7/22 20Y-R · 7/22 40Y JGB · 7/23 TIPS · 7/27 2Y+5Y; BND-10 VOID on the kinetic-Iran clause). **BND-12 was its hardest test and it passed the right way: the threshold FIRED (30Y >5.00 for 8 straight) and the mechanism held.** Say "threshold fired, mechanism intact" out loud each time — the two get conflated under time pressure, and the whole thesis is the distinction.

- **⚠️ A TAIL CANNOT BE SCORED FROM TREASURYDIRECT — never key a gate on one (learned the expensive way, 2026-07-28).** A tail is `high yield − when-issued yield at the bid deadline`, and **TreasuryDirect publishes no when-issued.** This is a *structural* limit of BOND's primary source, not a per-session data gap, so a tail-keyed threshold is **unscoreable by construction and can never fire on evidence.** It had quietly infected the thesis kill, the TLT-put re-arm, the KEY THRESHOLDS table, PROTOCOL's outbound triggers, and a joint falsifier co-registered with HENRY — that falsifier then "passed" on 7/27 purely because its DENY branch was unfireable. **Every auction gate is now composition-keyed** (indirect % and dealer % of *competitive accepted*, benchmarked to trailing-12 **per tenor**). Wire-reported tails are `[med-conf]`, recordable in notes, and may never move a branch. *Corollary: when a spec can't fire, that is a **Status** defect (unscoreable), not a confidence adjustment — cf. `[[finding_resolvability_defect_is_status_not_confidence]]`.*

- **Cover and composition can move in OPPOSITE directions — grade the pair, and let the cover fire the vector without letting it kill the thesis (2026-07-27, the worked example).** The 5Y printed **BTC 2.28, its lowest since Sept-2022**, while **indirect ROSE with duration across the same session** (2Y 56.59% → 5Y 59.24%) and dealers took only +0.64pp more than the prior month. A duration-demand/term-premium story requires foreign money stepping *away* from duration; it stepped *toward* it. So: **fire the pre-registered cover trigger anyway** (I moved auction health 2→3 despite having a benign story — that is what pre-registration is *for*), but **only a composition failure kills the thesis.** Also hold open the non-obvious third cause: a **basis-trade withdrawal** removes repo-levered bid and produces thin cover with *unchanged* composition, because the departing bidder is neither foreign nor a dealer — don't force auction reads into a policy-path-vs-term-premium binary (FL-BND-13).
- **The demand-hole hides — grade COMPOSITION, not the headline cover (masked-hole discriminator):** a *firm BTC with no tail* can still be a demand hole if **indirect (foreign/custodial) falls while directs/dealers backfill** — the domestic bid papers over the foreign step-back, so the headline BTC/tail read clean. Reconciled with LIQUID to **ONE figure: 30Y indirect as %-of-competitive-accepted vs benchmark** (June-6/11 = 60.0%); a mechanically-clean print with indirect <55% + directs/dealers backfilling scores BND-11 TRUE **but still escalates the thesis.** This is the auction-level analog of the flow-level mask (April +$206B foreign inflow hid China's genuine UST exit) — the demand-hole hides the same way at both altitudes. Grade to the SAME denominator across agents (competitive-accepted %) to avoid a spurious 1–2pp divergence.
- **Correlation read-through moves the PRICE leg, not the COMPOSITION leg (JGB→UST):** Japan is a term-premium *correlation amplifier*, not a UST-flow seller (net UST buyer). So the JGB 30Y auction is a leading indicator for the US 30Y **term-premium/tail leg only** — near-silent on the **indirect-composition leg** (foreign-official base ≠ Japanese lifers). Weight a foreign leading indicator on the leg its buyer base actually touches; a JGB tail can't fire a US *composition* marker (indirect<52%) alone. And beware **one-root circularity** — JGB / US-30Y-level / thin-bid can be correlated expressions of the same term-premium root, not independent votes.
- **Confirm the instrument before keying a demand gate:** the 5/21 "10Y reopening" was a 10Y *TIPS* reopen (CUSIP 91282CPU9), not nominal — different buyer base; conflating them mis-specified an add-gate. - **⚠️ FR2004 / NY Fed primary-dealer API: NEVER hardcode a series break, and never hand-query it (2026-07-28).** The API partitions data into **series breaks**; a query against a stale one (`SBN2022`) returns **HTTP 200 with real data that simply stops at 2024-07-02** — which reads exactly like *"the API caps pre-2026."* Nothing errors. BOND carried this as an "env-blocked data gap" for **6 weeks**, re-attempted it three times, and **escalated it to Will — all without auditing the failing path.** Live break is `SBN2024`. Generalisation worth carrying: **an escalation raised before auditing the path spends the escalation** — and when a question has survived N re-attempts, the blocker is usually the PATH, not the data (`[[finding_audit_resolution_path_before_reattempt]]`, `[[finding_stale_executable_exits_clean]]`).

- **A RUN and a COUNT are two different statistics, and publishing one under the other's name is invisible for weeks (2026-08-15).** STATUS carried *"30Y in a 29-day run above 5%"* from 7/28. Recomputed off FRED `DGS30`: on the day I wrote it the **consecutive run was 16 sessions**; the figures that are actually true are **28 consecutive sessions (7/07→8/13)** and **44 cumulative days above 5.00% in 2026**. The 29 was neither — it conflated calendar days with sessions and/or the cumulative count with the run. **Nobody catches this by re-reading, because every version of the sentence is plausible; you catch it only by recomputing.** Two operational rules: (a) whenever a "streak" or "run" claim goes on a surface, **write both numbers** — consecutive AND cumulative — because the interesting one is usually the cumulative and the impressive-sounding one is usually the run; (b) **compute streak/extremum statistics off FRED constant-maturity closes, never off yfinance bars** — WALTER `SIG-W-20260813-002` documents intermittent NULL bars there, and a null **silently bridges a streak and truncates a max**, i.e. it corrupts exactly these two statistics and nothing else. Fleet analog `[[finding_plausible_stale_value_evades_review]]`; audit-by-recomputation, not by reading.
- **A threshold copied off a headline figure inherits that figure's INSTRUMENT and BASIS, not just its value (2026-08-15).** `T6`'s HOLD/EXTEND branch was frozen with a leg reading *"DGS30 prints a fresh high **>5.28%**"* — but **DGS30 has never printed 5.28**; its 2026 max is **5.27 (7/31)**. The 5.28 was a yfinance `^TYX` **intraday high** (5.281), and the date it was cited with, "8/2", is a **Sunday**. So the leg grades one series against another series' intraday extreme and is **unreachable by construction** — the retired auction-tail defect in a new costume (a threshold the grading source cannot produce). **The check is one line and belongs at authorship: pull the grading series' own max/min and confirm the threshold sits inside its achievable range.** Found 14 days before the hard close rather than at resolution — the first time this class has been caught early, and only because a WALTER signal asked a question about the instrument instead of supplying an answer. Corollary that made flagging it worth anything: **the defective leg favoured my own side of the test**, so raising it before the trigger fired was cheap and raising it after would have been worthless.

- **The staleness that costs you is never in the number — it is in the SPEC and the CALENDAR (2026-08-18, full-document sweep).** A Will-tasked sweep of every BOND core doc found the refreshable numbers were the *cheapest* part. What actually cost the desk: **(1)** the August quarterly refunding — **$125B, the quarter's largest supply event** — ran **ungraded** because it was **never on `CATALYSTS.tsv` at all**, so no boot, no monitor and no dashboard could surface it. A missing docket row is invisible in a way a stale number is not: **a stale number looks wrong eventually; a missing event looks like nothing.** **(2)** The thesis kill, the TLT re-arm, `PROTOCOL.md`'s outbound trigger and `TRADE.md`'s reactivation matrix all hardcoded **the 7Y's** composition cut-offs (`<56.4% / >13.2%`) as though general — the 10Y's indirect min is **63.95%** and the 30Y's **59.52%**, so the gate was simply the wrong bar on four surfaces, and in THESIS it sat *directly beside that file's own instruction not to reuse the 7Y numbers.* **Operational rule: at every closeout, check the DOCKET for the next 10 days against the issuer's own calendar, not against your own file** — `TA_WS/securities/upcoming` is one call. And **when you fix a spec defect, fix it by PATTERN across the tree, never by the line list in front of you**: I fixed 56.4 in THESIS and TRADE and `consumer_check.py --self` then caught it still live in STATUS and PROTOCOL at closeout.
- **My auction pre-registrations keep failing the same way, and it is always the same failure: a gate nothing can fire (2026-08-18, n=4 now).** The retired tail (unscoreable from TreasuryDirect by construction) · `BND-13`'s non-exhaustive branch set (a 13.05% dealer print would have graded nothing; we landed **0.03pp** away) · T6's `>5.28%` leg keyed to a level `DGS30` had never printed · and now the 20Y's composition-failure test, whose indirect-MIN and dealer-MAX **come from the same single auction (2026-02-18)**, so it fires only on a repeat of that exact print or worse. **The tell is always available at authorship and never at resolution: pull the grading series' own achievable range, and base-rate the gate JOINTLY, before freezing it.** Fleet: `[[finding_compound_gate_jointly_unsatisfiable]]`, `[[finding_base_rate_the_threshold_before_building_it]]`.

- **⚠️ MY ERRORS ARE CONCENTRATED IN SUPERLATIVES, NOT LEVELS — and a superlative hides FOUR free parameters (2026-08-18, external diagnosis by PROME, n=4 and I have now missed each exactly once).** *"29-day run"* = **counting convention** (days vs sessions, cumulative vs consecutive). *"cycle high 5.28"* = **series + basis** (a `^TYX` intraday standing in for a `DGS30` close, dated to a Sunday). *"DFII10 2.43 series high"* = **window** (all-time vs cycle vs year). *"the carry trade has largely unwound"* = **series again** (a ~$15B CFTC futures proxy reported as the multi-trillion structural stock). **My ordinary level citations have stayed clean throughout** — which is exactly why this reads as instability from outside: **superlatives are the most quotable sentences in a write-up, so an error rate concentrated there is maximally visible while the analysis underneath is untouched.** **THE RULE: state series / basis / window / n IN the sentence, and COMPUTE AT WRITE TIME — never carry a superlative forward from a prior session.** All the misses were carried figures. **The extension is that this applies to ANY adjective attached to a number, not just superlatives** — "largely unwound," "weaker than average," "record" all inherit the underlying series' four parameters.
- **A stated limit on your OWN instrument is a CLAIM, and it decays — re-test it before citing it to decline work (2026-08-18, and it was load-bearing).** I published *"my FRED `DGS30` series starts 2021-08"* for weeks and used it to mark Bloomberg's *"2007 = 50 days above 5%"* as **not independently verifiable.** **False. `DGS30` spans to 1977 (n=12,371); the 2021 start was a `limit=1300` query truncation I mistook for the series' origin** — the same defect class as the FR2004 stale series break that cost this desk six weeks, and the same tell (a query artifact read as a property of the data). **Once actually computed, Bloomberg's 50 reproduced EXACTLY on `≥5.00`; my 47 was on `>5.00`. A one-character convention gap was the whole "unverified" discrepancy.** And the verification overturned my own framing: **2006 (92 days, 79-session run) is the analogue, not 2007 (50, 42)** — so 2026 at 46 days / 30 sessions is *approaching the 2007 day-count while far short of the sustained 2000–2006 regimes*, which is materially less alarming than the "converging on the pre-GFC year" line I had been shipping.
- **A regime-label change must write back to the DURABLE doc in the same session it is ruled (2026-08-18).** The 8/10 C-36 downgrade reached `STATUS.md` and did not reach `thesis/THESIS.md` for **8 days**; `NEXUS_BRIEF` sat **18 days** stale. **The gap between what this desk thinks and what its files say is what manufactures the appearance of changing its mind** — an outside reader diffing two of my surfaces sees a flip-flop that never happened. Pair every label ruling with the THESIS write-back and the downstream brief refresh **in the same commit**.
