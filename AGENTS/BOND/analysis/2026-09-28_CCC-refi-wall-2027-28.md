## Bottom line
**The mechanism is real and it is narrow.** A primary source (PIMCO, data as of 8/26/2026) says CCC bonds due 2027–28 would see coupons roughly **double** if refinanced at index yields. The same source says the step-up is **negligible for the HY index as a whole and small for BB**. CCC yields have risen another **+157bp** since that measurement. In **aggregate** dollars the wall is larger in **2028–29 than in 2027**. *(Narrowed 9/28: that is an index-level shape. It does not rule out near-term refinancing stress for CCC issuers or single borrowers with 2026–27 maturities, where the coupon step-up applies now; the CCC-only 2027 figure is a GAP.)* Much of the low-rated 2028 debt is **floating-rate leveraged loans**. Those loans are already paying today's rates every quarter, so for them the maturity question is market access, not a coupon reset. **No free source gives a CCC-only dollar wall or a CCC industry split: both are GAPs.**

## 4 · Is the wall shrinking?
- **Yes, for the market as a whole.** S&P's spec-grade peak year moved **2028 (Jan 2026) → 2029 (Apr) → 2031 (Jul)**, as refinancing pushed maturities out.
- **Extensions by tier:** issuers rated below B- were ~**25%** of 2025 loan amend-and-extend volume (**up from ~17%** in 2024; i.e. 75% / 83% were B- or better; LCD, snippet). *(Narrowed 9/28: this line previously concluded "the residual wall is therefore concentrating in the weakest names." The share alone cannot show that. It needs the wall's starting tier mix and per-tier extension rates (extensions ÷ maturities at risk), and neither is in the free sources = **GAP**. If below-B- paper was ~25% of the wall, a 25% share is proportional. The 17%→25% rise, if anything, cuts the other way.)*
- **Distressed exchanges:** 65% of defaults in 2025 → 44% in H1 2026, while the default rate excluding exchanges rose to 2.8% (Moody's via a secondary source; scope unstated).
- 2026 YTD amend-and-extend volume: GAP. A named 2026 debt-swap wave tied to 2027–28 maturities: GAP.

## What this shows / does not show
- ✅ Supports BOND's mechanism claim (`KB-BND-341/347`): the stress is a **CCC-tail refinancing-cost** problem. That is why CCC sits at a 3-year high while the index spread is only at its 43rd percentile.
- ✅ Timing (**index level only**, narrowed 9/28): in aggregate dollars the big maturities are **2028–29**, so the INDEX-level pressure is a slow squeeze rather than a 2026–27 cliff. ⚠️ That does **not** rule out near-term refinancing stress for CCC issuers or individual borrowers with 2026–27 maturities, where the §1 coupon step-up applies now. The CCC-only 2027 figure is a GAP (§2), so **the near-term tail is UNMEASURED, not small.** Watch items: defaults/distressed exchanges and loan interest coverage, **plus the near-term tail: any CCC or single-name 2026–27 maturity that fails to refinance is evidence at the tail even while the aggregate reads slow.**
- ❌ No CCC-only dollar wall, no CCC sector split, no actual CCC coupon figure (all GAP at free sources). Sector names are wording and older all-rating tables only.
- ⚠️ The loan share cuts both ways: floating-rate borrowers are already paying today's rates (coverage stress now), while fixed-rate CCC bonds face the coupon cliff at refinancing.
