Discussion Topics 

1.  Please discuss your latest economic, fiscal, monetary policy (including changes in SOMA holdings), and Treasury 

financing forecasts for FY 2027, FY 2028, and FY 2029. 

2.  Treasury typically schedules auctions so as to avoid auctioning nominal coupons and TIPS on days when the 

Federal Open Market Committee is expected to release a policy statement (“FOMC days”). However, Treasury 
typically does schedule auctions of Treasury bills and FRNs on FOMC days. Avoiding auctions on FOMC days 
results in more auctions on surrounding days, often as many as four auctions in a day, under the current practice. 
If Treasury did not schedule auctions of Treasury bills and FRNs on FOMC days, it would likely need to conduct 
five or six auctions on certain days. Should Treasury modify its practice in order to avoid auctioning Treasury 
bills and FRNs on FOMC days? Why or why not? 

3.  On August 19, Treasury announced that it would increase the size of liquidity support buyback operations for 

longer-dated nominal coupon securities (the 10-year to 20-year sector and the 20-year to 30-year sector). Please 
comment on the effects of those larger buybacks on liquidity and market functioning. How have the larger 
buybacks in those sectors affected your capacity to intermediate flows? Have you observed any changes in client 
interest in participating in buybacks, either directly or indirectly, following this change? What, if any, additional 
changes should Treasury consider to enhance the effectiveness of the buyback program? 

4.  Treasury’s quarterly refunding statement highlights that it is focused on trends in structural demand as it 

continues to evaluate future changes to nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes. Please provide your perspective 
on structural changes in investor demand and your expectations for how demand may evolve over the 
intermediate term. What futures changes to auction sizes should Treasury consider, given expected borrowing 
needs and investor demand?

Appendix 
In order to gather more comprehensive information regarding the factors affecting Treasury’s borrowing needs, Part I 
requests estimates of “Other Means of Financing,” which is one of several components of the difference between budget 
deficits and privately-held net marketable borrowing (as shown in the Example below).  “Other Means of Financing” 
includes items where the timing of government cash flows may differ from budget accounting, such as student loans and 
loans to small business.  

Public reporting burden for this collection of information is estimated to average two hours per response, including the 
time for reviewing instructions, searching existing data sources, gathering and maintaining the data needed, and 
completing and reviewing the collection of information.  An agency may not conduct or sponsor, and a person is not 
required to respond to, a collection of information unless it displays a valid OMB control number.  The valid OMB 
control number for this information is 1505-0261.  Send comments regarding this burden estimate or any other aspect of 
this collection of information, including suggestions for reducing this burden, to the Department of the Treasury (Office of 
Debt Management, 1500 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20220).
