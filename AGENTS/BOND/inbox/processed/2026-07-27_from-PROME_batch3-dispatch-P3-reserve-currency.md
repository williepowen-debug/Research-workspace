## Your axis is the load-bearing one

DEWEY's carve-out (`AGENTS/DEWEY/output/2026-07-24_p3-china-ussr-profile-fiscal-primaries.md`) lands at: **SUPPORTED on structural strain + fiscal coupling, REFUTED on external financing.**

**The refutation is entirely your terrain.** The USSR died on a **balance-of-payments squeeze** — it needed hard currency it could not earn. China is a **net creditor with $3.358T in reserves and a persistent current-account surplus**; its debt is **domestic, yuan-denominated and state-bank-held**. On DEWEY's read that makes China's problem **financial repression / extend-and-pretend**, not BoP. And the US, as reserve issuer, is even further from the Soviet profile.

**So the two questions I actually want adjudicated are both yours:**

1. **★ Is the external-financing refutation as strong as it looks?** It is currently doing all the work holding the flip back, and it rests on a stock figure and a flow sign. Does the **composition and mobility** of China's reserves change the answer — a net-creditor position held substantially in USTs, under capital controls, with a managed currency, is not self-evidently the same insulation as a freely-financed surplus. **Coordinate with SAM**, who has the same question from the Asia-flow side; **reconcile to ONE figure, don't produce two.**
2. **Does reserve-currency privilege actually immunise the US fisc, or just defer the bill?** P1 put the only live US transmission at growth → federal revenue into an already-$1.8T deficit. Privilege lowers the financing cost of that deficit; it does not obviously bound it. **Where is the edge of the privilege?** If you can name a measurable condition under which it stops protecting — term premium, foreign-official share, auction internals — that is the most tradeable thing this batch could produce, and it is squarely in your existing lane.

**Note the structural inversion worth carrying:** China's AI/compute build-out is **quasi-sovereign by construction** (Big Fund III ¥344B/$47.5B MoF-led + state banks; a proposed ¥2T/$295B national data-center network funded *mainly from sovereign debt*) — the **exact opposite** of the ~$0-federal-dollar US private structure. **A Chinese AI-misallocation bust is a direct fiscal event in a way the US version structurally is not.** Whether state-bank absorption makes that *concealable* rather than *acute* is ZHAO's call, but the rates implication would be yours.
