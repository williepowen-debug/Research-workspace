# Primary Dealer Treasury Positioning & Capacity Post-eSLR Reform

**Date:** 2026-05-05  
**Agent:** BOND-SUB-DEALER  
**Sources:** NY Fed Primary Dealer Statistics, Federal Reserve Board orders/statement, Brookings Institution, Financial Times, Seoul Economic Daily, Boston Fed research, Coalition Greenwich

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Primary dealers have materially expanded Treasury books since the eSLR reform took effect on **April 1, 2026**. Net Treasury holdings have risen from roughly **$400 billion in 2025** to an average of approximately **$550 billion so far in 2026**—the highest level since 2007 and a **~37% increase**. This expansion is directly linked to relaxed supplementary leverage ratio (SLR) constraints, with Morgan Stanley and Bank of America publicly confirming increased capital deployment to Treasury trading.

Separately, Morgan Stanley’s internal reorganization—involving its **$85+ billion European investment bank (MSESE)**—received a controversial Section 23A exemption from the Fed/OCC on **March 26, 2026**, enabling the bank subsidiary to assume liabilities of a foreign affiliate. This is not a simple broker-dealer-to-bank asset transfer, but a larger structural move that could set precedent for other G-SIBs.

### 2.2 What the Data Shows

- The **NY Fed Primary Dealer Statistics** (FR 2004A data) are published weekly on Thursdays at ~4:15 PM ET, covering positions, transactions, financing, and fails.
- The **Financial Times** (cited by Seoul Economic Daily and BigGo Finance) performed calculations on this weekly data, arriving at the **~$550 billion** 2026 average.
- **Brookings** (Zhu & Liang, updated Feb 2026) independently noted the **$477 billion** year-end 2025 figure and estimated that full central clearing of repo could free up to **$1.3 trillion** in additional balance-sheet capacity—nearly 3x the year-end position.

## 3. eSLR Reform & Balance Sheet Capacity

### 3.1 Rule Change

- **Final rule effective date:** **April 1, 2026** (optional early adoption January 1, 2026).
- **What changed:** The enhanced supplementary leverage ratio (eSLR) requirement for U.S. G-SIBs and their insured depository institutions was reduced. The eSLR buffer standard now applies in addition to the 3% SLR minimum, but the overall requirement is lower than before.
- **Impact:** Because Treasuries count as assets for SLR purposes, a lower requirement directly frees balance-sheet capacity for low-risk, low-return Treasury market-making.

### 3.2 Capital Buffer Context

- **Coalition Greenwich** (end of 2025): The six systemically important U.S. banks held capital buffers averaging **2.4 percentage points above regulatory requirements**.
- **Minal Chotai** (Coalition Greenwich): Noted that with capital rules adjusting, "the rationale for maintaining these large excess buffers has disappeared," implying more capital can be deployed to trading.

### 3.3 Dealer Confirmation

- **Morgan Stanley:** Confirmed it has **deployed more capital to Treasury trading** following the SLR revision.
- **Bank of America (Mark Cabana):** "Dealer holdings of Treasuries have increased significantly over the past few months, which is evidence that SLR is indeed having an impact."

## 4. Morgan Stanley Restructuring: The $85B+ European Affiliate Move

### 4.1 The Transaction

- **Date approved:** March 26, 2026
- **Requester:** Morgan Stanley Bank, N.A. (Salt Lake City, Utah)
- **Affiliate involved:** **Morgan Stanley Europe SE (MSESE)**, Frankfurt am Main, Germany
- **Exemption:** Section 23A of the Federal Reserve Act (joint Fed/OCC approval; FDIC did not object)

### 4.2 Size & Scope

- **MSESE assets:** Exceed **$85 billion** (as of June 30, 2025, per Fed Governor Barr’s dissent).
- **MSESE liabilities:** Exceed **$75 billion**.
- **Covered transaction:** Valued at **billions of dollars** as of September 30, 2025, and exceeds the bank’s quantitative limits under Section 23A for covered transactions with a single affiliate and with all affiliates.
- **Nature:** MS Bank would **acquire MSESE** and assume a significant amount of its liabilities as part of a one-time internal corporate reorganization.

### 4.3 Why It Matters

- **Precedent:** Governor Barr noted this would be the **largest 23A exemption granted outside of a financial crisis** and the first to enable a U.S. bank to move a large foreign investment bank underneath it.
- **Safety net expansion:** Following the reorganization, transactions between MS Bank and MSESE would **no longer be subject to Section 23A, 23B, or Regulation W**, because MSESE would become a subsidiary of the bank. This allows FDIC-insured deposits to fund foreign non-bank activities without arm’s-length restrictions.
- **Prior exemption:** In late 2025, Board staff (under delegated authority) granted MS Bank a separate Section 23A exemption to acquire the fixed-income derivative business of another affiliate. Barr stated the two reorganizations together would represent a covered transaction amounting to **"multiple times the bank's capital."**
- **Systemic risk:** Barr warned that granting this exemption could encourage other large banking organizations to move foreign non-bank subsidiaries under their U.S. banks, potentially bringing **over $1.5 trillion in foreign nonbank activity** into the federal safety net.

### 4.4 Europe SE Context

- **Morgan Stanley Europe SE** is a full-service European investment bank (not a small subsidiary).
- **ECB approval:** On **January 16, 2026**, the ECB approved MSESE’s application to convert to a bank and become a subsidiary of Morgan Stanley Europe Holding SE.
- **Annual reports:** MSESE files standalone annual reports (2024 and 2025 available). Trading assets include €14,134 million of securities as of 2025 (up from €9,163 million in 2024).

## 5. Market Structure Tensions

### 5.1 Banks vs. Hedge Funds

- While banks are returning, **hedge funds' share of the Treasury market has climbed to a record 8%**, supported by >$6 trillion in repo and prime brokerage financing.
- **OFR data (end of 2025):** Hedge funds held **$2.4 trillion** in long Treasury positions and **$1.6 trillion** in short positions—nearly tripling in three years.
- **Basis trade dependency:** The dominant strategy (arbitraging cash vs. futures) is highly leveraged and funding-dependent. A forced unwind could transmit shockwaves across global fixed-income markets (per Apollo’s Torsten Slok).

### 5.2 Skepticism on Bank Return

- **JPMorgan (Jay Barry):** "Primary dealers will not return to their pre-2008 role... the landscape where hedge funds and high-frequency traders hold a larger share is not going to reverse."
- **Vanderbilt (Yesha Yadav):** Banks have no statutory market-making obligation; rolling back balance-sheet rules "does not guarantee effectiveness."
- **Barclays (Ajay Rajadhyaksha):** Structural constraints have not been fundamentally eliminated.

## 7. Implications for BOND / System

1. **Dealer capacity is expanding.** The ~$150B increase in net Treasury holdings is concrete evidence that eSLR relief is translating into larger dealer books—not just theory.
2. **Morgan Stanley is restructuring at scale.** The MSESE move is not a marginal adjustment; it is a **>$85B affiliate acquisition** that removes 23A constraints and could be replicated by other G-SIBs. This changes the geography of risk within bank holding companies.
3. **Hedge fund leverage remains the offsetting risk.** Even as banks return, the market is more leveraged than ever via the basis trade. If funding conditions tighten, the unwind could overwhelm the newly expanded dealer capacity.
4. **Data to watch:** NY Fed weekly Primary Dealer Statistics (Thursdays), FICC sponsored repo volumes, and any follow-up 23A exemption requests from other banks (JPM, BofA, Citi).
