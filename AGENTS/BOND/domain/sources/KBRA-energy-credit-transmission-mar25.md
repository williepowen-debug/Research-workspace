# KBRA: Energy Market Disruption — Corporate Credit Implications (Mar 25, 2026)
**Source:** KBRA research report

## Five Credit Transmission Channels

| Channel | Operational/Financial Change | Credit Impact |
|---------|------------------------------|---------------|
| Oil/Product Prices | Higher crude + refined costs, input cost volatility | Margin compression (non-energy); earnings uplift (upstream) |
| Supply Disruption | Impaired logistics, refining outages, constrained flows | Volume risk, operational inefficiency, revenue disruption |
| Pricing Power | Uneven pass-through across sectors | Margin resilience differentiation |
| Working Capital | Higher inventory values, increased funding needs | Liquidity pressure, leverage increase |
| Macro Conditions | Inflation, demand weakening | Broader credit deterioration, reduced revenue visibility |

## Key Differential
- **IG:** short-lived shock → FCF pressure, delayed capex. Not immediate credit deterioration.
- **HY:** same conditions → **rating pressure if leverage increases, coverage weakens, or refi risk rises.** Limited scale + tight liquidity = faster transmission.

## Upstream E&P (CF $130C context)
- Clearest beneficiary. Higher prices → immediate EBITDA/FCF improvement.
- Three filters: hedge book, basis exposure (Brent-WTI spread), capital allocation discipline
- EIA: US production response builds more in **2027 than 2026** → supply gap persists
- Not durable if prices retrace

## Most Exposed Sectors (from report)
- Chemicals, industrials, transportation, fuel/feedstock-intensive with limited pass-through
- Overlap with Q4 Surveillance near-term maturity concentration: Consumer Retail, Media, Chemicals/Containers/Metals

## Physical Disruption Documented
- Brent briefly >$120, Hormuz flows "fallen to a trickle" (IEA)
- Ras Laffan: 5-year rebuild (two liquefaction trains)
- >3 mb/d refining capacity shut, >4 mb/d at risk
- "Not solely geopolitical risk premium" — actual supply destruction

## Hard Price Data (EIA, Mar 2-16 2026)
- Gas: $3.015 → $3.720 (+23%). Now $3.98. EIA projects +$0.60-0.70/gal above prior forecast for Q2.
- **Diesel: $3.897 → $5.071 (+30%).** Trucking/freight/ag blood.
- EIA: gasoline ~60¢/gal higher than prior outlook for Mar, ~70¢ higher in Q2

## IEA Demand Destruction
- Global demand growth cut >1 mb/d for Mar+Apr combined
- 2026 growth forecast: -210 kb/d to 640 kb/d
- SPR: 400M bbl release agreed Mar 11. Global inventories 8.2B bbl (highest since Feb 2021). Stopgap only.

## Sector Hierarchy (KBRA)
1. **Clear positive:** upstream E&P (CF), selected services
2. **Mixed:** midstream (fee-based insulated, export-exposed vulnerable), refining (crack spreads up but feedstock/logistics constrained)
3. **Clear negative:** chemicals/petrochemicals/fertilizer (feedstock + pricing lag), airlines/trucking/freight (diesel spike + volume risk), broad industrials/consumer/packaging (margin erosion, working capital)
4. **Duration-dependent:** if short → FCF pressure only. If sustained → leverage, coverage, defaults across sectors.

## KBRA's Key Variables
1. **Duration** — short = contained, sustained = defaults broaden
2. **Policy response** — SPR = stopgap. Sanctions enforcement relaxed but not a fix.
3. **Pass-through timing** — costs reprice immediately, revenue lags. The gap = margin compression.
