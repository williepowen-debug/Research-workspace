# VIX, Credit Spreads and Threshold Effects

**Source:** Soper, Carolyne & McAlley, Eric (2025). "Investigating the VIX Index Relationship with High Yield & Investment Grade Bond Spreads: Exploring Structural Breaks & Threshold Effects" (Forthcoming JBES). SSRN.

**URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5213881.pdf

---

## Key Findings

- **Threshold Regimes:** Study categorizes VIX into three distinct regimes using threshold regressions:
  - **Low VIX:** < 20 (normal/complacent)
  - **Intermediate VIX:** 20-30 (elevated concern)
  - **Elevated VIX:** > 30 (crisis/stress)

- **Regime-Dependent Relationships:** The VIX-credit spread relationship changes significantly across these thresholds
  - In low VIX regimes: weak correlation between VIX and credit spreads
  - In elevated VIX regimes: strong positive correlation emerges

- **Federal Funds Rate as Moderator:** The FEDL rate significantly moderates the VIX-spread relationship
  - Higher rates amplify the sensitivity of credit spreads to VIX changes
  - This suggests monetary policy regime affects credit-vol transmission

- **Structural Breaks:** Multiple structural breaks confirmed in the VIX-spread relationship over time
  - Relationship is not stable; breaks occur around crisis periods
