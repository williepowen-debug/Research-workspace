# Disentangling Credit Spreads and Equity Volatility

**Source:** Davernas, Adrien. "Disentangling Credit Spreads and Equity Volatility." Job Market Paper.

**URL:** https://www.adriendavernas.com/papers/adriendavernas_jmp.pdf

---

## Key Findings

- **Joint Determination:** Credit spreads and equity volatility are jointly determined in equilibrium
  - Both reflect underlying firm fundamentals (leverage, asset volatility)
  - Merton (1974) framework: equity is a call option on firm assets

- **Illiquidity Component:**
  - Aggregate illiquidity measure is closely connected to VIX
  - Part of VIX reflects liquidity premium, not just fundamental volatility
  - Credit spreads also contain liquidity component

- **Decomposition:**
  - Credit spreads = default risk component + liquidity component + risk premium
  - Equity volatility = fundamental volatility + risk premium + illiquidity
  - Common factors drive both — explaining high correlation

- **Causality:**
  - Evidence of bidirectional causality between credit spreads and equity vol
  - In stress periods: equity vol leads credit spreads
  - In normal periods: credit spreads may lead equity vol (information asymmetry)

## Relevance to VIOLET's Thesis

**MEDIUM-HIGH:** Provides theoretical foundation for credit-vol link and explains lead-lag dynamics.

1. **Transmission Mechanism:** Explains WHY credit and vol are linked (common fundamental drivers)
2. **Lead-Lag Dynamics:** Suggests direction of causality changes by regime
3. **Illiquidity Factor:** VIX spikes may reflect liquidity crises, not just fundamental repricing

## Actionable Implications

- **Regime-Dependent Causality:**
  - VIX < 20, stable: Watch credit spreads for early signals
  - VIX > 30, stress: VIX leads, credit follows (momentum effect)

- **Illiquidity Warning:**
  - VIX rising faster than credit spreads = liquidity-driven vol spike
  - Both rising together = fundamental repricing

- **VIOLET Refinement:**
  - Monitor VIX/credit spread ratio
  - Rising ratio = vol leading (stress regime)
  - Falling ratio = credit leading (early warning)
