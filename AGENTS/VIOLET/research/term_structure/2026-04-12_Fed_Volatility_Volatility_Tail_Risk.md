# Volatility of Volatility and Tail Risk Premiums

**Source:** Federal Reserve Board (2013). "Volatility of Volatility and Tail Risk Premiums." Finance and Economics Discussion Series No. 2013-54.

**URL:** https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201354/index.html

---

## Key Findings

- **VVIX Decomposition:**
  - VVIX² = RVV (realized vol of vol) + VVRP (vol of vol risk premium)
  - RVV = realized variance of 5-minute front-month VIX futures over past month
  - VVRP = VVIX² - RVV (market price of vol uncertainty)

- **Predictive Power:**
  - Both RVV and VVRP significantly predict future tail risk hedge returns
  - RVV (realized component) more statistically significant than VVRP
  - VVIX predicts: tail risk hedges, cross-sectional returns, VIX options returns

- **Tail Risk Premium:**
  - VVRP represents compensation for bearing vol uncertainty
  - Higher VVRP = higher expected return for tail risk hedges
  - VVRP varies over time and across market conditions

- **Relationship to VIX:**
  - VVIX and VIX correlated but distinct
  - VVIX can remain elevated after VIX declines (uncertainty persists)
  - VVIX leads VIX in some stress episodes

## Actionable Implications

- **VVIX Components:**
  - High RVV = actual vol instability (measured)
  - High VVRP = market pricing vol uncertainty (expected)
  - Both rising = strong tail risk signal

- **Tail Risk Hedging:**
  - High VVIX = tail hedges cheaper relative to expected payoff
  - VVRP > 50 = attractive tail hedge entry
  - RVV > 30 = vol unstable, hedges more likely to pay off

- **VIOLET Application:**
  - Monitor VVIX components if data available
  - VVIX > 120 = tail risk regime
  - VVIX/VIX > 6 = extreme vol uncertainty
