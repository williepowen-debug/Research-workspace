## Abstract

We reconstructed the only back-to-back SKEW divergence cluster in the 19-year sample (episodes #13 and #14: 2024-11-22 → 2025-01-22) to identify which leading indicators fired before VIX peaked at 27.86 within the 60-day window (and eventually 52.33 at 76 days on the April 2025 tariff shock). Of 19 indicators tracked, **12 were Class 1 (leading)** — credit, rates, vol complex, and yield curve all moved decisively before the stress peak. We then translated each tell against our current April 2026 setup: **the translation is mixed — 5 match, 4 partial, 3 diverge** — consistent with our 66% Scenario B probability but not confirming the high-severity tail.

## 2. Peak-Day Reconstruction

**April 8, 2025 — VIX closes at 52.33** (highest since March 2020)

**Trigger:** On April 2 ("Liberation Day"), President Trump announced sweeping reciprocal tariffs — 34% on China, 20% on EU, 24% on Japan. Markets sold April 3-4 (VIX 30 → 45). Over the weekend, China retaliated with 34% counter-tariffs. Monday April 7 saw continuation selling (VIX 47). Tuesday April 8 hit peak panic at VIX 52.33 on full trade-war fears.

**Amplifiers:**
1. Systematic/CTA selling — trend-following models hit stop-loss cascades
2. Dealer gamma squeeze — massive put hedging forced market makers to sell futures
3. Margin calls across leveraged positions
4. Speed of drawdown (fastest 4-day SPX drop in decades) created vol-of-vol feedback

**Resolution:** April 9 — Trump announced 90-day pause on most tariffs (except China). VIX collapsed from 52 to 33 in one session.

**For our purposes:** The exogenous trigger (tariff escalation) was not forecastable from vol structure alone. The SKEW pattern correctly identified vulnerability, but the specific catalyst came from outside the financial system. This is a recurring pattern in our dataset — the SKEW divergence identifies fragility, the trigger is usually external.

### Pattern Summary

The dominant leading-phase pattern was **compression**: vol compressed (VIX, VIX9D, VVIX down), credit compressed (HY/IG/CCC OAS at lows), while rates tightened (yields up, real yields up). This created a "coiled spring" — maximum complacency in vol and credit simultaneously with tightening financial conditions. SKEW was the outlier that stayed elevated (telling you smart money was buying crash protection even as realized vol collapsed).

**The tell in one sentence:** When everything compresses to complacency lows EXCEPT SKEW, the spring is coiled.

## 7. Synthesis: What This Means for Scenario B

**The analog supports Scenario B (66%) but at moderate severity, not tail severity.**

**Matching:** The core "coiled spring" signature is present — vol compressed from a stress peak, SKEW elevated, credit near lows, equity at highs, VVIX divergence. This is the setup that preceded 15/16 historical stress episodes.

**Diverging:** The absence of CCC stress and soft real yields suggest financial conditions are looser than the 2024 analog. The 2024 cluster had genuine financial tightening (10Y from 3.7→4.6, real yields surging) driving the compression. Our 2026 environment has rates elevated but stable, not accelerating.

**Translation:** Our setup maps better to the **central-case outcome** (VIX 25-30 within 60d) than the **tail outcome** (VIX 50+). The 2024 tail required an exogenous policy shock (tariffs) that amplified pre-existing fragility. We have the fragility but would need a similar external catalyst for the tail.

**Revised probability distribution within Scenario B:**

| Outcome | Probability | Requires |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| VIX peaks 22-25 | 35% | Normal vol mean-reversion from compressed base |
| VIX peaks 25-30 | 40% | SKEW persistence + mild credit widening + any catalyst |
| VIX peaks 30-40 | 15% | Credit break (CCC widens) + external shock |
| VIX peaks 40+ | 10% | Tariff/geopolitical escalation + credit cascade |

## 8. Caveats

1. **Single analog.** This is the only back-to-back cluster. N=1 is not predictive — it's suggestive.
2. **Survivorship in tell classification.** The 10% threshold for Class 1 is arbitrary. Some "moderate" indicators may be more informative than the classification suggests.
3. **The tail was exogenous.** The VIX 52 peak was driven by tariff escalation, not endogenous financial stress. SKEW identified fragility; it did not predict the catalyst.
4. **Our SKEW peak is lower.** 156.9 vs 172.4/180.0. The 2024 cluster had more extreme SKEW readings. We're in the high cohort but at its lower boundary.
5. **Different macro regime.** 2024-25 had aggressive rate tightening; 2026 has stable-to-easing. The transmission mechanism may differ.
6. **60d vs 76d.** The catastrophic peak fell outside the strict 60-day window. Our backtest statistics (94% hit rate for ≥15% rise within 60d) remain valid — the 85% rise to 27.86 by Mar 10 confirmed the pattern within the window.
