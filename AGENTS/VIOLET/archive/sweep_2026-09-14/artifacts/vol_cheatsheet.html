02

The one idea that ties it together

The surface — one thermometer

All four gauges are built from the same S&P options. When they move together, that's one opinion moving — like four thermometers in one room.

VIX VVIX SKEW TERM

So four gauges agreeing is not four confirmations. It's one.

Independent rooms — real confirmation

A signal is real when a separate market agrees with the surface. These are the rooms that matter.

CREDITjunk-bond stress — real money
MOVEthe bond market's own VIX
POSITIONINGwho's actually betting which way
OIL-VOLenergy-shock transmission

Surface and these rooms pointing the same way = a signal to respect. Surface alone = usually noise that fades.

Where we stand

Snapshot · 17 Aug 2026 close, with VIX live at ~09:45 ET on 18 Aug · these numbers go stale; the meanings above don’t
The one thing worth knowing today
  • The three-year low in crash cover is fully retraced — SKEW 126 → 143 in nine sessions, back through the 140 line it fell out of. People are buying protection again
  • But the longer-run regime measure broke the other way on the very same day. The 20-day average dropped below its line on 17 Aug, ending a 49-day stretch of elevated demand that began on 5 June
  • ⚠️ Both are true, and they answer different questions. The daily reading says what protection costs today; the 20-day average says what regime we have been in. Quoting the wrong one gives you the opposite conclusion — so always ask which measure a SKEW claim is about
  • The regime ending is not a close call: even if demand holds at today’s level, the average stays below its line for at least ten more sessions on arithmetic alone
Where the rooms stand now
  • Everything bid together on Monday — and that is NOT the coiled spring. VIX +6.6%, vol-of-vol +7.4%, crash cover +3.3%, bond vol +8.7%. The coiled-spring pattern needs crash cover rising while the others fall. All of them rose. This is a near-dated event bid, not the setup described above
  • ⚠️ And I have not ruled out the boring explanation. The nearest-dated gauge jumped +16.8% — far more than any other — two days before a large VIX expiry. That is as consistent with expiry mechanics as with fear. I am not calling it fear until I can separate them
  • Term structure never inverted and is not close — 1.25, still steep, though flattening as the near end got bought. Inversion is at 1.00
  • The quiet mechanism worth knowing: how much stocks move together is up 25% off its low. When stocks move separately, index vol is mechanically suppressed — so part of the calm we had was arithmetic, not confidence. That arithmetic now runs the other way, and it can lift VIX with no new fear at all
  • Positioning: fast money bought the 4 Aug spike and lost — out a week later at lower vol, and short again now. ⚠️ That read is five sessions blind; Friday’s print is the first that sees Monday

"Is the break actually real?" Waits on purpose — an independent room must confirm — so we don't chase a fake-out. Stops us buying into noise.

Together they bracket the decision: one stops us missing the cheap window, the other stops us chasing a false alarm. We currently hold no VIX position, unchanged — the call spread you approved on 27 Jul closed on 30 Jul for a loss of $111.60, returning $176.10 of the $287.70 risked. Worth knowing why: the vol call was right and the trade still lost. VIX options settle on a forward price that moves less than the headline number, so a spike in VIX lifts the position by less than you would expect. It still came good briefly — at Wednesday’s close it was through its strike. But nothing in the plan said “take the profit when it is simply worth more than you paid”; every exit trigger required the move to go further still. It round-tripped overnight. The lesson is a missing rule, not a bad instrument.

Read gauges together, never alone. A high SKEW can be fear or just hedges rotating into cheap far-out options — which mechanically pushes VIX down. Pair it with other reads before judging. Data lags — but know which. SKEW publishes same day, around 5pm ET (we had this wrong until 28 Jul and it cost us two sessions of calling a gauge unreadable). Credit lags a day; positioning about a week. Every number carries its own as-of date. Coincident, not predictive. VIX reacts to what already happened. The edge is in the divergences and the independent rooms, not the headline number. Internal VIOLET desk reference — orientation, not trade instruction.
