## 1. HEADLINE

**The KILL's premise is FALSE at the name level for one of the four banks.**

**BankUnited (BKU) is not a bystander to the FHA/VA buyout channel — it is a principal in it.** BKU's stated business is *buying non-performing FHA and VA insured mortgages out of GNMA securitizations from the third-party nonbank servicers who exercised the buyout right*, and holding them on balance sheet for re-performance and re-securitization, with the economics **shared with the servicer**.

That is precisely the counterparty node the parent report declared did not exist at banks. **BKU's balance is 4.5× the single Wintrust datapoint the KILL was built on, against a $3.0B equity base.**

The other three (SSB, AMTB, SBCF) are clean at the disclosure level, with one flagged monitoring gap at AMTB.

## 3. WHAT THE BKU DISCLOSURE ACTUALLY SAYS (verbatim, sourced)

From BKU's 10-K for FY2025 (filed 2026-02-26), Business section:

> "We also acquire non-performing FHA and VA insured mortgages from third party servicers who have exercised their right to purchase these loans out of GNMA securitizations. Such loans that re-perform, either through modification or self-cure, may be eligible for re-securitization. **The Company and the servicer share in the economics of the sale of these loans into new securitizations.**"

From the Q1-2026 10-Q (filed 2026-05-07), MD&A:

> "The Company acquires non-performing FHA and VA insured mortgages from third parties who have exercised their right to purchase these loans out of GNMA securitizations upon default ("Buyout Loans")… **The balance of Buyout Loans totaled $851 million at March 31, 2026.**"

Glossary definition, both filings: *"Buyout Loans — FHA and VA insured mortgages from third party servicers who have exercised their right to purchase these loans out of GNMA securitizations."*

### Delinquency INSIDE the buyout pool is deteriorating

From the Q1-26 10-Q credit-quality table (columns: current / 30-59 / 60-89 / 90+ / total; foots exactly):

| Date | Current | 30-59 | 60-89 | **90+ DPD** | Total | **90+ as % of pool** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12/31/2025 | $604.3M | $101.7M | $25.9M | **$159.1M** | $891.0M | **17.9%** |
| 3/31/2026 | $575.6M | $83.1M | $28.1M | **$196.6M** | $883.4M | **22.3%** |

**90+DPD inside the pool rose +$37.5M (+23.6%) QoQ and the 90+ share rose +440bps in one quarter.** (12/31/2024 comparative: $226M 90+, per the FY2025 10-K.)

Residential loans in process of foreclosure at 3/31/26: **$115M total, of which $105M government insured** (vs $104M / $96M at 12/31/25).

### The honest counter-argument — and where the KILL still holds

**BKU carries a ZERO allowance on this entire $883M book.** Its own stated policy, twice: *"The Company expects to collect the amortized cost basis of government insured residential loans due to the nature of the government guarantee, so the ACL is zero for these loans."* And: *"For these loans, traditional measures of credit quality are not particularly relevant considering the guaranteed nature of the loans and the underlying business model."*

**So on the narrow question the KILL asked — does a regional bank absorb the FHA/VA *principal credit loss*? — BKU's audited answer is NO.** The federal guarantee does the absorbing. That part of the KILL survives, and BKU is arguably the strongest *confirming* evidence for it: a bank willingly holds $883M of 22%-90-days-delinquent paper at zero reserve precisely because the sovereign eats the credit.

**What the KILL got wrong is the premise, not the conclusion.** It concluded "no bank exposure" from "no bank origination share." BKU proves origination share is the wrong metric — BKU **does not originate residential mortgages at all** and still carries the single largest disclosed bank FHA/VA position I could find. The residual exposure is **not credit** — it is:

1. **Timeline / non-reimbursable cost.** FHA/VA claims reimburse on a schedule with caps; foreclosure timelines that exceed the caps leave interest curtailment and non-reimbursable expense with the holder. The zero ACL covers principal, not carry.
2. **Negative carry on a growing non-earning block.** $197M is 90+DPD and $105M is in foreclosure, funded at BKU's cost of deposits.
3. **Counterparty dependence on the exact nonbanks under stress.** BKU's exit is *re-securitization*, and the economics are **shared with the servicer**. If a servicer counterparty fails, BKU's re-performance/re-securitization pipeline is impaired even though the loans' principal is guaranteed.
4. **Warehouse lending to nonbank mortgage companies, growing fast** — $876.8M at 6/30/26, +49.7% in 18 months, 29.2% of equity, in exactly the funding channel that VASP's termination made more load-bearing.

This is the **credit-absorbed vs. liquidity-transmission** distinction: the channel is dead on the credit axis and alive on the funding/timeline/counterparty axis. Both can be true at once and the KILL only tested one.

## 4. AMTB — the flagged monitoring gap ("not disclosed" ≠ "zero")

AMTB discloses no FHA/VA/GNMA exposure at all. But it is **aggressively buying residential loan portfolios** and its own new Q1-2026 risk factor says so:

> "During the first quarter of 2026, we increased our exposure to residential mortgage loans **through portfolio acquisitions** and expect to further expand this exposure through additional residential loan purchases in 2026."
> "Our residential mortgage portfolio includes a significant concentration of **jumbo and other nonconforming** loans."

Single-family residential balances: **$1,515.2M (12/31/25) → $1,680.8M (3/31/26) → $1,954.2M (6/30/26)** = **+29.0% in six months**, against $914.4M of equity.

AMTB characterizes the purchases as jumbo/nonconforming, **not** government-insured — and its 10-K carries a boilerplate accounting policy for "government insured residential loans" with no balance anywhere. **Today this is de minimis. The gap is that AMTB does not break out government-insured within a book growing 29% per half-year.** If FHA/VA-adjacent paper entered the book it would not be separately visible at current disclosure granularity. Recommend: check the Q2-2026 10-Q (due ~Aug 2026) loan-composition note for a government-insured line item.

## 6. VERDICT

**SURVIVES-WITH-CAVEAT — with the caveat being load-bearing, not cosmetic.**

**Confidence: HIGH** on the name-level facts (all four banks' primary filings read directly this run, tables foot-checked); **MEDIUM** on the conclusion, because the three residual gaps — Call Report data, Ginnie issuer status, and unnamed warehouse/buyout counterparties — all point the same direction (they could only *add* exposure, never subtract it).

**Reason:**
- The KILL's *conclusion* — banks do not absorb FHA/VA **principal credit loss** — **SURVIVES**, and is affirmatively corroborated by BKU carrying a **zero ACL** on $883M of paper that is 22.3% ninety-days-delinquent.
- The KILL's *premise* — that no watched bank has material FHA/VA balance-sheet exposure, inferred from a ~5% bank origination share — **FAILS at BKU.** BKU originates **zero** residential mortgages and still holds **$883.4M of government-insured resi (29.3% of equity)** plus **$876.8M of mortgage warehouse lending (29.2% of equity)** — ~58% of equity across the two, against the single $187.8M Wintrust datapoint the KILL was built on.
- Therefore: **do NOT switch off the watch on BKU.** Switching off SSB and SBCF is supported by name-level evidence. AMTB can be downgraded to a disclosure-gap watch pending its Q2-2026 10-Q.
- The live BKU channel is **not** credit — it is timeline/carry/counterparty and the warehouse funding line. Any monitoring should track (a) the 90+DPD share inside the buyout pool, (b) MWL balance growth, (c) any named nonbank counterparty disclosure.
