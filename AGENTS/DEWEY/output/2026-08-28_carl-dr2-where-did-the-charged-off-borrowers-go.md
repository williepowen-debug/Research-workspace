# WHERE DID THE CHARGED-OFF BORROWERS GO — THEY STOPPED PAYING VOLUNTARILY

**Date:** 2026-08-28 | **Mode:** Thesis | **Confidence:** High (the collections-channel evidence, supply/price, and bankruptcy counts — all primary, two independent issuers plus a government count) / **Medium** (the survivorship reconciliation, which is my inference) / **NOT-AVAILABLE** (the credit-invisible population trend — CFPB's canonical series stops at December 2020)
**Commission:** CARL-DR-2, Will-approved 2026-07-31 · target ~8/25 · **Consumers:** CARL (action) · RED / REGINALD (info)

---

## Key Finding — branch (a): EXIT-AND-DETERIORATE. Masking confirmed independent of issuer data.

**The direct test CARL asked for — a measurement of the exited cohort that does not route through issuer survivor pools — is the collections CHANNEL mix at the debt buyers, and it is unambiguous. The charged-off cohort has stopped paying voluntarily.**

At **PRA Group**, US cash collections rose $16.1M in Q2 2026 — composed of **legal collections +$31.1M offset by call-center and other channels −$15.3M.** Year-to-date the same shape: legal **+$60.9M**, call-center and other **−$17.1M**. **More than 100% of US collections growth came from litigation, while the voluntary channel shrank outright.** [PRIMARY: PRAA 10-Q, period 2026-06-30]

That extraction is getting more expensive: PRA's **legal collection costs rose 39.8% YoY** against total cash collections up 4.2%, taking the cost of collection from **7.01% to 9.40% of collections (+239bps).** Encore shows the same direction more mildly — US legal collections **+25.4%** versus call-center and digital **+12.7%**, with legal's share of US collections rising 34.0% → 36.6%.

**Terminal outcomes corroborate.** Chapter 7 filings rose **+14.7%** in the 12 months to 2026-06-30 (382,161 from 333,321) against Chapter 13 at **+7.6%** — liquidation growing roughly twice as fast as repayment, and Ch-7's share of total filings rising 61.4% → 62.8% [PRIMARY: US Courts Table F-2]. **A second, independent instrument agrees:** PRA's purchases of *bankruptcy* paper rose **+12.3%** YoY, tracking the court's **+12.2%** total filing growth almost exactly — while its *non-bankruptcy* Core paper purchases **fell 12.6%.**

**And the supply of charged-off paper is rising while its price falls.** The two largest US-listed debt buyers together bought **+34.5% more face value** for only **+3.8% more dollars** — cents-on-dollar down **22.9%** YoY (12.23¢ → 9.44¢). Encore's management: US *"supply increased and pricing remained at favorable levels,"* with **record** US purchases. PRA's: *"charge-off rates continued to support portfolio supply."*

**⚠️ The single most important analytical point: the apparent counter-evidence dissolves under CARL's own logic.** Encore *raised* its expected future recoveries (+$18.0M in Q2-26 vs +$3.3M in Q2-25) and PRA reports *"stability in customer payment activity."* That looks like containment. But **382,161 Chapter 7 discharges in twelve months remove the worst accounts from the collectible pool entirely**, leaving the residual paper adversely selected toward more-collectible accounts. **"Stable recoveries on the remaining book" is exactly what a survivor pool produces.** This is CARL's masking framework one level down — applied to the debt buyers' books rather than the issuers'. *(This reconciliation is my inference, not a measurement — see Counter-Evidence.)*

## 1. The direct behavioural test — voluntary payment is declining

This is the leg that answers the commission, because it observes the exited population's behaviour without passing through any issuer's surviving book.

| | PRAA US, Q2 2026 | PRAA US, H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Total US cash collections | **+$16.1M** | **+$43.6M** |
| — legal collections | **+$31.1M** ("higher volume") | **+$60.9M** |
| — call center and other | **−$15.3M** | **−$17.1M** |

[PRIMARY: PRA Group 10-Q, period 2026-06-30]

**The voluntary channel is shrinking in absolute dollars.** Every dollar of growth, and more, came from the courts.

**Encore, same quarter, same direction:**

| US collections channel | Q2-2026 | Q2-2025 | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Call center and digital | $360,718K | $320,152K | +12.7% |
| **Legal** | **$209,186K** | **$166,811K** | **+25.4%** |
| Collection agencies | $1,985K | $3,389K | −41.4% |
| Legal share of US collections | **36.6%** | 34.0% | +2.6pp |

Encore attributes its over-performance to *"enhanced collections strategies"* — **its own operational actions, not borrower recovery.** [PRIMARY: ECPG 10-Q, period 2026-06-30]

**The cost of extraction is rising fast:**

| PRAA | Q2-2026 | Q2-2025 | H1-2026 | H1-2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Legal collection costs | $52,525K | $37,583K | $100,983K | $70,977K |
| Total cash collections | $558,545K | $536,288K | $1,110,473K | $1,033,724K |
| **Cost of collection** | **9.40%** | 7.01% | **9.09%** | 6.87% |

⚠️ **One honest alternative reading:** portfolio *age* pushes accounts into the legal channel mechanically — older paper has exhausted voluntary contact. Some of this shift is therefore portfolio maturation rather than borrower behaviour. But that cannot explain PRA's call-center collections **declining in absolute terms** while its book grew, and it does not explain the cost ratio moving +239bps in a single year.

## 2. Supply and price — the exit wave has not cleared

| Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025 | face value purchased | dollars paid | cents on the dollar |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **ECPG** | $5,717M vs $3,435M (**+66.4%**) | $444M vs $367M (+20.9%) | 7.76¢ vs 10.69¢ (**−27.4%**) |
| **PRAA** | $2,130M vs $2,399M (−11.2%) | $297M vs $347M (−14.4%) | 13.93¢ vs 14.44¢ (−3.6%) |
| **Combined** | **+34.5%** | **+3.8%** | **9.44¢ vs 12.23¢ (−22.9%)** |

[PRIMARY: both 10-Qs, identical construction — face value and purchase price for portfolios acquired in the period; I verified the two tables are on the same basis before combining.]

⚠️ **Two caveats that materially qualify the combined figure.** First, **the two firms diverge on volume** — Encore made record purchases while PRA reduced its. A market-level "supply" reading from two firms with opposite deployment strategies is partly a story about their capital positions, not only the market's. Second, **Encore's blended cents-on-dollar mixes US and Europe** (Europe rose from 13.6% to 16.1% of its purchase dollars), and face value is not disclosed by geography — so some of that −27.4% is mix, not price. **The direction is well-supported; the magnitude is not precise.**

**What is NOT ambiguous is Encore's own vintage economics.** Its US purchase-price multiple (total estimated collections ÷ purchase price) runs 2.4 (2023), 2.5 (2024), 2.4 (2025), **2.3 (2026)** — roughly flat and slightly declining. **So the cheaper paper is not a price concession Encore is capturing; expected collections per dollar of face fell roughly in line with the price.** The paper is cheaper because it is worth less.

## 3. Terminal outcomes

| 12 months ending 6/30 | 2026 | 2025 | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Chapter 7** | **382,161** | 333,321 | **+14.7%** |
| Chapter 13 | 215,490 | 200,290 | +7.6% |
| Non-business (consumer) | 581,570 | 519,486 | +12.0% |
| **Total** | **608,511** | 542,529 | **+12.2%** |
| Ch-7 share of total | **62.8%** | 61.4% | +1.4pp |

[PRIMARY: US Courts, Table F-2, press release 2026-07-28]

**Chapter 7 is growing about twice as fast as Chapter 13.** Ch-7 requires passing a means test — it is the chapter for filers with no disposable income to support a repayment plan. **A rising Ch-7 share is a composition shift toward filers who cannot repay at all.**

Corroboration from an independent methodology: the NY Fed reports **~137,000 consumers with a new bankruptcy notation in 2026Q2, "a small increase from the previous quarter"** [PRIMARY: NY Fed HHDC 2026Q2]. Note this is a *consumer* flow from credit files, not a *case* count — same direction, and its QoQ language is more muted than the ~12-15% YoY court pace.

And the market instrument agrees with the government count: **PRA's insolvency-paper purchases +12.3% vs court filings +12.2%**, while its Core paper fell 12.6%.

## 4. The informal layer — real, but much smaller than it looks

Both listed pawn operators report strong growth:
- **FirstCash US pawn receivables +20.4% total, +19% same-store**, the twelfth consecutive quarter of double-digit same-store growth; **pawn loans *made* (origination flow) +41.7%** ($667.6M vs $471.3M). Management: *"more customer transactions and an increase in the average loan amount requested."*
- **EZCORP US Pawn PLO $254.5M, +15% total, +13% same-store**, *"an increase in average loan size and continued strong loan demand"*; average pawn balance per store $439K vs $389K.

⚠️ **But gold rose +22.1% YoY** (Jun-2025 $3,294 → Jun-2026 $4,023, GC=F), and **jewelry is 69% of EZCORP's collateral, up from 64%.** A fixed quantity of pawned gold collateralises a materially larger loan when gold is up 22%.

**What survives the confound, and what does not:**
- **Does NOT survive cleanly:** EZCORP's +13% same-store PLO is *below* the mechanical gold effect on its jewelry book alone (0.69 × 22.1% ≈ +15.2%). FirstCash's +19% is only modestly above it. **The balance measures are largely a collateral-revaluation story.**
- **Does survive:** FirstCash's **origination flow +41.7%** against gold +22.1% leaves roughly +16% real growth, and FirstCash separately cites **more customer transactions** — a count, not a value.

**EZCORP's CFO explicitly rebuts this reading:** *"the average loan size is going up, but it's because of demand for cash. It's not because gold is going up that people need more money. And so they need more money and they're using gold to get that."* That is a claim about borrower **motive**; it does not dissolve the **arithmetic** on balances. Both can be true. I report the caveat and the rebuttal, and lean on the flow and transaction-count evidence rather than the balances.

## 5. Reabsorption evidence — real, but pointed at a different population

The commission's branch (b) requires evidence that the cohort is getting credit again. There **is** such evidence, and it deserves full weight:

- **Subprime auto share of vehicle financing rose to 15.31% (Q4 2025) from 14.54% (Q4 2024)** — Experian's own framing: the largest subprime share since 2021 [PRIMARY: Experian press release, 2026-03-05]. *(Q1-2026 figures of 15.75% vs 14.40% circulate but I could not verify them — Experian's deck locks its numbers in chart images.)*
- The NY Fed's **620–659 near-prime** auto-origination band rose from 9.4% to 13% of originations [INSTITUTIONAL, secondary read of NY Fed charts].
- **FirstCash's AFF subprime POS book shows flat loss rates** — provision as % of gross transaction volume 29% vs 30%; allowance as % of leased merchandise 40% vs 41%.
- FICO reports **auto, card and personal-loan delinquency stabilized or improved**; the score decline is driven by student loans and mortgages.

⚠️ **But none of this is measured on the exited cohort.** The 620–659 band is **near-prime — above subprime, not the charged-off population.** Subprime auto origination growth is at least as consistent with lenders competing for the healthiest fringe of *never-defaulted* near-prime borrowers. **No dataset I found links newly-originated subprime borrowers to the 2024-26 charge-off cohort specifically.** That link is inferential in either direction.

⚠️ **And AFF's headline contraction is NOT a credit signal** — I nearly reported it as one. Leased merchandise fell 17%, but management attributes it to *"continued weakness in the furniture industry"*, and finance receivables fell only **2%** once off-balance-sheet loans held by its bank partner are included (versus −15% on-balance-sheet). A merchant-sector demand issue plus an accounting shift, not tightening underwriting.

## 6. ⚠️ The pre-registered read does not fit — and that is a finding

CARL's two branches each bundle several indicators. **The world produced a combination neither branch describes:**

| Indicator | Branch (a) predicts | Branch (b) predicts | **Observed** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charged-off paper supply | rising | — | **rising (+34.5% face)** → (a) |
| Pricing | falling | **stable** | **falling (−22.9%)** → (a) |
| Recovery rates on held paper | weakening | — | **over-performing; forecasts raised** → (b) |
| Voluntary payment behaviour | — | recovering | **DECLINING in absolute terms** → (a) |
| Chapter 7 | rising | — | **+14.7%, share rising** → (a) |
| Subprime origination | — | recovering | **rising** → (b) |
| Credit-invisible population | growing | — | **NOT MEASURABLE** |

**Branch (a) requires "weakening recovery," which is not what the debt buyers report.** Branch (b) requires "stable pricing," which is not what they charge. **I am reporting (a) because the two indicators that observe the exited cohort *directly* — voluntary payment behaviour and Chapter 7 — both point that way, and because the recovery-rate evidence has a survivorship explanation that the others do not.** But the read is a judgement between mismatched packages, not a clean trigger, and CARL should know that its discriminator did not resolve mechanically.

## Counter-Evidence

1. **The survivorship reconciliation is MY inference, not a measurement.** I have not demonstrated that Ch-7 discharge composition explains Encore's forecast raise. An equally available reading is the simple one: the cohort *is* paying better and Encore's models were conservative. **This is the load-bearing weakness in my verdict** and it deserves to be named as such.
2. **PRA states plainly it observed "stability in customer payment activity in both the U.S. and Europe."** That is an issuer's direct characterisation of the population in question, and it cuts against me.
3. **Encore's over-performance rate does not clearly deteriorate once normalized.** Recoveries above forecast were 7.2% of collections in Q2-26 vs 8.0% in Q2-25, but 6.8% vs 6.3% for H1 — mixed. The raw "+25% more over-performance" is mostly book growth.
4. **Subprime credit IS being extended again** (Experian, primary). If the exited cohort is being reabsorbed, my "they stopped paying" reading describes a transitional state, not a durable one.
5. **The pawn signal is substantially a gold story** on the balance measures, and I could not obtain transaction *counts* for either operator — the one series that would settle it.
6. **The single best discriminator does not exist.** CFPB's credit-invisibles series — designed to measure exactly the population outside the system — **has no vintage after December 2020** (2.7% / 7.0M adults). Confirmed absent, not unfetched. **The commission's central question has no federal instrument pointed at it.**
7. **FICO's average score is measured on the still-scored population** and structurally excludes anyone who fell out of it — so its decline (714, first back-to-back annual fall in a decade) says the *remaining* population is bifurcating, not that the exited cohort worsened.

**What would overturn this:** debt-buyer disclosure showing voluntary/call-center collections re-growing, or a Chapter 7 rollover. Both are observable within two quarters.

## Process Report

**Searches run:** primary-pull-led. Four SEC 10-Qs pulled and read directly (the spine, and where the verdict came from); one sub-agent on the population/reabsorption breadth under DATA-RETURN + budget guardrails; two targeted gold pulls to test a confound I raised against my own finding.

**What worked / didn't:**
- **The collections-CHANNEL split was the whole run.** The commission asked for supply and pricing as the price signal; those turned out to be ambiguous (the two buyers deploy in opposite directions, and Encore's blend is mix-contaminated). The unambiguous measurement was one line in PRA's MD&A showing the voluntary channel *declining*. **The commission's named instruments were not the ones that answered it.**
- **The sub-agent's reconciling hypothesis was better than my own framing** and I adopted it: Ch-7 discharge as a survivorship filter on the debt buyers' books. It converted my strongest counter-evidence into supporting evidence — which is exactly the move I should be most suspicious of, so I flagged it as inference and put it first in Counter-Evidence.
- `scripts/edgar_doc.py` — fixed yesterday — carried all four filings cleanly, including the inline-XBRL primary-document path that was broken before.

**Errors caught in-run (3), all in the same direction — the alarming reading had a mundane explanation:**
1. **I nearly reported FirstCash's AFF contraction (−17%) as "underwritten subprime credit shrinking while pawn booms."** Management attributes it to **furniture-industry weakness**, and the decline is −2% not −15% once off-balance-sheet loans are included. A seductive discriminator that wasn't one.
2. **I nearly reported pawn growth as clean distress evidence.** Gold +22.1% with jewelry at 69% of EZCORP's collateral means the mechanical revaluation effect (~15.2%) *exceeds* EZCORP's +13% same-store PLO growth. Only the origination flow and transaction-count evidence survive.
3. **I nearly read Encore's "+25% more recoveries above forecast" as a behaviour signal.** Normalized by collections it is 6.8% vs 6.3% (H1) and 7.2% vs 8.0% (Q2) — mostly book growth. The scale-robust figure is the forecast *revision*, which I used instead.

**A fourth, avoided by stopping:** I did not combine the two firms' cents-on-dollar without first verifying both 10-Qs compute face value and purchase price the same way. They do — but the levels differ so much (7.76¢ vs 13.93¢) that a blind combination would have looked authoritative and meant little.

**Confidence:** High on §§1–3. Medium on §4 (confound-adjusted). Medium on §5 (chart-locked sources). The verdict is a judgement between mismatched indicator packages, stated as such in §6.

**If I had more time/tools:** `poppler-utils` (`pdftoppm`) to render chart-locked PDF pages — Experian, NY Fed and Cox Automotive all publish their real numbers as images, and this is now a recurring blocker class rather than a one-off. That single tool would unlock the sub-620 origination share, which is the closest available proxy for the reabsorption question.

**Suggestions:** (a) install poppler-utils and add a `--render` path to `pdf2text.py` for slide-deck PDFs — going to BACKLOG; (b) a methodological note worth carrying beyond this report: **when a commission pre-registers a two-way read, check early whether the branches' indicators can come apart.** Here they did, and the honest answer required saying so rather than forcing a branch.
