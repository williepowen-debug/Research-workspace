## The Discount Window Passage — VERBATIM (HIGH VALUE)

Barr Review, §Failure of SVB:
> "During the final days before its failure, SVB's operational weaknesses became apparent as it struggled to execute on its CFP. For example, **SVB did not test its capacity to borrow at the discount window in 2022 and did not have appropriate collateral and operational arrangements in place to obtain liquidity.** While stronger operational capacity to obtain contingency funding in March 2023 would likely not have prevented SVB's failure, it could have facilitated a more orderly resolution."

Barr Review, §Liquidity in 2023 (p.60) — the fullest statement:
> "SVBFG was not able to monetize (immediately raise funds against) its investment securities. **SVBFG had not arranged for enough access to repo funding and had not signed up for the Federal Reserve's Standing Repurchase Agreement facility. SVBFG had limited collateral pledged to the Federal Reserve's discount window, had not conducted test transactions, and was not able to move securities collateral quickly from its custody bank or the FHLB to the discount window.** While contingent funding may not have been able to prevent the failure of the bank after the historic run on the bank, the lack of preparedness may have contributed to how quickly it failed."

And:
> "Supervisors focused on the potential for the firm to pledge additional collateral to the FHLB or the discount window, but **SVBFG's inadequate preparedness to access contingent funding sources likely contributed to the failure of the bank on the morning of Friday, March 10.**"

**Note:** the "late-in-the-day failed transfer" specific is **NOT** in the Barr Review in that form. What the Barr Review says is the general inability to move collateral quickly from custodian/FHLB to the DW. Do not cite a specific failed transfer to this document — see GAPS.

## The repo question — DID THE FED/BIS SAY REPO WAS CALM?

**Answer: I could not source an explicit Fed or BIS statement that repo / short-term funding markets functioned well or remained orderly in March 2023.** This is a genuine negative finding, and the near-miss is a trap:

- The Fed FSR May 2023 **does** say markets "continued to function throughout the episode without severe dislocations or reports of investors being unable to transact" — **but that sentence is about TREASURY markets, not repo.** Do not repurpose it. Verbatim: *"Despite these strains, **Treasury markets** continued to function throughout the episode without severe dislocations or reports of investors being unable to transact. By early April, the most acute strains had dissipated..."*
- The FSR's actual framing of funding is the **opposite** of calm at the bank level: *"Substantial withdrawals of uninsured deposits contributed to the failures of SVB, Signature Bank, and First Republic Bank and **led to increased funding strains for some other banks**"* — mitigated by policy intervention, DW borrowing, BTFP, and +$250B FHLB issuance.
- Directionally, the FSR is consistent with **market-wide funding infrastructure holding up while individual-institution funding failed**, and the sequence supports **deposits/equity moving first** (Mar 8–9) with credit/funding backstops responding Mar 12–16. But the Fed does not state a "repo was calm" conclusion, and I will not manufacture one.
