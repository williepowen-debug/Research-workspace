# Oversupplied US short-term-rental markets — a ranked national catalog (with a hard data caveat)
**Date:** 2026-07-24 | **Mode:** Thesis (catalog) | **Confidence:** Medium (direction/occupancy) / Low (dollar levels — single-scraper, unreconciled)

## Key Finding
Genuine STR oversupply is **real but concentrated in a specific set of metros**, and the national picture is **bifurcating into still-worsening vs already-self-correcting** — not uniformly deteriorating. The most robust tell is **low occupancy (<45%) alongside a supply surge**; the "revenue-per-listing" figures are a **denominator trap** and should not be trusted at the dollar level. **Data-quality caveat is load-bearing:** every metric traces to commercial scrapers (mostly AirROI) that disagree materially and contain impossible figures; the adversarial verifier **REFUTED** a clean "these are all genuinely oversupplied" ranking because AirROI's own trailing-12mo data shows *rising* per-listing revenue for many flagged metros (survivorship-inflated). Treat this as a **directional map, not a precise leaderboard.**

## Tier 2 — Oversupply with a clean RevPAR-*decline* tell (the more trustworthy signal)
These have an AirDNA point-in-time YoY RevPAR drop (harder evidence than trailing scraper aggregates):
- **Destin / 30A, FL — RevPAR −19.9% YoY** (worst named decline in the data), occupancy 39%, supply still surging. *The strongest FL Gulf oversupply signal.*
- **Gatlinburg / Pigeon Forge / Sevierville (Smokies), TN — RevPAR −10.6% YoY** while adding +11–45% supply.
- **Vail, CO — RevPAR −9.1%** while +11% supply *(confounded — 2025/26 snow drought cut bookings, a demand shock as much as oversupply)*.
- **Fort Myers Beach, FL** — occ AND RevPAR both falling (−5%), supply +19%.
- **Napa −11.6% / Palm Springs −12.2% / San Diego −7.4% / Denver −8.4% (RevPAR YoY), CA/CO** — rate compression.
- **Cape Coral (occ 35%, supply +92%), Tampa (43%, +26%), Jacksonville (41%, +24%), FL** — low occ + supply surge + flat/negative per-listing revenue.
- **Broken Bow/Hochatown OK (37%, +31%), Wisconsin Dells (31%, +71%), Lake of the Ozarks MO (32%, +21–28%), Big Sky MT (35%), Napa** — smaller resort/lake gluts.

## Tier 3 — SELF-CORRECTING (were oversupplied, now fixing as hosts exit)
The important nuance — these look bad on *level* but are improving on *trend*:
- **Austin, TX** — occupancy crushed 68→45%, but listings now **falling −12%** from peak; pricing held (ADR $294).
- **Phoenix, AZ** — grew 500% (2017→2025), listings now **falling** (7,282→6,359).
- **Nashville, TN** — listings **−15 to −22%** (permit enforcement + host exit); occupancy *rising*, revenue recovering.
- **Orlando, FL** — listings **−10%**, occ +3%, revenue **+8.9%**.
- **Asheville, NC** — listings **−18 to −22%**, RevPAR **+20% YoY** (rebounding post-hurricane).
- **Palm Springs, CA** — listings −7.8% (though revenue still down).

## Tier 4 — NOT actually oversupplied (big supply growth, but demand is absorbing it)
Do **not** put these on an oversupply list despite high listing growth:
- **Scottsdale AZ** (occ 64%, ADR rising, +56% supply absorbed), **Kissimmee FL** (+52–106% supply but revenue +8%), **Salt Lake City UT** (+17% supply, revenue +12%), **Nags Head/OBX NC** (ADR $470 holding), **Savannah GA**, **Clearwater/St. Pete FL**, **Snowmass/Park City/Jackson Hole**, **Joshua Tree CA**, **Hocking Hills OH**, **Gatlinburg** (park demand cited as resilient — though it also shows a RevPAR decline, so it straddles Tier 2/4).

## National backdrop
- Record **~1.76M US active STR listings** (+6.1% YoY, mid-2025); national occupancy fell ~57% (2024) → ~50% (spring 2025, AirROI) / ~57% (AirDNA — the two disagree on the denominator); demand growth decelerating 15.8% (2021) → ~5.5% (2026 proj).
- **AirROI's 5 oversaturation signals:** >20% supply growth · occupancy <50% · RevPAR compression · ADR stagnation amid falling occupancy · booking lead-time <30 days.
- **The Gatlinburg paradox:** highest supply growth in the panel (+45%) yet strong per-listing economics — proof that supply growth *alone* ≠ distress; demand-absorption is the discriminator.

## Counter-Evidence / why the verifier REFUTED a clean ranking
- On AirROI's *own* trailing-12mo data, a **majority** of flagged metros show *rising* revenue-per-listing (survivorship-inflated: dead listings churn out of the "active" denominator). Phoenix, PCB, Destin, Sarasota, Naples all show +rev on that measure — AirROI literally captions them "demand outpacing new inventory."
- The refutation itself rests on the **same single scraper** — which contains impossible figures (Nashville +24,469%, Vegas +99.9% supply) — and **no independent cross-check was reachable** (Rabbu 403, AllTheRooms/hospitable 404, Chalet 429, AirDNA 403/WebSearch capped).
- **Revenue-per-listing is a denominator trap:** more listings mechanically lowers it even with flat/rising demand.

## Source Quality Assessment
**Low–Medium.** No primary or institutional source exists for private-platform STR performance — everything is commercial scrapers (AirROI, Rabbu, AirDNA, Airbtics) with materially conflicting methodologies (Houston occupancy reads 35–57%; Bozeman listing count 469 vs 717; Denver supply −5.8% vs +35.6% by source). **Direction (occupancy level, who's exiting) is far more reliable than any dollar level.** The one hotel-grade institutional input (Cornovus/CoStar) covers *hotels*, not STR.

**Data gaps / frustrations:** the whole exercise is scraper-bound — no primary STR data exists; cross-checks 403/404/429'd; session WebSearch cap (200/200) hit before ~8 named markets. AirDNA 403-on-WebFetch is a recurring blocker (→ BACKLOG).
**Confidence:** Medium on tier placement by occupancy/trend; Low on dollar magnitudes. **This is a directional map, not a precise ranking** — the one thing that would settle it (metro YoY occupancy + cohort-controlled same-store revenue) requires a paywalled listing-level panel (AirDNA/Key Data/AllTheRooms).
