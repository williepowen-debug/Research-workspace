## Evidence

### LEG 1 — The loss waterfall: who absorbs a defaulted FHA/VA loan

**The federal wrap absorbs the credit loss; the servicer absorbs timing penalties + carry.**

| Layer | Who bears it | Magnitude / mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| **Borrower** | Loses equity | Thin for 2022+ cohort → larger residual for the insurer |
| **MBS investor** | ~Zero | Ginnie guaranty (full faith & credit); made whole by servicer P&I advances |
| **FHA credit loss** | **FHA MMI Fund** | Insurance claim pays ~all UPB + eligible debenture interest + capped expenses |
| **VA credit loss** | **VA guaranty (25–50%)** capped; residual → servicer/issuer | Runs off "net value" = FMV − VA acquire/dispose cost (38 CFR 36.4301) |
| **Servicer residual** | **Nonbank Ginnie servicer** | Curtailed debenture interest + non-reimbursable holding/overhead + **advance carry + par buyout funding** |

- **FHA MMI Fund is at a record capital ratio of 11.47%** as of Sept 30 2025 ($188.9B capital, +$16.1B YoY, **5.7× the 2.0% statutory minimum**, 11th straight year above) [PRIMARY: HUD FY2025 Annual Report to Congress on the MMI Fund, Nov 2025, hud.gov/fhammifrpt]. The fund can absorb a >5× rise in foreclosure rates before the statutory floor is threatened [ACADEMIC: Urban Institute]. **The credit loss is nowhere near escaping the federal wrap.**
- **FHA servicer penalty (non-credit, non-reimbursable):** three curtailment gates — first-legal, reasonable-diligence, 30-day conveyance — each forfeiting **all** debenture interest from the missed date to conveyance (cliff, not day-prorated). 2015-16 data: ~43% of claims curtailed; avg first-legal curtailment $5,360/loan (~7× the annual servicing fee); ~47% of property-preservation expense non-reimbursed [ACADEMIC: Urban Institute, "Reforming the FHA's Foreclosure and Conveyance Processes," 2017]. HUD OIG quantified the pool: **~239,000 properties, ~$2.23B FHA cost, ~$2.09B non-reimbursed holding costs** [PRIMARY: HUD OIG]. FHA replaced the COVID waterfall with a new permanent one (partial claim / payment supplement) mandatory **Oct 1 2025** (ML 2025-06); partial claims **DEFER** loss as an MMI receivable rather than realize it [PRIMARY: HUD ML 2025-06].
- **VA guaranty** is capped at 25–50% of the original loan (~25% for typical/large loans). **VASP (VA Servicing Purchase) was terminated May 1 2025** (announced 4/3/25, Circular 26-23-25); its replacement **Partial Claim Program (PCP)** (H.R.1815, signed 7/30/25) did not open for submissions until **June 15 2026** — a **~13–14 month loss-mit gap** (VA moratorium also ended 12/31/24, no blanket freeze in the interim) [PRIMARY: VA circulars / Federal Register; NEWS corroboration]. VASP had modified 17,000+ veteran households and, per MBA, averted "tens of thousands" of foreclosures; post-termination, **Ginnie VA buyout speeds rose** — i.e. loss migrating back onto servicers/issuers. Advocacy/press (not yet VA-VALERI-confirmed): **>10,000 veterans lost homes, ~90,000 VA loans seriously past due, ~33,000 in foreclosure** since termination [INSTITUTIONAL/NEWS — flagged as lead, not primary-banked].

### LEG 1c — Ginnie advance/buyout = the drain mechanism
- Ginnie issuers **must advance their own P&I to bondholders in full and on time** regardless of collection, until final resolution — **no reimbursement float** (recovered only at reperformance / foreclosure / insurance claim) [PRIMARY: Ginnie Mae MBS Guide Ch.15].
- **Buyout (EBO):** at **3 consecutive months unpaid (~90 dpd)** the issuer may repurchase the loan out of the pool at **100% of RPB less principal already advanced** (Ch.18 §18-3(B)(3)) — funding ~par of a nonperforming loan out of its own liquidity/warehouse. Stops the open-ended advance but is itself capital-intensive [PRIMARY: Ginnie Mae MBS Guide Ch.18].
- **2024 Risk-Based Capital Rule:** nonbank SF issuers must hold ≥6% RBCR (eff. 12/31/24) with a **punitive 250% MSR risk weight**; MFERs add min liquidity 10bps of SF MBS + net worth $2.5M+35bps of Ginnie UPB [PRIMARY/INSTITUTIONAL: Ginnie APMs]. MBA formally proposed a Ginnie EBO securitization (12/12/24) precisely because IMBs lack balance sheets to fund buyouts in stress.

### LEG 1d — Where BANK / GSE loss can (and can't) arise
- **GSE (Fannie/Freddie) exposure ≈ ZERO — programs are structurally separate.** FHA/VA → Ginnie Mae (federal); conventional → GSEs. No credit channel [PRIMARY, CONFIRMED].
- **Banks DO rebook delinquent Ginnie loans (ASC 860) at ~90+ dpd** onto the balance sheet (Call Report RC-C) — WFC bought back **~$14B (>7% of servicing book)** in 2020 [NEWS, CONFIRMED]. **But banks have largely EXITED FHA/VA: ~5% of Ginnie originations (Apr 2024); nonbanks service 83% of Ginnie (GAO 2024) → ~96% (Urban, Nov 2025).** Documented current bank EBO balances are immaterial — Wintrust $187.8M EBO-HFI vs a multi-billion equity base [PRIMARY, CONFIRMED]. *(A finder's "nonbank servicing 27%→66%" figure was REFUTED in verify as an agency-wide/Ginnie-specific mix-up; the Ginnie-specific figure is 83–96%.)*
- **Residual bank channel is SECOND-ORDER:** banks are the *warehouse / EBO / servicing-advance financiers* of the nonbanks (e.g. Texas Capital Bank markets an EBO product) — counterparty exposure, not FHA/VA LGD [INSTITUTIONAL, CONFIRMED]. **REGINALD's Apr-14 close already localized this to Apollo/Atlas SP ($6.9B at PFSI = 78% of that book), not US regionals.**

### LEG 3 — Student-loan defaulters feeding FHA DQ (mechanism-plausible, aggregate-corroborated, not FHA-isolated)
- Student-loan **on-ramp ended 9/30/2024**; 90+ DPD jumped to **7.74% Q1 2025** (from <1%), stayed ~10% through 2025; Treasury Offset Program restarted **5/5/2025**; **~1.0M defaults Q4 2025 + ~2.6M Q1 2026**; defaulters' scores fell **91 pts (567→476)** [PRIMARY: NY Fed HHD; ED]. ## Counter-Evidence (weighted equally)

1. **The federal backstop is nowhere near impaired** — MMI at a record 11.47%; VA guaranty intact. This is the single strongest argument that the channel is *absorbed*, not transmitted-as-credit-loss.
2. **Urban Institute: the FHA DQ rise is "back to 2017-18 levels," and NONE of five leading hypotheses (relaxed DTI, GSE competition, weak HPA, loss-mit incentives, …) fully explains it.** The "2022 thin-equity catastrophe" framing is only *partly* supported: 2022 is the worst modern non-pandemic vintage (first-yr SDQ 4.82%) **but** FHA's 95%+ LTV share actually *declined* (64.8%'17 → 57.0%'24). Don't overstate vintage-specific collapse.
3. **The big public servicers are compliant and well-capitalized** (PFSI, Rocket); **no confirmed discrete 2025-26 stress event** (facility draw, covenant breach, emergency transfer) at a mainstream servicer was found.
4. **The student-loan→FHA-DQ link is corroborated in aggregate but not FHA-isolated** — no dataset proves the 2023-24 FHA-vintage borrowers *were themselves* the student-loan defaulters. Mechanism-plausible, not causally banked.
5. **VA leg is much milder than FHA** (PFSI VA 60+ 1.7% vs FHA 8.0%) — the VASP-gap foreclosure spike is real but off a smaller, higher-FICO (733) base.
