## Key Finding

**The checkpoint is 11 days out and it is sharper, earlier, and differently-shaped than the fleet's record says.** The date is not "early September" — it is **Monday September 7, 2026**, filed verbatim as the "Scheduled Termination Date," and it falls on **Labor Day**, so the operative business days are Friday Sept 4 (a weekly liquidity test date) and Tuesday Sept 8. The two extensions are not general-purpose: **Sept 21 requires a binding, unconditional financing commitment; Nov 6 requires signed definitive sale documents** — and all three extension paths but one require the Special Committee to determine the transaction produces **Payment in Full of a ~$318M claim**, against an equity market currently pricing the whole company at **$19.2M** (8,327,329 shares × $2.31, live 2026-08-27).

**Who decides is a three-key answer, and the third key is the one nobody has written down:** Silver Point holds the binary; the Special Committee holds the "Payment in Full" judgment that unlocks every extension; and the **Special Committee also holds a 3-of-4 consent right over any voluntary bankruptcy filing** — a committee on which **two of four members were installed at the lenders' own demand** as a milestone of the waiver. Neither management nor the lenders can file alone.

**And the cascade runs the direction most readers will assume backwards.** The ABS is non-recourse for *payment*, but **Section 8.1(p) makes any servicer-termination or termination event at any of the five live trusts an Event of Default on the Silver Point term loan.** Transmission is trusts → term loan, not term loan → trusts. Silver Point has already pre-wired the other direction too: **SST is installed as Backup Servicer, SST-or-whomever-the-Agent-appoints is Successor Servicer, and §8.2 pays the Backup and Successor Servicer FIRST in the enforcement waterfall — ahead of the Agent and ahead of the Lenders.** A servicing transition is a built structure here, not a tail risk.

**⚠️ THE SINGLE MOST DECISION-RELEVANT FINDING: 9/7 is not a covenant test date — it is the date a pre-built lender control package arms in one step.** Amendment §2.1 is a *three-part* standstill, and all three parts expire together. The Lenders agreed (i) to waive the Specified Defaults, **(ii) not to exercise any rights or remedies with respect to any Additional Reporting Period or Cash Dominion Period, and (iii) not to deliver a Servicing Centralization Period Notice** — each **"during the Specified Period"** [PRIMARY: Amendment §2.1]. So on 9/7, absent extension, three levers re-arm simultaneously: **acceleration**, **cash dominion**, and **the servicing-transfer trigger**. And a Cash Dominion Period is already triggered on the underlying terms — it commences on any Event of Default, or on Liquidity below $35M for five consecutive business days, against a revised covenant floor of **$7M** — while *exiting* it requires average daily Liquidity **≥$40M for 30 days** plus a Collateral Coverage Ratio **≥1.55x for three consecutive months**. On the disclosed numbers that exit is unreachable on any near horizon. **The servicing consequence is not a second-order downstream effect; it is armed on the same day as the covenant.**

**The most useful number for OTTO is not a base rate — it is Honor Finance's 75%.** There is **no** 2023-26 precedent of a subprime auto lender entering a negotiated, milestone-bearing standstill and reaching a public outcome; that base rate does not exist and I will not manufacture one. What does transfer is the transfer-disruption number: in the one clean, **non-bankruptcy, orderly** servicer handoff on record, the successor reported **75% net loss-to-liquidation in its first two months**.

## 1. Who decides — three keys, and they are not held by the same party

### Key 1 — Silver Point (Agent) and the Requisite Lenders hold the binary

The waiver converts to a **permanent** waiver of the Specified Defaults if, as of the Scheduled Termination Date, (i) all Milestones are satisfied, (ii) the Borrowers comply with minimum Liquidity and the minimum Collateral Coverage Ratio, (iii) no other Event of Default has occurred, and (iv) all Amendment conditions are met [PRIMARY: First Amendment and Limited Waiver §2.3, 8-K 2026-06-25, acc `0001171843-26-004311` Ex-10.1].

That reads as mechanical, but three provisions hand the discretion back to the Agent:
- **"Milestone Deadline" means the date in the Milestone Schedule "or such later date as the Agent may reasonably agree"** — the Agent can move its own tripwires [PRIMARY: Amendment, definitions].
- **§6.5: no Credit Party may take any "Material Action" without the prior written consent of the Requisite Lenders** — covering new debt, asset sales >$500K, distributions, org-document changes and affiliate transactions.
- The Specified Period ends immediately on **failure to satisfy *any* Milestone**, and such a breach "shall constitute an immediate Event of Default... without the requirement of any notice, demand, passage of time, presentment, protest or forbearance of any kind" [PRIMARY: Amendment §"Specified Period Termination Date", clauses (c)–(d)].

### Key 2 — the Special Committee holds the "Payment in Full" judgment

Every extension turns on a **Special Committee Majority** determination. **"Special Committee Majority" means a vote of at least three out of four members** — so **any two members can block** [PRIMARY: Amendment, definitions].

The committee was reconstituted under the Amendment itself. Originally (5/22) Adam Paul (Chair), Jonathan Buba, Joshua Welch; it now consists of **Adam Paul, Jonathan Buba, Gilbert E. Nathan and Michael J. Wartell**, with Welch off and **Nathan and Wartell appointed 6/23/2026 "in accordance with the Company's obligations under the Amendment"** [PRIMARY: DEF 14A filed 2026-08-14, acc `0001628280-26-057070`, "Special Committee"; 8-K 2026-06-25 Item 5.02].

These are not generalist directors. Paul is a former global Co-Head of Restructuring at Mayer Brown and a Senior Managing Director of Gordian Group; **Nathan is Managing Member of Jackson Square Advisors, which provides "board representation, trustee and fiduciary services, and consulting services to creditors' committees," and has held "numerous other independent director, trustee and plan administrator positions for companies undergoing restructurings or wind-downs"**; Wartell co-founded Venor Capital and sits on Grupo Aeroméxico's board — a post-chapter-11 reorganized issuer [PRIMARY: DEF 14A 2026-08-14, director biographies]. Their engagement letters are filed as Exhibits 10.2/10.3 to the 6/25 8-K, contracted through their own advisory entities (Jackson Square Advisors; Bluerose Associates).

### Key 3 — the bankruptcy filing decision sits with that same committee

The Amendment requires the Credit Parties' organizational documents to **"require the consent of the Special Committee Majority for the filing of any voluntary bankruptcy or other Insolvency Proceeding with respect to any Credit Party,"** and to provide that any inconsistent modification is **"ultra vires and void ab initio"** [PRIMARY: Amendment, "Special Committee Actions" and §5.21].

**This is the answer to DR-6's decision question.** A voluntary filing needs 3 of 4 votes on a committee where two seats were created by the lenders' own milestone. Management cannot file over the lenders' objection; the lenders cannot force a filing either. Read against §8.1(g) — where a board resolution *authorizing* a filing is itself an automatic Event of Default with automatic acceleration — the filing decision has been deliberately routed through a body the lenders helped compose.

**The five outcomes, with the gate each requires:**

**A — Permanent waiver (9/7).** All milestones met + covenant compliance + no other EOD. The milestone set includes **"execution of a support agreement with the Agent and requisite Lenders"** — i.e. an RSA [PRIMARY: 8-K 2026-06-25 Item 1.01; 10-K Note B]. **No support agreement has been publicly announced as of 2026-08-27.** An RSA with lenders would be a material definitive agreement requiring an Item 1.01 8-K; none exists. *(Caveat: the milestone's deadline is redacted, so its absence may mean "not yet due" rather than "missed.")*

**B — Extension to 9/21 (the only path that does NOT require paying Silver Point out).** For an **Equity Financing or a Permitted Warehouse Facility**, the Special Committee must determine it results in Payment in Full **"or a permanent waiver of the Specified Defaults pursuant to Section 2.3."** For a **refinancing** of the term facility, only Payment in Full qualifies [PRIMARY: Amendment, "Scheduled Termination Date" (a) and (b)].

⚠️ **This asymmetry is the single most easily-missed provision in the document, and it is the most plausible live path.** A new warehouse facility that restores compliance can buy time **without** repaying $318M — and management's own going-concern plan names **"establishing a new revolving warehouse facility"** [PRIMARY: 10-K Note B]. Both (a) and (b) still require a **binding commitment from a credit-worthy counterparty "not subject to internal approvals, completion of diligence or other conditions"** — a signed, unconditional commitment, not a term sheet or an LOI.

**C — Extension to 11/6 (sale).** Requires **binding definitive purchase documentation** for a sale of all or substantially all equity or assets, conditioned **solely** on customary closing conditions including HSR — explicitly **not** subject to diligence, internal approvals or financing — plus a Special Committee determination of Payment in Full. The extension then runs to the earliest of termination of those documents, five business days after closing conditions are satisfied, or 11/6 [PRIMARY: Amendment, "Scheduled Termination Date" (c)].

**D — Specified Period terminates.** On 9/7 with conditions unmet, on any milestone miss, on any other EOD, or on any breach of the Amendment. Remedies become available; acceleration follows at the Requisite Lenders' request. Default interest steps up **+2.50%** [PRIMARY: conformed Credit Agreement §2.7, §8.1]. The company states plainly it **"would not have sufficient liquidity to repay such indebtedness if it were accelerated"** [PRIMARY: 10-K Note B]. **Acceleration is the loudest of the three levers but not necessarily the first: Cash Dominion and the Servicing Centralization Notice arm on the same date and require no acceleration to use.** A lender wanting control of collections and servicing without triggering the cross-defaults an acceleration sets off has, by construction, exactly that option.

**E — Voluntary chapter 11.** Requires Special Committee Majority (3 of 4). Under §8.1(g) the enabling board resolution is itself an automatic EOD with **automatic** acceleration — no lender vote required.

## 3. ⚠️ What is NOT observable — read this before building any monitor

**Both covenants that decide the 9/7 outcome are measured on data that is delivered privately to Silver Point and never filed.**

- **Liquidity** = "all Unrestricted Cash of the Credit Parties," reported to the Agent in a **weekly Liquidity Report** [PRIMARY: Amendment §6.7].
- The **Collateral Coverage Ratio** is computed off a **Borrowing Base Certificate and Data Tape** delivered semi-monthly to the Agent [PRIMARY: Amendment §6.7(b)].
- A **13-week cash flow budget** with a **$2.0M maximum unfavourable variance in Unlevered Free Cash Flow** per Budget Testing Period [PRIMARY: Amendment §6.8(b)].
- **The Milestone Schedule (Annex II) is REDACTED** — omitted under Item 601(a)(5) of Regulation S-K on the stated basis that it "does not contain information material to an investment or voting decision," even though **failure of any milestone is the Specified Period's primary termination trigger** [PRIMARY: Amendment, Annex II as filed].

**Consequence:** the public record cannot tell you whether CRMT is in compliance today, and cannot tell you when the next milestone falls. **No amount of monitoring closes this gap before an 8-K.** The first public data covering the waiver period is the **Q1 FY2027 10-Q due ~9/9 — two days after the decision date.** Any instrument keyed to "watch the covenant" is untrippable by construction; the only observable is an Item 1.01/2.04/5.02 8-K, or the 10-Q.

### The cascade, in the direction it actually runs

**Trusts → term loan (the one people miss).** §8.1(p) "Non-Recourse Debt Facilities Events" makes **any "servicer termination event", "event of default" or "termination event" — or any event permitting exercise of remedies against the collateral or replacement of the servicer — at any Permitted Non-Recourse Debt Facility an Event of Default under the Silver Point credit agreement**, once past cure and unwaived. ABS-level trouble does not stay ring-fenced; it detonates the term loan. Note the architecture is deliberate: §8.1(b)'s general cross-default to Material Indebtedness **expressly carves out 8.1(p) debt**, routing ABS events through their own gate.

**Term loan → trusts.** Payment recourse does not travel: "term securitization investors have no recourse to the Company's assets beyond the related auto finance receivables, the amounts on deposit in the reserve account, and the cash from collections" [PRIMARY: 10-K Note C]. What travels is **servicing**. **The Company or a subsidiary services every one of the five live trusts** [PRIMARY: 10-K Note D]. And the company's own risk factor states the consequence:

> "Back-up servicers are typically third-party specialty servicers that rely on call-center-based collection models and may have less familiarity with the Company's customer base, dealership network, and in-person payment collection practices important to a significant portion of its customers. As a result, delinquencies, defaults, and credit losses on receivables serviced by a back-up servicer **could be higher** than if the Company had continued to service them." [PRIMARY: 10-K Item 1A]

This is not hypothetical for CRMT specifically: **60 dealerships were closed in FY2026 (154 → 94)** and the company discloses that "a large number of the Company's customers have historically made their scheduled contract payments **in person**," with that transition "ongoing" [PRIMARY: 10-K Item 1A].

**Silver Point has already built the transition.** **SST (Systems & Services Technologies, Inc.)** is the **Backup Servicer** under a Backup Servicing Agreement; the **Successor Servicer** is "SST... **or any other Person appointed by the Administrative Agent**"; §6.9 obliges cooperation with the Backup Servicer during a defined **"Servicing Centralization Period"** and records that the Credit Parties **have already been cooperating**; §6.10 requires delivery of an executed Servicing Agreement with the Successor Servicer. Most tellingly, **§8.2 applies enforcement proceeds FIRST to the Backup Servicer and Successor Servicer — ahead of the Agent, ahead of the Lenders** [PRIMARY: Amendment §§6.9–6.10, 8.2; conformed agreement definitions].

**And SST sits on BOTH sides.** SST is not only Silver Point's backup servicer under a **freshly-executed Backup Servicing Agreement dated the same day as the Amendment (2026-06-19)**; it is also the **named Backup Servicer in every trust-level Sale and Servicing Agreement** [PRIMARY: 10-K exhibit index — e.g. the 2024-02 SSA dated 10/9/2024 among the trust, ACM Funding LLC, America's Car-Mart, Deutsche Bank as Indenture Trustee, and "Systems & Services Technologies, Inc. as Backup Servicer"]. The same institution is pre-positioned to take servicing at the trusts and at the term-loan collateral.

**At the trust level, the operative term is "Servicer Replacement Event"** — not "servicer default" or "early amortization." On such an event the Indenture Trustee, **at the direction of a majority of the Controlling Class**, issues a Servicer Termination Notice, and **the Backup Servicer may then appoint itself or a successor without noteholder consent** [PRIMARY: ACM Auto Trust 2025-4 Sale and Servicing Agreement, Art. VII, Ex. 4.3 to the 8-K filed 2025-12-23]. Notably, that document states **"the Transaction Documents do not require any policies or procedures to monitor any performance or other triggers and events of default"** — consistent with these being **term** deals amortising from collections rather than revolving structures, i.e. **no cumulative-net-loss or delinquency early-amortisation trigger was found**. ⚠️ **Honest gap: the itemised definition of "Servicer Replacement Event" lives in Appendix A, which did not render in the fetched exhibit — so whether CRMT insolvency is an enumerated trigger is READ-ADJACENT, NOT READ.** The 10-K's own risk factor implies it is: each securitization "requires that a back-up servicer be in place and be capable of assuming servicing upon the occurrence of a servicer default, termination event, **insolvency**, or other specified event with respect to the Company as servicer" [PRIMARY: 10-K Item 1A] — but that is the company describing its program, not the trigger list itself. **I have not asserted the trust-side trigger and neither should a consumer of this report.**

## 5. Base rates — the honest verdict

**The base rate DR-6 asked for does not exist.** Across 2023-26 I found no US subprime-auto lender that entered a negotiated, milestone-bearing lender standstill and reached a public outcome. The three findable failures — **Tricolor** (Ch.7, 2025-09-10), **American Car Center** (Ch.7, 2023-03-14), **U.S. Auto Sales/USASF** (Ch.7, Aug 2023) — are 3-for-3 liquidations, but **none of them had a standstill at all**: they went from first visible distress to operational death in ~5 days, ~2-3 weeks and ~4 months respectively. A 3-for-3 rate off a cohort that never entered the state being asked about is **not** the requested base rate, and reporting it as one would be a category error.

⚠️ **Selection runs hard against this cohort.** Fraud and regulatory collapse (Tricolor's double-pledged collateral; USASF's CFPB judgment) generate indexable press; a quietly-extended waiver generates none. Treat 3-for-3 as an **upper bound**, not a point estimate. CRMT is a liquidity/covenant case with **no fraud or misconduct allegation** and a **material weakness that was remediated with an unqualified ICFR opinion at 4/30/26** — which places it closer to the unobserved quiet-workout population than to Tricolor.

**What does transfer — the transfer-disruption anchor, and it is the number to carry:**

**Honor Finance / Westlake, 2018.** Honor resigned as servicer (notice to the indenture trustee 7/13/2018); senior noteholders approved a Westlake Financial unit as successor; Westlake took over ~9/1/2018. **In its first two months Westlake reported net loss-to-liquidation of 75%**, with 30-90 day delinquencies at **31.69%** at end-August — and the reporting attributes the recovery-rate deterioration substantially to the fact that **Honor had dropped its collection activity prior to the handover** [INSTITUTIONAL: S&P, via Asset Securitization Report and Auto Finance News, 2018]. This was an **orderly, non-bankruptcy, consensual** transfer of a single deal and it still produced that. ⚠️ *Provenance note: verified in two independent trade outlets citing S&P, but not re-confirmed against an S&P primary — carry it tagged.*

The mechanism — the *outgoing* servicer disengaging before handover — is precisely what CRMT's **$2.594M of executive cash retention awards** (CEO $1.2M, CFO $563K, COO $531K, CAO $300K, approved 6/3/26, repayable if employment ends before the earlier of a change of control or one year) exist to prevent [PRIMARY: 10-K Note Q].

**Severity anchor (tail, not modal): Tricolor.** Wilmington Trust issued servicer-termination notices across all 7 TAST deals; Vervent (the designated backup servicer) was court-authorized as successor 2025-09-19; September, October and November 2025 distributions were **missed**; KBRA downgraded all 34 tranches to **D(sf)** and withdrew them. Fleet record adds: **~3.2% projected recovery from vehicle auctions** against $2B+ debt, sub-10¢ ABS pricing, and a **$113M distribution gridlock** with Vervent blocked from its initial servicer report while trustee, banks and the Ch.7 trustee disputed ownership [PRIMARY-adjacent: KBRA publications; fleet: `AGENTS/OTTO/MEMORY.md` entries 2026-05-21/22]. ⚠️ **Tricolor was a fraud case** (~29,000 double-pledged loans, ~30,000 missing vehicles). It bounds the severity of a disorderly outcome; it does not forecast CRMT's.

## Counter-Evidence

**Against the distress read:**
1. **The lenders funded time rather than taking the keys.** Silver Point could have accelerated at the 6/19 stipulated Events of Default and did not; it granted a ~11-week runway, took $18M of fees **as PIK added to principal rather than in cash** (liquidity-preserving), and required a marketing process. That is a lender optimizing for going-concern value, not liquidation.
2. **Path (a) does not require Payment in Full.** A new warehouse facility restoring compliance unlocks 9/21 without repaying $318M — a materially lower bar than the headline reading suggests, and it matches management's stated plan.
3. **CRMT retained ABS access more recently than the narrative implies** — a $161.3M deal in **December 2025**, *after* the Silver Point closing.
4. **No fraud, and the material weakness was remediated** with an effective ICFR opinion at 4/30/26. Every findable comparable failure in the cohort was fraud- or misconduct-driven.
5. **The equity is not zero:** $2.31 and +4.52% on the day.
6. **Three ABS trusts were repaid in full** (2023-1, 2023-2, 2024-1) — the structures have been paying down as designed.

**Against the sanguine read:**
1. **Management concluded its own plans do NOT alleviate the substantial doubt** [PRIMARY: 10-K Note B] — the strongest possible form of the disclosure.
2. **The company stipulated in writing that the Specified Defaults are Events of Default that "cannot be cured"** [PRIMARY: Amendment §7.2] — this is not a technical or anticipated breach.
3. The 8-K's own forward-looking language names **"the potential need to seek protection under applicable bankruptcy or insolvency laws"** and **"a significant or complete loss"** for common holders.
4. **Nothing has been publicly announced in the 69 days since the Amendment** — no support agreement, no warehouse, no equity, no sale. With 11 days left and every path requiring a *binding, non-contingent* commitment, the silence is running out of room.
5. Silver Point's warrants strike at **$22.63** against a $2.31 stock — it has **no equity upside** and behaves as a pure secured creditor.

**What would disprove the central finding:** an Item 1.01 8-K disclosing a signed support agreement, a binding warehouse/equity commitment, or definitive sale documents. Any one of those flips the tree to A/B/C and would arrive before or on 9/7.

## Process Report

**Searches run:** Primary-pull-led, per Engine sizing — this was a single-name issuer-filing question, class (c) of the carve-out, so the spine was EDGAR and the fan-out was sized to the residual (two targeted sub-agents: subprime-auto workout base rates; CRMT ABS structure). ~15 direct SEC document pulls; 2 targeted WebSearches (post-8/14 developments; Honor Finance verification). The load-bearing verdict came entirely from the primary pull — again.

**What worked / didn't:**
- **The conformed credit agreement was attached as Annex I to the Amendment** — 576KB in one exhibit. Nearly everything decisive (§8.1(p), §8.2 waterfall, SST, the trust list, original §6.15 levels) was in there, not in the 8-K narrative. **The 8-K summary omits every one of them.** A run that stopped at the 8-K would have missed the entire cascade architecture and reported a materially thinner answer.
- **Fleet-memory check before searching paid off twice:** BROCK's 7/28 packet gave me a verified baseline so I spent zero budget re-deriving $300M/$458.7M, and OTTO's MEMORY.md supplied Tricolor detail (Vervent, ~3.2% recovery, $113M gridlock, the corporate-side *denial* of the Wilmington full-exit allegation) that was better-adjudicated than what a fresh search returned.
- `scripts/edgar_fetch.py` **threw an unhandled HTTPError traceback** on `--help`/bare invocation — logged to BACKLOG.
- SEC's `index.json` for an accession **returned only the wrapper files and omitted the actual documents**; the `-index.html` page listed all three exhibits correctly. Worth encoding: don't trust `index.json` alone for exhibit discovery.

**Data gaps:** the redacted Milestone Schedule (unclosable); private covenant reporting (unclosable before the 9/9 10-Q); trust-level servicer-default trigger text (not read at primary); 2025-4's indenture trustee.

**Source frustrations:** none material — SEC behaved with a declared UA throughout. The genuine wall is **redaction plus private reporting**, not access.

**Errors caught in-run (3):**
1. I initially inferred from the credit agreement's trust list that **CRMT had done no ABS since August 2025**. The 10-K's XBRL tags showed a **2025-4** issuance; it closed **December 2025**, after the credit agreement's list was struck. The inference was wrong by four months and in the direction that overstated distress — caught by cross-checking the XBRL tag list against the narrative.
2. I nearly reported the **Wilmington Trust → Deutsche Bank trustee migration as a response to Tricolor.** It began with 2025-1 in **January 2025**, eight months before Tricolor collapsed. Flagged in-text as a checked-and-rejected inference so no downstream reader re-derives it.
3. I initially read the extension paths as uniformly requiring **Payment in Full**. Path (a) also accepts "**or a permanent waiver**" — which makes the warehouse/equity path materially easier than the other two and changes which outcome is most plausible. Caught on a re-read of the definition.
4. **A PRIMARY-VS-PRIMARY trustee conflict that I would have shipped.** My draft table gave **Wilmington Trust** as indenture trustee for the live 2024-2 deal, taken from the conformed credit agreement's "Existing Securitizations" definition. The ABS leg came back with **Deutsche Bank**, from the 10-K exhibit index. I resolved it at primary rather than averaging or deferring: the exhibit index incorporates *the actual Indenture dated 10/9/2024* by reference to its own 8-K, and it wins over a recital list drafted a year later. **All five live trusts are Deutsche Bank; there is no live Wilmington Trust exposure**, and the migration date I was about to publish (Jan 2025) was also wrong (it was ≤ Oct 2024). **The transferable lesson: "PRIMARY" is not one tier.** An operative contract's recital/definition list is *weaker* evidence about a third-party document than an exhibit index pointing at that document — and it is the kind of error a single-source primary pull cannot see. It surfaced only because two independent legs read two different primaries and disagreed. `[[finding_reconcile_mismatch_does_not_say_which_side_is_wrong]]`

**Confidence:** High on architecture, dates, the ABS map, and the covenant arithmetic — all filed-document primary. **Explicitly NOT-KNOWABLE** on current compliance. Medium on the ABS cascade's trust-side mechanics (the term-loan side is primary; the trust side is inferred from structure and precedent and is labelled as such).

**If I had more time/tools:** read the trust-level indentures and sale/servicing agreements at the depositor CIKs for actual servicer-default and early-amortization trigger text; that is the one substantive leg left, and it is reachable — ACM Funding LLC / ACM Auto Trust filings on EDGAR.
