# New World screwworm: cattle supply, beef prices and food CPI

**Date:** 2026-10-22 | **Mode:** Thesis | **Confidence:** Timeline Medium-High (events primary; case count news-grade) · Cattle supply and imports High (NASS/ERS primary) · Price levels and CPI weights High (BLS/ERS primary) · Scenario CPI ranges Low (DEWEY assumption arithmetic, not forecasts) | **Request:** REQ-DEWEY-20261022-004 (WALTER) | **Recipients:** CARL (action); FERT, AEOLUS, RED, PROME (info)

## Key Finding

Screwworm has not yet shown up in beef prices. Retail beef CPI peaked in July 2026 and fell in August and September, to +3.5% YoY. Cattle futures are roughly flat since the day before the first US case. The 2024–25 beef inflation was the herd cycle, which predates any US case. The supply channel that has actually bitten is the Mexican feeder-cattle border closure, a shortfall of about 2M head over 21 months. No count of US livestock deaths was found. Even in a stylized regional-establishment scenario (cattle +10–20%, pass-through 0.4–0.6×), the mechanical effect is **+0.3 to +0.9 pp on food at home and +0.03 to +0.08 pp on headline CPI**, spread over several months. That is a real food-at-home leg worth one watch row, but a rounding error for headline CPI.

The historical record shows two patterns. Small or isolated areas clear fast, such as Curaçao in 1954 and the Florida Keys in about 6 months. Areas next to an infested source re-infest: Sanibel, and Texas in 1972 [INSTITUTIONAL: FAO/Novy]. Today's Texas and New Mexico cases sit next to an infested source, with Mexican cases ~87 mi from the border in Coahuila (9/28/2026, snippet-grade). Fly supply is about one-fifth of USDA's buildout figure until a US plant produces in 2027. The FAO paper gives no beef retail price record for 1972, so **no historical retail-price analogue for a screwworm outbreak was found**.

The supply effect so far runs through **placements**: fewer Mexican feeders now mean fewer fed cattle at slaughter several months later. The feeding-period length was not sourced in this run [UNSOURCED]. The effect does not run through US animal mortality. **Mechanical CPI arithmetic (first-order; ignores substitution; excludes restaurant beef in food away from home):**

| Retail beef move | Δ Food at home (pp) | Δ Headline CPI-U (pp) |
|---|---|---|
| +3% | +0.23 | +0.019 |
| +5% | +0.38 | +0.032 |
| +10% | **+0.77** | **+0.064** |
| +12% | +0.92 | +0.077 |
| +20% | +1.53 | +0.128 |

Formula: Δ FAH ≈ (0.638/8.325) × Δ%beef; Δ headline ≈ 0.00638 × Δ%beef [PRIMARY inputs: BLS RI Dec 2025; DEWEY arithmetic]. With pass-through of 0.4–0.6×, **a 10–20% cattle shock gives +4–12% retail beef, which gives +0.3–0.9 pp on food at home and +0.03–0.08 pp on headline, over several months.**

Two reading notes for CARL. First, retail is still catching down: the farm-to-retail spread is wide, so retailers have margin to absorb an early cattle rise before shelf prices move [inference from ERS spreads]. Second, base effects alone pull beef YoY toward about +2–3% in Q4 2026 if the retail level stays flat, with no screwworm effect [DEWEY arithmetic on BLS API]. A falling YoY print therefore does not show that screwworm risk has gone away.

The horizon scaling matters. Feeder losses reach fed-cattle supply only after a feeding period (length [UNSOURCED] here), and retail lags farm prices by up to a year [PRIMARY summary: ERS TB-1874, not document-verified]. Within 6 months, even scenario B shows mostly in feeder futures, not CPI. The 12–18 month window is where the CPI ranges above would land. For scale, the largest recent beef shock, the 2014–15 drought liquidation, took food at home to only +3.7% YoY and left headline at or below 2.1% [PRIMARY: BLS API].

## Source Quality Assessment

The price, weight and cattle-supply legs rest on primary series: the BLS API and relative-importance tables, ERS trade, spreads and outlook files, and NASS inventory and cattle-on-feed reports. All were pulled or read on 2026-10-22. The outbreak timeline is primary for events (APHIS, USDA, TPWD, TAHC, CDC, FDACS) but **news-grade for cumulative counts**, because the APHIS dashboard was unreachable. Futures are Yahoo Finance daily closes for named CME contracts, not CME settlements, and the event-table reading is not an event study. Pass-through lag evidence relies on summaries of two ERS publications whose PDFs returned 404. The scenario cattle-shock bands are assumptions. No source quantifies screwworm-specific pass-through, which is why the scenario component is rated Low. Mexican border-state case figures are relays of SENASICA, not a SENASICA primary.
