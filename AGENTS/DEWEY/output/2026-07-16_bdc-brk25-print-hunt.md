## Key Finding

**BRK-25 does NOT fire. No arms-length sub-90¢ BDC/private-credit transaction printed in the window** — and the six-week-old watch that was supposed to fire it is **refuted at its root**: the "Apollo shopping captive BDC @ $0.85/NAV" figure was never a bid. Every sub-90¢ number that surfaced is disqualified on vintage, instrument, or relatedness — usually more than one.

> **The four abundant "sub-90¢" numbers are all traps.** Each looks like a fire and is not. Full adjudication in §3.

**$0.85/NAV ≈ MFIC's May stock price ÷ NAV.** Consistent with a single unattributed relay misreading the trading ratio as a bid. **It is also stale: the discount has widened to ~0.72×.**

### Sale status: UNRESOLVED — and the precise wording matters
- WSJ 5/10/26: Apollo in talks to sell MFIC, ~$3B, buyer "would likely be another BDC," possibly paying in its own shares; **no certainty of a deal** [NEWS, via Reuters relay].

**This is *"no evidence a deal was reached"* — NOT *"talks were abandoned."* No source reports either outcome.** Talks can die privately with no 8-K; silence is consistent with both.

**Arms-length determination is PREMATURE, not unanswerable** — there is no buyer, price, or agreement to test. ## 3. The four traps — every abundant "sub-90¢" number, adjudicated

**This is the most reusable part of this report.** Each of these looks like a fire to a keyword search.

**(1) Campbell Lutyens "13.6% average LP-led discount" (= 86.4¢) — fails on four counts.**
- **Vintage:** it is the **2025** average (vs 13.3% in 2024) — pre-window.
- **Asset class:** **buyout-dominated — 71% of total LP-led deal value.** Private credit is **6%** of volume.
- **The private-credit read is the *opposite*:** verbatim — *"private credit, which is dominated by direct lending… As the quality funds within these strategies continue to **price at tight discounts**, selling volume remains healthy."*
- **Instrument:** LP fund-interest ≠ BDC loan mark (trades on liquidity/vintage/GP quality).
- **The PC-specific number is 8.6% discount = 91.4¢ — the TIGHTEST of any strategy tracked** (Infra 8.9%, Mega buyout 9.8%, RE 32.5%); CL: *"Private credit achieved the highest average pricing across strategies in 2025."* [PRIMARY: CL FY2025 Secondary Overview]

**(2) Jefferies "credit pricing 91% of NAV" — right instrument, right category, ABOVE threshold, out-of-window.** Trend 91% (end-2024) → 92% (H1-2025) → **91% (FY2025)**; vs overall LP portfolios 87%, VC/growth 78%, RE 70%. Published **2026-02-10**, FY2025 data. [PRIMARY]

**(3) Lazard "~14% of Private Credit GP-led transactions priced ≤90% of NAV" — the most interesting near-miss, still disqualified on three counts.** The tail is **measured, not inferred from a mean** (explicit buckets <80% / 81-90% / 91-95% / 96-100% / >101%; callout: *"~86% of total Private Credit GP-Led transactions were priced above 90%"* → ~14% at/below, of which ~2% <80%, ~12% 81-90%). Disqualifiers: **(a)** FY2025, pre-window; **(b)** wrong instrument — CV/loan portfolios; **(c)** **GP-led CVs are sponsor-organized and the report gives no arms-length-vs-affiliate breakdown** — precisely the distinction the question turns on. *The sibling reading of these as "arms-length prints" was **refuted 0-3** by adversarial verify.*

**(4) BCRED's "bottom 5% of private debt investments at a weighted-average mark of 68.3"** (vs 96.1 debt-portfolio-wide, @ 4/30/26) [PRIMARY: BCRED 6/4 letter, endnotes 4-5]. **This is a sponsor Level 3 fair-value mark, emphatically NOT an arms-length transfer.** It is the single number most likely to be miscited downstream as a sub-90¢ print. It is not one.

> **Structural finding: the secondary-advisor reports CANNOT answer an in-window question.** Lazard/Jefferies/Campbell Lutyens publish **annual** reviews (Feb 2026, covering CY2025). The prompt's "H1-2026 reports" refers to *publication* date, not coverage period. **No Lazard H1-2026 interim exists; no Setter Capital H1-2026 figure surfaced at all.** This is a cadence limit, not a search failure — and it means this leg is *structurally* incapable of confirming a fresh print. `[[finding_deep_research_stale_vintage_headline]]`

## Counter-Evidence

**This is the strongest section against the stress read — carry it.**

1. **Net flows are MILD; the gauge is hot on *demand to exit*, not on *net outflow*.** OCIC: $872M gross inflows vs $988M tender → **net outflow $116M = <1% of NAV.** BCRED: ~2% inflows vs 5% repurchase → ~3% net outflow, which BCRED calls *"consistent with Q1."*
2. **Liquidity buffers are large and leverage is BELOW target.** OCIC $11.3B liquidity ≈ **11× its own tender**; BCRED >$15B. OCIC leverage **0.80× vs a 0.90–1.25× target** — room, not distress.
3. **Sponsors honored NAV in full out of inflows and balance sheet — they are not discounting.** BCRED went *beyond* its cap rather than gate.
4. **Blue Owl attributes the tender wave to sentiment, not credit:** *"Tender activity was elevated across the non-traded BDC industry in the first quarter of 2026, reflecting a period of heightened negative sentiment toward the asset class that has intensified as peers have reported tender results"* [PRIMARY: OCIC 8-K Ex-99.1, 4/2/26]. **~90% of 90,000 shareholders did not tender; ~1% of shareholders represented the majority of tenders** — concentrated and likely advisor-driven, not a broad retail run.
5. **Private credit is the TIGHTEST-priced secondary strategy, not the widest** (8.6% discount vs buyout 9.8%, RE 32.5%) — the opposite of what a capitulation thesis predicts.
6. **MFIC's $61.1M markdown is only ~half credit.** Powell: *"roughly evenly split between market-related factors and credit-related weakness"*; the market half = *"credit spread widening and multiple compression, particularly within the technology sector."* **Reading the full $61.1M as credit deterioration overstates it** — the spread-driven half mechanically reverses if spreads retrace. `[[finding_decouple_idiosyncratic_from_systemic_leg]]`
7. **Against my own $0.85 refutation:** absence of a *reported* multiple is not proof none was discussed. A real 0.85× bid could exist in unreported private negotiation; the numerical coincidence with the May trading ratio is strong but **circumstantial**. Settled only by a merger agreement's exchange ratio or a Q2 disclosure. **The paywalled WSJ original is the one source that could overturn it.**
8. **ADS shows no distress at all:** NAV/share **$23.92 @ 4/30/26, UP from $23.90**; distributions declared and maintained (9.03% annualized, Class I) [PRIMARY: ADS 8-K 5/19/26].

**Supporting the stress read regardless:** MFIC's $61.1M markdown, non-accruals **3.9%→5.3%** (cost basis), halted originations, and a discount widening 0.845× → 0.716× are all real and primary-sourced.

## 6. Corrections to the fleet record (3)

**(a) MFIC non-accruals — the number needs its BASIS label.** Verbatim [PRIMARY: MFIC 10-Q, 5/6/26 — **DEWEY-verified against the filing text**]:

| Basis | 12/31/25 | 3/31/26 |
|---|---|---|
| **Amortized cost** (WSJ's "default rate") | 3.9% | **5.3%** |
| **Fair value** (what management said on the call) | 2.6% | **3.5%** |

**Both WSJ's 3.9%→5.3% and the call's 2.6%→3.5% are CORRECT — the same deterioration on two bases.** WSJ used cost without labeling it. Anyone reconciling call against WSJ concludes one is wrong; neither is. **The fair-value basis flatters — the gap (5.3 vs 3.5) is itself the markdown already taken.** `[[finding_number_carries_threshold_unit_source]]`, `[[finding_ratio_gauge_denominator_branch]]`

**(b) The prompt mis-scopes OTF.** Prompt 16 lists OTF among *"Blue Owl/Blackstone/Apollo **non-traded** vehicles (BCRED, OTF, OCIC, ADS)."* **Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp is NYSE-LISTED** — `TICKERS: ['OTF'] | EXCHANGES: ['NYSE']` [PRIMARY: SEC submissions metadata, CIK 1747777, **DEWEY-verified**]. It runs **no share tender program**; its only N-23C-2 filings (4/30/25, 10/15/25) are *debt* redemption notices. Verified against its full filing history — "filed nothing in the window," not "couldn't locate."

**(c) "$61M markdown" label is CORRECT; several trade-press outlets get it wrong.** MFIC's *"net realized and change in unrealized gains (losses)"* = **$(61.1)M** (vs $(4.0)M in Q1'25 — **15×** deterioration). ## Source Quality Assessment

**Overall: HIGH for the no-fire verdict; MEDIUM for sweep completeness.**

- **Strongest:** the tender/filing record (six vehicles, all [PRIMARY] SEC filings, price-at-NAV unambiguous) and the MFIC arithmetic (NAV from 10-Q XBRL, price from a live pull — **two independent source paths agreeing**, not circular).
- **A no-fire verdict is epistemically easier than a fire** — it requires no single artifact to be right, and four independent evidence classes converge on it. Confidence is high *because* the classes are independent.
- **The negative is bounded by reach, not by evidence quality.** It rests on (i) non-publication of in-window secondary reports, (ii) paywalled trade press, and (iii) an unreadable Moody's site — **not** an exhaustive transaction tape. **A private arms-length sub-90¢ print could exist and be unpublished.** That is the honest ceiling on this verdict.
