## Key Finding
**Helium restrictions have reached at least one named semiconductor factory: Polar confirms one supplier has placed it on allocation. Its October 19 statement does not report a production shutdown.** This is factory-level evidence of restricted supply, with mitigation through three suppliers; it does not establish widespread wafer-production losses. [PRIMARY: Polar, October 19, 2026, S1]

DOE's October 23 designation is verified. It establishes policy concern about helium supply, refining constraints and semiconductor dependence, rather than a measured factory shortage. [PRIMARY: DOE, October 23, S2]

## Counter-Evidence
Polar's sourcing diversification and Linde's July contracted-customer supply record show that an upstream shortage need not produce a factory shutdown. Air Products' June-period volume improvement and Air Liquide's cavern use establish mechanisms for absorption. These mitigations have dates and cannot certify unlimited endurance. [PRIMARY: S1, S4–S7]

No reliable current numerical inventory runway, supplier allocation percentage, matched delivered helium price or helium-attributed lost-wafer volume was obtained for TSMC, Samsung or SK hynix. This search therefore documents **restriction at a named fab**, **documented historical mitigation**, and **unmeasured current industry breadth** separately. It does not certify zero production effects outside the evidence located.
