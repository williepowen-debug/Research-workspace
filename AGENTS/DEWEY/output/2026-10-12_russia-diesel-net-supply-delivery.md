## 1. Instruments and their distinct scope

**US:** OFAC GL135, dated 10/9, authorizes Russian-origin diesel sale/delivery/offloading/import-related transactions otherwise prohibited under 31 CFR587/589, explicitly including US imports, through **12:01 a.m. EDT April7, 2027**. It excludes debits to the specified Russian sovereign accounts held at US financial institutions. There is no tonnage quota, repair commitment or delivery guarantee in the license. It does not cover every possible restriction or redefine all petroleum products as diesel. [PRIMARY: [OFAC one-page license](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937216/download?inline), re-read10/12.]

**US statutory caveat survives:** P.L.117-109 §2 separately bans Russian chapter27 imports; §3 provides a certification/90-day termination route. The text does not by itself settle whether this temporary license is a permitted implementation action under §2 or conflicts with that statute. No completed current-law audit or customs implementation finding is claimed. Do not translate "OFAC licenses imports" into "all US legal clearance is established." [PRIMARY: [govinfo statute](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-117publ109/html/PLAW-117publ109.htm), re-read10/12.]

**Russia:** the reported500,000t exception is an initial authorized quantity, not a monthly recurring quota. Contracting begins in coordination with the government. Signed decree number, eligible-supplier annex and full conditions were not recovered. Thus Y3 is supported by a reported official decision, with instrument-text verification still incomplete. Do not assume the entire producer/non-producer ban has disappeared. [TASS/Reuters above.]

**Europe:** Russian refined-product import restrictions remain a separate EU instrument; the US license does not amend them. Russian exports to other markets can nevertheless displace US exports and ease the Atlantic balance indirectly. [PRIMARY: [Council sanctions explanation](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/sanctions-against-russia-explained/), read10/12; displacement is an inference, not a measured flow.]

## Counter-evidence, causal accounting and pre-registration verdict

**Against a large assured addition:** only an initial exception is established; later quantities are plans, diesel-grade restoration may lag crude processing, strikes can recur, legal/customs and ship evidence remain incomplete.

**Against "no supply can arrive":** the depressed ban-era baseline leaves room for released inventories or resumed exports without restoring every damaged unit; short terminal outages can recover from stocks; the initial exception is real and news-window cracks fell. No confound set points uniformly one way.

**Accounting caveat:** strike losses that already caused the low baseline are not subtracted again from an export increment against that same baseline. Compare exports with/without the deal under a specified strike path. A common-basis November threshold is: if the124.2kb/d promise were wholly incremental, additional loss/displacement of124.2kb/d would erase it. That is a break-even scenario, **not measured loss or incremental delivery**. Net Russian imports, diversion among existing Atlantic buyers and stock depletion must also be netted when identifying extra available supply. No authenticated counterfactual series establishes the sign through April.

**Tests:** Y1 supported; Y2 regulation-level supported with statutory caveat; Y3 reported partial lift, decree-text gap; Y4 mixed/horizon-dependent; Y5 not established; Y6 contemporaneous reaction, causal exclusivity unmet. N1 not met; N2 not met at regulation level; N3 overturned by partial-lift announcement; N4 condition/period ambiguity remains but old export baseline replaced; N5 not quantified; N6 this named ship no longer supports a positive diesel-delivery claim; absence of other deliveries is not established; N7 not met on event-window arithmetic. **Answer: conditional easing channel supported; realized net addition and whether strikes offset/exceed it remain UNKNOWN, not zero.**
