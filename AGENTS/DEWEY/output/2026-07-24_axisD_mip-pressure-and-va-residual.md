## D(i) — Is the buffer politically self-eroding?

### 1. MIP cut history — verified at primary

**🔑 The hypothesis's premise does not hold.** Only **one** of three cuts (2023) occurred at a high ratio. The 2015 cut was made at **0.41% — below the statutory floor** — and the 2017 cut was attempted at **2.32%, essentially at the floor.** MIP policy in the observed record tracks the **administration's housing-affordability posture**, not the capital ratio. The 2017 suspension is the cleanest proof: the *ratio did not change* between Jan 9 and Jan 20 2017; the *administration* did.

Corroborating: HUD's Nov-2017 decision not to reinstate the cut noted that had the Jan-2017 cut taken effect, FY2017's ratio would have fallen 2.09% → **1.76%, below the minimum** — i.e. FHA cut *into* a thin buffer, then declined to cut when thin.

### 4. HUD/FHA leadership on the surplus
No official has proposed distributing it. FY2025 report language is risk-management framed (*"strong oversight of the MMI Fund to protect taxpayers from unnecessary risk"*). Turner's public framing is deregulation-as-affordability, **not** premium-as-affordability — 150+ streamlining actions since Jan 2025, zero premium actions.

### 🔴 NEW — the one thing that DOES change on this axis
**ROAD to Housing Act §702 (now law, 7/11/2026): HUD must report to Congress MONTHLY on the MMI Fund capital ratio, and notify Congress if it falls below the statutory minimum.** *(Verified verbatim in the HFSC official section-by-section.)*

This does **not** cut premiums — it cuts **staleness**. It converts the capital ratio from an annual, ~10-month-lagged actuarial figure into a **monthly congressional disclosure**. Two consequences:
1. **Axis C's "backward-looking stock" critique gets a fix with a date on it** — first monthly report should land in the next few months. Put it on the calendar.
2. It creates a **monthly political surface** on which either a cut campaign *or* a deterioration alarm can be run. The pressure channel the axis hypothesized now has a recurring trigger it previously lacked — even though the cut itself does not exist.

## D(ii) — VA is only partially guaranteed

### 1. Residual-loss mechanics (primary — 38 CFR Part 36)

- **VA guaranty is a stop-loss, not insurance.** Max **25%** of the original loan amount for loans >$144,000 (lesser of $60,000 or 25%); 25–50% tiers apply at smaller balances. Contrast: **FHA insures 100%.** *(AEI/Tobias Peter, House VA Cmte testimony 2025-03-12: VA "features a stop loss of 25% of the loan amount," "unlike the FHA, which insures 100%.")* — the parent's framing is correct.
- **"Net value"** (38 CFR 36.4301) = property FMV minus VA's modeled acquire/manage/dispose costs; the net-value percentage factor is set annually by VA and published in the Federal Register. Net Value Bid = minimum credit to indebtedness for claim computation.
- **🔑 The "no-bid" is the loss-transfer valve.** If VA determines acquiring/disposing would *increase* rather than reduce government loss, it declines to set a bid, **pays only the guaranty up to the cap**, and the servicer/holder forecloses and eats:
  **residual loss = total indebtedness − (guaranteed amount + foreclosure proceeds)**
  This is the exact channel the parent report's "full federal absorption" language does not cover. **Mechanism CONFIRMED at regulation.**
- **Magnitude NOT reachable.** ❌ Could not find current no-bid frequency, VA loss severity vs the 25% cap, or servicer write-down disclosure. Searched: VA circulars, Ginnie issuer 10-Ks, VA VALERI publications, AEI testimony. Only hits were pre-2006 RMBS prospectus boilerplate and a 1996 10-K. **Binding constraint: the residual is real but unquantified. Do not size it.**
- **Practical mitigant:** 25% of *original* balance against a book with substantial embedded HPA means the cap binds mainly on high-LTV recent cohorts with negative equity. The 25% stop-loss is explicitly designed to *align* servicer loss-mit incentives (AEI) — it bites only where equity is gone.

### 3. Since PCP opened 2026-06-15
- Authorized by **VA Home Loan Program Reform Act (H.R.1815), signed 2025-07-30**; opened for submissions **2026-06-15**; **servicers have until 2026-11-28 to implement into their systems.**
- **❌ NO uptake data exists.** Zero submission counts, approval rates, or volume published by VA as of 2026-07-24. Searched VA News, benefits.va.gov servicer FAQs, Federal Register, trade press. **~5.5 weeks elapsed and the implementation runway runs another 4 months — meaningfully zero uptake is the expected state, not a finding.**
- Structural limit (Urban Institute 8/27/25): the statutory partial claim **cannot reduce principal or subsidize modifications**, leaving VA borrowers with *fewer* options than FHA/GSE borrowers. PCP is narrower than VASP was.
- **Watch date: 2026-11-28** (servicer implementation deadline) — first point at which uptake data becomes meaningful.

### 4. Ginnie VA buyout speeds post-VASP
- **⚠️ Partially answered, and the sign is counterintuitive.** VA buyouts **remained elevated** *after* the 5/1/2025 termination (BofA, via NMN 7/1/2025) — but that is **mechanical wind-down, not stress**: VASP *itself was* a buyout-then-sell-to-VA pipeline, and TPPs ran through 8/31/2025 with payments through 9/30/2025.
- **🔑 The regime change is what happens to a bought-out VA loan afterward.** Under VASP the servicer EBO'd and **sold to VA** — off balance sheet. Post-VASP, an EBO'd VA loan **stays with the servicer/issuer**, funded on warehouse/advance lines. *This is a direct feed into Axis A and the parent's "second-order counterparty exposure" line, and it argues the exposure grew rather than shrank.*
- Heaviest VASP users (BofA): **Village, The Money Source, NewRez, Planet Home, CrossCountry** — all nonbank; **flag for Axis A's warehouse-lender question.**
- ❌ Could not obtain post-termination VA buyout CPR/volume series. BofA itself notes *"direct data disclosure on VASP is not available."* MBA has separately proposed an EBO securitization program precisely because this timing mismatch strains issuer liquidity.

## VERDICTS

**D(i): Does MIP-cut pressure materially weaken the "federally absorbed" claim? — NO. Confidence: HIGH (0.85).**
Nothing is proposed, announced, or enacted at any level of government, and the ROAD to Housing Act — the largest housing bill in 30 years, now law — contains zero premium provisions. The hypothesis's historical premise is also wrong: two of three cuts happened at or below the statutory floor, so the ratio does not drive the policy. And the one high-ratio cut (2023, −30bp, ~35% revenue reduction, ~$2.6B/yr forgone) was **absorbed with the ratio ending higher.** *Residual risk kept small, not zero:* trade-group pressure is live and unanswered, and §702 monthly reporting gives it a recurring surface. **Recommend: not a channel, but a NAMED WATCH with a trigger — first §702 monthly report, and any HUD ML touching premium rates.**

**D(ii): Does VA's partial guaranty materially weaken the claim? — YES, but narrowly and not yet quantifiably. Confidence: MEDIUM (0.6) on mechanism, LOW (0.3) on magnitude.**
The 25% stop-loss plus the "no-bid" mechanism is confirmed at regulation: **VA is genuinely not full absorption, and the residual lands on servicer/issuer.** Two real qualifiers: **(a)** VA's DQ is mild (PFSI VA 60+ 1.7% vs FHA 8.0%), so it does not bite today; **(b)** the advocacy figures survive as *counts* but their causal story does not — ~80,000 VA loans were already seriously delinquent seven weeks *before* VASP terminated, so the gap explains ~10,000, not 90,000. The durable finding is not the counts but the **post-VASP balance-sheet regime change**: bought-out VA loans that used to be sold to VA now **stay with the servicer**, funded on warehouse/advance lines at the same nonbanks Axis A is examining. **That points back INTO Axis A rather than standing on its own — and it argues the counterparty exposure grew.**

**Net effect on the parent's KILL:** the KILL **survives axis D**, but "federally absorbed" should be narrowed to **"FHA-absorbed"**, with VA carried as a capped guaranty whose residual is real, currently small, and unquantified.
