# HYPERSCALER DEPRECIATION SCHEDULES — how much EPS came from useful-life extensions, and what reversion does

**Date:** 2026-08-02 | **Mode:** Thesis | **Confidence:** **High** — every figure is a company's own quantified change-in-estimate disclosure, pulled from 10-Ks this session. The reversion sensitivity is **Medium** (see §5).
**Commission:** WALTER DR-2, ledger `REQ-DEWEY-20260731-002` (Will-approved slate 7/30) | **Deadline:** ~8/12 — delivered 8/2
**Consumers:** HENRY (action) · VULCAN / RED (info)

---

## Key Finding

**Across 2020–2025 the four US hyperscalers disclosed 10 server/network useful-life EXTENSIONS worth $26.8B of avoided depreciation and $21.4B of net income — and each single change was worth 1.3%–9.5% of that year's reported diluted EPS, with 3–4% the modal figure.** Oracle disclosed none.

**But the headline finding is that reversion is no longer hypothetical — Amazon has already done it, and said AI is why.** Effective 1 January 2025 Amazon **shortened** the life of a subset of servers and networking equipment **from six years back to five**, stating: *"The shorter useful lives are due to the increased pace of technology development, particularly in the area of artificial intelligence and machine learning."* Realized cost: **+$1.4B depreciation, −$1.0B net income, −$0.10 per diluted share.** Amazon separately took **$920M of accelerated depreciation** in Q4 2024 to **retire servers early**, for the same stated reason.

**And in the very same month, Meta went the other way** — extending most servers and network assets **to 5.5 years**, worth **+$2.92B of avoided depreciation and +$1.00 per diluted share (4.3% of EPS)**, the largest single-year EPS effect in the entire dataset.

**Two of the four largest buyers of AI compute on earth re-assessed the same asset class in the same month and moved in opposite directions.** That divergence — not the aggregate — is the decision-relevant object here.

**⚠️ One more thing HENRY and RED should not miss: Amazon offset its own reversal.** Effective the same date, it extended **heavy equipment from ten to thirteen years, worth +$0.9B of 2025 operating income** — against the −$0.7B guided server hit. **A reader watching only aggregate D&A would have seen approximately nothing happen.** The obsolescence signal is real, and it is invisible at the aggregate line.

## 2. 🔴 The reversion has already started — and it is a divergence, not a trend

**Amazon, effective 1 Jan 2025** [PRIMARY: AMZN FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks]:

> *"We completed our most recent servers and networking equipment useful life study in Q4 2024, and are changing the useful lives of **a subset of our servers and networking equipment, effective January 1, 2025, from six years to five years**… we anticipate a decrease in 2025 operating income of approximately **$0.7 billion**."*
>
> *"In 2024, we also determined, primarily in the fourth quarter, **to retire early certain of our servers and networking equipment**. We recorded approximately **$920 million of accelerated depreciation** and related charges for the quarter ended December 31, 2024… The accelerated depreciation will continue into 2025 and decrease operating income by approximately **$0.6 billion** in 2025. **These two changes above are due to an increased pace of technology development, particularly in the area of artificial intelligence and machine learning.**"*

**Realized, per the FY2025 10-K: +$1.4B depreciation, −$1.0B net income, −$0.10 per basic and diluted share, "which primarily impacted our AWS segment."** ⚠️ **The realized hit exceeded the guide** — Amazon guided ~−$0.7B operating income and the outturn was +$1.4B of depreciation expense.

**Meta, the same month, in the opposite direction** [PRIMARY: META FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks]: most servers and network assets extended **to 5.5 years**, guided at ~−$2.9B depreciation and realized at **−$2.92B depreciation / +$2.59B net income / +$1.00 per diluted share.**

**This is the finding.** The two disclosures are three weeks apart, cover the same asset class, and point opposite ways — one company citing AI as a reason assets wear out *faster*, the other extending lives into the same AI build-out. **Any thesis that treats "hyperscaler useful lives" as a single directional variable is now falsified by the primaries.** What exists is a **dispersion**, and dispersion is testable in a way a trend assertion is not.

**⚠️ And the offset that hides it.** In the same Q4-2024 study Amazon extended **heavy equipment from ten to thirteen years, "an increase in 2025 operating income of approximately $0.9 billion."** Against the −$0.7B guided server hit, **the net guided effect on operating income was roughly +$0.2B.** The AI-obsolescence signal was almost exactly cancelled at the aggregate line by a fulfilment-equipment extension. **You cannot see this in D&A, in operating income, or in EPS — only in the note.**

## 3. What can and cannot be answered: "X% of cumulative EPS"

The commission asked for a falsifiable number for RED: *"X% of cumulative EPS from estimate changes."* **The disclosures do not support a precise cumulative figure, and here is exactly why:**

- **Companies quantify only the year of change.** Each disclosure computes the effect *for that fiscal year*, against the asset base existing at the change date. **The benefit persists in every subsequent year** (the assets keep depreciating more slowly) but is **never re-disclosed**. So the $26.8B is a **lower bound on one year's worth of each change**, not the cumulative benefit — the true cumulative figure is materially larger and is not derivable from the filings.
- **The asset base grew enormously across the window**, so later changes act on much bigger balances. META's 2025 change is **3.4× its 2022 change** ($2.92B vs $860M) for a smaller life extension — that ratio is the growth in the server book, not a change in accounting aggression.
- **No company breaks out server carrying value.** PP&E is disclosed net and by broad category; "servers" is not a reported line. **Without it, an exact reversion sensitivity cannot be computed from public filings** — this is a real disclosure limit, not a research shortfall.

**What RED can have instead, and it is falsifiable:** **per-change EPS contribution runs 1.3%–9.5% of that year's diluted EPS, modal 3–4%, and the one observed reversal cost −1.4%.** That is a defensible band, it is stated per-change rather than cumulatively, and it is checkable against any future disclosure.

## 4. What this does to HENRY's HEN-36

**HEN-36 (FCF compression) is unaffected — and that is the point.** Useful-life changes move **depreciation, operating income and EPS**. They do **not** move cash capex or operating cash flow. **The earnings-quality leg and the FCF leg are independent, and this run shows they can move in opposite directions**: Meta's FY2025 EPS carried +$1.00 of extension benefit **while** its FCF compressed. That is the cleanest available illustration of why HEN-36 should be graded on cash, not on earnings — and it strengthens rather than complicates the existing 4-of-4 resolution.

**The earnings-quality leg HENRY wanted now exists, with a number:** in every year one of these changes landed, **3–4% of reported diluted EPS was an accounting estimate rather than an operating result** — 9.5% in Amazon's 2020 case. It is disclosed, quantified by the company, and it recurs.

**⚠️ A caution HENRY should carry, given the 7/31 calibration mark against over-broad thresholds:** *do not write a threshold on "hyperscaler useful lives extend."* The 2025 evidence is **two-directional**. A rule that fires on extension alone would have fired on Meta and been silent on Amazon's reversal — the more informative event.

## 5. Reversion sensitivity — what the evidence actually supports

**The only honest sensitivity is the observed one, because the disclosure needed to model it does not exist.**

- **Observed, Amazon:** a **one-year** reduction (6→5) on a **subset** of servers and networking = **+$1.4B depreciation, −$0.10 EPS, −1.4% of EPS.**
- **Scaling logic, directional only:** the disclosed *extension* benefits at each company are the mirror of what a full reversion would cost at the same asset base — MSFT's 4→6 was +$3.7B operating income at its FY2022 base; GOOGL's 4→6 was +$3.9B at its 2022 base. **Reverting those today would cost more than the original benefit**, because the server book has grown several-fold since. **I am not putting a number on "several-fold"** — that requires server carrying value, which is not disclosed.
- **Asymmetry worth stating:** extensions were taken in steps of 1–2 years across the whole book; Amazon's reversal was **one year on a subset**. **The reversal was smaller and narrower than any of the extensions.** Whether that reflects genuine asset-level differentiation or a preference for gradualism is not determinable from the filings.

## Counter-Evidence

1. **The extensions are defensible on their stated merits.** Every company gave an operational rationale — Microsoft cited *"investments in software that increased efficiencies"* and *"advances in technology"*; Amazon and Meta cited **longer refresh cycles** and *"continuous improvements in our hardware, software, and data center designs."* Nothing here evidences manipulation; longer-lived assets are a plausible consequence of better infrastructure, and auditors signed each one.
2. **The effects are disclosed, quantified and prominent** — in MD&A *and* in the notes, with per-share figures. **This is not hidden earnings management; it is exactly the disclosure regime working.** Anyone who wanted this number could always have had it.
3. **3–4% of EPS is real but not dominant.** These are not companies whose earnings are mostly accounting.
4. **Amazon's reversal is narrow** — a *subset* of servers, one year, −1.4% of EPS. Reading it as the leading edge of a general reversion is an extrapolation the filing does not make.
5. **Meta's opposite move is the larger and more recent of the two**, and Meta is at least as exposed to AI-driven obsolescence. If AI shortened economic lives generally, Meta's January 2025 assessment should have found it and did not.
6. **VULCAN-07's 0-of-4 finding stands for its own window** — no *new* server/network life change in the 2026 filings, and MSFT's 15→25y change was **buildings/DC shell**, not servers. My findings are historical and do not contradict it. ⚠️ **But a reader carrying "0 of 4" as "no obsolescence signal exists" would be wrong** — Amazon's shortening and its $920M early-retirement charge are both AI-attributed obsolescence data, they simply predate VULCAN's window.

**What would disprove the finding:** a second hyperscaler shortening server lives in the January-2027 filings (VULCAN-08's registered test) would turn Amazon's move from idiosyncratic into a trend. Conversely, Amazon re-extending, or Meta/Google/Microsoft extending again, would confirm the divergence is company-specific asset management rather than a shared obsolescence signal.

## Source Quality Assessment

**As strong as this class of question gets — every number is a company's own audited, quantified disclosure**, retrieved from the 10-K text this session, with the EPS denominators pulled independently from XBRL (`EarningsPerShareDiluted`, 10-K/FY facts) rather than from the same narrative. 27 filings were pulled and searched, so the negatives are enumerated rather than assumed: **the absence of a change in a given year means I read that year's 10-K and it disclosed none.**

**Explicit negatives — what I searched for and did NOT find:**
- **Oracle discloses no server/network useful-life change, and no quantified impact, in any 10-K FY2019–FY2026.** Its PP&E note gives only a blanket *"one to 40 years."* A server-specific life — *"Comprised primarily of servers and networking equipment with estimated useful life of six years"* — **appears for the first time in the FY2026 10-K**, with no change-in-estimate note and no quantified effect. **This is a disclosure-granularity change; I cannot tell from the filings whether the underlying life changed.**
- **No company discloses server carrying value**, so an exact reversion sensitivity is not computable from public filings.
- **No cumulative disclosure exists** — every company quantifies the year of change only.
- **Microsoft's FY2025 and FY2026 10-Ks disclose no new server/network life change**, consistent with VULCAN-07.

⚠️ **One commission premise did not hold cleanly.** The packet said *"companies state it in the year of change."* Mostly true — but **Microsoft's FY2023 note states the change and its effective date in one sentence and the quantification in the next**, and my first pass on a sentence-level grep returned the change without the $3.7B/$0.40. **A sentence-scoped search under-reads these notes; the quantification often sits in the following sentence or in a parallel MD&A block.** Worth knowing before anyone re-runs this.
