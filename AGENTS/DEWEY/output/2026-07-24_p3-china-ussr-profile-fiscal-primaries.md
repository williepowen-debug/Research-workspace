## Counter-Evidence (what argues against the direction-flip)

1. **China is not USSR-like on the axis that actually killed the USSR.** $3.36T reserves + current-account surplus + capital controls + yuan-denominated domestic debt = no balance-of-payments kill-switch. This is the single strongest disanalogy and it cuts directly against the flip.
2. **Growth/TFP still positive (5.0%).** The USSR had negative TFP by the 1970s (P0). China's stagnation *rhymes* (deflation, property bust) but has not reached Soviet-style contraction; a functioning market economy retains adjustment capacity the command economy lacked.
3. **State direction cuts both ways.** State-coupled financing makes AI misallocation a *more direct* quasi-fiscal risk (supports flip) — but the same state control lets Beijing socialize/absorb losses administratively, *suppressing* the market-driven credit-event mechanism a US-style "bust" would need (cuts against a US-analog bust).
4. **Debt level ≠ debt fragility.** 124–130% augmented looks alarming next to a headline, but domestic + repressible is a different risk object than externally-financed. Global Times (state) notes China's debt/GDP is "lower than G20 average" [state source — discount, but the composition point stands].
5. **Official-vs-augmented gap is contested.** The 124% vs ~130% spread and the ¥14.8T (PBoC) vs ~¥71.4T (IMF) LGFV gap show the number is definition-dependent; don't over-anchor on a single figure.
