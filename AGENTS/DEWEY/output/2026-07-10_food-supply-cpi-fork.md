## Evidence

### (1) Fertilizer price triangulation — urea reconciles on a BASIS distinction; spike was April, now ROLLING OVER
**Both "$850 4-yr high" and the retail levels are true on different bases; the $585 does NOT reconcile.** The urea spike peaked in **April 2026** and has since fallen sharply on both bases:
- **International benchmark** (World Bank Pink Sheet, urea E.Europe prill fob ME): **>$850/mt in April** (+80% since Feb, highest since Apr-2022) [INSTITUTIONAL: World Bank blog "Fertilizer prices surge as Strait of Hormuz disruptions tighten", pub 5/14/2026] → collapsed to **$453.1/mt in June** (from $770.5 May, ~−41% MoM); WB: fertilizer prices "plunged 21.8% in June" [PRIMARY: WB Pink Sheet July-2 edition, June data].
- **US-retail** (DTN Progressive Farmer weekly survey): ran $826 (wk 3/23-27, +35% MoM, first >$800 since Nov-2022) → ~$838 → **~$858 April peak** → fell for weeks to $764 (6/8-12) → **$720 (6/22-26, −13% MoM/+10% YoY) → $718 (6/29-7/2)** [PRIMARY: DTN weekly, all prints WebFetch-verbatim].
- **The "$585/t US May-1" figure (CARL's May row) does NOT sit on the DTN retail basis** (DTN implies ~$816-868 mid-May) — likely a **different basis (NOLA/wholesale FOB) or a UAN mislabel**. → **CARL STATUS urea-row correction owed:** the $585 is basis-inconsistent; the correct US-retail May level was ~$820-860, and the current (late-June) US-retail is **~$718 and FALLING**.
- **Companions (DTN retail, late-Jun/Jul):** DAP **$910/ton**, anhydrous ammonia **$1,036/ton** (−7% MoM, well below the Apr-2022 $1,534 record); MAP $953. Benchmark basis (WB Pink Sheet, June): DAP $783.8/mt. *(Pink Sheet has NO ammonia benchmark row — ammonia is US-retail/trade-press only.)*

**Net:** fertilizer SPIKED to a 4-yr high in April on real supply drivers (Hormuz + Russia + QAFCO), then **rolled over hard through June on both bases** — *disinflationary on the fertilizer input channel* as of the latest vintage. ⚠️ **All price prints PRE-DATE the 7/8 Hormuz re-escalation** — the June decline could reverse (caveat below).

### (2) Russia AN/urea export status — real but MANAGED constraint
Two 2026 restrictions [PRIMARY/INSTITUTIONAL: Interfax/Ag-Ministry, CRU, verified 3-0]: (1) a one-month **ammonium-nitrate export SUSPENSION Mar-21→Apr-21 2026** (AN-only, urea not named; to secure spring-planting domestic supply); (2) a **20 Mt fertilizer export QUOTA for Jun-1→Nov-30 2026** (8.7+ Mt nitrogen, 4.2+ Mt AN, 7+ Mt complex). A quota, not a ban — supply constrained but flowing. *(Open: how much of the 8.7 Mt N tranche actually ships vs. late-June drone-strike output damage — unquantified.)*

### (3) Qatar / Iran ammonia — supply DISRUPTED; the NEXUS 6/27 de-rate is REFUTED
**QAFCO (Qatar — world's largest single urea site, 5.6 Mt/yr, ~14% of global supply) has been OFFLINE since March 4 2026** [WSJ, corroborated CRU]; per the WB 5/14 vintage, Qatar suspended urea/ammonia/sulfur and Iran halted ammonia production. **The 7/8 Hormuz RE-ESCALATION means the strait was NOT fully reopened** — Qatari LNG tanker Al Rekayat + Saudi tanker Wedyan struck 7/7; US reimposed Iran oil sanctions + CENTCOM strikes 7/7-8 [Reuters/CENTCOM]. **→ The NEXUS 6/27 de-rate (which assumed post-Hormuz-reopening Qatar/Iran resumption) does NOT hold — treat resumption as UNVERIFIED, and the fertilizer supply-risk rail is RE-ARMED by 7/8, not de-rated.** ⚠️ Tension: supply is still constrained yet retail prices FELL through June — demand/seasonal (post-planting) drove the retrace; the 7/8 shock post-dates it and could re-tighten. *(Post-7/8 QAFCO/Iran operational status = DEWEY-flagged open, needs a terminal/newswire pull.)*

## Counter-Evidence
- **Against a disinflationary read (supply stays hot):** the 7/8 Hormuz re-escalation post-dates every fertilizer print; QAFCO (~14% global urea) offline since Mar-4 + Russia's 20Mt quota mean supply is *still* constrained even as prices fell — the June retrace looks demand/seasonal (post-planting) and is fragile to a re-spike. A strengthening El Niño is a real Q4/winter ag risk. Corn/wheat stocks are tightening (corn −170mbu; "lowest wheat production since 1970/71"). F&V CPI is genuinely elevated (+6.74% YoY).
- **Against a supply-shock read (cost-push fading):** fertilizer is deflating on both bases; crop farm prices flat; CA drought mild (~5.7% D1-D4); the acute produce catastrophes are *pre-window* winter freezes surfacing via FSA designation-lag, not fresh Apr-Jun events; NASS citrus was only −2% US. The elevated F&V print is a lagging base effect that should age out.
- **What would flip the read:** the 7/14 DTN weekly print *rising* (June nitrogen decline reversing on the 7/8 shock); a fresh in-window systemic produce event confirmed in NOAA/NCEI Storm Events; El Niño plume pushing toward the strong-super threshold.
