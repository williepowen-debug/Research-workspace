# What replaced NCUA's request for vendor-examination authority?
**Date:** 2026-10-23 (research 15:01–15:29 ET) | **Mode:** Thesis | **Request:** Will-directed thread 3 (`inbox/2026-10-23_from-WILL_three-research-threads.md`; no WALTER REQ) | **Confidence:** High on what the documents say and when the request disappeared (primaries fetched and text-checked); Low on what the "information sharing agreements" are (no document found)

## Key Finding
No NCUA document describing the "information sharing agreements" could be found, and American Banker reports that NCUA has published none. The only written basis is FSOC's 2025 footnote. The documents show NCUA **dropped its 10-year request** for authority to examine credit-union technology vendors **between March and September 2025**, under a one-member Republican board. Over the same period NCUA's own inspector general (OIG) wrote that the agency "continues to need" that authority. No replacement mechanism is documented.

### (3) The "agreements": what can and cannot be found
- **None named.** A search of ncua.gov returned no information-sharing agreement with vendors or with the OCC, Fed or FDIC. American Banker (2026-09-21): "The agency has published nothing explaining which information-sharing agreements now suffice where they did not before." [NEWS: https://www.americanbanker.com/news/overheated-data-center-knocked-23-credit-unions-offline]
- **What existed before.** NCUA, 2022: "in 2016, the federal banking agencies discontinued the NCUA's participation in SSP program examinations [FFIEC joint exams of large tech vendors] because the NCUA is not statutorily authorized … The NCUA's ability to evaluate risk in credit union vendors by leveraging the banking agencies' authorities is now very limited." [PRIMARY: 2022 white paper]
- **Why the gap matters.** In the November 2023 ransomware attack on a credit-union vendor (OIG-25-07 cites the "Ongoing Operations after-action report"), about 60 credit unions were affected, with $912M in assets and 93,000 members. Bank regulators had no visibility because the vendor serves mainly credit unions [PRIMARY: OIG-25-07].
- **DEWEY inference [UNVERIFIED]:** the most likely candidates are the existing memorandums of understanding with state supervisors and FFIEC/FBIIC working groups described in OIG-25-07. None of these gives access to vendors.

## Counter-Evidence
- **Against "NCUA abandoned oversight":** credit-union trade groups (NAFCU, America's Credit Unions) have long argued NCUA should get vendor exam findings through FFIEC rather than new powers [INSTITUTIONAL, 2024]. A working information-sharing route is therefore plausible. NAFCU also says NCUA has sometimes obtained joint exam reports [INSTITUTIONAL, undated]. Absence of a published agreement is not proof that none exists.
- **Against FSOC's framing:** NCUA's own OIG said in June 2025 that statutory authority is still needed. That is the only 2025–26 primary on the substance, and it contradicts "may have sufficient authority."
- One vendor blog (defensestorm.com, undated) claims Hauptman asked Congress to restore the authority. Every Hauptman testimony checked contradicts it, so it is treated as stale [UNVERIFIED].
