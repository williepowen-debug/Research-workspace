# Capital One modified-card delinquency: Discover cause check

**Date:** 2026-11-11 | **Mode:** Thesis | **Commission:** WQ-672, PROME C9 bounded question | **Verdict:** NO SOURCED CAUSE | **Confidence:** High on disclosed balances; cause UNKNOWN.

## Key finding

Capital One's Q2 and Q3 2026 loan notes, relevant MD&A and issuer Q3 earnings deck do not attribute the modified domestic-card delinquency increase to Discover, a definition/perimeter change or a system conversion. Discover remains a possible explanation, not an established one. The scoped question is answered; the economic cause remains unresolved.

## Counter-evidence and limits

The issuer's [October 20 earnings deck](https://investor.capitalone.com/static-files/36a3bfb0-0fb4-4a31-b153-bedfa4b9cbdd), Domestic Card slide, reports whole-book delinquency of **3.74% versus 3.39% in Q2 and 3.89% a year earlier**, while domestic net charge-offs fell to **3.98% from 4.71% and 4.63%**, respectively. Its allowance slide attributes the release primarily to favorable domestic-card credit performance. These facts argue against extrapolating the small modified group to the entire book, but do not explain that group's movement. Discover integration expenses and general integration-risk disclosures are not a causal bridge.

**Clarification to November 4 wording:** the falling current balance and rising delinquent balance do not prove that the same accounts migrated between buckets. A changing 12-month group also admits entry, exit, repayment and charge-off effects. The earlier statement that current balances fell mainly because balances moved into delinquency was stronger than these aggregate tables establish. Preserve the observed change; withdraw that causal interpretation.

No sourced cause does not mean no integration effect. No legacy split means the candidate cannot be isolated here. No misstatement, hidden delinquency, class-wide deterioration, or trade recommendation follows.
