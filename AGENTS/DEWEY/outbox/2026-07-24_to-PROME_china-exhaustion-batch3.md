## Proposed Batch-3 slate

### P2 — Efficiency asymmetry: is the US overspending *inefficiently* vs a cheaper China? (the corrected SDI mechanism)
**Decision question:** SDI "mattered" (to the tiny extent it did) by threatening to force *inefficient* Soviet spending. The modern test: is the US getting AI capability at a materially *worse* cost-to-capability ratio than China — i.e. is the US the one spending inefficiently? If yes, the exhaustion frame has a live mechanism; if no, it's a normal race.
**In-bounds:** (1) **DeepSeek/cheap-model claim — handle carefully:** P1 flagged the "$5.6M" as *overstated* (notional marginal training cost; excludes R&D, infra, failed runs, the ~$1.6B DeepSeek datacenter capex). Get the **real total-cost-of-ownership** frontier-capability comparison, not the viral number. (2) **Export-control direction:** do US controls force the US to spend MORE (subsidized redundant fabs, stranded capacity) or China more (indigenization, stockpiling, yield loss)? Who bears the inefficiency? (3) **Energy/power cost asymmetry** — China's cheaper electricity + fast grid buildout vs US power-constrained, paying-up data centers; is *power* the real cost-asymmetry axis? (4) **Is US AI capex productive or sterile?** — depreciation/GPU-obsolescence/FCF-cliff; the more returns disappoint, the more it resembles the USSR's *sterile* military spend.
**Out-of-bounds:** the sovereign-transmission question (answered by P1); re-litigating the USSR history (P0); trade construction.
**Owner map:** **VULCAN** (lead — AI-capex/semi cost-to-capability; already owns the memory-cycle + Taiwan) · **WATT** (power-cost asymmetry) · **ZHAO** (China chips/export-control direction) · **HENRY** (AI-capex returns/FCF — HEN-36 adjacency). **DEWEY carve-out:** the primary DeepSeek total-cost figure + US-vs-China electricity-cost primaries.

### P3 — Who actually carries the USSR profile? (the direction-flip test — highest leverage)
**Decision question:** P0 established the USSR's kill-mechanism was **structural stagnation + external-financing/commodity dependence + a revenue shock** — *not* military overspend. Score the US vs China rigorously on *that* mechanism. If China scores higher, the whole thesis direction flips (China is the exhaustion candidate, not the US).
**In-bounds:** (1) **China structural-strain scoreboard** — LGFV/property/demographics/youth-unemployment/deflation/TFP: is China in Soviet-style structural stagnation? (2) **Financing-coupling asymmetry** — China's AI/compute spend is *state-directed* (state banks, LGFVs, national funds), so unlike US private capex it IS quasi-sovereign; does that make China's "AI-misallocation bust" a *direct* fiscal risk the US version isn't? (3) **The reserve-currency / external-financing axis** — the USSR died on a balance-of-payments squeeze; does *either* modern power carry that (US = reserve issuer; China = big reserves + capital controls + surplus, but land-sale-revenue-dependent local govts)? (4) **Each power's "oil-1986" revenue-shock analog** — China: export/property-land-revenue collapse; US: the AI-bubble→tax-revenue channel from P1.
**Out-of-bounds:** trade construction (→ P4); re-deriving the US private-vs-sovereign split (P1 did it).
