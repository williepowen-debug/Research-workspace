## DECISION QUESTION
**Is AI-infrastructure order growth carrying systematic double-ordering — and if so, what does the unwind look like on the historical base rate?**

## WHAT CHANGES ON THE ANSWER
This is the difference between **"AI capex is a durable demand regime"** and **"the AI order book contains a phantom."** Five desks sit downstream: **VULCAN** (capex/semis), **ZHAO** (memory/China), **WATT** (power demand), **HENRY** (concentration/FCF), **VIOLET** (positioning). **A confirmed phantom re-prices the whole AI_INFRA_CAPEX cluster — 67 signals — and a confirmed clean order book removes the fleet's main structural short-side hypothesis on it.** Both outcomes are worth the spend.

## THE NAMED ANALOGUE — use it, do not re-derive it
**`-045`'s own chart annotates "2021-22: a prior over-ordering cycle that unwound in the bust."** ⇒ **The 2021-22 semiconductor cycle is the base-rate case and should be the backbone of the answer**, not a footnote.

## INSTRUMENTS — where to actually look
1. **Memory pricing structure:** DRAM and HBM **contract vs spot** spreads. ⚠️ **Sustained contract-over-spot premia are the classic hoarding tell; the inversion is the unwind tell.** Track the spread, not the level.
2. **Capacity reconciliation:** memory makers' announced capacity additions (Samsung / SK Hynix / Micron / CXMT) against **NVDA's own published phasing — $92B remainder-FY27, $87B FY28, $88B FY29.** ⚠️ **If committed procurement exceeds credible industry capacity for those years, the excess is definitionally not all deliverable.** That is the cleanest single test available.
3. **Turbine backlogs:** GE Vernova / Siemens Energy / Mitsubishi order backlog vs **delivery** schedules and slot dates. Bernstein's 75% is a survey of intent; the backlog is the record.
4. **Cancellation economics:** what do these contracts cost to cancel or reschedule? **A high cancellation penalty makes a backlog real; a cheap option makes it a queue position.** This is the most decision-relevant leg and the least reported.
5. **2021-22 base rate:** how many quarters did the inflated order book persist before it corrected, what fraction of peak backlog was ultimately cancelled or rescheduled, and **what was the FIRST observable that turned?**

# ② `REQ-DEWEY-20260829-002` — HOW MUCH OF NVDA'S GROWTH DOES NVDA'S OWN BALANCE SHEET UNDERWRITE?

## DECISION QUESTION
**What share of NVIDIA's revenue growth is supported by NVIDIA's own capital — guarantees, cloud prepayments, and equity stakes in its customers — and what is the failure mode if a guaranteed counterparty cannot refinance?**

## WHAT IS ALREADY ESTABLISHED (do not re-verify, build on it)
From `SIG-W-20260828-045`, verified at the Q2 FY2027 10-Q:
- **$105B** of guarantees to **SB Energy Corp.** affiliates **on behalf of OpenAI** — ~**4.25 GW** of IT load, PORTS Technology Campus, Pike County, Ohio.
- **$3.5B** further guarantees for select AI cloud partners' data-centre leases on default.
- **$29B** of cloud agreements; plus long-term data-centre leases and equity investments.
- Terms: effective **on lease commencement**, phasing across **nine construction phases**, **first expected FY2029**, **default-triggered**, decreasing over **20-year** lease terms.

## WHAT CHANGES
Vendor financing is not itself a defect — **it becomes one when the vendor's revenue depends on customers who exist because the vendor financed them.** ⇒ **The answer sets whether NVDA's revenue quality should be discounted, and by how much.** VULCAN owns the call; HENRY and VIOLET carry the concentration and positioning consequences.

## THE ANALOGUE, and it is the reason to run this at all
**Lucent and Nortel, 1999-2001** — vendors financing customer purchases to book revenue, which functioned until the customers could not refinance. ⚠️ **Do NOT assume the parallel holds. Test it.** The differences may be decisive: NVDA's guarantees are **contingent and default-triggered** rather than direct customer loans, they begin in **FY2029**, and NVDA's cash generation is of a different order. **A finding that the analogue FAILS is as valuable as one that it holds** — this fleet does not currently hold either.

## INSTRUMENTS
1. Full census of NVDA customer-directed capital: guarantees, prepayments, equity stakes, cloud commitments — **from filings, with the accounting treatment of each named.**
2. **Which customers**, and what fraction of revenue those same names represent. **The overlap between "financed" and "large customer" is the entire question.**
3. What triggers a guarantee call, what NVDA's recourse is, and where the exposure would land on the balance sheet.
4. The Lucent/Nortel structure — **how it was disclosed, and how long between peak vendor financing and the revenue break.**

## SCOPE FENCES
- ⛔ **Do NOT reintroduce the "impossible to pay its bills" framing.** `-045` refuted it on timing AND denominator (**$22.4B is cash-only; liquidity is $56.6B with debt securities, $99.4B with all marketable securities**). **A commission that re-argues it will be rejected at intake.**
- ⛔ **Not a solvency question.** It is a **revenue-quality** question.
