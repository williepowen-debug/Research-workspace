## MARCO-DR-1 — THE CANADIAN-FLORIDA SPENDING BASE
**Consumers:** MARCO action · **REGINALD, CORAL info** (REGINALD consumes the output of this number as a bank/CRE collateral input)

### Decision question
**How much do Canadian visitors actually spend in Florida per year, and what is the defensible current figure with its basis fully stated?**

### Why now — this is a retraction candidate, not an addition
MARCO's single biggest forward claim is a **$600M–$1.2B Canadian-snowbird spending hole in winter 2026-27**, dated in the thesis TIMELINE and routed to REGINALD as a bank/CRE collateral input. Tracing its citation today found four defects, and I have marked it **do-not-re-cite** pending this work:

1. **Scope mismatch.** Derived as a **three-county** figure (Broward / Palm Beach / Lee) but cited fleet-wide as *"the FL $ hole"* and even *"aggregate $ stress."* Either the label or the number is wrong.
2. **No derivation.** The base is `~$6B annually (3.4M visitors × $1,800 avg)` — **no source and no vintage on either factor**, and no stated basis for whether $1,800 is per-trip or per-season. A snowbird staying three months and a same-day land crossing cannot be averaged into one figure. The 10–20% haircut producing the range is **asserted, never computed** from the capacity evidence it cites.
3. **Possibly inconsistent with MARCO's own data.** At −25% (air) to −28.7% (total 2-yr stack) on a $6B statewide base, the implied loss is **~$1.5–1.7B — above the modeled range.** The three-county scope may reconcile that, or may not.
4. **The source surface is shelved** and its own banner says not-maintained, as-of 2026-05-31.

**A well-supported "no defensible per-visitor figure exists" is a WIN here.** I will retract rather than carry an underived number. Please do not manufacture a synthesis to fill the gap.

### Scope — find and verify, each with full basis stated

1. **Canadian visitor volume to Florida**, annual, most recent available. Split three ways where the data allows: **overnight visitors** · **same-day / land-crossing trips** · **seasonal residents** (multi-week/multi-month stays). Likely primaries: Visit Florida research, StatCan (return-trip and travel-spending tables), NTTO, county tourist-development councils.
2. **Average spend per Canadian visitor in Florida — and on what basis**: per trip, per night, per party, or per season. If published sources blend day-trippers and seasonal residents into one average, **say so explicitly** — that is itself the finding I need.
3. **County concentration** — how much Canadian FL spend sits in **Broward, Palm Beach and Lee** versus statewide. This is the specific reconciliation that resolves defect #1.
4. **Canadian-owned Florida residential property** — counts and/or assessed value, ideally Broward / PB / Lee / Sarasota / Charlotte. Non-homestead and seasonal-resident property rolls are the likely route; some county appraisers publish owner mailing-address country. ⚠️ **This leg matters disproportionately: snowbird *owners* spend without any hotel or STR trace, so they are structurally invisible to lodging-tax and TDT data** — which is most of what MARCO currently watches.
5. **Any published estimate of Canadian-boycott dollar impact on Florida specifically**, 2025–2026, **with its own method stated.** If an estimate circulates without a method, report it as unmethoded rather than as a figure.

### Counter-evidence explicitly wanted
Florida's aggregate tourism data currently does **not** show this hole — MIA pax +0.52%, MCO at record, Orlando TDT at a record 14-month streak, FL leisure-hospitality employment positive. MARCO explains that as timing-delay plus metro-masking. **If the evidence says Canadian spend was substituted away (domestic, overseas, Latin American) rather than lost, that is the most valuable thing you can return** — it would falsify a channel that is currently MARCO's highest-conviction one, and it has never been falsification-tested.

### Known trap
**Do not use `3.4M visitors × $1,800` as a starting point or a sanity check.** It is the number under audit; anchoring on it would confirm what I am trying to test.
