## The four attack axes, in priority order

### A. 🔴 HIGHEST VALUE — the KILL was tested on SECTOR AVERAGES, never against the actual names

The bank-exit evidence in the parent is: **~5% of Ginnie originations (Apr 2024, sector-wide)**, **nonbanks service 83–96% of Ginnie (sector-wide)**, and **exactly ONE bank's EBO balance** (Wintrust, $187.8M). That is a **sector** argument used to kill a **firm-level** channel — and a sector average cannot refute an idiosyncratic concentration.

**Two separate errors are possible here and you should test both:**
1. **Origination share is the wrong metric for balance-sheet exposure.** "Banks exited FHA/VA *origination*" does not establish that banks lack FHA/VA exposure. The distinct channels are: **(a)** retained/rebooked EBO loans (ASC 860, Call Report RC-C), **(b)** *servicing* Ginnie loans, **(c)** **warehouse lending to the nonbank servicers**, **(d)** **servicing-advance and MSR-facility lending**, **(e)** FHLB advances funding any of the above. The parent explicitly calls (c)/(d) "second-order counterparty exposure" and then localizes it to Apollo/Atlas SP — **on the basis of REGINALD's April work, not a fresh check.**
2. **The specific names were never examined.** REGINALD's live FL-heavy small-tier watch is **BKU (BankUnited), SSB (SouthState), AMTB (Amerant), SBCF (Seacoast)** — plus the broader regional set in `AGENTS/REGINALD/BANK_EXPOSURE_MATRIX.md`. **None of them appear anywhere in the parent report.**

**Do:** pull **Call Report / FR Y-9C / 10-Q** data for BKU, SSB, AMTB, SBCF (and any FL-heavy regional the matrix names) and answer, per bank, with figures: **GNMA/FHA-VA loans held for investment or rebooked (RC-C); mortgage warehouse lines outstanding and to whom; servicing-advance or MSR-backed facilities; any Ginnie issuer/servicer status.** Then state whether the exposure is material **against that bank's equity** — the parent's own materiality test (Wintrust $187.8M vs multi-billion equity) is the right test, just applied to one arbitrary bank.
**Report the negative explicitly if it's clean** — "these four banks have de minimis FHA/VA and warehouse exposure, here are the numbers" is a genuinely valuable confirmation and would harden the KILL from sector-inference to name-level fact.

### B. 🔴 HIGH VALUE — does the loss-mit waterfall DEFER loss rather than absorb it, and is the record capital ratio flattered by that?

The parent contains one under-explored line: **"partial claims DEFER loss as an MMI receivable rather than realize it"** (HUD ML 2025-06; the new permanent waterfall — partial claim / payment supplement — became **mandatory Oct 1 2025**).

If a material share of FHA loss-mit resolution now converts realized losses into **deferred MMI receivables**, then **"the MMI Fund absorbed it" and "the MMI Fund has not yet recognized it" look identical in the capital ratio** — and a record 11.47% partly built on deferral is a different object from one built on absorption.

**Do:** establish from HUD primaries — **how partial claims are carried in the MMI Fund's accounting** (receivable vs realized loss), **the volume/balance of outstanding partial claims** and its trend, **the assumed recovery rate on those receivables**, and whether the FY2025 actuarial review discusses re-default risk on partial-claim loans. **Name the re-default rate on partial claims if it exists** — that is the number that determines whether the deferral is benign.

### C. 🟠 MEDIUM — the 11.47% is a BACKWARD-LOOKING stock, and it predates the deterioration

**11.47% is as of Sept 30 2025 — roughly ten months stale**, and it is an actuarial figure computed on house-price and delinquency assumptions set before the current deterioration was visible. Meanwhile the parent itself reports **FHA DQ 11.88% in Q1-26, the highest since Q2-21, with SDQ +212bps YoY**, and Ginnie/FHA DQ averaging **9.2%** Oct-25→Feb-26 against Ginnie's own 5.0% DQ3+ limit.

**"Record capital ratio" and "rapidly deteriorating book" can be simultaneously true** — the ratio is a lagging measure of a forward risk.

**Do:** identify the **house-price and default assumptions** in the FY2025 actuarial review, whether realized 2026 experience is tracking inside or outside them, and **when the FY2026 review lands** (that is a dated catalyst worth putting on the calendar). Also stress the Urban Institute claim the parent leans on — *"can absorb a >5× rise in foreclosure rates"* — **what model, what assumptions, and does it hold with the 2022-vintage loss severities?** A modeled cushion is not the same evidentiary class as the primary-sourced mechanics.

### D. 🟠 MEDIUM — the buffer is politically self-eroding, and VA is only PARTIALLY guaranteed

**(i) Premium-cut pressure.** A capital ratio at **5.7× the statutory minimum** is historically the precise condition that generates **MIP-reduction pressure** (there is a repeated pattern of cuts / attempted cuts when the ratio runs far above 2%). **A record buffer is politically the cause of its own erosion.** Establish whether any MIP cut is proposed, pending, or enacted for FY2026-27, and quantify what a cut does to the ratio's trajectory. **This channel is entirely absent from the parent.**

**(ii) VA's guaranty is capped at 25–50%, so the residual above the guaranty flows to servicer/issuer — VA is genuinely NOT full absorption.** The parent correctly notes VA's DQ is mild today (PFSI VA 60+ **1.7%** vs FHA **8.0%**), so it does not bite now. But the **VASP-termination → PCP gap (May 1 2025 → June 15 2026, ~13-14 months)** is exactly the window in which loss was forced to migrate. **Quantify the residual** where you can, and **upgrade or refute the advocacy/press figures the parent flagged as leads-not-banked** (>10,000 veterans lost homes; ~90,000 VA loans seriously past due; ~33,000 in foreclosure) against **VA VALERI primaries**.
