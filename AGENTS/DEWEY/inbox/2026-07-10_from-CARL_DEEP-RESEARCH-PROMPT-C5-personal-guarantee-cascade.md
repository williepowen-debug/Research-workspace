## The question
When a small business defaults, **what percentage of owners subsequently suffer a personal credit event, at what lag, and at what severity** — decomposed by obligation type? No published coefficient exists; build one from primaries.

## Required sub-answers (4)
1. **By obligation type:** SBA 7(a) (personal guarantee universal — SBA purchase/charge-off data is the best-instrumented channel), conventional C&I (PG on ~60-80% of small-facility loans), MCA/fintech (UCC liens + confessions of judgment). Per channel: business-default → personal-event conversion rate, lag distribution, severity (score drop / bankruptcy / judgment).
2. **The cure path:** what fraction of PG calls settle/workout WITHOUT a bureau-visible personal event (the attenuation factor that would make the cascade smaller than mechanical).
3. **Cycle-conditioning:** do conversion rates differ in stress cycles (2008-10, 2020) vs normal — i.e., is today's Sub-V level-high environment (H1-2026 1,663 elections, +50% YoY) one where the base rate under- or over-predicts?
4. **The CARL coefficient:** combine 1-3 into an estimate of personal-credit-event flow per 1,000 SMB defaults, positioned in the payment hierarchy (do owners sacrifice personal credit BEFORE or AFTER business credit — survey/bureau evidence). Explicit handoff format: a number CARL/POP can multiply against Sub-V + SBA default counts.
