## Answer, first: RAISES P(executed hit on Saudi export infra), modestly, not a regime shift.

You asked me to argue both sides and challenge your comfortable read (Iran keeps sparing Gulf oil infra even while shooting at military targets). I did. **Raises wins, but by less than the headline "co-belligerent now" framing suggests** — because the target set was already escalating *before* Saudi Arabia joined the Iraq counter-strike, so this isn't a fresh causal trigger, it's an additive one layered on top of a trend already running.

## Both directions, argued straight

**RAISES (my lead case):**
1. **The actors actually hitting Saudi oil infra are proxies, not Iran directly** — Houthis (Jazan 7/25, Yanbu targeted-and-intercepted 7/25) and Iraqi militias (Abqaiq 7/27). Co-belligerency deterrence logic is a state-to-state calculus; it binds Iran's own restraint (which was already holding on oil infra specifically) far more than it binds proxies who operate on reciprocity/grievance logic, not cost-benefit vis-à-vis a US-Saudi coalition. **Saudi becoming a shooter doesn't obviously deter an actor that wasn't deterred by "victim" status either.**
2. **The Iraq corridor is a new mechanism, not just a new narrative.** Abqaiq's drones launched from Iraqi territory (7/27, Saudi MoD's own attribution) — the first demonstrated non-Yemen launch vector against Saudi oil assets this war. That's closer geographically to Eastern Province infrastructure (Abqaiq, Ras Tanura, the Petroline's eastern terminus) than Yemen is to anything except Yanbu/Jazan on the Red Sea coast. A second geographic front means Saudi air defense (Patriot batteries, radar coverage) now has to cover two separated axes instead of one — that's a real capacity-stretching effect independent of anyone's intent.
3. **Interceptor depletion is now live on two fronts.** You flagged Patriot stocks as a CJCS-named concern already; a second active vector raises the demand rate on a finite, consumable resource. Holding attack rate constant, more fronts = higher probability of an eventual unintercepted hit.
4. **Base rate of this war:** every prior widening of belligerents (Saudi-Houthi truce break 7/25, Iraqi militias entering 7/27) has been followed by an attack on Saudi assets, not a stand-down. The demonstrated pattern is escalation-follows-widening, not deterrence-follows-widening.

**LOWERS (the case I was asked to stress-test, and it's real, just thinner):**
1. **Deterrence/skin-in-the-game** — a target that shoots back raises the political and military cost of hitting it; US co-execution with Saudi in Iraq tightens the implicit US retaliation guarantee over Saudi territory, which should raise the bar for anyone weighing a strike that would trigger it.
2. **Iran's revealed pattern still holds for the state actor specifically** — the Jordan ballistic-missile salvo (7/28) targeted a MILITARY base, all intercepted, and Iran itself has not directly and overtly struck Gulf oil infrastructure this war (every oil-infra hit has come from a proxy with a deniable or bilateral-not-Iran framing: Houthi-Saudi truce break, Iraqi-militia-attributed Abqaiq). Co-belligerency doesn't obviously change a calculus Iran wasn't running against oil infra in the first place.
3. **Legitimized-target logic cuts both ways** — if Saudi is now explicitly a belligerent, Saudi (and the US) also gain explicit standing to expand air-defense integration and interceptor resupply, which could raise interception rates even as raw attack surface grows. I can't size this against point 3 above (interceptor depletion) — they're pulling in opposite directions and I don't have a number for either.

## Why RAISES wins, but only modestly

The informational content of "Saudi is now co-belligerent" is smaller than it looks, because **Saudi assets were already being struck three separate times (Jazan hit, Abqaiq hit, Yanbu targeted) while Saudi was still nominally a bystander.** The frame-breaker was already breaking. What's genuinely new as of 7/27-29 is the **Iraq launch corridor** — that's a mechanism-level change I'd weight far more heavily than the co-belligerency label itself. Strip the "belligerent" framing out entirely and the corridor argument alone still points the same direction (raises), which is why I'm confident in the direction even though the co-belligerency logic proper is closer to a wash.

**Magnitude:** call it a few points of added probability on your D-scenario "second/larger salvo" or R-realized-loss branch over the next 2-3 weeks — not a level-shift, not a regime change.
