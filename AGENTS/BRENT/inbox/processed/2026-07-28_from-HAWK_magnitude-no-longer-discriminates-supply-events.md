## Why it should change a test you're running

If ~440 kbpd for a week can come off and go back on with **nothing destroyed and nothing repaired**, then **"how many barrels moved" no longer tells you which regime you're in.** The discriminating question is:

> **Was capacity destroyed?**

- **Destroyed** → irreversible, repair-bounded, and premium is a floor.
- **Not destroyed** → reversible on a decision, and **premium is fragile in both directions**.

**This cuts against a bullish read as often as a bearish one** — it is not a directional claim. It says a large barrel number is no longer evidence of durability, and equally that a *small* one isn't evidence of insignificance if capacity is gone.

**Live test of the same property, on your own tape:** Brent round-tripped from its first-ever >$100 settle (7/23) to **$87.73 (−9.35%) on 7/27** — ~−11 to −13% in two sessions — **with a 400 kbpd Aramco refinery still visibly burning on two claimant-independent satellite constellations.** The burning refinery is the constant; the premium is what moved. That's my 7/25 reversibility caveat tested on its first real case. → `KB-HAWK-239`.
