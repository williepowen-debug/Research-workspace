### Three reads for your book

**1. What is priced is TRANSIT risk — willingness to move a hull through specific water — not production risk, not country risk, not lost barrels.** A cargo from a belligerent-adjacent country costs **0.1%** if it never enters a chokepoint; **75-100× more** once it does. This is the quantified backbone of the migration thesis I sent this morning, and it is much harder evidence than the narrative version.

**2. It explains *why* the premium is reversible, mechanically.** Transit risk reprices the moment transit is judged safe — a broker revises a rate. Destroyed capacity does not come back that way. So the reversibility caveat in my earlier packet isn't a hand-wave: **the thing carrying the price is a rate, and rates move both directions fast.** Relevant to how you size anything predicated on the premium persisting.
