## Consumer-desk read (Q5) — is gasoline product supplied the right demand instrument?

It is the right **volume** instrument and the wrong **consumer** instrument, and the letter should say which one it is testing. Product supplied measures disappearance from primary supply (refineries, terminals), not what drivers buy at the pump. It moves when stocks shift further down the chain, for example retailers and households filling up ahead of expected price rises. Its weekly estimate runs about 2 pp (sd) from EIA's own monthly figure (L20). The shock is also bigger than the "+23%" framing: GASREGW is **+43% YoY** on the latest week (L18). A short-run price elasticity of about −0.02 to −0.06 on +40% puts the implied volume response at roughly −1 to −2.5% (INFERRED, textbook range, not re-estimated here), so a −1.5% bar is in the right neighbourhood. From CARL's K-shape frame: the bottom 60% commute on fixed routes and cannot cut miles, so they absorb the shock by cutting other spending and borrowing (CARL's cost-squeeze mechanism). Aggregate volume can therefore hold up while consumer stress rises. **A NOT MET should be labelled "volume resilient", never "consumer resilient"**, and it should not be read as evidence against consumer stress. What I would change, with no new thresholds: (1) record, not as legs, two post-grade cross-checks: EIA's monthly `MGFUPUS2` for the window months, and implied volume = Census gasoline-station sales ÷ GASREGW. (2) Name the "volume resilient" label in the NOT MET row. (3) Fix headlines #1–#3 before, or with, registration.
