## 1. Instrument selection — and the shortcut I rejected

**The tempting move, stated so it is not re-attempted:** Ras Tanura and Ju'aymah are **Persian Gulf** terminals, so any Gulf-coast reallocation must exit via **Hormuz**. My own PortWatch `chokepoint6` series shows **10 tanker transits in the whole of w/c-8/3** (all flags, both directions, all cargo). A +1.6–2.2 mb/d reallocation needs ~1 loaded VLCC/day — a large share of every hull observed. That reads as arithmetic refutation of the reallocation branch.

⛔ **REJECTED.** PortWatch is **AIS-derived**, and `[[finding_ais_port_export_darkfleet_blind]]` is explicit: such series run **~90%+ blind** to dark cargo and print literal zero through entire *normal* export months. **The only sound direction is refuting — a NONZERO print proves flow continues; a LOW print proves nothing.** Using a low count to prove absence is the disallowed direction.

**And the confound is live, not hypothetical:** the Yanbu datum under adjudication is itself *"2.38 mb/d, **100% dark**."* In a regime where Saudi cargo is demonstrably going dark, assuming Gulf-coast hulls stay AIS-visible is unwarranted.

⇒ **The discriminator had to come from a dark-fleet-capable instrument.** It did: **EU Sentinel-2 satellite imagery**, which is indifferent to transponder state.

## 4. ★ A THIRD MECHANISM — not on FALCON's or PROME's list

Both framed this as constraint **vs** reallocation. The sequence fits a third reading better, and it makes the other two **sequential rather than competing**:

**AN OFFTAKE CONSTRAINT AT YANBU.**

1. Houthi maritime embargo declared **7/20**; Yanbu already running **"at maximum, no room"** (7/14).
2. Charterers/owners grow unwilling to load in daylight → **loadings go dark from 7/23**, then fall.
