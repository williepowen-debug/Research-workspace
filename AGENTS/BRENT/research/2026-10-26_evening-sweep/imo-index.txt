The ongoing instability in the Middle East creates a rapidly evolving situation for global shipping. IMO is closely monitoring developments to protect thousands of seafarers in the region, including those stranded on vessels unable to exit the Strait of Hormuz. This page provides data updates, official statements, regional contacts, incident list and guidance from IMO and partner organizations. Media queries should be sent to media@imo.org Incidents data Data on attacks to commercial ships in the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, and Gulf of Oman regions are compiled by IMO following a rigorous validation process including Flag States, coastal States, and credible, verified third-party sources. Full list of confirmed incidents (updated 22 October) Latest IMO updates Stop attacking merchant ships and seafarers: IMO Secretary-General to Member States (16 September) Six months of uncertainty for seafarers in Strait of Hormuz (28 August) More IMO news on Strait of Hormuz Seafarers IMO is deeply concerned about the wellbeing and safety of seafarers affected by the ongoing situation in the Strait of Hormuz. Thousands of seafarers, as well as port workers and offshore crews, are impacted in the region. While the disruption to global trade is significant, IMO’s primary concern remains the humanitarian and safety implications for seafarers on board ships operating in the area. Regional contact points here Support for seafarers and stranded ships here Testimonies from seafarers affected by conflicts here IMO safe evacuation framework Following the IMO Council's call ( C.ES.2, 18-19 March ) for the establishment of a safe maritime framework as a provisional and urgent measure to facilitate the safe evacuation of merchant ships confined within the Gulf region, IMO developed a voluntary evacuation mechanism in line with international law. This evacuation plan is currently paused . For further details, see the Operational FAQs – IMO Strait of Hormuz Evacuation Plan . Strait of Hormuz  - shipping route The existing Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS) in the Strait of Hormuz was proposed by Iran and Oman and adopted by IMO in 1968. It designates shipping lanes for maritime traffic in order to reduce collisions and  improve safety.
