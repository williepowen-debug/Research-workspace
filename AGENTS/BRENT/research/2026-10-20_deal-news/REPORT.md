## Judgment and what would change it

The new verified policy developments add substance to the relief branch already present in v5.13. Today's diplomatic story adds a credible-to-markets possibility, still anonymous and unsigned. The productive question is how much of the expected disruption premium survives partial normalization, not whether prices must wait for physical barrels.

Three separate questions remain: official assent and actual terms; implementation of safe commercial access/insurance/blockade changes; delivered additional crude/products net of overlapping releases and rerouting. They are assessment inputs, not new registered triggers or a requirement to wait for delivery before responding to a signed event under an existing spec.

For crude, normalization expectations can hurt outright prices immediately. For refiners, diesel scarcity can persist after crude falls, but accelerated diesel releases can directly compress that advantage. Today's provisional matched product/crude bars are not a held-VLO settlement grade. A view that shortages persist for months gives no guarantee for an option expiring in days. No automatic rebound, trade or roll recommendation.
