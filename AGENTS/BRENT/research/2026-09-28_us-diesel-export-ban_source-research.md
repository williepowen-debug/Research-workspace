**Other options that have been floated (reporting, mostly without named sources):**
- Suspend the federal diesel excise tax (~24¢/gal). Industry is pushing this. It needs Congress, which is in recess.
- Expand legal road use of red (off-road) diesel. LA, AL and NE are already doing this at state level. Exempting red diesel from the Renewable Fuel Standard is put at ~30¢/gal, an industry estimate.
- Adjust the Renewable Fuel Standard more broadly.
- State tax holidays.
- **Jones Act waiver**, already in place since March and extended to **Nov 15** ([Daily Signal 9/23](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/09/23/a-diesel-export-ban-wont-solve-our-fuel-crisis-diesel/) [SNIP]).
- Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve (NEHHOR): ~1 mb, less than one day of exports (HFIR 9/25 [PAGE]).

## 2. Legal mechanism
| Route | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EPCA §103 (42 USC 6212) | **Repealed** by Consolidated Appropriations Act 2016 (P.L. 114-113, Div. O §101(a)). The old §103 authority to restrict *products* by rule went with it | [42 USC 6212a, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/6212a) [PAGE]; [Chaos&Order 9/18](https://chaosandorderinsight.substack.com/p/what-washington-can-do-about-diesel) [PAGE]. That the old §103(a) covered products is [MINE] |
| 42 USC 6212a(b) | Bars restrictions on **crude oil** exports only. It says nothing about products. The (d) "safety valve" (licensing for up to 1 year, renewable, after a national emergency or a Commerce+DOE finding) is written around crude | LII [PAGE] |
| 6212a(c) savings clause | Preserves presidential authority under IEEPA, NEA, EPCA Part B and TWEA "to prohibit exports" | LII [PAGE] |
| **IEEPA + national emergency declaration** | The commonly cited route. Georgetown's Peter Harrell: "a very straightforward thing to do". *Learning Resources v. Trump* (SCOTUS, 2/20/2026, 6-3) held IEEPA does not authorize tariffs; commentators say its **prohibition** power is left intact, but it faces litigation risk | Semafor [PAGE]; [FreightWaves 9/25](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/could-washington-ban-diesel-exports-what-truckers-need-to-know) [PAGE] |
| DPA | Mentioned as "more complex" | Semafor [PAGE] |
| EAR/BIS short-supply controls (Part 754) / ECRA | **Not verified this session.** [MINE]: ECRA exempts export-control rules from APA notice-and-comment, so BIS could impose licensing by interim final rule within days of an executive order. Whether ECRA's authority reaches short-supply controls for fuel is unclear to me. **Needs a CRS or counsel check** | CRS R46814 exists ([link](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R46814)) [SNIP only] |

**Speed (my assessment):** an IEEPA emergency declaration plus an executive order can be signed same-day, with no rulemaking. Implementation would go through BIS licensing or Customs holds. A voluntary program needs no legal authority at all. **I found no CRS product specific to diesel in 2026.**

## 3. Precedent (2022)
- **6/2022:** the White House weighed fuel-export limits short of a ban. Granholm backed off at a June meeting with refiners (Reuters via [NGI](https://www.naturalgasintel.com/news/granholm-wants-refiners-to-export-less-fuel-amid-domestic-infrastructure-constraints/) [SNIP]).
- **8/18/2022:** Granholm wrote to 7 refiners (including Valero, Exxon and Chevron) urging them not to increase exports and to rebuild inventories, or the administration "will need to consider additional federal requirements or other emergency measures". The Northeast gasoline and heating-oil reserves (2 mb) were put on "active standby" [SNIP, Reuters via Investing/EnergyNow].
- **10/2022:** officials floated export limits and minimum-inventory requirements at a meeting with Exxon, MPC, PSX and Shell. AFPM and API wrote to Granholm on 10/4/2022 ([API pdf](https://www.api.org/~/media/files/misc/afpm-granholm-letter-on-refined-product-exports) [SNIP]).
- **Outcome:** **nothing was imposed.** "Examined a diesel export ban but never carried it out" [SNIP, EnergyInDepth/AEA 2026]. **I did not verify whether refiners changed export behaviour in 2022.** The 2022 DPA use was limited to information requests [SNIP].
- **Earlier history** [MINE]: short-supply licensing of petroleum products in the 1970s under the EAA and EPCA §103. This 2026 episode would be the first US energy export restriction since 2015 (Investing 9/23 [PAGE]).

## Bottom line (ASSESSMENT)
- The formal policy is still unset. Trump's on-record words (9/22, 9/27) lean toward a ban. Wright's on-record words (9/23, ~9/25, 9/28) and the Cruz-relayed White House assurance (9/26, source unnamed) lean toward voluntary curbs or tweaks.
- An IEEPA-based ban could legally be imposed within days, with no rulemaking.
- **The single most decision-relevant unknown: whether any refiner has agreed to voluntary limits.** Nobody is on the record either way.
