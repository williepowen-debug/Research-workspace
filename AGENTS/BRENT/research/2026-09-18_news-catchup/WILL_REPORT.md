## The story in five sentences
Oil is holding just above $100 a week after Saudi Arabia's Hormuz-bypass pipeline was knocked out, and the market is now trading the repair rather than the outage. Saudi says it will get half the line back "within days" and all of it in about six weeks, and it is selling the barrels it can't ship from the Red Sea out of the Gulf instead, by shuttling them through the Strait of Hormuz to waiting tankers off Oman. That relief is why Brent has fallen from ~$108 on Monday to ~$104 today, but it is a plan plus a workaround, not restored flow. Meanwhile the fighting has widened: Saudi Arabia and the Houthis are now striking each other directly, a tanker was hit in Hormuz this morning, and Ukraine hit another large Russian refinery. The physical market is much tighter than the futures screen shows: prompt cargoes of North Sea crude are trading above $130 and diesel above $220 a barrel.

## What is actually known vs. claimed
- **Petroline (Saudi East-West pipeline, shut since 9/11):** two pumping stations damaged by drones from Iraq. The Saudi ministry gives no restart date. "Half back within days, full in six weeks" is an Aramco objective reported by Bloomberg, not a statement that pumping has resumed. Nobody has reported oil moving in the line yet.
- **Yanbu (the Red Sea export port):** no crude has loaded since 9/11 per Kpler, a single tracker. About 3–5 days of stock remains in tank. Net Saudi barrels actually lost to the world are still unknown, because Gulf-side loadings roughly doubled to ~4 mb/d (Reuters, unnamed sources) and ~60 million barrels were sold for September/October pickup off Oman.
- **The Oman workaround is not a bypass.** The crude still loads in the Gulf and rides through Hormuz on shuttle tankers, then transfers ship-to-ship off Sohar. It puts more, not fewer, Saudi hulls through the strait that Iran is contesting.
- **Hormuz this morning:** Iran's Guard says it struck and detained a Togo-flagged tanker, the Trend, for "illegal passage." The UK maritime agency reports a fire, crew safe, no spill. Whether the ship was loaded is unknown, so this does not meet our frame-breaker test, which requires confirmed loss of cargo or of Gulf export throughput.
- **Saudi–Houthi:** direct exchanges of fire 9/17, first Saudi-announced death from a drone intercepted over Taif, about 150 km from Yanbu. No ship has been attacked in the Red Sea since 8/24 and traffic there is recovering. So this is a risk to the value of any Yanbu restart, not a current loss.
- **Russia:** Yaroslavl refinery (~300 kb/d) hit 9/17. That is the third large refinery this week. It supports the diesel-shortage story, not a crude-shortage story.
- **Physical tightness:** Dated Brent above $130 and diesel above $220 are vendor assessments (Saxo, Rigzone, Kpler), not exchange settles, but they are consistent across sources. US retail diesel hit a record $6.27/gal this week.
- **War-risk insurance (Lloyd's Joint War Committee):** new circular 9/16 changes only the Black Sea. The Gulf, Gulf of Oman, southern Red Sea, Saudi Arabia and Yemen all stay on the listed-areas map. Our "corridor is not normalised" test is unchanged.
