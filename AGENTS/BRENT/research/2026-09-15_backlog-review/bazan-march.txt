Rating Action Overview
•   On March 20, 2026, Oil Refineries Ltd. ("Bazan" or the "Company") reported that as a result of an
    Iranian missile attack, localized damage occurred to the infrastructure supplying electricity to a utility
    facility and in an open area near an administrative building, at the Company's compound in Haifa
    Bay. Also damaged was external infrastructure owned by a third party located outside the
    compound, which is essential to the Company's operations. According to company reports, most of
    the production facilities are operating and the rest are in the process of resuming operation in the
    immediate term.

• Bazan continues to implement the rehabilitation plan for its facilities damaged in the Iranian missile
    attack in June 2025. As of the date of approval of its financial statements, the Company has
    completed the operation of steam facilities that enable full ongoing operation of its facilities with
    sufficient redundancy, and is working to achieve full redundancy. Due to the damage to the
    electricity turbines, the Company is currently relying on purchasing electricity.

• Despite the lower EBITDA in 2025, Bazan maintained a leverage ratio commensurate with the
    rating, due in part to substantial amounts it received from its insurance company for lost profits and
    advances it received from the Compensation Fund to cover costs for the damage caused.

• Due to the intensification of security risks, including the additional localized physical damage to
    Bazan's assets, in light of the Company's strategic importance to the local economy and given the
    geographic concentration of its operations, its facilities remain at risk and a deviation from our base
    scenario is possible.

• Accordingly, on March 25, 2026, we affirmed our ‘iilA+’ issuer rating on Oil Refineries Ltd. and our
    ‘ilA+’ issue rating on its unsecured bonds.

• The negative outlook reflects our concerns regarding Bazan's ability to consistently maintain
    operating and financial performance commensurate with the rating, given the additional damage to
    the Company’s facilities and the increased risk due to the war with Iran and the conflict in Lebanon.


Rating Action Rationale
In the missile attack on northern Israel on March 19, 2026, there were localized hits at the
Company's compound in Haifa Bay, but Bazan estimates that the damage is negligible and that
the expected loss of profits is not material. The Company reported damage to the electrical
infrastructure that supplies a utility facility, as well as damage to an open area near an administrative
building. Also damaged was external infrastructure owned by a third party located outside the

compound, which is essential to the Company's operations. This is the second impact on the
Company's facilities, following more significant damange sustained in the Iranian missile attack in
June 2025, after which the facilities were shut down. We understand that most of the production
facilities were already operating after the current impact, and Bazan estimates that the remaining
facilities will gradually resume operations, until full completion and a return to optimal production
outputs, in the coming days. The Company reported that the damaged external infrastructure has
been restored to operation. According to the Company's preliminary assessment, the direct damage
caused to its facilities is negligible, and it has begun procedures with the Property Tax and
Compensation Fund. The Company estimates that fixing the damages would only take a few days,
and accordingly, the loss of profits would not be significant. In addition, the Company has an
insurance policy that provides some coverage against loss of profits resulting from acts of terrorism
and war. The scope of coverage is $400 million above initial damage of $50 million, for an indemnity
period of up to 30 months, subject to a 30-day deductible. In the event of additional physical damage
or if the rehabilitation period is prolonged and the date of return to full operation is significantly
delayed, we believe that receiving property tax payments for physical damage and compensation for
loss of profits will mitigate the impact on financial results. In such a scenario, if the costs of damage or
production are higher than the Company’s estimates and compensation and insurance payments are
insufficient, the likelihood that the Company will deviate from the characteristics commensurate with
the rating will increase, as well as the negative pressure on the rating.

Bazan continues to implement the rehabilitation plan for its facilities damaged in the Iranian
missile attack in June 2025. As a result of the attack, the energy center responsible for a significant
portion of steam and electricity production used by the Company's facilities was directly damaged. In
order to reinstate steam production, which is crucial for the distilling process, as of the date of
approval of its financial statements the Company has completed the operation of steam facilities that
enable full ongoing operation with sufficient redundancy, and is working to achieve full redundancy.
Due to the damage to the electricity turbines, the Company is currently relying on purchasing
electricity, which increases its production costs and adversely affects its profitability.

Despite the damage to its facilities, Bazan maintained a coverage ratio commensurate with the
rating, thanks in part to significant insurance receipts and advances from the Compensation
Fund. In 2025, the Company posted a decrease in adjusted EBITDA, but it was higher than our
expectations, despite only partial recognition of the collection amounts for lost profits, and amounted
to about $365 million compared to about $380 million in 2024. The decrease in EBITDA was mainly
due to the facilities’ shutdown due to the missile strike, periodic maintenance and lower polymer
prices. However, the Company recognized revenue of about $160 million in respect of an insurance

policy claim for loss of profits resulting from acts of terrorism and war (the Company claimed $218
million), of which it has received about $155 million to date. In addition, the Company received an
advance of about $48 million from the Compensation fund to cover rehabilitation costs, and is working
to receive additional advances in accordance with its expenses. As a result, and despite investments
in renovation and the acquisition of about 52% of Management Cantium LLC and the corporations it
controls ("Cantium"), Bazan posted only moderate growth in adjusted debt to about $1.4 billion in 2025
from about $1.3 billion in 2024, and its gross debt-to-EBITDA leverage ratio was about 3.8x, at the
upper end of the range commensurate with the rating.

We believe that despite the additional damage to facilities, Bazan will post growth in adjusted
EBITDA in 2026 and its leverage ratio will remain within the range commensurate with the
rating, but there may be a deviation from our base case scenario due to increased security
risks. In our base case scenario, we estimate that the Company will post growth in adjusted EBITDA
to $400 million - $420 million, mainly in light of higher refining margins and in accordance with the
hedging the Company has implemented to date and our expectations for dividend receipts from
Cantium. Also, based on the Company's business strategy and financial policy, we do not expect a
material change in debt levels, so that the adjusted gross debt-to-EBITDA ratio will remain in the
3.0x-4.0x range.

The war with Iran disrupted oil supplies and led to a sharp increase in oil prices and refining margins.
We believe that in the short term, the higher refining margins contribute to Bazan’s and Cantium’s
profitability. If oil prices remain high in 2027, the Company's working capital needs may increase to a
certain extent, and cash flows may come under pressure, which may lead to a deviation from our base
case scenario. In accordance with S&P Global Ratings' assumptions, in our base case scenario we
assume a gradual decline in oil prices and refining margins later this year, resulting in limited impact
on the Company's 2026 results.

We believe that Bazan constitutes essential national infrastructure for the Israeli economy,
which supports our business risk assessment. In 2025, the Company's market share of diesel
consumption in the domestic market was about 67% and its market share of gasoline consumption
was about 59%, in addition to the production of jet fuel, which is especially essential in times of war,
and the increased supply of diesel for electricity producers due to the shutdown of some gas rigs. The
temporary exemption the Company received from obtaining a permit under the Planning and
Construction Law for the construction of buildings, facilities and steam boilers required to replace
buildings and facilities damaged by missiles, reinforces our assessment of the importance of the
Company's operations to the economy. Therefore, and in the absence of infrastructure required to

Outlook
The negative outlook reflects our concerns regarding Bazan's ability to consistently maintain operating
and financial performance commensurate with the rating, given the additional damage to the
Company’s facilities and the increased risk due to the war with Iran and the conflict in Lebanon.

We acknowledge that, with the onset of another round of fighting with Iran, security and geopolitical
risks may increase, which may affect macroeconomic indicators in Israel and increase capital market
volatility. At this point, uncertainty remains regarding the scope, duration and effects of the war. As the
situation evolves, we will update our assumptions and estimates accordingly.

Downside Scenario
We may lower the rating by one notch or more if Bazan’s operational continuity is jeopardized and we
estimate that its business risk profile has deteriorated. We may also consider a downgrade if the
Company posts adjusted gross debt to EBITDA above 4x over time, or in the event of a deterioration
in its liquidity position.

Upside Scenario
We may consider revising the outlook to stable if we assess that security risks have materially and
consistently subsided, and that the Company continues to maintain stable business operations. We
believe that gross debt to EBITDA of 3x-4x is commensurate with the rating given the business cycle.
Revising the outlook to stable also depends on the implementation of Bazan’s financial policy, which
includes maintaining a balance between investments and dividends, and its ability to handle any
changes that may arise due to volatile market conditions.


Company Description
Bazan operates in the refining and petrochemical markets, mainly through a single manufacturing site
in the Haifa Bay. It uses a combined manufacturing process such that some of the refining products
are used in petrochemical production and some petrochemical products are used in the refinery.
Refining activity generates about 90% of the Company’s revenue, and petrochemical activity

generates the remainder. In addition, the Company holds about 52% in Cantium (a nonconsolidated
affiliated company), which operates shallow water oil and gas assets in the Gulf of Mexico.

The main shareholder in the Company is Israel Petrochemical Enterprises Ltd., which holds about
24.7% of its shares. About 7.2% of the shares are held by Mr. Jeremy Blank, and the remaining
shares are held by the public.


Base Case Scenario
Our base case scenario is underpinned by the following assumptions:
•   In 2026: about 5% GDP growth in Israel and inflation rate of about 2.2%. In 2027: about 3.8%
    growth and inflation rate of about 2%.
•   Refining spread of about $9-$10 per barrel in 2026-2027.
•   A margin of about $680-$700 per ton of polyethylene in 2026-2027.
•   A margin of about $640-$660 per ton of polypropylene in 2026-2027.
•   Utilization rate of about 86%-90% in the refining facilities and about 80%-90% in the petrochemical
    facilities in 2026-2027, taking into account planned periodic maintenance.
•   Annual investments as part of the restoration plan, as well as planned maintenance activities,
    totaling about $300 million - $320 million in 2026-2027.
•   Dividend receipts from Cantium totaling about $30 million - $35 million in 2026-2027.
•   Dividend distribution according to company policy.

Under our base case scenario, the expected adjusted ratios in 2026-2027 are as follows:
•   Adjusted gross debt to EBITDA of about 3.0x-4.0x
•   Adjusted EBITDA interest coverage of about 4.1x-4.7x
•   Adjusted EBITDA margin of 7%-9%


Liquidity
According to our criteria, the Company’s liquidity is “adequate”. We estimate that the ratio between the
Company's sources and its uses will exceed 1.2x in the 12 months starting January 1, 2026,
considering refining margins in line with our base case scenario and the large cash balance at
year-end 2025. Over the years Bazan has maintained a good relationship with the banking system
and benefits from access to the capital market, which contribute to our assessment of its financial
flexibility. On the other hand, our assessment of the liquidity profile is constrained at this stage by the
exposure to business cycle and refining spread volatility.

Covenant Analysis
The Company has several financial covenants, including minimum equity, minimum cash and cash
equivalents and maximum debt to EBITDA, as per specific definitions in the Company’s financing
agreements and bond debentures. The Company is also required to maintain stand-alone cash and
unused committed credit facilities, as defined in the financing agreements, of at least $75 million. We
understand the Company meets all its financial covenants.

Environmental, Social, And Governance
Environmental factors are a negative consideration in our credit rating analysis of Bazan. As part of
the refining industry, Bazan is subject to the risks inherent in the growing decarbonization of the
economy and expected decreasing demand for oil over time. It is experiencing growing scrutiny of its
activities alongside increasing legal claims of environmental damage. Its location in the Haifa Bay
increases the negative sentiment towards its operations.

Simulated Default Assumptions
•   Simulated year of default: 2030
•   A deep recession in the Israeli economy accompanied by a sharp decline in refining margins will
    lead to lower cash flows and jeopardize the Company's ability to service its debt.
•   The Company will continue operating as a going concern, an assessment supported by its
    strategic importance as the supplier of about 60% of local liquid fuel demand.
•   In the hypothetical default scenario, the Company will utilize credit facilities, including factoring
    facilities.
