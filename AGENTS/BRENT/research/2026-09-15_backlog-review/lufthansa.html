Dear Shareholders,

In the first half of 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s business performance was shaped by a challenging market environment due to the crisis in the Middle East. While the higher kerosene price and temporary route cancellations adversely affected the Group’s operating and financial performance, strong demand on its Asian and African routes had a positive impact. 

Overall, our Network and Point-to-Point Airlines reliably carried more than 60 million passengers to their destinations, thanks to higher load factors. However, the earnings trend was negative in both of these business segments in the first half of the year due to the effects of the Iran war, as well as strikes.

The Logistics and MRO business segments continued to develop successfully in the first half of 2026 and once again achieved positive earnings figures that were higher than in the previous year. Lufthansa Cargo in particular benefited from changed freight routes and logistics flows as a result of geopolitical upheavals.

Adjusted EBIT for the Lufthansa Group came to EUR -229m in the first half of 2026, which represents a EUR 378m decrease year-on-year. However, in the second quarter a positive Adjusted EBIT of EUR 383m was achieved. For the year as a whole, the Lufthansa Group expects an Adjusted EBIT of between EUR 1.7 and 2.2bn. The further course of the Middle East conflict and its effects on kerosene prices in particular are key factors shaping the outlook.

Consistent implementation of our strategy will enable us to overcome the volatility facing our industry and the impacts of geopolitical crises. In response to the strong rise in kerosene prices, we have decided to move forward with a number of measures to streamline our operational processes more rapidly than originally planned. These include the removal of Lufthansa CityLine’s flight operations from the service offering, the associated decommissioning of the entire Canadair CR-9 sub-fleet of 23 aircraft as well as the early retirement of fuel-intensive long-haul aircraft such as the Airbus A340-600 and the temporary grounding of two Boeing 747-400s from the beginning of the winter flight schedule. This will reduce fuel consumption as well as the impact on earnings of the non-hedged portion of the Group’s fuel. We are also reducing the complexity of our fleet and thus improving our competitiveness. 

Moreover, the continuing implementation of Lufthansa Airlines’ turnaround programme is having a substantial positive impact on earnings and operational stability. Our restructuring of the Lufthansa Group is also proceeding according to schedule. Lufthansa City Airlines’ fleet is continuously growing, its fleet modernisation and cabin refit measures are progressing and the updated Future Onboard Experience (FOX) on long-haul flights has been successfully introduced across all travel classes at Lufthansa Airlines. We are thus improving the first-class premium experience for our passengers and setting new standards in the area of customer satisfaction.

Our integration of ITA Airways is also rapidly progressing. In June, we exercised our option to acquire a majority stake in the Italian airline as planned. Our equity investment will thus increase from 41 to 90%. Following the completion of the transaction at the start of next year, ITA Airways will also be fully integrated within the Lufthansa Group in organisational terms and financially consolidated.

Together, we intend to take the Lufthansa Group forward into a successful future as an integrated airline group with our four central business segments Network Airlines, Point-to-Point Airlines, Logistics and MRO. For the benefit of our shareholders, customers, passengers and employees.

We are pleased that you are accompanying us on our journey.

 

Marc-Dominic Nettesheim

Head of Investor Relations

Financial Development January - June 2026

Business Development

Lufthansa Group’s earnings below previous year’s level

In the first half of 2026, the business performance of the Lufthansa Group’s passenger business was substantially impacted by the effects of the crisis in the Middle East. While the higher kerosene price and the temporary route cancellations adversely affected operating and financial performance, strong demand on its Asian and African routes had a positive impact on traffic. Various strikes staged by the Vereinigung Cockpit pilots’ union (VC) and the Unabhängige Flugbegleiter Organisation union for cabin crew (UFO) also had a negative impact.

Capacity in the Lufthansa Group’s passenger business declined by 2% year-on-year in the first half of 2026. On the other hand, the passenger load factor went up by 1.9 percentage points to 82.4%.

The Network Airlines’ Adjusted EBIT decreased to EUR -468m in the first half of 2026 (previous year: EUR -113m). In the Point-to-Point Airlines business segment, Adjusted EBIT fell to EUR -252m (previous year: EUR -137m).

In the Logistics business segment, the positive operating and financial trend continued in the first half of 2026. As a result, Lufthansa Cargo expanded its volume of traffic and achieved an Adjusted EBIT of EUR 199m, which was 47% higher than in the previous year (previous year: EUR 135m).

In the MRO business segment, a continued high level of demand for flights strengthened demand for maintenance and repair services. The Middle East conflict, the weaker US dollar, inflation- and growth-related cost increases and punitive US tariffs had a negative effect. In the first half of 2026, Adjusted EBIT at Lufthansa Technik rose by 2% to EUR 315m (previous year: EUR 310m).

The Lufthansa Group’s revenue increased by 8% year-on-year to EUR 19,887m (previous year: EUR 18,449m) due to higher passenger traffic yields as well as strong growth in its Logistics and MRO business segments.

The Lufthansa Group’s operating expenses rose by 7% to EUR 21,390m (previous year: EUR 19,963m), above all due to a significant increase in fuel expenses.

The Lufthansa Group’s Adjusted EBIT came to EUR -229m in the first half of 2026 and was thus lower than in the previous year (previous year: EUR 149m).

The Adjusted EBIT margin fell by 2.0 percentage points to -1.1% (previous year: 0.8%).

Free cash flow roughly on par with previous year

The Lufthansa Group’s cash flow from operating activities fell by 20% in the first half of 2026 to EUR 2,264m (previous year: EUR 2,831m). This decline is primarily due to lower Adjusted EBITDA as well as reduced cash inflows from working capital.

Adjusted free cash flow amounted to EUR 1,015m and was thus roughly at previous year’s level (previous year: EUR 1,024m). The decrease in cash flow from operating activities was compensated for by the decline in net capital expenditure. 

As of 30 June 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s overall available liquidity amounted to EUR 10,727m (31 December 2025: EUR 10,715m).

Increase in the Lufthansa Group’s indebtedness

As of 30 June 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s net indebtedness amounted to EUR 6,697m; EUR 290m higher than at year-end 2025 (31 December 2025: EUR 6,407m).

Net pension obligations were down compared to the end of 2025 and came to EUR 1,585m (31 December 2025: EUR 1,902m), above all due to positive valuation effects for the plan assets.

The Adjusted net debt/Adjusted EBITDA ratio in the past twelve months stood at 2.0 as of 30 June 2026 and was thus higher than at the end of 2025 (31 December 2025: 1.8).

Share Price Development

Lufthansa Share

Lufthansa share price recovers significantly in the second quarter of 2026

The share price of Deutsche Lufthansa AG showed a volatile but overall positive performance in the first half of 2026. After a positive start to the new stock market year and a temporary rise to EUR 9.50 on 9 February 2026, the share price initially came under significant pressure during the rest of the first quarter.

In the second quarter of 2026, the share price recovered significantly. As of 30 June 2026, the Lufthansa share was trading at EUR 10.01, around 19% above the year-end 2025 closing price. The recovery was supported by an overall improved perception of the European airline sector and a partial stabilisation of capital markets. At the same time, the market environment remained volatile due to geopolitical uncertainties, high energy prices and industry-specific operational challenges.

The MDAX also developed positively in the first half of 2026, but with an increase of around 4% compared with year-end 2025, it significantly lagged behind the performance of the Lufthansa share. Shares of the main European competitors also recovered in some cases significantly from the price declines recorded in the first quarter. Compared with year-end 2025, the Lufthansa share therefore outperformed the MDAX and was among the stronger performers within the European airline peer group.

Outlook

Outlook subject to uncertainties

The financial outlook for the Lufthansa Group is subject to a heightened level of forecasting uncertainty due to short-term booking cycles in the passenger business, the fact that cargo business is largely driven by the spot market, the uncertain geopolitical and macroeconomic environment, the lack of clarity regarding the outcome of collective bargaining and the possibility of various professions striking, and the uncertainty regarding the exact delivery dates of new aircraft.

In particular, the Group’s operational and financial performance is strongly influenced by the crisis in the Middle East. This is currently leading to operational disruptions such as the temporary cancellation of flight connections to the Gulf region. At the same time, demand on routes to Asia and Africa in particular has picked up significantly. This is likewise attributable to the situation in the Middle East – and to the hubs in the Gulf in particular – which is driving shifts in passenger flows.

The significant increase in kerosene prices is putting considerable pressure on the Lufthansa Group passenger airlines’ cost position, even though around 86% of the volume of kerosene required for the current year is already covered by fuel hedging through derivatives on various oil products.

Uncertainty in the macroeconomic outlook – in particular, trade policy tensions, scarce resources and supply bottlenecks, along with the effects of anti-inflationary measures implemented by the major central banks – may significantly impact customer demand.

Measures initiated for accelerated implementation of strategy

The Lufthansa Group has accelerated the implementation of its strategy in response to the current situation and removed all of Lufthansa CityLine’s aircraft from the service offering. It has also initiated additional capacity reduction measures such as retiring old aircraft types.

This package of measures will have a disproportionate impact in terms of fuel cost savings. On the one hand, particularly inefficient aircraft will be withdrawn from flight operations ahead of schedule. On the other, the volume of kerosene saved will reduce the non-hedged portion of the Lufthansa Group’s fuel requirements.

Lufthansa Group predicts Adjusted EBIT of between EUR 1.7 and 2.2bn for the 2026 financial year

For the 2026 financial year, the Lufthansa Group expects available capacity for the Network and Point-to-Point Airlines to be roughly in line with the previous year’s level. In its previous forecast, the Lufthansa Group had anticipated an increase of around 0 to 2%.

The Lufthansa Group continues to predict a clear increase in revenue for the 2026 financial year. In addition, it anticipates Adjusted EBIT of between EUR 1.7 and 2.2bn. In its previous forecast, the Lufthansa Group had anticipated Adjusted EBIT significantly higher than in the previous year. The upper end of the range continues to represent an earnings level which is significantly higher than in the previous year.

The range reflects the significantly higher level of uncertainty and increased risks in terms of the extent to which the rise in fuel costs due to the Middle East crisis can be made up for by a higher volume of revenue. In this context, the trend on the Asia, Africa and transatlantic routes is expected to have a positive impact on revenue. The main drivers of this are, in particular, the sustained high level of demand and the strong yield trend as a result of higher ticket prices. The currently strong cargo business is expected to deliver an additional revenue increase.

Net capital expenditure without acquisitions and disposals of equity interests is expected to amount to around EUR 2.5bn in the 2026 financial year. In its previous forecast, the Lufthansa Group had anticipated net capital expenditure of EUR 2.9bn. It has revised its predictions due to fewer aircraft deliveries than originally planned.

On the basis of the forecast earnings development, Adjusted free cash flow of around EUR 0.9bn is expected for the 2026 financial year.

The outlook for the individual business segments of the Lufthansa Group is as follows:

The Lufthansa Group continues to expect a clear increase in revenue for the Network Airlines business segment, based on the ongoing strong demand environment. Adjusted EBIT of EUR 750 to 1,150m is expected. In its previous forecast, the Lufthansa Group had anticipated Adjusted EBIT significantly higher than in the previous year.

A significant increase in revenue is expected for the Point-to-Point Airlines business segment. This will be driven by factors including the ongoing development of Eurowings Holidays’ business. Adjusted EBIT is forecast to be between EUR -50 and 20m. In its previous forecast, the Lufthansa Group had anticipated Adjusted EBIT of between EUR 0 and 20m.

The fuel cost and unit revenue trends over the remainder of the year are key earnings drivers for the ranges predicted for the Network Airlines and Point-to-Point Airlines business segments. In particular, the recent rise in volatility of fuel prices and the significantly shortened booking cycles for the Passenger Airlines business are hampering forecasting and are the key factor behind the predicted range.

The Lufthansa Group continues to expect a significant increase in revenue and Adjusted EBIT year-on-year in the Logistics business segment.

For the MRO business segment, the Group continues to anticipate a clear increase in revenue and a significant increase in Adjusted EBIT.

Annual General Meeting

Lufthansa shareholders approve all items on the agenda at the Annual General Meeting

Deutsche Lufthansa AG’s 73rd Annual General Meeting took place on 12 May 2026. Approximately 1,600 shareholders participated in the Annual General Meeting. A total of 51.33% of the share capital was represented. Nine items were on the agenda for the Annual General Meeting. The shareholders approved all items by a large majority.

By a large majority, they thereby granted discharge to the members of the Executive Board and the Supervisory Board for the fiscal year 2025.

The agenda items for the Annual General Meeting included, among other things, the appropriation of retained earnings, which provides for the distribution of a dividend of EUR 0.33 per share, as well as the election of members of the Supervisory Board of Deutsche Lufthansa AG. Dr. Johannes Teyssen, former Chairman of the Executive Board and CEO of E.ON SE, and Wolfgang Nickl, former member of the Executive Board and CFO of Bayer AG, were newly elected to the Supervisory Board. Karl Gernandt, Chairman of the Board of Directors of Kühne Holding AG, was reelected to the Supervisory Board.

The shareholders bid farewell to Dr. Karl-Ludwig Kley, who had been a member of the Supervisory Board since 2013 and its Chairman since 2017. In his speech, CEO Carsten Spohr addressed Karl-Ludwig Kley and thanked him for his service: “You have shaped our transformation. Our shared path to success, with its highs, lows, achievements, and crises. And especially during crises, you have always been a unique source of support for me personally.”

Following the conclusion of the Annual General Meeting, the Supervisory Board elected Dr. Johannes Teyssen as its new Chairman at its constituent meeting.

Fleet

Lufthansa Group orders ten Airbus and ten Boeing long-haul aircraft

At its meeting on 11 May 2026, the Supervisory Board of Deutsche Lufthansa AG approved orders for a total of 20 new long-haul aircraft. The Executive Board had previously decided to order ten Airbus A350-900s and ten Boeing 787-9s. These aircraft are due to be delivered between 2032 and 2034.

Carsten Spohr, Chairman of the Executive Board and CEO of Deutsche Lufthansa AG, says: “By ordering 20 additional long-haul aircraft, we are making a sustainable investment in the future of the Lufthansa Group. It is a clear commitment to a modern fleet, to premium quality, and to further reducing CO2 emissions. After all, aircraft featuring the latest technology are the most powerful tool for more climate-friendly air travel. The state-of-the-art Airbus A350 and Boeing 787 are more fuel-efficient, quieter, and have lower emissions than their respective predecessors. We are thus continuing the largest fleet modernization in our history.” 

The Airbus A350-900 and the Boeing 787-9 will replace older and therefore less efficient models starting in 2032. 

The gradual standardization of the fleet reduces complexity and increases efficiency, operational flexibility and operational stability. At the same time, maintenance and operating costs will be reduced. Additionally, further synergies will arise, for example in the areas of cockpit and cabin crew licensing and spare parts management. 

Including today’s order, the Lufthansa Group currently has a total of 232 state-of-the-art aircraft on its order list, including 107 next-generation long-haul aircraft.

Investments

Deutsche Lufthansa AG exercises option to acquire majority stake in ITA Airways

In June 2026, Deutsche Lufthansa AG exercised its option to acquire a majority stake in ITA Airways. Its equity investment will thus increase from 41 to 90%.

The acquisition of the additional 49% stake will take place at a previously agreed purchase price of EUR 325m. The transaction is subject to regulatory approvals, primarily from the European Commission and the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027. Following this, ITA Airways will be fully integrated into the Lufthansa Group.

Carsten Spohr, Chairman of the Executive Board and CEO of Deutsche Lufthansa AG, says: “Following the acquisition of the first 41% stake in ITA Airways last year, we promised the fastest airline integration in our history. We aimed to complete all major integration steps into the Lufthansa Group within just 18 months.

We have not only kept this promise. We were even faster: All customer-facing interfaces are already integrated today—with the exception of North Atlantic flights, where, as is well known, regulatory approval for our merger is still pending.

Passengers already experience ITA Airways as an integrated part of the Lufthansa Group. With unified booking, sales, and fare systems, the Miles & More frequent flyer program, Star Alliance membership, and access to our global network of premium lounges.

Integration is also progressing in the cargo business. Since last year, Lufthansa Cargo has been marketing ITA Airways’ cargo capacity, which alone corresponds to the additional capacity of three Boeing 777 freighters. 

In light of this success story, we decided to exercise our option to acquire an additional 49% stake as early as June of this year.”

Investments

Lufthansa Group submits bid for minority stake in TAP Air Portugal

On 29 July 2026 the Lufthansa Group submitted a bid to Parpública, the Portuguese state-owned investment company, as part of the bidding process for TAP Air Portugal. The acquisition of an initial minority stake is intended to establish a long-term partnership between the Lufthansa Group and TAP Air Portugal in order to secure TAP’s successful future as Portugal’s national airline on a sustainable basis.

Carsten Spohr, Chairman of the Executive Board & CEO of Deutsche Lufthansa AG, says: “We want to strengthen Portugal’s national airline as part of the Lufthansa Group and as the leading airline for the South Atlantic, with Lisbon as a strategic hub. With SWISS, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, and ITA Airways, we have demonstrated how the European integration of our Lufthansa Group secures prospects and growth for our home markets and hubs.”

The Lufthansa Group has already successfully developed national airlines such as SWISS, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, and most recently ITA Airways, while preserving their national identities. As the largest airline group outside the U.S., the Group offers the necessary scale, experience, and financial stability to create sustainable value and strengthen TAP Air Portugal’s role as the country’s ambassador around the world.

The partnership would strengthen Lisbon’s position as a strategically important Atlantic hub within the Lufthansa Group’s network. Connectivity between Europe and other regions, such as Latin America, Africa, and North America, could be expanded.

Miles & More

More experiences, more connections, and more relevance: Miles & More is becoming Lufthansa Group Miles & More

The Lufthansa Group is increasingly using its newly introduced umbrella brand in communications with customers. By the end of 2026, the group’s loyalty brand, “Miles & More,” will evolve into “Lufthansa Group Miles & More” and be brought under the umbrella brand.

With its new name, the program will undergo further development to become a unifying loyalty system for the entire group. The program will focus on providing even greater relevance and personalized experiences for members, including in terms of content. 

"Now that we have launched our new group brand, the next logical step is to integrate select group companies more closely into our brand architecture. This makes their affiliation with the Lufthansa Group more visible and tangible. The reposition-ing of Lufthansa Group Miles & More demonstrates our group’s cohesion while preserving the strengths and identities of our brands. This creates a consistent and compelling brand experience for our customers, partners, and employees across all touchpoints,” says Dieter Vranckx, Lufthansa Group Executive Board Member and Chief Commercial Officer.

As a future integrated brand, Lufthansa Group Miles & More will further consolidate its loyalty program across all of the group’s airlines and various customer touchpoints. This allows customers to benefit from useful synergies and a consistent brand presence and image. 

By the end of 2026, Lufthansa Group Miles & More will have a completely new visual identity. The new corporate design will translate the company's strategic direction into a modern and distinctive visual identity that will be easily recognized.

Corporate Responsibility

Flying more sustainably with “shark skin”: Lufthansa Group tests next-generation AeroSHARK

An Airbus A319 of Lufthansa City Airlines is the world’s first test aircraft for a new generation of fuel-efficient AeroSHARK technology. Lufthansa Technik will test approximately 70 transparent film patches measuring 20 × 20 centimeters each on the short-haul aircraft with registration D-ABGK together with its development partner Surventis (formerly BASF Coatings) through February 2027. These material samples are attached to selected areas on the sides of the fuselage as well as on the inside and outside of the engine cowlings. Their performance will be analyzed over the next few months during regular flight operations between Munich and Hamburg and is expected to provide insights for the further development of the AeroSHARK technology. The long-term goal is to tap into additional efficiency potential in flight operations through the broader use of AeroSHARK, thereby contributing to greater sustainability in air travel.

To date, the fuel-saving AeroSHARK film has been used on the fuselage and engine cowlings of long-haul aircraft certified for this technology. With their current research and development work, Lufthansa Technik and Surventis are now taking an important step forward. Together, they are developing the next-generation material, which could also be used on wings as well as horizontal and vertical stabilizers in the future. These areas are exposed to higher aerodynamic loads.

To date, AeroSHARK is the only riblet technology approved for commercial aviation. It consists of a specially developed surface film with microscopically fine grooved structures modeled after shark skin. These grooves - known as riblets - reduce frictional drag and improve flow characteristics

As of July 2026, the Lufthansa Group has already equipped 22 aircraft with AeroSHARK. As a result, the Lufthansa Group’s AeroSHARK fleet saves 19 metric tonnes of fuel and 60 metric tonnes of CO₂ daily. AeroSHARK is also gaining traction across the industry: Airlines outside the Lufthansa Group are also already using this innovative film.
