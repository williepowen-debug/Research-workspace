## Match the payoff to the mechanism

| Economic hypothesis | Expression to evaluate | What can break the link |
|---|---|---|
| Crude becomes scarcer globally and replacement demand tightens U.S. crude | USO / WTI exposure | U.S. supply, Cushing availability, export freight and origin discounts can cushion WTI even while delivered foreign oil becomes dearer. |
| Gulf access and alternative crude routes deteriorate, tightening seaborne replacement supply | Brent exposure, with BNO as the exchange-traded candidate | An existing Brent premium, recovery and demand destruction may absorb the event. Fund futures/roll/expenses differ from physical spot. Brent is not a direct Saudi barrel or incident contract. |
| Diesel/product prices stay high relative to crude at operating refineries | Refiner equity, existing VLO and conditional MPC/PSX research | A crude rally alone does not establish better margins. Own-site losses, feedstock and freight costs, policy and valuation matter. |
| Cargo rerouting consumes more vessel-days and raises profitable freight demand | Tanker equity research | Longer routes can increase demand, but lost cargo, inaccessible routes, hull exposure and operating costs can offset. Product/crude fleets differ. Higher delivered costs do not alone prove shareholder upside. |

These are distinct payoffs; no measured return correlation, hedge or independent confirmations are claimed. Neither a refiner nor a tanker should be substituted automatically for crude exposure because a geopolitical headline sounds bullish for energy.
