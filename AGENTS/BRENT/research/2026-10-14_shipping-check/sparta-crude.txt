FOBs and Freight Ate My Margin
Published
12 OCT 26 - 07:47
Reading time
minutes
Will the Houthi Let Saudi Barrels return to Europe?
Will NW Europe and USAC get pinched from both sides?
Can North Sea FOBs Collapse?
Conditions deteriorated over the weekend in the AG as the
Houthi unleashed deadly attacks on Riyadh
and several more ships were hit near Hormuz by what is presumed to be Iranian weapons. Despite US President Trump declaring an ‘energy ceasefire’ between Ukraine and Russia, neither country has acknowledged the agreement publicly as of this writing. The solace taken in last week’s announcements of the Saudis providing full volumes for European clients in November, and the Russians exporting 1 million tonnes of diesel fuel to the US, may be overshadowed by the familiar anxiety of supply continuity.
One of the big headlines from last week is the
announced return of Saudi barrels to European buyers
. For the past month or so, flows from the AG have been strong but going almost exclusively to Asian buyers. As we covered last week, the Asian markets seem content with the volume of crude headed their way. While freight is a headwind everywhere, adequate supply from the AG to Asian markets is making it tough on alternate supply regions. WAF, in particular, has seen
FOB prices fall sharply
as Asian buyers, well supplied from the AG, have stepped back from that market.
Even at discounted levels, the freight burden to Asia remains prohibitive, making NWE and USAC the natural outlet for these barrels. The newly affordable WAF grades have helped to bring Johan Sverdrup diffs back down from the astronomical $23/bbl levels seen at the end of September. In a vacuum, the Saudis’ intention to supply European customers again, and the potential of OECD European SPR crude release, should bring North Sea FOBs back down simply because of more supply. In the greater context of high freight and a quickly escalating situation in the AG, though, it is difficult to be bearish.
[Several WAF grades land at a discount to Dated in NW Europe, WTI VLCC and Qua Iboe (not shown) land at a $10-$10.30/bbl premium to Dated]
Last week we discussed the high FOBs seen in the North Sea market and how they are helping WTI land into NWE at such a relatively good value even though
TD25 freight is at record highs
. If the Forties FOB were to collapse back down to normal pre-war levels (a move from $19/bbl over Dated to $1–2/bbl over Dated), the WTI arb would slam shut. WTI would either have to stay in the USGC, and we could see the return of a contango, or WTI/Brent would need to keep doing the heavy lifting and price in the entire freight component. With TD25 currently pricing around the $25/bbl region, we would need to see WTI/Brent keep widening to around $25/bbl.
[Even Forties is expensive]
European refiners are at a severe disadvantage to their US counterparts, especially those in the USGC, in terms of the market price of crude supply. To our thinking, this is why news of
Saudi resuming flows to European clients punches above its weight
. Conversely, if the Saudis are not able to provide barrels to Europe because of military intervention, high freight and high FOBs will continue to eat into European refining margins. Both NWE and USAC refiners are being pinched by the high cost of landed crude. With all the headlines out this weekend regarding attacks on Saudi facilities, we would urge European and USAC crude buyers to take a hard look at WAF grades.
[Typical mainstay of NW Europe and USAC refiners, Forties Blend margins are near break-even levels]
As of Sunday,
Hurricane Isaias had shut in 6MB of Gulf of Mexico production according to the BSEE
. The good news is that the daily shut-in and evacuated rigs statistics peaked on Friday and both numbers have since started to come back down. As opposed to the speculative nature of this communication thus far, we are quite confident in the numbers presented by the BSEE. In normal years, this sort of outage would have a limited impact on the market, but as things stand today this 6MB outage could have a larger ripple effect on the WoS medium crude market.
[Isaias has shut-in 6 mb of GOM crude oil so far]
While consumers in both Europe and the US welcome the prospect of lower pump prices via the proposed
OECD Europe SPR diesel releases
and flows of Russian diesel fuel to the US respectively, these interventions may not be so welcome to refiners. By our calculations, European and USAC margins running North Sea light grades are teetering on the brink of profitability already. As we said above, these refiners are being pinched by the high cost of landed crude, and now the golden ticket of diesel margins could also become a pinch point. We at Sparta have been claiming for a while that diesel is the real issue and crude oil, while tight globally, was still affordable in the context of monster heat or gasoil cracks.
These diesel releases could upset that balance for refiners. If the issue is compounded by another period of limited flows from the Red Sea and/or Hormuz, it could cause real pain for refiners. While the knee-jerk answer may be for North Sea grades to have FOBs drop back into single digits, it is not clear how NWE would pull enough volume from Atlantic Basin producing regions in the current freight environment.
North Sea FOBs are already showing early signs of softening from recent highs
, but the freight environment means any meaningful volume pulled from the Atlantic Basin remains constrained.
Given the headlines from Saudi and Hormuz over the weekend, we
will add length to last week’s trade idea of long Jan Brent/Dubai EFS trade (we got in at $3.50/bbl)
. We will also start bidding for Dec DFL around the $4/bbl area with a relatively close stop at recent lows (for the Dec contract) at flat. As we lean towards a more bullish outcome from an escalating situation in the AG, these trades could be contrarywise undone by a sudden stabilisation in the region. These are not ‘fire and forget’ trades, so we will need to keep a close eye on headlines.
[We are long the Jan Brent/Dubai EFS (and looking to add more length), and will be looking to buy Dec DFL up to the $4/bbl area]
