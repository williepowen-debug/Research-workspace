East of Suez diesel heads west as December arbs reopen
Published
13 OCT 26 - 07:38
Reading time
minutes
Diesel risk premium remains elevated as attacks on Saudi Arabia and vessels in Hormuz keep supply-security risk in the price. But ICE gasoil prompt spreads have softened on the combination of potential diesel stock releases and Trump’s move to ease restrictions on Russian diesel trade. China has approved around 3.7 mt of October Clean product exports, Washington has licensed Russian diesel trade until 7 April 2027, and the IEA is pushing members to accelerate stock releases, with diesel the priority ahead of its 15 October board meeting.
Russian diesel is not a direct relief barrel for Europe, but it can still loosen the wider system. Trump’s stated 300 kt immediately and 500 kt in November equals 800 kt, or roughly 6 mb across two months. That is only around eight days of Russia’s typical diesel exports. The licence is politically fragile, with lawmakers from both parties pushing to reverse it.
December arb is starting to look better than November. For 11–15 December delivery, the diesel arb is open on paper from Yanbu (LR2, +$37.0/mt) and the USGC (MR, +$11.9/mt), with Sikka via the Cape near breakeven (-$1.3/mt). For 11–15 November delivery, only Yanbu (+$0.9/mt) and the USGC (+$4.2/mt) marginally clear.
Gasoil arbs into Singapore for 11–15 November delivery are shut on paper. Sikka LR2 is closed at -$11.0/bbl, while Ulsan MR is closed at -$4.4/bbl. Sikka nets back about $6.4/bbl more into Rotterdam than into Singapore for December delivery. Singapore 10ppm clears into Rotterdam for 11–15 November delivery only on a Suezmax (+$17.5/mt).
The reopening is a freight and curve story, not a richer Rotterdam. TC20 balance-October hit a 52-week high of $205.7/mt on 9 October, but November is $55.7/mt cheaper. That roll-down more than offsets ICE gasoil’s $41.1/mt November/December backwardation and makes December arrivals easier to price.
Europe is still tight, but structure is starting to give. The December 2026 ICE gasoil/Brent crack swap holds near $79.7/bbl, yet December 2026/January 2027 has eased to +$34.4/mt from +$37.6/mt at Friday’s close. ARA gasoil stocks are reportedly rebuilding, with October arrivals likely outpacing September, while European refineries are starting to come back online after peak turnarounds.
Asia is steady, but Europe is doing the work. The November 2026 Singapore 10ppm/Dubai crack is $62.8/bbl, about $1/bbl above Friday’s close, while the November gasoil E/W has widened to +$131.0/mt from +$96.0/mt on 5 October, largely on ICE outperformance. That is enough to move Asian barrels west on large ships, but still below November Singapore-Rotterdam LR2 freight of $180.3/mt.
Trade idea: sell ICE gasoil December 2026/January 2027 at around +$35.5/mt. We prefer December/January over November/December because December is when the reopened arbs should land.
