USGC MR Market Report: 12 October 2026

Cross BarrelCrudeDieselDistillateFreightFuel OilGasolineJetNaphtha
Analyst briefsChart packsDeep divesEventsOpinionsPodcastsPressWebinarsWhitepapers
Tightest tonnage count since 24 September meets Russian diesel licence news.
Published12 OCT 26 - 10:22 Reading time  minutes

Vessel supply for the 17 to 26 October load window stands at 13 ships against a 90-day average of 19, a differential of -6 and the tightest count since 24 September. It is a clean owners’ market on the supply side and the first sustained tightening since the correction that followed the collapse of the US diesel export ban story.

Incremental demand has turned with it, at +5 vessels against neutral demand to close out last week. The Freight Supply and Demand (FSD) model now forecasts spot TC14 at WS 298 for the window against a spot assessment of WS 282. Last done is already above the assessment. The Ardmore Endurance fixed late on 9 October for a 12 October laycan, US Gulf to UK Continent, at WS 300 for a USLD cargo.

Last week’s selloff in freight has helped improve arb margins. Houston to Rotterdam diesel is open at +$7.25/mt for November and widens to +$13.50/mt for December and +$19.25/mt for January. Houston to Buenos Aires diesel sits at +$17.50 cpg for November, Houston to San Jose gasoline at +$17.20 cpg, and Houston to Santos diesel at +$9.35 cpg improving every month out to +$12.25 cpg in January. Implied demand has improved materially compared with the start of last week.

Prompt October loader margins still have weak spots. Houston to Rosarito gasoline is shut at -$16.00 cpg, Esmeraldas gasoline at -$8.60 cpg and Barcelona diesel at -$20.75/mt. The improvement in demand is stronger in November and December.

OFAC General License 135, issued on 9 October at President Trump’s direction, muddies the improving outlook. It authorises the sale, delivery and importation of Russian-origin diesel, including into the United States, through 7 April 2027. The delivered New York board shows the window the licence lands in: non-Jones Act Houston to New York diesel at +$7.42 cpg for 21 to 25 October loaders and +$9.39 cpg for 26 to 31 October, easing from +$9.36 cpg and +$11.33 cpg on 9 October, with Rotterdam shut at -$23.95 cpg for 26 to 31 October and Aliaga at -$39.79 cpg for 1 to 5 November.

The US Gulf is the only economic waterborne supplier of diesel into New York through the midterms, and the licence does not change that by 3 November, because a Baltic loader fixed today arrives on the US Atlantic coast around three weeks later. Any pre-election relief comes through the paper market and not through barrels discharging in New York.

The faster route is Europe, where Primorsk to Rotterdam is under a week and Russian barrels in ARA would pull European demand off USGC diesel and build domestic length. That needs Brussels to move first though.

For freight the licence is likely bearish either way. Short-haul Baltic to ARA replacing long-haul USGC into Europe strips tonne-miles out of the Atlantic MR fleet, TC14 demand wanes from the point substitution starts and intra-regional European fixtures pick up. This is a medium-term drag on rates sitting directly on top of a short-term tightening in tonnage supply, which is why the TC14 outlook is conflicted.

November TC14 paper at WS 405 is mispriced. It sits WS 123 above spot and above the recent spot high of WS 395, a high only reached because business was pulled forward on the risk of a US diesel export ban that never came. December at WS 379 carries the same problem, but is a more palatable level as there is more time after the midterms for a bullish export catalyst to appear.

WS 300 looks like fair value for spot. There are not yet enough bullish signals for owners to push much beyond that. The spread between spot and November paper is too wide on current fundamentals, and the November premium to December also looks wrong. Watch for Baltic ULSD fixtures into ARA: they will be the first hard confirmation that the licence matters, and they will follow Brussels rather than Washington. Sell the TC14 November/December spread.
