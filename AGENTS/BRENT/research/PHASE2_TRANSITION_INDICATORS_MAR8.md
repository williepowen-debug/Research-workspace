## PART 1 — HISTORICAL ANALOG ANALYSIS

### Cycle A: 1990 Gulf War ($21→$41→$20)

**Timeline:** Iraq invaded Kuwait Aug 2, 1990. Oil doubled from $21 to $41 by Oct 1990. Traded $25-$32 range Nov-Jan. Desert Storm launched Jan 17, 1991 → price collapsed $10 in a single day (largest one-day drop ever at the time). Within 48 hours, back to pre-invasion levels (~$20).

**What led the top:**
1. **Futures curve flattened 4-6 weeks before Desert Storm.** The war premium was visible in backwardation — M1-M12 spread peaked in Oct 1990 and began narrowing in November even as spot held $25-30. The market was slowly pricing in a military resolution.
2. **SPR release announced Nov 1990 (IEA coordinated).** First credible government supply response. Market read it as "they know this will be resolved."
3. **Speculative long liquidation.** NYMEX open interest data (pre-COT granularity) showed declining open interest in Nov-Dec 1990 while price held — classic distribution pattern.
4. **No demand destruction signal at all.** The $40 peak lasted only ~2 weeks. Price corrected on RESOLUTION, not demand destruction. The war premium evaporated when uncertainty about supply resolved.

**Key lesson for us:** In resolution-driven corrections, there is NO demand destruction leading indicator. The signal is diplomatic/military: ceasefire, escort ops, or decisive strikes. The M1-M3 spread narrowing was the ONLY market-based leading signal, and it led by 4-6 weeks.

**Lead times:**
| Indicator | Signal | Lead time before price collapse |
|-----------|--------|-------------------------------|
| M1-M12 spread narrowing | Started Nov 1990 | 6-8 weeks |
| SPR release announcement | Nov 1990 | 8 weeks |
| NYMEX OI declining while price flat | Dec 1990 | 4 weeks |
| Actual price collapse | Jan 17, 1991 (Desert Storm) | 0 (event-driven) |

### Cycle B: 2008 Super-Spike ($90→$147→$33)

**Timeline:** Oil rose from $90 in Jan 2008 to $147 on July 11, 2008. Collapsed to $33 by Dec 2008. The peak-to-trough was 78% in 5 months.

**What led the top:**
1. **US gasoline demand turned negative YoY in Q1 2008** — the first and earliest leading indicator. EIA weekly data showed gasoline supplied declining vs. prior year starting in February 2008, a full 5 months before the price peak. This was masked by surging Chinese demand, so the aggregate global number stayed positive longer.
2. **CFTC managed money positions.** Speculative net longs peaked in early 2008 and were DECLINING through Q2 even as price rose. By July, speculators held 48% of open interest — maximum extraction point. The divergence between falling speculative positioning and rising price was a classic blow-off top signal, leading by ~3-4 months.
3. **Dollar bottomed.** USD index hit its low in March 2008 and began strengthening. Oil is priced in dollars — dollar strength mechanically pressures commodities. Led by ~4 months.
4. **Backwardation did NOT lead.** The curve was in backwardation during the run-up and stayed there. This is the critical difference from 1990: in a demand-driven super-cycle, backwardation persists until the very end. It's not a useful leading signal when the driver is demand destruction rather than supply resolution.
5. **Lehman signal.** Bear Stearns collapsed March 2008 (4 months before peak). The financial system was cracking but oil kept rising on EM demand narrative. The credit market was leading the equity and commodity markets.
6. **SemGroup bankruptcy July 2008.** A major oil distributor's failure at the exact top was both a symptom (short squeeze created the final spike) and a signal (forced liquidation cascaded).

**Lead times:**
| Indicator | Signal | Lead time before price peak |
|-----------|--------|----------------------------|
| US gasoline demand YoY negative | Feb 2008 | **5 months** |
| Managed money net long declining | Q1 2008 | **3-4 months** |
| USD bottoming | Mar 2008 | **4 months** |
| Credit market cracking (Bear Stearns) | Mar 2008 | **4 months** |
| Backwardation narrowing | NOT A SIGNAL | N/A |
| Spot price peak → collapse | July 11 | 0 |

### Cycle C: 2022 Russia-Ukraine ($78→$139→$85)

**Timeline:** Brent was ~$78 on Jan 3, 2022. Invaded Feb 24. Peaked $139 on March 8. Second peak ~$124 in June. Declined to $85 by Dec. Below $90 by September.

**This is the most relevant analog because:**
- Geopolitical supply shock (like ours)
- Pre-existing tight market (like ours)
- Occurred in an inflationary macro environment with Fed tightening (like ours)
- Dual supply AND demand catalysts for the decline

**What led the top (March peak):**
1. **SPR announcement.** Biden announced historic 180M barrel SPR release on March 31, 2022. This capped the March spike and created a structural ceiling. The first IEA coordinated release was announced March 1 — Brent peaked March 8. **7 days lead time.** However, the announcement stabilized price; the actual decline came over months.
2. **China COVID lockdowns.** Shanghai full lockdown announced March 28, 2022. Removed ~1M bpd of demand from the world's largest importer. This was the demand destruction accelerant that SPR alone couldn't provide.
3. **Backwardation peaked March 2022, narrowed gradually through May-June.** M1-M6 Brent spread went from ~$15 in March to ~$8 by June. This is the spread compression signal — it LED the second peak (June $124) by approximately 0 weeks (concurrent, not leading). It LED the sustained decline by 2-3 months.
4. **US gasoline demand turned negative YoY by Q2 2022.** EIA confirmed that Q2 2022 gasoline supplied was BELOW Q2 2021 levels. This was first visible in weekly data by late April/early May. Led the June second peak by 4-6 weeks. Led the sustained decline by ~2 months.
5. **Managed money positioning.** Net longs peaked in late Feb/early March 2022 and declined through Q2. Price held above $100 through May while positioning was falling — same blow-off divergence pattern as 2008.
6. **VLCC rates did NOT follow oil higher.** Despite oil at $130+, VLCC rates actually FELL 59% from the invasion week through March. This is because the Russia-Ukraine disruption initially froze trade flows rather than extending ton-miles (opposite of rerouting dynamic). When VLCC rates eventually spiked, it was Q4 2022 (Dec EU embargo/price cap rerouting) — completely detached from the oil price cycle. **VLCC rates were a lagging indicator in 2022, not leading.**

**Lead times:**
| Indicator | Signal | Lead time before sustained decline |
|-----------|--------|-----------------------------------|
| SPR release announcement | Mar 1/Mar 31 | Immediate cap; structural decline 2-3 months |
| China lockdown (demand shock) | Mar 28 | 2-3 months before sustained decline |
| M1-M6 backwardation narrowing | Mar→June | Concurrent with second peak; 0-2 months lead |
| US gasoline demand YoY negative | Late Apr/May | 4-6 weeks before June peak |
| Managed money net long declining | Late Feb/Mar | 3 months before sustained decline |
| VLCC rates | NOT A SIGNAL | Lagged, not led |

## PART 2 — WHAT'S DIFFERENT ABOUT 2026

Before building the matrix, I need to flag structural differences that change which indicators matter:

### 1. Physical blockade vs. trade rerouting
2022 was a trade rerouting — Russian oil found alternative buyers (China, India). Total global supply barely fell. 2026 is a PHYSICAL CLOSURE — 12.7 mbpd net deficit with no alternative routing for most barrels. This means:
- **Backwardation will be MORE extreme and persist longer** (no alternative supply path)
- **Demand destruction will take LONGER to register** (supply isn't being rerouted, it's removed)
- **Resolution correction will be MORE violent** (bigger premium to unwind)

### 2. Insurance-driven vs. military-driven blockade
Our closure is financial (insurance withdrawal), not physical (mines, attacks on ships). This means:
- **Resolution could be faster** — a single credible naval escort announcement could restart insurance
- **Or resolution could be slower** — P&I clubs move deliberately; even if military situation improves, reinsurance takes weeks to reinstate
- **The key variable isn't Hormuz transit count** (it's already zero) — **it's P&I club coverage announcements**

### 3. Pre-existing labor market weakness
The -92K NFP is unique to this cycle. In 2008, employment was positive through most of H1. In 1990, recession started before the oil shock. In 2022, labor market was extremely tight. Our setup has OIL SHOCK + LABOR WEAKNESS simultaneously, which means demand destruction could front-run the historical 20-24 week timeline.

### 4. We have TWO Phase 2 triggers, not one
- **Trigger A (Resolution):** Hormuz reopens → Gulf War Syndrome snap-back → 15-25% price drop in 24-48 hours. This is the 1990 analog.
- **Trigger B (Demand Destruction):** Hormuz stays closed but oil at $90-100+ destroys demand → gradual price plateau then decline. This is the 2008 analog (slower, messier).

**The matrix must distinguish between these two paths because the leading indicators are completely different.**
