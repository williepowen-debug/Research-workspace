## Diplomatic/Military Developments (Weekend May 22–25)

### Friday May 22 — Oil posts weekly loss on deal signals
Oil prices fell on Friday as US and Iran signaled continued progress. Brent ended the week below $103 on deal optimism. Brent lost ~6% on the week as markets priced in supply relief. [CNBC May 22]

### Saturday May 23 — Trump: Deal "largely negotiated"
President Trump told reporters the US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is "largely negotiated" and will be announced "shortly." Framework as described:
- 60-day ceasefire extension ("memorandum of understanding")
- Hormuz to be demined and reopened immediately upon signing; prewar conditions within 30 days
- US lifts naval blockade on Iranian ports
- US issues temporary oil sanctions waivers to allow Iran to sell oil freely
- Iran commits to never pursue nuclear weapons; agrees to negotiate uranium enrichment suspension
[CNBC May 23]

### Sunday May 24 — Framework details emerge; deal not yet signed
WaPo reported details of the potential deal: Hormuz would be under Iranian management per latest text exchanged (conflicting with Trump public statements). No agreement signed as of Sunday. CNN published "What's in the proposed deal" explainer. Axios exclusive: framework includes sanctions waivers for Iran's oil exports. [WaPo May 24; Axios May 24; CNN May 24]

### Monday May 25 — Trump walkback; oil drops 5%+
Brent fell to $98.12, WTI to ~$91.73 (both down ~5%). Trump partially walked back May 23 statement, saying the deal "isn't even fully negotiated yet" and blockade remains until agreement is complete. He also said he "won't rush." The market priced in significant supply-relief optimism regardless — a lot of fear premium came out fast. Bloomberg noted analysts saying "a lot of oil was trading on worst-case assumptions for weeks." [Bloomberg May 25; Fortune May 24]

### P&I Insurance Status
- **NOT resumed** — deal not signed, war risk premiums remain elevated
- Industry estimates: premiums still at 3–8% of vessel value per transit (vs ~0.25% prewar)
- Only 5–6 vessels/day transiting Hormuz (vs peacetime 138/day average)
- Khaleej Times noted: even after Hormuz reopens, war-risk premiums unlikely to fall quickly; insurers want months of sustained stability before restoring normal cover [Khaleej Times; albanyantree May 6]

### Iran's Position — Hormuz Management
Iran's Parliament committee said Hormuz will remain under Iranian management in the latest draft text, including potential transit fees ("specialized services"). This conflicts with Trump's public framing. **Key sticking point for final deal.** [Iran media; WaPo May 24]
