## 1.3 Hormuz: Closure Mechanics

**The mechanism (why it's different from 1980s):**

Unlike the Tanker War (1984-88) when physical attacks barely disrupted 2% of traffic, this closure is driven by **financial infrastructure**, not physical blockade:

1. P&I clubs and war-risk underwriters withdrew coverage
2. Commercial operators are legally barred from entering without insurance
3. Result: -90 to -94% traffic reduction [Argus, Daily Sabah — Mar 6]
4. No historical modern precedent for >30-day closure at this scale [HORMUZ_HISTORICAL_PRECEDENTS.md]

**Bypass capacity (why it can't substitute):**
- Saudi East-West Pipeline: 5.5 mb/d capacity, currently at max utilization
- UAE AdCorp Pipeline: 1.8 mb/d capacity, at max
- Combined: ~7.3 mb/d available
- Normal Hormuz flow: 20 mb/d crude + LNG
- **Gap: 12.7+ mb/d structurally unroutable** [OPEC_CURRENT_STATE_MAR2026.md]
- Cape of Good Hope rerouting adds 10-20 days to voyages, absorbs shipping capacity, inflates freight [HORMUZ_HISTORICAL_PRECEDENTS.md]

**What would reopen it:**
1. US naval escort coalition (like Operation Earnest Will 1987) — NOT YET ANNOUNCED
2. Ceasefire + diplomatic de-escalation
3. Decisive defeat of Iranian missile/naval capabilities

**Historical resolution timing precedents:**
- Tanker War: 4-year conflict, never truly closed at this scale
- 1990 Gulf War: 5-month premium, resolved when Operation Desert Storm began (33% single-day price drop)
- 2012 sanctions threats: Resolved within 3 weeks via naval deterrence signals
- 2019 Abqaiq: 14-day price reversal despite massive supply hit
- **Key insight:** When closure resolves, price reversal is VIOLENT and FAST — "Gulf War Syndrome"
