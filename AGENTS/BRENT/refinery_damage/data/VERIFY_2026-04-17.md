## Item 1 — RF-012 ADCOP Habshan-Fujairah pipeline fire

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with date adjustment: incident dates Mar 30, not Mar 31)**

The single-source @silvertrade claim is substantially corroborated by multiple independent outlets reporting fires at two pumping stations along the Habshan-Fujairah / ADCOP pipeline. Satellite imagery (attributed to Clash Report) showed active fires; Caliber.az, Pravda USA, Pravda EN, and bne IntelliNews all ran the story on Mar 30, 2026. The capacity figure (1.5M bpd, up to 1.8M bpd design) matches Wikipedia/GEM references. Cause remains officially unconfirmed by UAE authorities — framed by regional press as an Iranian drone strike consistent with the Mar 14 and Mar 16 Fujairah-area attacks (CNBC), but the UAE has not attributed. **Recommend: upgrade RF-012 source tier from B-2 to A-2** (satellite imagery + multiple wire-adjacent outlets; cause still unattributed), and set `date_first = 2026-03-30`. @silvertrade was early by ~1 day but the event itself is real.

## Item 2 — RF-002 (Mar 13) vs RF-019 (Apr 7) Kharg Island

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED — RF-019 "struck, 90% export capacity offline" claim is not supported by reporting. Apr 7 strikes DID happen, but oil infra was AGAIN spared.**

A second U.S. strike on Kharg Island occurred Apr 7, 2026, ahead of a Trump Hormuz-reopen deadline. Multiple sources (CNN, Bloomberg, Air Force Times, Jerusalem Post, AGBI, houseofsaud.com) confirm the Apr 7 mission hit 50+ military targets — "restrikes on what we hit previously" per a U.S. official — and deliberately avoided oil facilities for the second time. Iran's semi-official Mehr News Agency reported maritime/oil infrastructure "suffered little damage" and "continues to operate as normal." Oil still spiked ~3% on fear pricing (Brent >$110, WTI ~$116) but export capacity was not physically disabled.

**Answer to Q1:** No separate event hit oil infrastructure on Apr 7. **Q2:** N/A. **Q3:** The BRENT STATUS "STRUCK Apr 7 — 90% Iran export capacity" phrasing appears to conflate (a) Kharg's 90% share of Iran's export throughput (a standing fact, not damage) with (b) the fact that strikes occurred Apr 7. The capacity fraction is correct as a pre-existing descriptor; the implied operational offline state is not.

**Recommend: DELETE RF-019 as a separate damage record.** Add a note on RF-002 that a second U.S. strike wave occurred Apr 7 but oil infra was again spared — or log RF-019 as a `THREAT_EVENT` incident_type with `bpd_offline_est = 0` and status `ATTACKED_INFRA_INTACT`. Do NOT carry 1,500,000 bpd offline for Kharg.

## Item 3 — India and China refinery operational status Mar–Apr 2026

**Verdict: UNVERIFIED for physical damage; CONFIRMED operational events found for India (supply-driven, not damage).**

**India — two real events worth adding (both supply-shortage, not physical damage):**

1. **MRPL (Mangalore)** — Mar 4–5, 2026: Reuters/Bloomberg/BusinessToday/Free Press Journal reported MRPL (300k bpd) shut a 100k bpd CDU plus secondary units including a hydrocracker due to West Asia crude-supply disruption, and halted fuel exports / declared force majeure on some export commitments. MRPL officially **denied** the shutdown via a regulatory filing Mar 7, 2026, saying operations are normal. Conflicting reporting — log as `OPERATIONAL_STRESS` not damage.

This is a deferred turnaround, not an incident. Relevant because it further tightens India product output during the Hormuz crisis.
