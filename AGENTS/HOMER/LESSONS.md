### [Data] — Verify the Year Explicitly Before Citing a Load-Bearing Metric
**Mistake:** HOMER's Spawn 5 cited "Trepp CMBS MF May 2026 6.57% (−46bps from Apr 7.15% ATH)" as load-bearing two-source cross-confirmation of a multifamily-stress reversal, and built a downgrade State Vector off it. The source article (Multifamily Dive) was dated Jun 16 **2025** and reported May **2025** data — a year mismatch masked by relative phrasing ("in May") and a cluster of correctly-dated 2026 results nearby giving false confidence. Reality: Trepp Apr 2026 = 7.71% NEW ATH (+56bps from Mar), the opposite direction. Caught in Spawn 6 (Will Part A stress-test) via fresh queries.
**Rule:** Before citing any web-pulled metric as **load-bearing** (capable of changing a prediction confidence, a STATUS color, or a transmission-chain status), confirm the year explicitly from the primary source — either the article's publication date or an explicit year-stamp on the cited number itself. Relative phrasing ("May print," "latest data," "fell to X%") is insufficient. If the primary source isn't fetchable, the metric stays in an Open Questions / unconfirmed bucket, not in the dashboard.
**Secondary rule:** When two series are claimed to "cross-confirm" a regime change, verify each independently from its own primary source before treating them as multi-source corroboration.

### [Data] — The Year-Trap Points at Your Own Prediction: a Stale Article Can FALSELY CONFIRM You
**Near-miss (2026-07-31, caught before it entered any file).** HOM-01 Leg-1's first resolver (FMHPI June-2026 data) was due the day of the session. Three separate searches returned CalculatedRisk's *"Freddie Mac House Price Index Declined in June; Up 2.0% Year-over-year"* — and the search engine's own summary asserted it as the June-**2026** print each time, with a clean-looking prior-month comparison (May +2.3%). **Direct fetch showed a publication date of July 31, 2025 — it was June-2025 data.** The actual June-2026 release had not yet posted.
**Why this one was more dangerous than the Spawn-5 error:** those 2025 numbers, dropped into the grading sheet, read **J (2.0%) < M (2.3%) → "Leg-1 CONFIRMED."** The stale data pointed *toward* my own registered prediction. The Spawn-5 year-misread at least felt surprising enough to stress-test; **a figure that confirms your prior does not trigger the instinct to re-check.** The correct outcome — *print not out, do not grade, do not substitute Case-Shiller or FHFA* — was already written in the grading sheet's own "release delayed/missing" row, and only survived because the year got verified first.
**Rule:** Year-verify **hardest** when the number would CONFIRM a live prediction, not just when it would surprise you. Two specific triggers: **(a)** an annually-recurring release (monthly HPI, quarterly DQ surveys) whose prior-year article occupies the same headline shape — search relevance ranks these near-identically and the engine's *summary layer* will restate them in the present tense; **(b)** a resolver you are actively waiting on, where "the print is out" is the outcome you want. **A prediction's resolver is the single worst place to accept a search summary.** Fetch the article, read the publication date, then grade. If the print is not out, the answer is "not gradeable yet" — which is a real, publishable result, not a failed session.
**Corollary:** the same session's real finding (Fannie SF 0.58% / Freddie SF 0.60% / Fannie MF 0.60%) shows the sibling hazard — **look-alike numbers across books.** Always carry book + basis with the figure.

### [Data] — Revision Discipline Was Applied to the PREDICTION and Not to the DASHBOARD
**Mistake (found 2026-07-31, carried ~5 weeks).** HOMER's STATUS carried *"New-home sales 580K SAAR May (−7.3% MoM); months-supply **10.3, tied 2008-09 bust high**"* as a live 🔴 condition. Census had since revised **May up to 618K (+38K)** and **months-supply down to 9.4**; the June print then came in at 628K / 9.3. **The "bust-high tie" — the most quotable claim in the row — described no live figure at all.** It was the original CB26-106 vintage (rel 6/24), superseded by the very next monthly release.
**The irony that makes this worth writing down:** HOM-01's registration contains an explicit, carefully-reasoned **revision rule** ("FMHPI revises history monthly → grade on each release's as-published figures"). The discipline existed, was written down, and was applied *only to the prediction ledger*. **The dashboard — which is what other agents actually read and cite — had no equivalent rule.** Monthly Census/BEA/BLS series revise the prior month *every single release*; a dashboard row is a **snapshot presented as a condition**, and snapshots of revising series rot silently in the direction of whatever was most dramatic at the time (first prints overshoot; that is why they get revised).
**Rule:** For any series that **revises prior months as a matter of course** (Census new-home sales, starts/permits, construction spending, BLS payrolls, BEA GDP, FMHPI), a dashboard row must either **(a)** be refreshed from the newest release — carrying the revised prior alongside the current print — or **(b)** carry an explicit vintage stamp (*"as originally published <date>; not revision-tracked"*). **Never leave a first-print superlative ("tied the bust high," "steepest since X," "lowest since Y") standing unqualified**, because the superlative is precisely the part revision is most likely to erase.
**Tell:** if a row's punchline is a record/tie/extreme drawn from a **single month of a revising series**, treat it as provisional until a second release confirms it.

### [Data] — I Gave the Headline to a Cohort Too Small to Be the Driver, Because the Multiple Was Dramatic
**Mistake (2026-07-31, published midday, retracted the same evening on PROME's audit).** Freddie's Q2 10-Q breaks multifamily DQ into credit-enhanced and non-credit-enhanced legs. The non-CE leg went **0.13% → 0.58% — a 4.5× jump** — and I published: *"the stress is **concentrated in the unprotected leg**, and the blended 0.51% understates it."* **I never weighted it.** Non-CE is **8.3% of the book** ($41.8B of $505.0B) and contributes **~3.4bps of the ~6.5bps** move — **about half, not the driver.** Worse, its denominator **shrank 23.7%**, so delinquent non-CE *dollars* rose **3.4×, not 4.5×**: part of the multiple was the base falling out from under it.
**What made it seductive:** 4.5× is the most quotable number on the page, it sits in the "unprotected" bucket which *sounds* like where stress ought to concentrate, and it pointed the same direction as my thesis. **A number that is dramatic, intuitive, and thesis-confirming gets three separate reasons not to check it.**
**The part that stings: the correct read was MORE bearish than the one I published.** Weighting the table shows the deterioration is **broad** — the credit-enhanced leg (91.7% of the book) also worsened, and its largest bucket, **Subordination at $311.8B (62% of the portfolio), moved +9bps vs the +7bps blended**, i.e. faster than the headline. Meanwhile the *improving* bucket (MSCR/MCIP) **grew**, so the blended rate is partly **mix-suppressed**. I traded a strong, broad-based finding for a weak, thin-slice one by reaching for the biggest multiple.
**Rule:** **When a source breaks a rate into segments, compute each segment's share and its contribution to the change BEFORE writing any sentence with "concentrated," "driven by," or "the real story is."** Three lines of arithmetic. A large *rate* move on a small *weight* is not a driver, and a large multiple on a shrinking base is partly an artifact of the base. **Report levels and weights alongside any ratio or multiple** (`finding_cohort_too_small_to_move_the_index`).
**Second-order rule:** check whether a multiple's denominator moved. **0.13% → 0.58% with UPB −23.7% is a different fact from 0.13% → 0.58% at constant UPB**, and only the dollar series distinguishes them.
**Tell:** the sentence contains a multiple ("4.5×", "tripled") and no share-of-total anywhere near it.

### [Process] — I Asserted a Delivery That Had Not Happened, in the Same Voice I Use for Verified Facts
**Mistake (2026-07-31, caught by PROME's artifact audit within ~2 hours).** My CORAL packet on the 8/3 GSE condo mandate closed §4 with *"**cc CARL** — this is a consumer-transmission channel ... and **I've flagged it to them too**."* **No such packet existed.** My only 7/31 CARL packet was the HPI-figures one; there were zero condo / 8-3 / warrantable hits anywhere in CARL's inbox. PROME routed the flag to CARL itself that evening, so the transmission leg is covered — **by PROME, not by me.**
**Why this is worse than a missing packet.** A gap gets noticed. **A false claim of delivery actively closes the question** — CORAL reads "CARL knows," so CORAL doesn't route it, and nobody looks again. It is the exact inversion of my own standing finding `finding_delivery_check_is_not_a_knowledge_check` (an inbox answers *"did it arrive?"*, never *"do they know?"*): here I skipped even the arrival question and asserted the knowledge question. The sentence cost nothing to write, which is precisely why it went unchecked — **it was a courtesy line, not a claim I thought I was making**, and courtesy lines get drafted in the same past tense as findings.
**Rule:** **Never write a delivery, routing, or notification in the past tense until the artifact exists.** Concretely: (a) if the packet isn't written yet, write the intention — *"routing to CARL next"* / *"CARL: not yet sent"* — never *"I've flagged it to them"*; (b) **write the cc packet before the sentence claiming it**, or drop the sentence; (c) at closeout, grep the recipient's inbox for every "sent/flagged/routed/cc'd" claim made that session — the check is one `ls`. **A cc line is a factual claim about the world, and it is load-bearing for the recipient's own routing decisions.**
**Tell:** any past-tense verb about another agent's awareness (*flagged, told, sent, cc'd, routed, looped in*) written in the same paragraph as analysis. Analysis and logistics get drafted in one flow; only the analysis gets verified.
**★ SECOND INSTANCE, FOUND THE SAME EVENING, SEVEN DAYS OLDER — and it shows the rule needs a structural clause, not just vigilance.** The PROME lane-query answer (written 2026-07-24 in response to PROME's 7/16 ratify-or-rewrite ask) was written **to my own `outbox/` and never delivered.** Zero hits anywhere under `AGENTS/PROME/`. My `STATUS.md` carried it for a week as *"answered 7/24 — still unconsumed,"* a phrasing that **blames the recipient for my omission** and closes the question twice over: it asserts I sent it AND explains the silence. Found only because Will asked "does anything need to go out?" **A directory named `outbox/` reads like a mailbox and functions like a drafts folder.**
**Structural rule (this is the durable one):** **`outbox/` is my drafting surface; the recipient's `inbox/` is the wire. A packet in `outbox/` is UNDELIVERED by definition, no matter how finished it looks.** Delivery = the file exists in the recipient's `inbox/` **and is committed**. ⚠️ **`orphan_check.sh` structurally cannot catch this** — the file sits inside `AGENTS/HOMER/`, so it is correctly committed and correctly silent. **The detector everyone relies on is blind to this exact failure**, which is why it survived seven days. At closeout: `ls` every recipient inbox named in a "sent" claim, and treat a non-empty `outbox/` as a **queue to drain, not an archive.**
**Corollary on late delivery:** send the **verbatim original** plus a dated note saying it is a *first* delivery, not a re-send (`finding_backstop_redelivery_is_a_paraphrase_not_a_copy`) — and flag any staleness for the recipient to apply, rather than silently rewriting the content to look current.
**Corollary — correct in place, don't quietly delete.** The packet was still unprocessed in CORAL's inbox, so the fix was a **dated correction note naming who actually told CARL**, not a silent rewrite. Provenance of *who informed whom* is itself the thing that was wrong; erasing the line would have destroyed the record of the error while leaving CORAL with no idea the routing came from PROME.

### [Calibration] — Honor a Pre-Registered Invalidation Trigger Even When Inconvenient
**Pattern (heritage, not a mistake):** CRL-03 (Fannie MF DQ breaches 0.80% GFC peak) carried a pre-registered invalidation rule: "MF DQ reverses below 0.65% for 2 consecutive months." ### [Verification] — I Read My Own Redaction Mask As Data, and Asked Will to Spend on a False Premise
**Mistake (2026-08-13, caught by PROME within the hour, retracted same session).** Diagnosing a FRED fetch failure I ran `grep -oE 'FRED[A-Z_]*=' FORGE/tools/market-data/.env`. It printed `FRED_API_KEY=`. I read that as *"the key is present but empty,"* wrote it into a report, and **asked Will to register a new API key.** The key was present and 32 characters long; the sanctioned `fetch.py fred` path worked the whole time.
**The mechanism, which is the reusable part:** my pattern **ends at the `=` and excludes the value by construction.** It prints **identically** whether the value is a 32-character secret or nothing at all. **I chose that pattern deliberately so a secret would not land in the transcript — the right instinct — and then treated its output as evidence about the thing it was designed to hide.**
⚠️ **Note the near-miss on the diagnosis too:** PROME's reconstruction was that I had read the repo-root `.env` (which genuinely has no FRED line). Plausible, and wrong — I had `cd`'d to the correct directory. **Had I accepted the offered explanation, the real mechanism would have gone unrecorded and the trap would still be live.** Accepting a correction is not the same as accepting its stated cause.
**Rule:** **A redacting or truncating command's output can never be evidence about the redacted value.** To test presence/absence without disclosure, measure a property that survives redaction — **length** (`awk -F= '/^KEY=/{print length($2)}'`), a checksum, or an exit code. Never infer a value from a pattern you wrote to exclude that value.
**Tell:** you are about to make a claim about *the content of X* using a command whose output you deliberately made *not contain X*.
**Second-order:** this reached an **ask directed at Will** — a spend request on a false premise. Any claim that triggers an external cost (register an account, buy data, escalate to another agent) deserves one confirming check by an independent method **before** it is sent, not after.

### [Process] — A Wrong-Reasoned RETRACTION Is As Dangerous As a Wrong Claim (SECOND INSTANCE, n=2)
**Mistake (2026-08-13).** Freddie's own monthly table showed Sep-2025 at 0.51%, tying Jun-2026 — which correctly kills my carried *"post-GFC high is defensible on the monthly series."* **Good finding. Then I over-reached:** I also wrote that the *"0.39% monthly prior peak is **contradicted by every month in the file** and is RETIRED."* **That reasoning is false.** `consumer_check` traced 0.39% to `BOARD/SIG-W-20260511-039`, where it is claimed as **Freddie's ~2010 Great-Recession peak, "already BREACHED."** **A file spanning Jun-2025→Jun-2026 cannot refute a 2010 value**, and current months sitting *above* 0.39% is exactly what *"already breached"* **predicts** — my evidence was **consistent with** the claim I said it contradicted.
**Why it repeats:** this is the **same failure as the 7/31 "FL condo −9.9% is unsourced" kill**, which also reached the right conclusion (retire the figure) via a false reason (it *did* have a dated source; the real defect was vintage + index type). **Both times I reached for the most sweeping available refutation instead of the narrowest one my data actually supported.** A retraction feels like rigor, so it does not get audited the way a claim does.
**Rule:** **A retraction is a claim and carries the same evidentiary burden.** Before publishing one, state (a) exactly which assertion is being withdrawn, (b) the evidence, and (c) **the span/scope that evidence covers** — then check (b) actually reaches (a). **If your data cannot reach the claim, the honest verdict is "unverified by me," never "refuted."**
**The specific trap:** *a recent-window series cannot refute a historical-peak claim.* Values above a stated prior peak **corroborate** "already breached."
**Tell:** the retraction is broader than the finding that prompted it. Here the sourced finding (Sep-2025 ties) was **sufficient on its own** — 0.39% never had to be wrong. **When the conclusion already stands, stop; the extra refutation is pure downside**, because the first challenger takes the correct conclusion down with the bad reasoning.

### [Tooling] — A Summarizing Fetch Silently Normalized Gaps Out of an Enumerated List
**Near-miss (2026-08-13, caught by pulling raw HTML).** Checking whether Fannie retired the Florida PERS requirement, a WebFetch of the Selling Guide chapter TOC returned the subsections as a tidy contiguous list (`-01…-05`) and reported that sections had been **renumbered**. On that basis I began retracting a reading that was in fact correct. **Raw `href`s told a different story:** the live sections are `-02, -03, -05, -06, -07` — **`-01` and `-04` are DELETED, leaving gaps.** The summarizer had rendered a gapped list as a sequential one.
**Why it mattered:** **the gaps were the entire finding.** `-01` was *Limited Review Process* and `-04` was *Geographic-Specific Condo Considerations* — their **absence** is the primary evidence that the policy changes are real and operative. A summary that closes the gaps destroys exactly the signal being looked for, and it does so *plausibly*.
**Rule:** **When the question is about structure — what exists, what is missing, ordering, numbering, counts — read the raw artifact, not a summary of it.** Summarizers optimize for a clean reading and will regularize irregularities. Reserve summarizing fetches for prose content; use `curl` + `grep` on links/IDs whenever **absence or sequence** is the evidence.
**Tell:** you are about to conclude something from a list's *shape* (contiguous, complete, ordered) that came out of a model rather than out of the document.

### [Process] — My Delivery-Verification Check Read a CONSUMED Packet as a LOST One
**False alarm (2026-08-13, closeout).** My own LESSONS rule says to grep the recipient's inbox for every "sent" claim. I implemented that as *"does `AGENTS/<X>/inbox/<file>` exist?"* — and it reported the WALTER packet **MISSING**, minutes after I had verified it on origin. **It was not missing: WALTER had consumed it**, and consumption `git mv`s the file to `inbox/processed/`.
**Why the check was wrong:** it tested a **location** when the thing being verified is an **event**. A packet's inbox path is occupied only in the window between delivery and consumption — **so the check's failure mode fires precisely on the SUCCESS case, and fires harder the more responsive the recipient is.** A fast, attentive counterparty looks identical to a lost packet.
**Rule:** verify delivery as **`inbox/<file>` OR `inbox/processed/<file>`, plus git-tracked.** Better still, when the path is absent from both, check `git log --diff-filter=RD -- <inbox path>` for an `R100` rename before concluding anything — **the rename IS the receipt, and it is stronger evidence than presence** (it proves someone read it, not merely that it arrived).
**Tell:** a verification that returns "missing" for an artifact you personally watched land. **Suspect the check before the world.**
**Generalizes:** any check keyed on a file sitting at a transient path will misreport the lifecycle completing. Ask whether the state you are testing is *terminal* or *in-flight*.

### [Process] — Session Output Follows the Files You Are Already Editing, and Skips the One That Is Only Ever *Read*

**Mistake (2026-08-22, found AFTER closeout — by PROME quoting my own finding back at me).** I corrected a false claim about ATTOM's publishing cadence and propagated it to `STATUS.md`, `workbook/PIPELINE.tsv`, `NEXUS_BRIEF.md` and **two packets to PROME**. **It never reached `docket/CATALYSTS.tsv` row 9 — the surface my own BOOT reads at step 5**, and the row that carried the original false verdict *and* an escalation condition built on it.
**Why the propagation stopped exactly there, which is the reusable part.** The correction was *discovered* while pulling data, so it flowed naturally into every surface that pull was already touching — dashboards, ledgers, the brief, outbound packets. **The docket was not part of that session's editing path.** It is a file I *read* at boot and *write* only when a catalyst changes. ⇒ **A correction propagates along the session's edit path, and a surface that is read-often / written-rarely is systematically off that path — which is precisely the profile of the most load-bearing files (dockets, calendars, threshold tables, boot checklists).**
**And it was worse than a stale row.** The uncorrected cell carried a **dated escalation condition derived from the false claim** — *"if still absent ~8/25, re-key to cadence BROKEN — investigate."* The correction showed ATTOM skips the monthly release in **3 of 6 months**, so that guard **would have fired on the publisher behaving completely normally** and manufactured the exact false miss the PAT-089 guard exists to prevent. **A stale row is documentation debt; a stale row containing a live trigger is a false-positive generator with a date on it.**
**Rule:** **A correction pass must ENUMERATE its surfaces before it starts, not follow the ones the session happens to be editing.** Write the list first — every file where the superseded claim could live — and tick them off. **Then grep for the superseded string across the whole agent directory** rather than trusting the enumeration (the 8/14 BANC "94%" residue was found exactly that way, after two fix passes aimed at it had missed it).
**Second clause, and it is the one with teeth: when the corrected claim had a TRIGGER, GUARD or DATE built on it, re-derive that trigger explicitly.** Fixing the fact does not fix the machinery downstream of the fact. **Ask: what fires because of what I just corrected?**
**Tell:** you corrected something and the propagation list happens to be exactly the files you already had open.
**⚠️⚠️ RE-SCOPED 2026-08-22, HOURS AFTER WRITING THIS ENTRY, BECAUSE IT FAILED TO FIRE ON ITS OWN FIRST OPPORTUNITY — IN THE SAME SESSION.** I graded the NAHB August HMI at issuer primary, wrote it to `STATUS.md` and `workbook/BUILDER.tsv`, and **left `docket/CATALYSTS.tsv` row 8 open and unresolved, still keyed `~2026-08-17` with notes ending at the July print.** Caught only when Will asked what remained open and I **enumerated the docket instead of reciting from memory.** ★ **The cause is a SCOPE ERROR IN THIS ENTRY, and that is the more useful finding than the third instance:** I wrote the rule about a **CORRECTION pass**. This was a **GRADE**. Same surface, same mechanism, same blind spot — and the rule as written did not cover it, so it could not fire. ⇒ **THE INVARIANT IS NOT "corrections." IT IS: ANY SESSION OUTPUT THAT A DOCKET ROW IS KEYED TO MUST CLOSE THAT ROW IN THE SAME PASS** — grades, resolutions, corrections, re-keys, kills, all of it. **A lesson scoped to the instance that produced it will miss its own siblings.** When writing one up, name the **mechanism**, not the **occasion** — the occasion is what you happen to be doing; the mechanism is what will recur. **Concrete closeout step this earns:** before committing, re-read the unresolved docket rows and ask of each one *"did this session produce its output?"* — the same enumeration that caught it here. It is one `awk` over the file.
**★★ SHARPENED 2026-08-22 EVENING BY PROME, AND THE REFRAMING IS WORTH MORE THAN THE INSTANCE COUNT — I HAD UNDER-CLAIMED MY OWN FINDING.** I wrote this up as *three cases of writing a decision in the wrong place.* PROME: *"They are three instances of **a decision whose fire-time reader is not the surface you were working on**."* **That is the better statement and I am adopting it, because "wrong place" implies a filing error I could fix by being tidier, and this is not a tidiness failure — it is that THE PERSON WHO NEEDS THE DECISION READS SOMEWHERE ELSE, LATER, UNDER DIFFERENT CONDITIONS.** ⇒ **The three instances, restated on the sharper axis:** the ATTOM correction's fire-time reader was **my own boot**, three days later, at docket step 5 · the NAHB grade's reader was **the same boot**, looking for what was still open · **and the $160B maturity-wall kill extends it hardest, because its fire-time reader was NOT ME AT ALL — it was REGINALD and CREED, nesting their CRE totals against a figure I had privately decided to retire.** **A dated kill living only in my SCRATCH is the exact condition under which a retirement goes unpublished** (`finding_retired_threshold_has_no_publisher`), and PROME registered it as a docket row with **the dispatch to those two desks as the deliverable, not the kill itself.** That framing is better than mine: **I would have retired a figure correctly and told nobody who was standing on it.** **⇒ THE OPERATIVE QUESTION IS NOT "did I write this everywhere it belongs?" — it is "WHO READS THIS AT FIRE TIME, AND DO THEY READ ANYTHING I TOUCHED TODAY?"** The second question is answerable in one line and catches the case the first one structurally cannot: **an external consumer whose surfaces I never edit at all.**
**Note the inversion worth banking.** Every prior instance of this class on my surfaces ran *working files → dashboard*, and I wrote it up as *"a correction lands on the working files and dies before it reaches the dashboard."* **This one ran the other way: it reached the dashboard and died before the docket.** ⇒ **The direction is not the invariant. The invariant is that correction stops at the boundary of the session's edit path**, whichever side the important file happens to be on.

### [Process] — I Wrote "Adopted" About My Own Surfaces Before Any Surface Carried It (SECOND INSTANCE, n=2)

**Mistake (2026-08-22, self-caught ~20 minutes after the packet was consumed and pushed).** PROME recommended a citation convention (ATTOM mid-year: publisher's site-post date wins over the wire date). I agreed, and wrote into my packet: *"**your rec adopted** — the publisher's own dated artifact wins, wire date recorded as alt."* **No surface of mine carried it.** `STATUS.md` still cited the wire date; the docket cell still recorded the two-date discrepancy as an open question. **I encoded it afterwards, after PROME had already consumed and pushed the packet.**
**Why this is the same failure as the 7/31 false "cc CARL," not a lesser cousin.** Both are **past-tense claims about the state of the world, written in the same flow as analysis, costing nothing to type.** The 7/31 case asserted an *outbound* artifact that did not exist; this one asserts an *inbound* change to my own files that had not happened. **The inbound direction is more insidious, because "adopted" reads as a decision rather than an action** — and a decision feels complete the moment it is made. **It is not: a convention nobody's file carries is not adopted, it is intended.**
**Consequence, which is the reason it matters:** the recommender **closes the loop.** PROME logged the rec as landed and moved on. Had I not caught it, the convention would have existed in exactly one place — a consumed packet in someone else's processed folder — while every citation I publish kept using the old basis, **with both desks believing it was settled.**
**Rule:** **"Adopted," "encoded," "applied," "re-keyed" and "now reads" are claims about ARTIFACTS and must not be written until the artifact carries the change.** Concretely: **make the edit, then write the sentence** — never the reverse, and never in the same breath as agreeing to the recommendation. If the edit is deferred, say so in the future tense and give it a date: *"encoding at next closeout,"* not *"adopted."*
**Tell:** a past-tense verb about your own files appearing in a packet in the same paragraph where you agree with someone. **Agreement and execution get drafted in one motion; only agreement is instantaneous.**
