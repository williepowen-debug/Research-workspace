## 4. ⛔ What does NOT verify, and one material omission

**(a) THE CONVERSION FIGURE IS WRONG AS STATED — and it is the one I most wanted.**
Transcript: *"lowering a buyer's rate by 1 percentage point costs the builder about **3.2% of the home sale price**."*
**AEI actually says: the average buydown discount is ~1.3 ppts, costing ~5% of the MORTGAGE AMOUNT.**
⇒ **Different denominator AND different rate** (~3.85%/ppt of *mortgage*, not 3.2% of *sale price*). On a 96.5%-LTV FHA loan those are close; on an 80%-LTV conventional they differ by a fifth. ⛔ **Do not adopt the transcript's number.**

**(b) The "≈10% price cut for the same monthly payment" claim** does not appear in the AEI article I verified. **Unverified — plausible arithmetic, no source located.**

**(c) ★ THE OMISSION THAT MATTERS MOST: this is a CONTESTED policy argument presented as settled fact.**
The video gives only AEI's side. **A published rebuttal exists — HousingWire / The Builder's Daily, 2025-12-09, John McManus — arguing buydowns bolster ACCESS rather than inflate prices, and that deep price cuts carry their own negative-equity harm.** It explicitly frames the AEI analysis, amplified by a WSJ piece, as having been turned into *a scandalous narrative*.
⇒ **AEI is an advocacy think tank making a policy case for a rule change. That does not make it wrong — its data checks out — but "regulators are already comparing it to 2008" is a characterisation of an argument, not a finding.** ⚠️ The Benesch memo is a **law firm marketing to builder/lender clients about enforcement risk**; incentive-flag it (`finding_incentive_flag_source_weighting`).

**(d) AEI's own headline stat is missing from the video: "over 40% of sales by large builders have concessions + permanent buydown cost in excess of 6%."** The video cites the narrower GSE/FHA split instead.
