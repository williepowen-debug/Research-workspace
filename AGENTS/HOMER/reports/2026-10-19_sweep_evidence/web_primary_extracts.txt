L160: # Housing Market Update: Asking Prices Fall For the First Time Since March
L161: 
L162: Published on October 15th, 2026 by cite52†Dana Anderson L163: 
L164: Pending home sales inched up this week, but they’re still near record lows.
L165: 
L166: What to know about the U.S. housing market for the four weeks ending October 11:
L167:   *     * Home prices are losing momentum. The median U.S. home-sale price rose just 0.5% year over year, the slowest growth rate since March. The median asking price fell 0.3% year over year, its first decline since March. Some relief could be on the horizon for buyers.
L168:     * Mortgage rates are still a vital stat for homebuyers and sellers. The weekly average mortgage rate hit a 3-year high of cite53†7.4%†www.freddiemac.com on October 8. Daily average rates have eased slightly in recent days, dipping to cite54†7.45%†www.mortgagenewsdaily.com on October 14 from 7.61% about a week earlier.
L169:     * The typical homebuyer’s monthly payment is $2,777, the highest level on record except the previous week. That’s due to high mortgage rates and still-increasing home prices.
L170:     * Homebuying demand remains near record low. Pending home sales ticked up 1.5% from a week earlier–perhaps thanks to rates easing ever-so-slightly–but were still near their lowest level in records dating back to 2021. That includes the end of 2023, when mortgage rates were nearing 8%. Pending sales declined year over year in all 50 of the most populous U.S. metro areas.
L171:     * Price drops are at a record high. As sellers come to terms with prices losing steam, more of them are dropping their prices: 22% of home sellers with active listings cut their asking price, up from 20.9% a year earlier and the highest level in our records.
L172:     * Housing supply is growing, but slowly. New listings of homes for sale inched up 0.4% week over week, while the total number of homes for sale rose 0.8%.

L19: # Housing demand finds a new home in rentals, up 19.2% (September Rent Report)
L20: 
L21: As elevated borrowing costs push up a buyer’s monthly payment, more shoppers are choosing to rent.
L22: 
L23: cite9†Image: Housing demand finds a new home in rentals, up 19.2% (September Rent Report)†www.zillowstatic.com L24: 
L25: cite10†Image: Kara Ng†www.zillowstatic.com L26: 
L27: Written by Kara Ng on October 15, 2026
L28:   * Rental demand is up 19.2% year over year nationwide, as measured by the Zillow Observed Renter Demand Index (ZORDI).^{cite11†1 }
L29:   * The typical U.S. rent was $1,932 a month in September, up 2.7% from a year ago.
L30:   * The financial advantage of renting is growing, as the typical new home buyer’s monthly payment^{cite12†2 } is climbing nearly twice as fast as rent, up 5% from a year ago to $3,037.
L31: Housing demand doesn’t disappear when buying becomes harder financially. Instead, it finds a new home in the rental market. The Zillow Observed Renter Demand Index (ZORDI), an indicator of rental demand, rose 19.2% year over year as elevated borrowing costs push would-be buyers to rent instead.
L32: Rents are climbing, but buying costs are climbing faster. Typical rent is $1,932 a month, up 2.7% from a year ago. By comparison, the typical new home buyer’s monthly payment has risen 5%, to $3,037, nearly double the pace.
L33: 
L34: A buyer’s monthly payment is climbing nearly twice as fast as rent, and for some shoppers, that gap is the start of a different plan. These deliberate renters keep their housing costs lower, put the difference toward saving and investing, and build wealth without a mortgage.
L35: And for many home shoppers, what they still want is a house. ZORDI shows demand for single-family rentals up 21.6% from a year ago, more than double the 10.1% rise for multifamily rentals. At a typical rent of $2,266, renting a house offers the space and lifestyle shoppers want without a down payment or the hidden costs of owning, and it costs $771 less each month than a buyer’s monthly payment.
L36: For shoppers weighing renting against buying, Zillow’s cite13†rent vs. buy research breaks down the break-even timeline by metro, that timeline being the number of years a household would need to own before buying comes out ahead.
L37: 
L38: Rental demand surges in the Bay Area and the Sun Belt
L39: 
L40: Rental demand rose the most in San Jose, where ZORDI is up 82.5% from a year ago, followed by San Francisco (66.9%), Austin (49.3%), Miami (48.9%) and Phoenix (43%).
L41: Rent growth over the past year was also fastest in California’s Bay Area, where typical rent is up 11.8% in San Francisco and 8.3% in San Jose. In the other markets where demand has jumped the most, rent growth was more moderate, at 0.7% in Austin, 1.8% in Miami and 1% in Phoenix, as new construction has kept supply in step with demand.
L42: Not every market is following the national trend. ZORDI is down year over year in markets that include Louisville (down 6.2%), Baltimore (-6.1%), Washington, D.C. (-5.6%) and Cleveland (-5.5%).
L43: 
L44: Affordability holds steady for renters
L45: The median household spends 26.3% of its income on a new rental, essentially unchanged from a year ago, compared with 41.3% spent by a new homeowner. A household needs an income of $77,278 to afford a typical rental versus $121,496 to afford a typical home, a gap of more than $44,000.
