The tally covers 46 properties, including 18 loans carried over from earlier foreclosure postings, according to Roddy’s Foreclosure Listing Service data reported by The Real Deal. Bexar County, home to San Antonio, accounted for 13 properties—the largest number among the counties covered. The October postings include apartment complexes, office buildings, hotels and other commercial properties across the state’s major metropolitan markets. The figures represent loans flagged for foreclosure, not completed sales or confirmed lender losses. Some borrowers and lenders could reach agreements that prevent auctions, while repeated postings can reflect ongoing negotiations or litigation. Major Loans Facing Foreclosure Two of the largest loans highlighted in the October report involve a Dallas office tower and a Houston apartment complex, illustrating that the distress extends across both office and multifamily properties. The competing circumstances underscore the importance of distinguishing alleged defaults and disputed enforcement actions from completed foreclosures. Repeat Postings Complicate the Picture Eighteen of October’s distressed loans had been flagged for foreclosure sales more than once. That means the monthly total includes previously identified troubled debt, rather than representing more than $1 billion in newly distressed loans. Among the larger repeat postings were an $85.2 million loan tied to The Richmond Apartments in Dallas, a $60.5 million loan on Highland Ridge Apartments in San Antonio, a $43.5 million loan on a San Antonio Embassy Suites hotel and a $40.4 million loan on an office building at 1900 West Loop South in Houston. The repeat-posting list also included apartment properties in Arlington and Fort Worth, hotels in Flower Mound and San Antonio, a Denton car wash and land in McKinney. The range of collateral shows that October’s foreclosure pipeline was not limited to a single property category. Credit and Recovery Implications For credit and collections professionals, the distinction between a foreclosure posting and a completed recovery is central to interpreting the report. October’s figures measure the debt associated with properties flagged for auction; they do not establish how much lenders ultimately will recover or whether every scheduled sale will proceed. The combination of large individual loans and repeated postings highlights both the scale of the troubled debt and the unresolved status of many cases. With negotiations and litigation potentially delaying or preventing sales, the October total is best understood as a measure of the foreclosure pipeline—not a tally of realized credit losses.
