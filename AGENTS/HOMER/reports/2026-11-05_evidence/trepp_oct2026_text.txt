The Trepp Commercial Mortgage-Backed
Securities (CMBS) Delinquency Rate decreased
by 15 basis points to 7.87% in October 2026.

Delinquency rates increased for three of the five major property types and decreased for

two. The office delinquency rate rose 17 basis points to 12.33%, retail rose 63 basis points

to  7.21%,  and  multifamily  rose  five  basis  points  to  8.09%.  The  lodging  rate  fell  24  basis

points to 5.94%, and industrial fell four basis points to 1.10%.

The improvement of the overall rate last month was driven by the reversion to the current

status of the $975 million loan in BX 2019-IMC. The loan, against a portfolio of showroom

properties  with  12.2  million  square  feet  in  High  Point,  N.C.,  and  Las  Vegas,  matured  in

2024 with a balance of $1.15 billion. It was extended and paid down to its current balance

The 10 largest newly delinquent loans, totaling $1.87 billion, are 67% office by balance. The

largest was the $384.29 million loan, securitized through NCMS 2019-MILE, against the

1.04 million-square-foot Wilshire Courtyard in Los Angeles, which matured in July. Cash

flow at the property falls far below the level needed for a full refinancing.

Also among the largest delinquencies was the $210 million mortgage against the 283-unit

Cove at Tiburon apartment property in Tiburon, Calif. The loan, in SGCMS 2020-COVE

and BBCMS 2020-C7, matured in March 2025 and was in and out of delinquency until it

was modified in November 2025, extending its term through October 2026. A request to

exercise a further six-month extension option is under review, which could mean the loan

reverts to performing status in the coming weeks.

Including performing matured balloon loans, the rate would register 9.56%, down 10 basis

points from September. The seriously delinquent rate decreased 16 basis points to 7.71%.

The  figures  assume  defeased  loans  are  included  in  the  denominator  unless  otherwise

and now matures in April 2027.

The  BX  2019-IMC  loan  was  among  the  $3.31  billion  of  previously  delinquent  loans  that

Monica, Calif. The floating-rate loan, which matured in July, was reportedly purchased by

the holder of a $120 million mezzanine loan against the two properties.

While  $4.12  billion  of  previously  delinquent  loans  were  either  cured  or  paid  off,  $3.47

billion of loans became delinquent.

WHAT IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CMBS 1.0 & CMBS 2.0+?CMBS 1.0 refers to CMBS issued prior to the 2008 financial crisis, characterizedby higher loan-to-value ratios and lower debt service coverage ratios. CMBS2.0+ refers to the second generation of CMBS issued after the financial crisis,incorporating reforms to restore investor confidence. These include enhancedunderwriting standards, increased disclosure requirements, improved loanservicing practices, and stronger risk retention rules in compliance with Dodd-Frank regulations. Looking for more Trepp content?
