## 0. THE ANSWER FIRST

**I am NOT proposing non-funding debt/equity as a firing class, and I am not proposing a threshold for it.** Two independent findings kill the naive version, and I found the second only by checking whether the panel can even produce the input.

Instead I propose it as an **explicitly non-firing PRECURSOR**, using a category the spec already contains. **That is ratifiable today and needs no number.**

---

## 1. What was observed, and it is real

**UWM Holdings, three consecutive quarters:**

| | Q4-2025 | Q1-2026 | Q2-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-funding debt / equity** | **1.90×** | **3.18×** | **6.13×** |
| Equity | $1,748M | $1,601M | $985.3M |
| Common dividend | $0.10 | $0.10 | **$0.10 → SUSPENDED 8/05** |

**The dividend sat unchanged at $0.10 for 22 consecutive quarters and was suspended only in the last one.** Non-funding leverage had already tripled. The terminated **Two Harbors** all-stock deal (announced 2025-12-17, terminated Q1-2026) sits inside that window and reads in hindsight as a rejected first attempt to repair the balance sheet with stock.

**On its face: leverage led, the payout lagged by two quarters.** That is what I reported on 8/14 and it is what Will authorized me to develop.

---

## 2. ★★ FIRST FINDING — "leverage led the dividend" is probably a property of the two instruments, not a discovery about credit

**A dividend is a discrete, sticky, board-controlled decision made once a quarter. A balance-sheet ratio is a continuous, mechanical accounting output that moves every day.** Dividends are famously sticky by construction — boards defend them precisely because cutting one is a signal.

⇒ **Of course leverage moved first.** It would move first at a healthy company having a bad year, and at a company that never gets into trouble at all. **"Leverage leads the dividend" may be true of every distress episode ever recorded, and therefore carries no information about WHICH name is distressed.**

**With n=1 I cannot distinguish "leverage is a leading indicator of distress" from "leverage responds continuously and dividends respond discretely."** The second explanation requires no credit story at all and predicts the same observation.

⇒ **The useful question was never "does leverage lead?" It is "does some LEVEL of leverage discriminate distressed names from healthy ones?"** That is a question about a panel, and it is the question I went to answer.

★ **This is the Class E price-marker lesson running forward instead of backward.** E's price marker fired 0 of 12 because nobody asked, before adopting it, whether the screen could discriminate within this cohort. **I am asking it first this time.**

## 3. ★★★ SECOND FINDING — the panel cannot produce the input, so a cross-name threshold is NOT CONSTRUCTIBLE

I checked whether "non-funding debt" is a reported line for the watch panel, at primary filings:

| Name | Reports "non-funding debt"? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **UWMC** | ✅ Yes | Q2-2026 disclosure — the source of the 1.90/3.18/6.13 series |
| **LDI (loanDepot)** | ✅ Yes | FY2025 10-K, 3 occurrences with figures (175,213 / 188,550 / 174,103) |
| **PFSI** | ⛔ **No** | FY2025 10-K — zero occurrences |
| **RITM (Rithm)** | ⛔ **No** | FY2025 10-K — zero occurrences |
| **COOP (Mr. Cooper)** | ⛔ **No** | FY2024 10-K — zero occurrences *(and COOP no longer trades — Rocket closed the acquisition 2025-10-01)* |

**Only 2 of 5 names report it.** And **LDI's own 10-K states the concept is issuer-defined**: *"non-funding debt is a function of our capital structure and is therefore excluded from Adjusted EBITDA."* **Each issuer draws its own line between funding and non-funding debt.**

⇒ **To build a panel series I would have to DERIVE the metric for the three non-reporting names — i.e. decide myself what counts as funding debt.** A metric whose definition I choose is **not comparable** to one the issuer reports (`finding_cross_entity_comparison_needs_same_perimeter`). **I would be manufacturing the cross-name comparability that the threshold depends on, and then base-rating my own construction.**

★★ **This is the SAME failure mode as Class E's price marker, one step earlier in the pipeline.** E screened on something this cohort's financings **don't do** (0 of 12 priced below VWAP; UWM's preferred was issued at par with warrants struck *above* market). A non-funding-leverage class would screen on something **3 of 5 names don't report.**

⇒ **The transferable rule, and it is the most valuable thing in this document: before proposing a class, verify that every name in the panel PRODUCES the input — not merely that the input is meaningful.** That is a **coverage test that precedes base-rating**, and **neither the original A–G spec nor my own 8/14 base-rating ran it.** Had it been run on Class E in July, the price marker would never have been written.

## 4. ⚠️ And today's rung census supplies a third caution

Even with a panel, I could not responsibly set a level without asking whether it would be **pinned**. Today's census found **9 of my 21 existing threshold rungs have never discriminated in any window I hold** — because levels get set from a single dramatic observation and then never tested for whether they can fail to fire. **UWMC's 6.13× is exactly the kind of dramatic single observation that produces a pinned rung.** If the other names routinely run at 3–4×, a threshold anchored on 6.13× fires never; if they run at 1–2×, it fires always for anyone leveraged. **Unknown, and unknowable without the panel I just showed cannot be built from primaries.**

## 5. ✅ WHAT I PROPOSE INSTEAD — a NO-VERDICT PRECURSOR, using a category the spec already has

**The spec already contains exactly this shape:** the ratified no-verdict band — ***"equity drawdown alone NEVER fires, at any magnitude."*** That rule exists because equity drawdown is informative to watch and useless to fire on. **Non-funding leverage is the same object.**

**PROPOSED — Class-less precursor, no letter, no threshold:**

> **NON-FUNDING LEVERAGE IS A NO-VERDICT PRECURSOR.** For any watched name **that reports non-funding debt as an issuer-defined line** (currently **UWMC and LDI only**), a **material rise in non-funding debt/equity across two or more consecutive quarters** raises watch intensity and obliges a look at the same filing's capital actions and dividend policy. **It NEVER fires a class, at any magnitude, on its own.** It is recorded with **the issuer's own figure and the issuer's own definition**, never a HOMER-derived one, and **never compared across names that define it differently.**

**Why this is the right shape:**
- **It needs no number**, so it cannot be a pinned rung or a manufactured base rate.
- **It respects the coverage finding** — scoped to names that actually report the line.
- **It preserves the 7/31 discipline** that made this watch honest: a continuous market/balance-sheet variable informs, a discrete credit event fires.
- **It is ratifiable today** rather than after a research project that the primaries cannot support.

⛔ **What it explicitly does NOT do:** it does not fire, it does not score, it does not create a letter class, and **it is not applied retroactively to UWM 2026-08-06.**

---

## 6. What would be needed to make it a real class (so this is not re-litigated)

1. **A comparable panel series** — which requires either (a) more issuers adopting the disclosure, or (b) a HOMER-derived definition applied uniformly, **explicitly labelled as HOMER-constructed with no issuer authority**, and base-rated as such.
2. **A discrimination test on that panel** — does any level separate the distressed from the healthy, and is that level non-pinned across the observed range?
3. **Only then** a proposed threshold, with its standing recorded separately (well-separated / one-observation / untested), per the 8/22 ruling's own convention.

**None of these is close. I am not proposing to do them now** — the HOM-01 resolver (~8/31) owns the calendar, and this instrument has no dated consequence.
