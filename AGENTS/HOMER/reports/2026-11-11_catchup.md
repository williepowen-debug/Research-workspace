## What changed in my read (plain words)

1. **Mortgage rates turned back up after three weeks of easing.** The 10-year Treasury went from 4.84% (11/05) to 4.98% (11/10) after October core CPI rose 0.3%, and the 2-year rose faster (4.46% to 4.73%). Mortgage News Daily's daily 30-year rate jumped from 7.18% to 7.30% on 11/10. Freddie's weekly rate (7.18% on 11/05) is next printed Thursday 11/12 and will probably stop falling (inferred from the daily series, not a registered forecast). My 30-year band was already past its top rung (Red, above 7.0%), so nothing re-grades; what changes is that my rate-amplifier leg is further from its kill line (6.50% for four weeks).
2. **Multifamily special servicing hit a new 2026 high: 9.22% in October, +69bp** (Trepp, 11/09). The line I set in advance was 9.08%. Unlike October's 8.09% delinquency print, which one loan probably explained, this rise comes through loans hitting maturity: Trepp names the $517.7M Blackjack portfolio, which reached its fully extended maturity on 10/09. The gap between special servicing and delinquency widened from 50bp to 113bp, so more loans are in workout than show as late. That leans against my own HOM-05 call (November delinquency below 8.09%, 55%); I record the tension and do not re-weight a registered call.
3. **Single-family credit kept worsening slowly.** NY Fed Q3: 1.13% of mortgage balances 90+ days late, against 0.83% a year earlier. 61,900 people got a new foreclosure on their credit report (+13% YoY). Early-stage cures got worse: 37.3% of 30–60-day-late mortgages returned to current, against 39.2% a year earlier. ICE: FHA cures fell 46% YoY in September (August −37%). ICE again did not print the all-loans cure figure my band grades on, so that band is ungraded for a second month.
4. **Nevada mortgage stress doubled:** 90+ day share 1.98% in Q3 against 0.99% a year earlier (NY Fed). Las Vegas sales are down 11.9% YoY with almost six months of supply; the median price is flat.
5. **loanDepot is shrinking to fund itself:** Q3 net loss $28.7M; 2.0% of its servicing book 60+ days late (1.5% a year earlier); about $19B of servicing rights sold since July; refinancing upcoming debt. That is liquidity management, not a liquidity event.
