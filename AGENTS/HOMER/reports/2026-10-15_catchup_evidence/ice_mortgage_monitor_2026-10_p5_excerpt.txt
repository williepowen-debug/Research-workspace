New defaults — the share of active mortgages that became 90 or more days past due — were down 3% annually in 
August, with the national default rate improving from year-ago levels in five of the past six months.

The cure rate among seriously delinquent mortgages, which has been the primary driver of rising serious delinquencies 
over the past 12 months, eased slightly to 7.8% in August from July's nine-month high of 8.0%.

New defaults among Federal Housing Administration (FHA) loans continue to improve, falling 15% from the same time 
last year and averaging a 15% annual decline over the past three months.

FHA cures from serious delinquency, however, slipped to 7.9% from July's 8.2% and remain 37% below the level of last 
August as the market continues to search for its new norm.

New defaults among Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) and government-sponsored enterprise (GSE) mortgages 
continue to trend higher than year-ago levels and continue to bear watching. VA defaults were up 23% annually in 
August, and GSE defaults were up 18% — the largest annual increase among GSE loans in 19 months.

VA remains the weakest performer on the cure side, with just 5.9% of seriously delinquent VA loans curing in August — 
the lowest of the three product types and roughly half the GSE rate of 10.4%.
