## Summary

Pressure language, thinking scaffolds, numeric caps and anti-formatting rules are absent across all 55 files scanned (max 0.4 caps-words/KB, zero scaffold hits). The fleet's instruction files are not written for older models. What they carry instead is **history**: the Group 2 pattern (rule + verbatim ruling quote + timestamp to the minute + record path + incident origin) is the house style, and it accounts for about a quarter of PROME's auto-injected charter.

Three findings matter most:

1. **`PROME/CLAUDE.md` is 34,340 B auto-injected into every PROME session, and 5,326 B of it is line 2** — a changelog of 22 "Prior:" entries, including a count of how many times the changelog itself was mis-stamped. Root `CLAUDE.md:3` already sets the repo's own convention ("exact amendment history is `git log -p`"). The body carries another 38 provenance parentheticals (3,933 B). The patch brings the file to 26,503 B with every rule, reason and record pointer intact (−7,837 B, −23%).
2. **Two runners carry live instructions marked "do not rely on this"** — `coldread/SKILL.md:9` (steps 2, 4, 5 WITHHELD since 2026-09-26) and `closeout/SKILL.md:17` (a step-8 clause ⛔ NOT VERIFIED). An instruction file that contradicts itself is the one Group 2 class the audit flags rather than patches: Will decides whether to repair the runners or point those steps at the governing bullet.
3. **The reconcile vintage is restated where root canon says it never is.** Root `CLAUDE.md:28`: "reconcile vintage lives in its own header, never restated elsewhere." `.claude/agents/anvil.md:30` restates it, and `reconcile/SKILL.md` step 9 instructs updating that restatement every run. History cannot order the two here, so it is flagged with the obvious direction (root wins by the fleet's own rule).

Also notable: a Fable-era fossil set in PROME's charter describing things that no longer exist (OpenClaw, `SOUL.md`, a retired manual, "no separate surface"), one literally duplicated sentence in a Process Controls bullet, and the fact that the fleet's read-cap instrument does not measure the charter files themselves while 20 of 42 desk charters exceed the 32,550 B budget it enforces on everything else.
