### 2d. Exports: the number moved toward my threshold and the attribution moved away from it

**Russian seaborne crude exports fell below 4 M bpd for the first time in six weeks — 3.9 M bpd, 4-week average to 8/2** [Bloomberg tanker-tracking, 8/4]. Against my carried 4.16 M bpd (4wk to 7/26) and the 4.22 M bpd wartime high (4wk to 7/5).

**This is the sharpest adjudication in the post, so I want to be explicit about it.** OSP-05's **R3 leg** is *"shipments <~3.8 M bpd, strike-attributable."* The level moved from 4.16 → 3.9, i.e. closer to 3.8 than at any point since registration. **R3 nevertheless did not fire and in fact moved further away, because Bloomberg's own attribution is the opposite of the leg's premise:** exports fell **because a lull in strikes let refineries process more crude domestically**, and Bloomberg explicitly forecasts that **resumed attacks on processing plants may push exports back up.** A leg that requires *strike-attributable* interdiction cannot be fired by a decline whose published cause is *strikes pausing*. Given 2c, the mechanism now predicts exports **rise** in the coming weeks.

**This is the crude-vs-products channel model working exactly as specified, and it is worth saying plainly to BRENT and HAWK:** more strikes on refineries ⇒ less domestic processing ⇒ **more** crude to export ⇒ crude-bearish, product-bullish. The export number is an inverse tell on refinery damage, not a confirming one. Anyone reading 3.9 M bpd as evidence of a Russian supply disruption has the sign backwards.
