## 2. My one-mechanism hypothesis, tested against FALCON's timeline — **partly refuted, and the replacement is better**

I pre-registered this with PROME before reading FALCON, so it can be graded rather than retrofitted:

> *"The 8/8 CPC carve-out is partly a downstream consequence of Gulf escalation — Washington intervened in my theater's maritime lane because oil prices were elevated, and per my own attribution guard that elevation is FALCON's theater. The wars are coupled through US policy response rather than merely divergent in direction."*

**What survives.** The price channel is real and documented: Bloomberg's account of the 8/8 understanding names *"a rise in global oil prices"* alongside disrupted Chevron operations as what prompted Washington to act. And my standing theater-attribution guard does say the elevated tape is FALCON's, not mine — Brent's $100 print (7/23) was Red Sea/Hormuz, and HAWK's own tape read this morning ($84.91, +3.2% off 8/7) is being driven by the Sunday **Gulf** escalation, not by anything in the Black Sea.

**What refutes it.** PROME asked the right question — *is Washington managing price via OSPREY's lane and security via FALCON's?* — and the answer is **no, and the price/security framing is the wrong axis.** Two things break it:

- **A simpler driver was available and I under-weighted it.** CPC's shareholders are **Chevron, ExxonMobil and Shell**. Chevron's CEO was discussing CPC on a 7/31 earnings call. Tengizchevroil is 50% Chevron. Washington did not need an oil-price motive to protect CPC — it had a **direct US-corporate-asset** motive, which is sufficient on its own and requires no Gulf channel at all. My hypothesis was not wrong so much as **unnecessary**, and an unnecessary mechanism that fits is the most dangerous kind.
- **FALCON's theater shows the US pursuing the same objective with a completely different instrument.** Protecting commercial shipping in the Red Sea is being done through a **14-nation coalition with a Riyadh HQ, a named commander, and an 8/12–13 planning meeting** — slow, expensive, institutional. Protecting CPC was done with **a phone call to Kyiv** — fast, free, reversible. Same goal, opposite instruments.

### 2a. The replacement mechanism: **the instrument is chosen by leverage over the ATTACKER, not by what is being protected**

> **The US has direct leverage over the attacker in my theater and none in FALCON's. Ukraine is a dependent client that can be asked to stop; Iran and the Houthis are adversaries that cannot. So in the Black Sea, Washington buys protection with diplomacy — instant, cheap, and leaving nothing behind. In the Gulf, the only available instruments are security instruments — slow, costly, and institution-building by construction.**

This is a strictly better hypothesis than my pre-registered one: it explains the *choice of instrument* in both theaters from a single asymmetry, where mine only explained why the US acted in one.

### 2b. And it derives HAWK's asymmetry rather than merely agreeing with it

HAWK §4 records, as an observed regularity, that *"the Gulf is institutionalising its chokepoint into a permanent tolled cost while the Black Sea is not — a Gulf de-escalation does not fully unwind, a Black Sea one does."* **I think the leverage asymmetry is the cause of that.** A client-state restraint leaves **no institution, no toll, no standing force** — there is nothing for the risk to be priced into. A coalition builds standing infrastructure that outlives the threat, and a toll once established has a constituency. **HAWK's asymmetry is downstream of the leverage asymmetry, and the two findings should be carried as one.**

### 2c. The uncomfortable corollary, which cuts against my own theater's good news

**A restraint that costs nothing to grant costs nothing to revoke.** The 8/8 understanding rests entirely on continued US leverage over Kyiv and on Kyiv's continued willingness to trade a working weapon for goodwill. **No physical fact changed on 8/8** — the drones exist, the crews exist, the terminal is in range, and Ukraine has publicly confirmed nothing. This de-escalation can evaporate in a day and leave no trace that it ever happened.

> **So the asymmetry runs deeper than HAWK's version: my theater's de-escalation is more fragile than FALCON's escalation is durable.** The Gulf is accreting permanent structure — a coalition, a toll, a demand list, an SNSC that has hardened through three secretaries. The Black Sea just acquired a promise. **For a Will-facing read: the Gulf's risk is becoming a cost; the Black Sea's remains an event.** Costs get priced. Events get re-priced, repeatedly, from zero.

**Falsifier, so this is gradeable and not just a story:** if the CPC understanding survives to ~9/8 (one month) **and** produces any written or institutional artifact — a published Ukrainian commitment, a notification mechanism with standing, a monitoring role for a third party — then I am wrong that it is structureless, and the two theaters are converging on institutionalisation rather than diverging. **The observable is a document or a standing mechanism, not a quiet absence of strikes.**
