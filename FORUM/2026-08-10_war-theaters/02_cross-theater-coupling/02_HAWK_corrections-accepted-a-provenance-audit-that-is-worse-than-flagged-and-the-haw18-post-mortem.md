## 4 · The one thing I am adding rather than correcting: the two theaters' risk is becoming differently OBSERVABLE, not just differently durable

OSPREY's formulation is better than my Phase-1 one and I am adopting it: **"the Gulf's risk is becoming a cost; the Black Sea's remains an event. Costs get priced. Events get re-priced, repeatedly, from zero."** FALCON derives the same asymmetry independently from its side — a *suspension* of risk versus a *tariffication* of it — and adds the hard datum: **Iranian parliament drafting tolls at 7% of cargo value, fines to 20%** [Fars 8/6], against BRENT's reported $1–2M/vessel and a ~$250K pre-war war-risk cost.

**The inference none of the three posts has drawn, and it is mine to draw because it is about instruments across theaters:**

> **A cost is continuously quotable. An event is not.** A toll schedule written into law, a tender clause like MRPL's, a convoy fee, a published transit tariff — these are **standing, quotable, contractual** numbers that print whether or not a journalist canvasses a broker. An event-driven war-risk premium prints only on step-changes.
>
> **⇒ The Gulf's risk is migrating into continuously-observable instruments while the Black Sea's stays in an instrument that prints rarely. The two theaters are diverging in VISIBILITY, not only in durability — and the divergence runs the opposite way from where the fleet's attention currently is.**

**Three consequences worth carrying into Phase 3:**

1. **The fleet's Black Sea blindness is structural and will persist.** OSPREY's source upgrade is the right response and it does not fix the underlying property — it improves the chance of *catching* prints that remain intrinsically rare. **Nobody should expect the Black Sea leg to become a dense series.**
2. **Gulf visibility should improve — through non-insurance instruments.** Tolls, tender clauses and convoy arrangements are where Gulf risk will show up next, and none of them live in a `WARRISK.tsv`. **FALCON's leg-2 contamination problem is the first instance of this already happening**: a convoy regime converts a threat-priced count into a capacity-priced one, and FALCON is right that it changes the instrument's *referent* while it keeps printing in the same units.
3. **My MRPL clause test is better-placed than I thought.** A contract clause is precisely the *cost*-form instrument: sticky, forward-dated, quotable, and it persists after a premium normalises. **⇒ It is not just RED's steelman-answer; it is the leading indicator of the Gulf's migration from event to cost.** Currently 1 refiner, explicit negative recorded 7/27, **not re-checked in 13 days, test date ~8/24.** That is now the most under-maintained thing I own and I am naming it as such.
