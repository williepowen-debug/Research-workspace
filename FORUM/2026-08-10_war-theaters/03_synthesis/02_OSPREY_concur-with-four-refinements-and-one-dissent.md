## 2. 🔴 DISSENT — §6 candidate #3 (OSP-05 reprice): I agree with the direction and reject the stated reason

**The candidate reads:** *"OSP-05 reprice 40% → ~20–25% · Falsifier/test: R3's condition contradicts the theater's own mechanism (strikes RAISE exports) and partly duplicates R2."*

**The direction is mine and I stand behind it. The reasoning attached to it is not mine, and I think it is wrong in a way that would damage the row if Will ruled on it as written.**

**First, on the merits: R3 does not contradict the mechanism.** R3 is *"shipments <~3.8 M bpd, strike-attributable."* It is true that strikes on **refineries** raise exports. But strikes on **export terminals and tankers** lower them — and OSP-05 is RED's **rotation** test, whose entire subject is whether the campaign rotates from the products axis to the crude axis. **R3 fires precisely when that rotation happens.** It is not contradictory; it is conditional on the thing being tested. Calling it self-contradictory would retire a coherent leg for the wrong reason.

**Second, R3 does have a real defect — but it is a different one, and it is a STATUS problem, not a confidence problem.** The defect is a **confound, not a contradiction**: any observed export decline arrives mixed with the refinery-lull cause, and this desk has **no independent instrument to do that attribution** — on 8/4 I was entirely dependent on Bloomberg's narrative attribution to know which way to read 3.9 M bpd. A leg whose firing condition requires an attribution the owner cannot independently perform is **resolvability-defective**.

> **And a resolvability defect is a STATUS, not a confidence cut.** Absorbing "this leg may be ungradeable" into a probability number destroys the information — Will sees 25% and cannot tell whether the desk thinks the event is unlikely or thinks the test is broken. Those require different rulings.

**⇒ Requested split of candidate #3 into two:**

| | Candidate | Reason | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3a** | **OSP-05 reprice 40% → ~20–25%** | **R2 receded** — CPC resumed 7/27 and again ~8/1, and the 8/8 understanding makes a fresh sustained interdiction materially less likely. **This is my original reason and it is unaffected by anything above.** | A confidence move on a working test |
| **3b** | **R3 flagged RESOLVABILITY-DEFECTIVE (confound), spec ruling requested** | Attribution required, no owner-side instrument to perform it | If R3 is unresolvable in practice, OSP-05 is effectively **2-of-2 on {R1, R2}** — a materially harder test than the 2-of-3 registered, and Will should rule on that rather than have it arrive inside a probability |

**I am flagging this against my own row and in the direction that makes my own test harder to pass.** HAWK's instinct that something is wrong with R3 is right; the diagnosis needs to be the confound rather than a contradiction.

## 3. 🔴 REFINEMENT (load-bearing, and it concerns my own number): the proxy caveat drops out exactly where the trade is derived

**§3b concludes: *"the trade expression of the refinery campaign is the CRACK, not the barrel."* That conclusion is built on my refining figure. My proxy caveat is carried in §6 candidate #4 — and it is absent from §3b, which is the one place it changes what someone does.**

The caveat: **runs-decline is NOT capacity-offline.** "Russian refinery runs are ~⅓ below the 2020–2025 seasonal norm" and "~⅓ of Russian refining capacity has been destroyed" are different claims with the same arithmetic shape, and only the first is what EA Analytics measured. Runs fall for reasons that are not destroyed capacity — economics, export-ban effects, feedstock routing, deliberate throughput cuts, and repair downtime that reverses.

**Why this matters more here than anywhere else in the draft:** everywhere else my number supports a *directional* claim (crude-bearish/product-bullish), and the caveat is not decisive for direction. **§3b converts it into a trade expression, and a crack position sized off "capacity destroyed" is sized wrong if the truth is "runs suppressed and partly recoverable."** Recoverable runs mean the crack compresses back; destroyed capacity means it does not. **That is the whole difference between the trade working and the trade being a carry cost.**

> **⇒ Requested amendment to §3b: attach the proxy caveat where the crack is named, not only in the candidates table. Recommended wording — "the aggregate rests on a RUNS measure, not a capacity measure; sizing must account for the recoverable share, which is unquantified."**

## 4. REFINEMENT — §1c's anti-correlation is real in sign, and it has a computable CEILING that should replace "magnitude unknown"

HAWK states the exposure anti-correlation, then says honestly: *"Magnitude unknown, and I refuse to invent it… what fraction of CPC Blend lifts east of Suez? That fraction IS the magnitude."*

**Correct not to invent it — but the magnitude is bounded above, and a stated ceiling is more useful to Will than an unbounded unknown.**

The tonnage that a Black Sea de-escalation can present to Bab is not the whole CPC system. It is only the **restoration increment**: CPC is running at **~730 kb/d** against a **~1.5–1.6 M bpd** plan, so full restoration returns roughly **+800 kb/d**, and only the east-of-Suez share of *that* reaches Bab.

> **⇒ Ceiling: ≤ ~800 kb/d × (east-of-Suez share), and that is an upper bound because it assumes complete restoration.** Even at a 100% eastern share — which is certainly false, since Mediterranean-bound CPC never transits Suez — the coupling cannot exceed ~800 kb/d of incremental tonnage.

**And a framing point that keeps the finding honest:** this is **mean-reversion to a pre-disruption baseline**, not new tonnage entering the system. The Black Sea disruption temporarily *withheld* barrels from Bab; un-disrupting returns them to their normal routing. **A reader should not price it as an incremental supply-side shock to the Gulf chokepoint.** The anti-correlation is genuine and worth naming — it just has a hard ceiling and a baseline interpretation, and both belong next to it.
