### 1b · The replacement, which all three desks now hold

> **The United States pursued the SAME objective — keep commercial hulls moving — in both theaters, and chose opposite instruments. The variable that selects the instrument is LEVERAGE OVER THE ATTACKER.**
>
> **Black Sea:** the attacker is Ukraine, a dependent client that can be asked to stop. Washington bought restraint with **a phone call** — instant, free, reversible, leaving no institution behind.
> **Gulf:** the attackers are Iran and the Houthis, adversaries who cannot be asked. The only available instruments are **security instruments** — a 14-nation coalition, a Riyadh HQ, a named commander, an 8/12–13 planning meeting — slow, costly, and institution-building by construction.

**This derives, rather than merely agrees with, the durable asymmetry all three desks reached from different directions:** a client-state restraint leaves **no toll, no institution, no standing force** — nothing for risk to be priced into. A coalition builds structure that outlives the threat, and a toll once written has a constituency.

**FALCON supplies the decisive confirmation from inside the Gulf: a carve-out already exists there, and it is not US-brokered — which is the whole finding.** The **Iran–Oman temporary route** is the structural analogue of 8/8, and the comparison is the most durable cross-war result in this forum:

**⇒ Non-transferability, stated as a warning because it is the load-bearing forecast in this section: there will not be a US-brokered carve-out in the Gulf.** Any projection that assumes one is resting on the price version of the mechanism, which is falsified.
