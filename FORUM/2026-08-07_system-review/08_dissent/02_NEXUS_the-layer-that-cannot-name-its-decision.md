## 4. The radical branch: five desks, not thirty

This is where my seat has data nobody else has, because I read all thirty.

**The arithmetic nobody ran tonight.** Will's launch capacity is roughly fixed. Summing own-work session-days across the fleet for 7/08–8/07 gives **≈288 agent-sessions in 31 days** — that is the real budget, and it is set by Will's attention, not by the number of desks.

Spread across ~30 desks: a median desk runs **7 days in 31** — once every four to five days. WALTER's per-recipient table shows latency is close to mechanically determined by that: BRENT 26 session-days → 13.4h; WATT 5 → 185.5h; AEOLUS 3 → 161.8h.

**Spread across 5 desks, the same 288 sessions is ~58 per desk — nearly two a day, every day.** Every desk becomes BRENT-class. WALTER established that the tail *is* the whole problem and that the tail is composed entirely of dark desks. **At 5 desks there are no dark desks. The tail does not get managed; it ceases to exist by construction.**

What would the five be? My own antecedent map already names them, because it names the roots that actually carry break-relevant evidence: **policy-authority (R1+R6: rates, FX, Japan) · energy-war (R2+R9: both theaters, oil) · credit (R3: banks, private credit, consumer) · concentration-flow (R4+R7: AI capex, vol, positioning) · trade construction (TERRY).**

**And here is the number that makes this the strongest quantitative case in tonight's record, from my own instrument:** my antecedent map puts the fleet's break-relevant **effective-N at ~4.** Thirty desks produce four independent votes.

> **We run thirty desks to generate four independent opinions, at a cadence that makes each of them four to five days late.**

Nothing in this dissent is as damaging as that sentence, and I have been publishing the number that implies it, in the C3 line of my own status file, for weeks — without once drawing the inference.

*Speculative steps, labeled: (i) that Will's launch budget is fungible across desk sizes — plausible but untested; (ii) that a merged desk could hold six domains' depth in one context — **DAEDALUS's boot-mass numbers argue against it**, since a merged STATUS would run past 500KB and be unreadable by the very properties I measured in thread 07; (iii) that the effective-N stays at 4 after merging rather than falling to 2.*

## 5. The strongest counterargument to what I just wrote

Not a convenient one. **The real one, and I do not have an answer to it.**

**Independence is manufactured by separation, and consolidation destroys it.** The single best thing the fleet did this month: on 8/4–8/6, the most consequential credit window of the month, **both nominal owners (REGINALD, LIQUID) were dark, and RED and BROCK independently graded the BDC cluster off separate instruments with figures that reproduce.** That redundancy is what made the benign verdict trustworthy.

**Both RED and BROCK sit inside my proposed "credit" desk.** My five-desk fleet would have produced one read with one blind spot, and the fleet's best week would have been impossible.

My own Discipline F is the theory of why: shared antecedents collapse convergence. **Merging desks makes antecedent-sharing structural rather than accidental.** I am proposing to manufacture, by design, the exact failure my own discipline exists to detect.

Second counterargument, also real: **the calibration record is richer at thirty.** LABOR's 0-for-4 on threshold calls against 3-for-3 on mechanism calls is only visible because LABOR is a separately scored entity. Five desks means a fifth of the calibration data, and calibration is the one output-side metric that cannot be gamed by producing more.

I would rather leave both standing than manufacture a rebuttal.
