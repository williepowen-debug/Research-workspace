## SITUATION AS OF MARCH 19, 2026

The Iran war (began Feb 28, 2026) has triggered a fuel shock that is now propagating through the tourism sector in real time. Jet fuel is ~50% above pre-war levels; gasoline at a national average of ~$3.58/gal (up ~27¢ in a single week in early March); and oil-price-sensitive cruise operators face margin compression with no demand signal yet breaking. Airlines say demand remains strong — but the structural damage may be booking itself into the summer season RIGHT NOW.

**This is an inflection window. Spring break and summer travel booking happens Feb–April. If fuel costs stay elevated, the demand destruction may not show up in bookings data until May — but it will be locked in by then.**

## 1. DIRECT TRANSMISSION CHANNELS

### A. Jet Fuel → Airline Fares → Leisure Demand Destruction

**Current data:**
- Jet fuel: **~$7.00/gal national average** (GlobalAir, Mar 18, 2026)
- Jet fuel is **~50% above pre-war levels** (pre-Feb 28) and has "approximately doubled since March" per Cathay Pacific
- American, Delta, United each absorbed **~$400M in higher fuel costs** in Q1 2026 alone
- Cathay Pacific already implementing **fuel surcharge increases** as of mid-March
- Deutsche Bank analysis: fares up **across the board** since war began; last-minute fares (business travelers) especially elevated

**Current airline posture (as of Mar 17 JP Morgan conference):**
- American Airlines: expects Q1 revenue +10% YoY (record); no guidance revision
- Delta CEO Ed Bastian: "strength in every market" — corporate, international, premium leisure, main cabin, domestic
- Demand described as absorbing fare increases "across all segments"

**⚠️ CRITICAL CAVEAT:** This is Q1 data. Spring break demand (March–April) was largely booked *before* the Feb 28 war onset. Summer bookings (June–Aug) are being made NOW at elevated fare levels. Demand destruction may not register until Q2 guidance calls in late April/May.

**The mechanism:**
- Jet fuel = ~20-25% of airline operating costs
- Pass-through to fares is not 1:1 — airlines hedge, absorb margin, and price dynamically
- Leisure travelers are more price-elastic than business; they have substitutes (drive, don't travel)
- A 50% fuel spike historically implies a 15-25% fare increase; at some price point, leisure demand breaks

**Historical elasticity:** Air travel price elasticity of demand ≈ -0.8 to -1.5 for leisure (vs ~-0.3 for business). A 20% fare increase can reduce leisure demand 15-25%.

### B. Gasoline → Road Trip / Domestic Tourism

**Current data:**
- AAA national average: **$3.842/gal** (current, post-shock)
- Earlier March reading: $3.25/gal (jumped ~27¢ in one week); visual capitalist tracking $3.58/gal mid-March
- California: **>$5.00/gal**
- Pre-war baseline (late Jan/Feb 2026): approximately $3.00-3.10/gal

**The mechanism:**
- Road trips are the budget alternative when airfare spikes
- But gasoline >$4/gal historically suppresses road-trip intent, especially for lower-income households
- The squeeze is double: airfares rose (no cheap escape) AND driving costs rose simultaneously
- Drive-to destinations (theme parks, beach towns, national parks) feel this directly

**Key statistic:** A family driving 500 miles round-trip in an average vehicle (~25 MPG):
- At $3.00/gal: ~$60 in fuel
- At $3.85/gal: ~$77 in fuel (+$17, ~28% increase)
- Marginal deterrent for a $1,000+ vacation? Modest alone — but it stacks with hotel/food inflation

### C. Diesel/Fuel Oil → Cruise Industry

**Current data (March 16-17, 2026):**
- Reuters/Reuters confirmed: cruise operators facing direct fuel cost compression
- **Carnival Corp (CCL): most exposed** — does NOT hedge fuel
  - A 10% increase in fuel cost/metric ton = **$145-156M reduction in 2026 net income**
- Royal Caribbean (RCL): hedged ~50% of 2026 fuel costs; $57M impact per 10% fuel move
- Norwegian Cruise Line (NCLH): hedged ~50% of 2026 fuel costs
- Oil prices currently in "mid-$70/barrel range" and rising

**Carnival's hedge-free status in a 50%+ oil shock = significant earnings risk**. The market hasn't fully priced this if the war persists.

**Cruise demand linkage:** ~12M Americans cruise annually, concentrated in FL and the Southeast. Fare increases to offset fuel hurt the price-sensitive mass-market cruise buyer (Carnival's core customer).

## 3. HISTORICAL PARALLELS

### 2008 Oil Shock
- Oil peaked ~$147/barrel (July 2008); jet fuel spiked accordingly
- Air travel demand faltered visibly by summer 2008 — airlines **cut capacity aggressively**
- Airlines for America: U.S. airlines collectively cut ~10% of domestic capacity in 2008
- Several carriers (ATA, Aloha, Skybus, Frontier) went bankrupt
- Hotel occupancy and RevPAR fell sharply through 2008-2009: **last total-year RevPAR decline was 2009 and 2020**
- CoStar/STR note: prior RevPAR declines only in 2009 and 2020 — we're now seeing forecast downgrades pointing toward possible 2026 decline

**Timing insight from 2008:** Demand destruction lagged the fuel shock by ~1-2 quarters. Airlines cut capacity first (visible spring 2008), bookings softened summer 2008, hotel data showed weakness by Q3/Q4 2008. The full consumer recession layer then compounded it.

### 2022 Fuel Spike
- U.S. gas hit $5/gal national average (June 2022)
- Jet fuel spiked alongside Ukraine war/Russia oil disruption
- **Counter-intuitive: demand held surprisingly well in 2022** — pent-up COVID demand overwhelmed fuel price deterrence
- TSA throughput in summer 2022 was near 2019 levels despite fuel costs
- Key lesson: **pent-up demand creates a buffer; once that buffer is exhausted, fuel elasticity reasserts**

**The 2026 difference:** Pent-up demand has been spent. Airlines have described 2024-2025 as the full normalization of post-COVID travel. There's no reservoir of suppressed demand to cushion this shock.

## 5. SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS

### Tourism Employment → UI Claims (FL, NV, HI)

This is the direct LABOR linkage.

- Tourism jobs are **highly elastic**: hotels cut housekeeping staff, restaurants reduce shifts, airlines furlough gate agents
- The lag is 60-90 days from demand softness to employment cuts
- **States to watch:**
  - **Florida:** 1.1M tourism jobs; any meaningful demand decline shows up in FL initial claims disproportionately
  - **Nevada:** Tourism/hospitality = largest employment sector; Las Vegas is a one-sector economy
  - **Hawaii:** ~180K tourism-related jobs in a state of 1.4M people; ~30% labor market exposure
- Our LABOR tracking should segment FL/NV/HI initial claims separately — they'll lead national leisure employment data

**The signal cascade:**
1. Fuel shock → airfares up → forward bookings slow (May/June)
2. Hotels see lower occupancy (June/July)
3. Hotels/restaurants reduce staffing (July/August)
4. FL/NV/HI initial claims spike (August/September)
5. Feeds into LABOR aggregate → Hamilton demand destruction acceleration

### Tourism REITs

Key names with direct leisure/resort exposure:
- **Host Hotels & Resorts (HST):** Largest hotel REIT; February 2026 investor presentation updated; monitor RevPAR guidance
- **Park Hotels & Resorts (PK):** "Soft travel demand, margin pressure, upcoming debt obligations" flagged Dec 2025; already under stress *before* fuel shock
- **Apple Hospitality REIT (APLE):** Upscale select-service; less leisure-dependent than PK
- **Braemar Hotels & Resorts:** Luxury/resort-focused; highest leisure concentration
- **Cruise adjacents:** Carnival (CCL), Royal Caribbean (RCL), Norwegian (NCLH) — all direct fuel exposure

**Bear case for hotel REITs:** RevPAR already forecast at -0.4% for 2026 (before fuel shock). Fuel spike is a demand shock on top of an already-soft RevPAR environment. If RevPAR goes to -2 to -3%, leveraged REITs with high fixed costs face real stress.

### Consumer Discretionary Reallocation

When fuel costs rise, consumers face a hard budget constraint:
- **Non-discretionary transport costs rise** (commuting, daily driving)
- **Vacation budget shrinks** as a residual
- The consumer who was going to spend $3,000 on a Florida trip now spends $500 more on gas getting to work → the vacation doesn't happen

This is the **Hamilton framework in action**: fuel prices as a direct tax on disposable income, with leisure travel as the first expenditure to cut because it's the most deferrable large-ticket item.

### Is Tourism the Canary?

**Yes, with caveats.**

Tourism is the sector most structurally exposed to fuel price transmission because:
1. Fuel cost is *direct* (jet fuel for airlines, gasoline for road trips)
2. Tourism is *purely discretionary* — the most deferrable spending category
3. Tourism is *highly employment-intensive* in states with concentrated exposure
4. Tourism demand is *forward-booked* — damage happens NOW but shows up in data 60-90 days later

**The leading indicator structure:**
- TSA throughput → real-time signal (check weekly)
- Airline booking pace / OAG seat capacity → 30-60 day lead
- Hotel RevPAR → 60-90 day lag
- Initial claims (FL/NV/HI) → 90-120 day lag on the fuel shock

**But the canary is early stage.** Airline executives as of Mar 17 say demand is still strong. This is consistent with Q1 bookings being pre-committed. The *real test* is Q2 booking pace — data coming in April earnings season.
