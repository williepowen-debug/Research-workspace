## SECTION 2: AIRLINE SECTOR SEQUENCING

### (a) Fuel Surcharges — Imposed/Raised

| Date | Event | Detail |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Late 2006** | International surcharges already embedded | Legacy carriers had fuel surcharges on int'l routes since 2004-2006 cycle |
| **Jan 4, 2008** | Domestic fare increase round | $5-$10/segment one-way added (effectively embedded domestic fuel surcharge; reported by NBC News) |
| **Q1 2008** | Multiple rounds of domestic fare hikes | Airlines tried to match rising fuel costs; each attempt often followed within 24-48 hrs by matching carriers |
| **May 2008** | Japan Airlines +40% surcharge | "Unprecedented increase" in fuel cited |
| **Jun-Jul 2008** | International surcharges reach peak levels | United/Delta/American at levels not seen before or since until 2022 |
| **Post-Aug 2008** | Surcharges begin rolling back | As WTI collapsed from $133 to $41, fuel surcharges were gradually eliminated; cruise lines removed them by late 2008/early 2009 |

### (b) Capacity Cuts / Route Elimination

| Date | Event | Scale |
|------|-------|-------|
| **H1 2008** | Capacity still GROWING | ASMs (Available Seat Miles) grew avg +6.7% YoY through H1 2008 even as load factors fell -1.5% — orders placed years earlier delivering |
| **May 21-22, 2008** | **American Airlines announces restructuring** | Scrap 75 planes (mostly regional jets); domestic seat capacity cut 11-12%; first checked bag fee ($15); ~85,000 employee payroll reduction (jobs unspecified) |
| **Jun 2008** | **United announces fleet retirements** | Ground entire Boeing 737 fleet (94 aircraft) + 6 B747s; close "Ted" product (56 A320s moved to mainline); targeting "oldest, least fuel-efficient" aircraft |
| **Jun 2008** | Continental announces fleet cuts | Ground all 737-300s and most 737-500s; keep -800/-900 generation |
| **Jun 2008** | US citizen international departures begin declining | First monthly YoY decrease (BLS data) |
| **Jul 2008** | Industry-wide cuts tallied | By August 2008: 450+ planes to be grounded; 25,000+ staff to be laid off across industry (ATA estimate: US airlines to lose ~$10B for full-year 2008) |
| **Late Jul 2008** | Route cuts to Orlando visible | Pali Research notes domestic airline routes to Orlando reduced seats by ~15% (cited in Disney Q3 FY2008 call, Jul 30) |
| **Sep 2008** | Last month of YoY capacity *increase* | October 2008 = first month of declining ASMs YoY; capacity then fell avg -5.1% through 2009 |

**Note on sequencing:** Airlines ANNOUNCED capacity cuts in May-June 2008 but many cuts were phased over H2 2008 and into 2009. The lag between announcement and effective capacity removal was 1-4 months. Southwest was the sole profitable US carrier in Q1 2008 and initially contemplated expansion; Goldman Sachs projected Ryanair as only profitable European carrier if oil stayed at $150.

## SECTION 3: HOTEL SECTOR — RevPAR SEQUENCING

| Period | RevPAR YoY | Key Detail |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| Jan–Aug 2008 | **POSITIVE** | 12-month trailing RevPAR growth averaged +2.5% through Aug 2008 |
| **Sep 2008** | **-3.1% YoY** | **INFLECTION: First negative month.** Lehman Brothers filed Sep 15, 2008. The coincidence is precise — oil was already falling ($103.90 avg in Sep vs $133.93 peak in Jun) but credit shock accelerated leisure demand collapse |
| Oct 2008 | Worsening | Occupancy falling faster than ADR can compensate |
| **Nov 2008** | **-12.9% YoY** | RevPAR $52.86 (vs $60.68 in Nov 2007); Occupancy -10.6% to 51.9%; ADR -2.5% to $101.81 |
| **Dec 2008** | **-9.7% YoY** | RevPAR $44.99 (vs $49.89 Dec 2007) |
| YTD Nov 2008 | **-1.3% overall** | ADR still +2.8% YTD (pricing held); full collapse was occupancy-driven |
| 2009 | -20.4% trough | Monthly RevPAR bottomed May 2009 at -20.4%; occupancy historical low 54.5%; ADR bottomed 2010 at $98.08 |

**Source:** STR Global, reported via hotelnewsresource.com (Dec 2008) and CoStar retrospective analysis.

**Critical distinction:** ADR (average daily rate) peaked for the decade at **$106.55 in 2008** — hotels *raised rates through the oil spike.* Demand destruction showed first in occupancy. RevPAR (which multiplies rate × occupancy) stayed positive until the credit shock hit simultaneously with demand destruction. This is important for the 2026 template: rate raises can mask early demand destruction.

**Key pattern:** FL showed clear tourism-related claims elevation starting **June-July 2008** when oil peaked — this is the oil transmission channel. Post-Lehman (Sep-Oct) all three states saw the *second wave* as financial crisis compounded oil demand destruction.

| **Jun 2008** | Surcharges increased | Carnival: $9/person/day (first two guests) + $4 for 3rd-5th; Royal Caribbean: $8/person/day + $3; NCL: $11/person/day (new bookings after Jun 20) |
| Jun 2008 | Fuel = 18% of Carnival operating costs (vs 13% in 2007, 9.5% in 2005) | 140% increase vs 2005 per passenger per day for Carnival |
| Late 2008/Early 2009 | Surcharges eliminated | As WTI collapsed to $41, cruise lines rolled back surcharges |

### Demand/Booking Impact

- **Booking tone through H1 2008:** Industry described as "resilient" though price-sensitive; cruise lines reporting surcharges partially recovering fuel cost gap of ~$4-6/passenger/day above historical norms
- **Broader impact noted (June 2008 article):** "Passengers are also affected in their daily lives and when travelling to the ships. Airfares are going up; some flight schedules have been reduced"
- **Itinerary adjustments:** Cruise lines reduced fuel consumption by adjusting sailing speeds and itineraries (slower steaming = less fuel)
- **Carnival full-year 2008:** Net income Q4 FY2008 (ending Nov 30, 2008) = $371M — still profitable despite crisis, but significantly reduced

### Stock Performance (CCL/RCL)
- Both stocks declined sharply through 2008 as oil rose and demand concerns grew
- Peak-to-trough 2008: CCL and RCL approximately -50-60% from 2007 highs
- Analyst 12-month price targets (as of June 2008): CCL ~$47, RCL ~$41 (vs actual prices that fell well below these)

## SECTION 9: SEQUENCING SUMMARY — KEY INFLECTION POINTS

In order of occurrence:

1. **Nov 2007** — WTI ~$95: Cruise lines announce fuel surcharges (first explicit consumer-facing oil cost pass-through in tourism)
2. **Dec 2007** — WTI ~$92: Leisure & Hospitality employment peaks (13,550K)
3. **Jan 2008** — WTI ~$93: Airlines begin domestic fare increases (effectively fuel surcharges)
4. **Feb 2008** — WTI ~$95: Cruise fuel surcharges go live ($5/day); L&H employment decline begins (technically Mar)
5. **Mar 2008** — WTI ~$106: L&H employment decline confirmed; employment falls 12K below peak
6. **May 21, 2008** — WTI ~$125: **American Airlines** announces -11-12% domestic capacity, 75 planes scrapped — first major carrier public restructuring
7. **Jun 2008** — WTI ~$134: **United, Continental announce fleet retirements**; Disney Disneyland attendance declines; US intl departures start declining; cruise surcharges raised; FL initial claims spike >19K/wk
8. **Jul 11, 2008** — WTI $147 intraday: **Oil price peak** — all tourism sectors already under stress
9. **Jul-Aug 2008** — Oil declining: FL initial claims elevated 19-24K/wk; L&H employment -87K from peak; aviation industry-wide restructuring accelerates
10. **Sep 15, 2008** — Lehman Brothers bankruptcy: **Hotel RevPAR turns negative (-3.1% YoY) — first negative month**
11. **Sep-Dec 2008** — Combined oil-demand-destruction + credit shock: L&H employment falls -294K from peak; hotel RevPAR worsens to -12.9%; FL unemployment 7-8%; NV unemployment 7.6-8.8%
12. **Nov-Dec 2008** — WTI $41-57: Oil demand destruction phase complete; tourism collapse now entirely credit/recession-driven

## SECTION 10: TEMPLATE FOR 2026 HAMILTON FRAMEWORK OVERLAY

### What Led vs. Lagged in 2007-08

| Indicator | Lag from Oil Spike Start | Notes |
|-----------|-------------------------|-------|
| Cruise fuel surcharges | **0 months** (Nov 2007 = WTI $95) | Instant pass-through; triggered consumer awareness |
| Airline fare increases | **1-2 months** after each oil move | Competitive matching; domestic surcharges added by Jan 2008 |
| L&H employment decline | **~3-4 months** after WTI crosses $90 | Hiring freezes precede layoffs |
| Airline capacity announcements | **~6-7 months** after oil crosses $85 | Major announcements May-Jun 2008 as oil crossed $125 |
| Disney/theme park attendance decline | **~6-8 months** after WTI $90 | Visible Q3 FY2008 (Apr-Jun calendar 2008) |
| Hotel RevPAR negative YoY | **~9-10 months** after WTI crosses $90 | Broke negative Sep 2008 — but credit shock was the trigger, not oil alone |
| State initial claims spike (FL) | **~7-8 months** after WTI $90 | FL spikes June-July 2008 |
| Hotel RevPAR trough | **~18 months** after WTI $90 | May 2009 at -20.4% |

### Hamilton Demand Destruction Thesis — 2008 Evidence

Hamilton's framework argues that sustained oil spikes cause demand destruction through:
1. **Direct consumer cost** — less disposable income for discretionary travel
2. **Transportation cost shock** — airline surcharges make the trip more expensive
3. **Precautionary behavior** — consumers anticipate future costs and cut spending

**2007-08 Evidence Supports This:**
- Employment declined 3-4 months BEFORE oil peaked (Mar 2008 vs Jul 2008 peak) — forward-looking signal
- Cruise lines explicitly named oil as demand headwind from Q1 2008
- Disney flagged airline capacity reductions to Orlando as attendance risk by Q3 FY2008
- FL/NV tourism-exposed unemployment rate deviated from national around same time

**Critical Confound:** The oil demand destruction cascade was INTERRUPTED and AMPLIFIED by Lehman (Sep 15, 2008). Hotel RevPAR didn't break negative until Lehman — it's impossible to cleanly separate oil-driven demand destruction from credit-crunch spending collapse in Sep-Dec 2008 data. The oil transmission was real and accelerating, but the final collapse was composite.

**For 2026 Application:** If WTI sustains $100+, watch for these precise sequences in this order:
- Cruise surcharge announcements (instant)
- Airline domestic fare increases (weeks)
- L&H employment GROWTH rate slowing, then declining (3-4 months)
- Airline capacity cut announcements (6-7 months from trigger)
- Theme park attendance soft data (2-3 quarters)
- Hotel RevPAR going negative (requires occupancy to break, not just ADR)
- State-level claims spikes in FL/NV/HI (6-8 months)
